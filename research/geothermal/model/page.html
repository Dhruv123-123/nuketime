Heat-Flux Dart and SENTINEL-COX

How to estimate hidden hydrothermal with real error bars, and the one sensor to build. Built from 109 agents: 34 data reviews, 6 rival model designs and 3 judges, 30 physical-build deep-dives, a scorer and 18 red-team votes.

Sensor to buildClustered 3 m logging heat-flux dart

Six temperature sensors from 0.5 to 2.95 m plus a heater that measures rock conductivity in place, so it reads heat flux rather than a temperature. Darts go in clusters of 3. About $160-230 in parts and $300 installed per dart. 30 units in 6-8 weeks.

ModelSENTINEL-COX

A value-of-information land engine (risked NPV per acre on 500 m parcels) wrapped around a Cox-process count of hidden systems that corrects for who searched where and how hard. AI extraction of scanned old temperature logs supplies the main new data.

What dominates the uncertainty

Ranked by how much each unknown moves the answer. The top three are what a sensor can shrink.

  1. Detection gap. How many systems hide where nobody has looked. This moves the hidden count by 2-5x, and 97% of the Great Basin has no 2 m reading.
  2. Permeability and flow per well. About 10x on MWe at a fixed temperature.
  3. Hydrologic masking and lid thickness. A shallow sensor's hit rate runs from about 0.8 down to about 0, depending on the cover (Argenta, Granite Springs, Kimama).
  4. Reliability of each cheap sensor under each cover type. Nothing has been published on this, and it swings value of information by more than 2x.
  5. Temperature at 0.5-2 km. There is almost no public data between 300 and 1,500 m.

The red team found one lesson that runs through every well-based tool: ranch wells sample outflow on valley floors. They mostly find again the kind of system that is already known. Masked ground needs a detector that reads heat flux, not water temperature.

The model

  • L1, readings. Each source gets its own error model: equilibrium logs, censored cold holes, skewed oil-well temperatures, mixed driller readings, 2 m and dart flux, chemistry.
  • L2, column. Each 500 m cell carries a latent temperature column with a conductive part, an upflow term, an outflow plume along groundwater heads, a cover type and a permeability class.
  • L3, count. A log-Gaussian Cox process on a 2 km mesh. Detection is thinned by dated search effort per channel and cover type, and sizes follow a lognormal-Pareto tail.
  • L4, decisions. VOI-Bank ranks every next action (buy, place darts, visit a well, drill) by expected value of sample information per dollar. The rule is to lock ground wherever RNPV per acre exceeds 3x the 10-year holding cost.
  • Certainty. About 4,000 joint posterior draws are summed draw by draw into regional MWe bands. They are checked by simulation-based calibration on synthetic Nevadas and by hindcasts frozen at past dates. USGS and NREL totals are held out as checks and never fitted.

Cheapest first validation (desk, about $5k, 10 weeks). First, benchmark AI log extraction against the 786-hole USGS archive (pass: median gradient error ≤10%). Second, fit Nevada with calibration on 20 synthetic Nevadas. Third, replay the bank from 2005 (pass: lift ≥1.5x over "lock the USGS top 10%").

Judge scores, out of 75: VOI-Bank 60, CREAM-COX 57.5, THERMOS-B 50.5, GHOST-SBI 49.5, GeoFM-West 47.5, HEATFIELD-PU 44.5. The final model is a hybrid built on VOI-Bank.

The dart

It reads heat flux (the temperature gradient times the rock's conductivity measured in place). Flux survives a dry lid that hides heat from a single 2 m reading. At Granite Springs, gradient holes run above 220 C/km, yet the 2 m survey peaked at only 3.4 C and missed it.

After the red team, the claim is smaller. Flux sensitivity is about 2-3x better than the logged 2 m probe, not the 8-15x first claimed. It reaches 100 C fluid at about 260-570 m under a dry lid, not 1 km.

Two modes. On BLM land it runs 30-60 day campaigns under casual use. On optioned private land and our own leases it records for 12 months.

Cost. About $4-5k per km² the first time, and $2.5-3k per km² when the hardware is reused. It goes on lines and high-value cells, not wall to wall.

No build cleared 15/25, so the earlier conclusion holds: the bank is the company and the sensor is its moat. The five well and rig builds all failed for the same reason. They see water that has already left the upflow.

Against the earlier sensor pick

  • Kept: casual-use sensing on open land under IM 2026-024, hand-placed E-field MT nodes for thick lids, and the ranch-well visit as the way we sign options.
  • Changed: the 2 m probe becomes the 3 m dart cluster. The logged 2 m probe stays only as a baseline on test lines.
  • Changed: the ranch-well visit is no longer the main detector. It is now the second detector for swept ground, and it carries a helium sampler and a temperature port.
  • Changed: no same-day e-signing. Expect a letter of intent, then a notarised option within 7 days.
  • Changed: lock rights up-gradient of a warm well, at the range front, not only on the farm that showed the warm water.

Kill conditions

  • The bankable hidden resource on land we can lock is under about 2 GWe P50.
  • Replay lift is under 1.2x against "lock the USGS top 10%".
  • Sensors can shrink less than 30% of the regional band. Then the company is a plain land bank.
  • Options cost more than $20/ac, or more than 70% of owners refuse.
  • The dart's field floor is above 0.4 W/m², or its gain over the 2 m probe is under 1.5x.
