# M12: Seismology for blind geothermal exploration (large-N nodes, ambient noise, microseismicity, Vp/Vs, attenuation, DAS on dark fibre)

Date: 23 Oct 2026. This file builds on `../../evidence/L14-geophysics.md` (Zanskar NREL CRADA note), `../../evidence/Z03-data-methods.md` (Zanskar seismology staff and the LBNL CRADA, OSTI 3015294), `../../evidence/L22-structural-ml.md` section 2.13 (seismicity and InSAR as permeability signals, San Emidio PSET report OSTI 1986084), `../../../hardware/evidence/H03-mt-seismic-nodes.md` (SmartSolo node specs, Hengill 498 nodes, Cornwall 450 nodes, the "add a geophone to every E-node" idea) and `M08-magnetotellurics.md`. It does not repeat those. It asks one question: **has seismology ever actually found blind upflow, and how should it enter our model and our sensor kit?**

Web budget used: 10 searches and 9 fetches. The OSTI page for the Zanskar/LBNL CRADA (https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3015294) and one NSF PAR paper (https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10408431) could not be fetched. Their content here comes from earlier repo files or memory and is marked (unverified) where needed.

## Bottom line

Seismology is very good at mapping **structure** (basin depth, buried faults, fault intersections, fracture zones) and **active stress** (which faults are slipping now). It is weak at seeing **heat** directly. I found **no published case where seismology was the primary tool that discovered a blind hydrothermal system.** Every "seismic detection of hidden geothermal" in the open literature is **retrospective**, at a field that drilling had already found. Brawley (Imperial Valley) was "located in two days" by DAS on 28 km of Zayo dark fibre, but oil and gas wells had already found Brawley. The San Emidio passive emission tomography (1,302 stations) lit up the known reservoir and a southern extension that wells had found in 2015-2017. The greenfield Crescent Valley target from the same method was never shown to be drilled. Coso and Brady used microseismicity, Vp/Vs and attenuation to split known reservoirs into compartments. So seismology is a **permeability and geometry likelihood, not a heat detector.** It is still worth a lot to us, for three reasons. (1) Free public catalogs (the UNR Nevada relocated catalog 2008-2023, NCEDC, SCEDC, ComCat) give a "critically stressed fault" feature over every valley in the Great Basin at almost no cost. (2) Ambient-noise Vs maps of sediment thickness and buried range-front faults fix the biggest error in the conductive heat model, which is basin depth. (3) Zanskar is clearly betting on passive seismic and DAS. It is hiring a passive-imaging postdoc and a DAS-processing intern, and its LBNL CRADA used large-N arrays to "guide well siting". So we must at least match the cheap version. **The sensor answer is to not build a seismic company.** Add a $40 to $60 3-component geophone channel to every H03 E-field MT node, so one carpet records MT and ambient noise together. On top of that, rent a DAS interrogator for a week at a time on dark telecom fibre that already crosses target valleys, as a regional seismic "scout line". Joint Vs plus resistivity at the same 250 m sites is data nobody else holds, and it is exactly what separates a hot clay cap from cold basin clay.

## Findings

### F1. What has actually "detected" hidden geothermal with seismology (all retrospective)
- **Brawley, Imperial Valley CA, DAS on dark fibre (LBNL, Rice, Zayo; DOE GTO).** About 28 km of unlit Zayo telecom fibre ran south from a Calipatria amplifier hut, across the Brawley Seismic Zone and next to the North Brawley plant. It was recorded continuously from Nov 2020 for several months (https://energygeosciences.lbl.gov/2020/12/16/field-access-allows-first-ever-geothermal-basin-characterization-using-dark-fiber/). Ambient-noise interferometry with spatial stacking, using a joint fundamental-mode and overtone surface-wave inversion, gave a **2D Vs model to about 3 km depth**. It found a **previously unmapped high-Vs, low-Vp/Vs body under the Brawley system**, read as hydrothermal mineralization and low porosity, and it agrees with heat flow, gravity and well logs (Rodríguez Tribaldos et al., https://essopenarchive.org/users/527791/articles/596714-using-dark-fiber-and-distributed-acoustic-sensing-to-characterize-a-geothermal-system-in-the-imperial-valley-southern-california). ThinkGeoEnergy reported that DAS "located the geothermal resource in two days". Brawley has no hot springs or fumaroles, but oil and gas drilling had already confirmed it (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/research-explores-the-use-of-telecom-fiber-for-geothermal-seismic-sensing/). **Lesson:** in a hot sedimentary basin, the signature of a blind system can be a *fast* (sealed, mineralized) cap, not a slow zone. Our model must not hard-code "low velocity = hot".
- **San Emidio and Crescent Valley NV, Passive Seismic Emission Tomography (PSET) (Ormat, LBNL, SubTerraSeis; DE-EE0007698; Warren et al. 2019).** Microseismic Inc. recorded **1,302 stations with 6 geophones each for about 180 h at San Emidio**, and **989 stations for about 75 h at Crescent Valley**. Image volumes were about 1.7 x 2.2 km and 300 to 600 m deep. No discrete earthquakes were found at Crescent Valley. Only beamformed ambient "emission" energy was used. At San Emidio, high-energy zones matched the known reservoir and the **newly discovered southern resource (>160 °C, found 2015-2017)**. Low-energy zones marked its eastern edge. The best way to separate productive wells from tight wells was **PSET semblance plus gravity and magnetic horizontal gradients**. MT resistivity added nothing, because basin-fill clay confuses it. The test used existing wells (17-21, 18-21, 25-21, 25A-21, 78-20, all producing >320 °F fluid), so it is retrospective. At Crescent Valley, energy anomalies sit basin-ward and down-dip of the hot springs on the range-front fault, alongside a conductor read as upflow. That target was proposed for Phase 2 on private land, and I found no drilling result (https://gdr.openei.org/files/1386/WarrenFinalReport.pdf ; data at https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1441). Ian Warren, the first author, is now at Zanskar (Z03).
- **Coso CA, relocated microseismicity plus joint Vp/Vs tomography (USGS).** More than 60,000 events from 1996-2009 were relocated with waveform cross-correlation, to relative errors of tens of meters. Seismicity outlines through-going faults plus much *diffuse* fracturing tied to permeability. **Vp/Vs splits the reservoir into two compartments** at -0.5 to 1.5 km below sea level, with a high-Vp/Vs ridge between the main field and the east flank (https://www.usgs.gov/publications/using-micro-seismicity-and-seismic-velocities-map-subsurface-geologic-and-hydrologic ; catalog paper https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70191533).
- **Brady NV, PoroTomo (238 nodes over 1.5 x 0.5 km for more than 2 weeks, plus vibroseis and 8.6 km of DAS).** Surface Vp varies by more than 50% and tracks mapped hot spots and faults. Shallow Vp/Vs is 3.5 to 7. **Attenuation (low Q) is highest in hot or heavily fractured zones, and Qs/Qp is largest along major faults and deepens under the injection point and fumaroles** (Matzel et al., SSA 2018, https://seismosoc.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/WED.Monroe.1445.Matzel.pdf). The active 3D tomography is public (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1070). Brady was already a producing field.
- **Soda Lake and Dixie Valley NV, ambient noise (UNR NSL, Tibuleac; $427,847 DOE).** Green's functions came out of a 6 km-aperture array at Soda Lake. The team expected a 5 to 15 Hz attenuation contrast between stations inside and outside the Dixie Valley production area. No blind find was reported (https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2015/06/f23/Track3_EGS_4.2_Tibuleac_3_0.pdf).
- **Abroad (from H03, not re-checked).** Hengill, Iceland: 498 nodes imaged shallow low-velocity zones and an intrusion at 3 to 5 km. Cornwall, UK: 450 nodes gave fracture orientation and stress that matched the wells. Both were known fields.
- **Zanskar Big Blind (Dec 2025).** The public release credits only a "prospecting toolkit" and an "AI-prediction engine". It gives two intermediate-depth wells drilled in Jul-Aug 2025 that hit a permeable reservoir at about 2,700 ft and about 250 °F. It does **not** say seismology was used (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-reveals-big-blind-the-discovery).

### F2. What Zanskar is building in seismology
- The LBNL CRADA (OSTI 3015294, Jan 2026) used **large-N arrays of low-cost seismometers, micro-earthquake tomography and "ambient fracture-seismic emissions"** to map faults and fluid flow, and the results "guided well siting" (Z03; the page itself was not fetchable this run, so this is unverified).
- Open roles in 2025-2026: "Postdoctoral Researcher - Passive Seismic Imaging", covering ambient noise tomography and microseismic analysis for basement depth, layering, fault structure and stress, with ObsPy pipelines at scale (https://jobs.lever.co/Zanskar/8e14d7bc-d4e6-414a-afc2-064b755d78d7). There is also a "Seismology Intern - DAS Processing" role (https://workingreen.jobs/offers/seismology-intern-position-das-processing-at-zanskar-salt-lake-city-utah). An older IRIS post asked for large-N array processing (https://ds.iris.edu/message-center/thread/8021/).
- **Read:** Zanskar uses seismology mainly for **basement depth and fault geometry at a prospect**, which tells it where to put a well. It is not a regional blind-system detector. Fibre DAS is on its roadmap.

### F3. Public microseismicity catalogs are a free regional feature
- **UNR Nevada relocated catalog 2008-2023 (Trugman, SRL 2024).** NSL analyst picks were located with a 3D state velocity model, a subset was relocated by cross-correlation, and magnitudes were recomputed with new station terms. CC-BY-4.0 on Zenodo (https://zenodo.org/records/11167510). The event count and completeness magnitude were not on the page. I believe it is on the order of 10^5 events with Mc around 1 in the western Nevada network core (unverified).
- **INGENIOUS already carries "earthquake density" rasters** (GDR 1391; L22 section 2.9). The Nevada Play Fairway used earthquake frequency as one of its 9 inputs (L01, L14). Wannamaker's Utah fairway used seismicity in its permeability layer (L22 section 2.12). A 2026 NMFk ML paper with 126 attributes did *not* use seismicity at all (https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Vesselinov.pdf). So the feature is public but under-used and crudely built: a smoothed count, not a "seismicity on critically stressed fault segments" score.
- **Operating fields change seismicity rates.** WHOLESCALE at San Emidio showed that pumping lowers microseismicity and shutdowns raise pore pressure (L22 section 2.13). Our training labels must therefore drop or flag catalog events inside operating fields. If they are kept, the model learns "seismicity = power plant" and not "seismicity = natural permeability".
- **Coverage gap:** network station spacing in central and eastern Nevada, western Utah, southern Idaho and SE Oregon is often 30 to 80 km (unverified). Mc there is probably 1.5 to 2.5. Small swarms over blind systems in those areas go unseen. This gap is real and can be filled cheaply (see "Gaps").

### F4. What each seismic observable means physically (for the likelihood model)
- **Vs from ambient noise (surface waves, 0.2 to 20 Hz):** this mainly gives basin fill thickness and buried fault offsets. Sediment Vs is about 0.3 to 1.5 km/s and bedrock about 2.5 to 3.5 km/s (textbook, unverified). That contrast makes ambient noise one of the best ways to find the **depth to bedrock and the buried range-front fault**, where Great Basin upflow lives. Heat effects on Vs are small (about 1 to 2% per 100 °C in dry rock, unverified). Fluids, clay and fractures matter more.
- **Vp/Vs:** high values (above about 1.9) suggest liquid-saturated, fractured or clay-rich rock. Low values (below about 1.65) suggest gas or steam, or quartz and silica-sealed rock (as at Brawley, and as in the classic Geysers steam case; unverified). The sign therefore depends on the system type. It needs P and S picks from local earthquakes, or a Vp model from active or passive P waves.
- **Attenuation (1/Q):** this rises with temperature, partial saturation and fracture density (Brady). It is the most "heat-sensitive" seismic observable, but also the noisiest (scattering versus intrinsic loss). It needs earthquakes or well-calibrated noise amplitudes.
- **Microseismicity:** it marks faults that are critically stressed now, and therefore permeable now. It is a direct permeability proxy, but only where events happen above the detection threshold.
- **Emission tomography (PSET/fracture seismic):** this measures where steady low-level acoustic energy comes from. It worked retrospectively at San Emidio. The physics is debated, and a cultural or pump noise source can masquerade as a geologic one (unverified critique).

### F5. DAS on dark telecom fibre
- One interrogator turns tens of km of existing fibre into a sensor every few meters. Brawley got a 3 km-deep Vs section from about 2 to 7 days of noise (Rodríguez Tribaldos; ThinkGeoEnergy). The data are public: "Imperial Valley Dark Fiber Project Continuous DAS Data" (https://catalog-old.data.gov/dataset/imperial-valley-dark-fiber-project-continuous-das-data-a0338).
- **Western US fibre geography helps us.** Long-haul routes follow I-80, US-95, US-50, US-93, I-15 and the railways. These cross many Basin-and-Range valleys at right angles to the range-front faults, which is the ideal geometry for a 2D Vs section across a buried fault (route claim unverified; carrier maps are not public in detail).
- **Limits:** it is single-component (axial strain), the coupling is poor where fibre hangs in conduit, it gives a line not an area, and access needs a carrier deal. Zayo did this for LBNL. Commercial dark-fibre DAS rental terms are not public (unverified). A DAS interrogator (for example OptaSense, Silixa, AP Sensing) costs about $100k to $300k to buy, or about $5k to $15k per week to rent (unverified).

## What dominates uncertainty (for this slice)
1. **No blind-test record.** No published seismic method has a hit rate on *undrilled* targets. Every success above is post hoc. The likelihood ratio for "a seismic anomaly means a hot, permeable system" is therefore unknown. It could be anywhere from about 1.5 to about 5 (my guess, unverified).
2. **Sign ambiguity by system type.** Fast (sealed) caps at Brawley, slow fractured zones at Brady and Hengill, and low or high Vp/Vs at steam versus liquid systems. A model that assumes one sign will be wrong in half the settings.
3. **Detection threshold of catalogs.** Absence of events in a sparse-network area means "unknown", not "quiet". Mc varies by a factor of about 30 in seismic moment across the Great Basin (unverified).
4. **Anthropogenic contamination.** Producing fields, mines, quarry blasts and pumping all change local seismicity and emission energy.
5. **Basin depth.** This is the main control on where conductive heat and upflow can sit, and it is still poorly known in most valleys. Seismology is the cheapest way to cut this uncertainty.

## What a sensor could fill
- **Basin depth and buried range-front fault position along every target valley** (ambient-noise Vs from nodes or DAS). This feeds both the conductive temperature-at-depth model and the structural-setting model.
- **Local microseismicity at Mc around -0.5 to 0** in areas where the public network sees only M1.5+. Six to ten cheap 3C stations at 1 to 2 km spacing for 2 to 3 months would see swarms on the fault segments that matter (unverified sizing).
- **Co-located Vs and resistivity.** This is the key discriminator between "conductive and slow" (cold basin clay or brine) and "conductive with a fast or high-Q root" (a hot sealed cap over upflow). No public dataset has both at a 250 m pitch.
- **Shallow attenuation (Qs/Qp) in the top 200 m**, read with the 2 m temperature probes (H01) as a hot-fluid indicator.

### Concrete sensor recommendation
1. **"Seismo-telluric" E-node, an add-on to H03.** Add a 4.5 Hz 3C geophone (about $40 to $60) to each E-field MT node on the same 24-bit ADC and the same GNSS clock, sampled at 250 Hz overnight and in bursts. With a 400-site carpet (250 m pitch) over 2 to 4 weeks, we get MT plus ambient-noise Vs plus a dense microseismic catalog in one deployment. Extra cost is about $25k per 400-node fleet (unverified BOM). Storage needs are about 3 GB per node per month (H03 SmartSolo figure).
2. **DAS scout lines.** Rent an interrogator for 3 to 7 days on dark fibre that crosses a target valley (carrier deal needed), and invert for a 2D Vs and fault section to 3 km depth (the Brawley recipe). Use it to rank valleys *before* buying options. The fibre already exists, so this is regional coverage without permits.
3. **Long-dwell micro-network.** Leave 8 of the nodes (with a larger battery and solar) for 3 to 6 months on each optioned prospect to capture swarms. The data also become baseline seismicity records that a buyer must have later for induced-seismicity permitting. That is a sellable data room item.

## Implications for a better model
- **Use seismicity as a permeability likelihood, not a heat feature.** Replace the smoothed "earthquake density" raster with per-fault-segment features: event count above a *local* Mc (taken from a detectability map), b-value, swarm flags and depth distribution. Join these to Quaternary fault segments and their slip and dilation tendency. Mask operating fields and mines.
- **Model "no events" as missing data where Mc is high.** Carry a detectability layer so that quiet areas with poor coverage do not get penalized.
- **Make the seismic-anomaly sign depend on the system type.** Use a latent class for system type (fault-hosted Basin-and-Range, sedimentary-basin hosted, volcanic, steam-dominated), each with its own expected Vs, Vp/Vs and Q signs. Learn these from Brawley, Brady, Coso, San Emidio and Hengill.
- **Feed ambient-noise basin depth into the conductive temperature model** as a hard covariate (sediment insulation and depth to the permeable bedrock contact). This is likely a bigger gain for the overall resource estimate than any direct "seismic heat" signal.
- **Treat a joint Vs-and-resistivity cell at a 250 m pitch as the strongest field likelihood.** Calibrate it on our own TG-hole temperatures. Each carpet plus a hole becomes a private label (as in M08).
- **Value of information:** a DAS line costs less than the option fees on one valley, so run DAS first on valleys where the posterior entropy (heat known, structure unknown) is highest. Then carpet only the valleys where DAS shows a buried step in bedrock under a 2 m or water-well temperature anomaly.
- **Do not expect seismology to beat Zanskar on its own.** Our edge is the *joint* cell (seismic, MT, temperature) at ranch and state parcels we option cheaply, plus regional DAS screening that Zanskar does not publish.
