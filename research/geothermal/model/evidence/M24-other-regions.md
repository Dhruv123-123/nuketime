# M24: Hidden hydrothermal outside Nevada. How much is there, region by region, and how well is each region measured?

Date: 23 Oct 2026. Slice M24, model track. This builds on `../../evidence/L05-usgs-assessment.md` (the 2008 USGS national numbers), `M01-usgs-assessment-methods.md` (the state numbers share one multiplier), `M03-play-fairway-results.md` (Hawaii, Snake River Plain, Cascades and Utah play fairway results), `M23-hydrology-masking.md` (rain curtain and cold-aquifer masks), `../../evidence/R11-canada-tenure.md` (BC tenure) and `../../evidence/L19-international.md` (Mexico). It does not repeat them.

Method note: I read the USGS 2008 fact sheet PDF directly and pulled its Table 1. I ran 6 web searches. The shared web-fetch budget ran out partway, so several regional numbers below come from what I already knew. Each of those is marked **(unverified)**.

## Bottom line

Nevada is only 14.5% of the USGS 2008 mean undiscovered resource (4,364 of 30,033 MWe). California holds 37.8% (11,340 MWe). The other eleven states hold **14,329 MWe (48%)**. Hawaii (2,435), Oregon (1,893), Idaho (1,872), Alaska (1,788), New Mexico (1,484), Utah (1,464), Colorado (1,105) and Arizona (1,043) each carry 1 to 2.4 GWe of mean undiscovered resource. Canada and Mexico add several more GWe (unverified, not assessed in the same way). The key fact for our model is this: **the ratio of undiscovered to identified resource goes up as data density goes down.** Arizona has 40x more undiscovered than identified resource, Colorado 37x, Hawaii 13.5x, New Mexico 8.7x and Utah 8.0x. Nevada has 3.1x and California 2.1x. These high ratios are not measurements. They are favorability times area, spread with one shared national multiplier (M01), in places with almost no wells. So outside Nevada the "hidden" number is both **bigger in total and much less certain**. That is where a cheap sensor gives the most value of information, and also where Zanskar's Nevada-trained models and the INGENIOUS layers are thinnest. The best near-term regions for our rights bank are: **(1) western Utah and the Black Rock-Sevier basins** (good data, SITLA trust land, real buyers, Fervo's Cape Station nearby); **(2) southern Idaho and eastern Oregon Basin and Range** (lots of private ranch land, large masked resource under cold aquifers); **(3) the Imperial Valley and Salton Trough** (the classic blind basin, where gradient holes found almost every field, with high prices but crowded land); and **(4) the New Mexico/Arizona rift basins** (very low data, high ratio, private ranch land, Lightning Dock proof). Hawaii, Alaska, BC, Yukon and Mexico are big on paper. But their rights regimes (state-reserved minerals, public tender, state concessions) or tiny grids keep them as later legs, not first targets.

Plain reading:
- **Outside NV and CA there are 14.3 GWe of mean undiscovered resource.** That is 3.3x Nevada's.

## 2. Region by region

Each row gives: what the hidden potential is, why it is hidden, the data density, and the rights fit. "Installed" figures are approximate and mostly unverified this run.

### 2.1 Utah (USGS mean undiscovered 1,464 MWe; 6 identified systems)
- **Hidden potential.** Western Utah is inside the Great Basin. It was flagged in 2008 as "high favorability but few identified systems" (L05). The Utah Geological Survey reported in 2012 that the **Black Rock Desert basin** south of Delta holds a deep sedimentary resource over about 100 square miles. It said this could support "hundreds of MW", and nine wells were drilled to test the idea. Source: ThinkGeoEnergy summary of the Salt Lake Tribune report, 30 Sep 2012, https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/the-utah-geological-survey-finds-geothermal-hot-spot-in-utah/. This is a blind, basin-hosted resource with no hot springs over most of it.
- **Next-gen proof next door.** Fervo's Cape Station (Beaver County, next to Utah FORGE and Roosevelt Hot Springs) is the largest EGS build. Press in Oct 2026 describes a 4 GW goal there (Deseret News, 14 Oct 2026, https://www.deseret.com/environment/2026/10/14/fervos-4-gigawatt-geothermal-plant-has-big-goals; headline only, detail unverified). It is EGS, not hidden hydrothermal. But it proves there are buyers and transmission in the Milford corridor.
- **Play fairway.** Wannamaker et al. (2020), *Eastern Great Basin PFA*, OSTI 1695591 (in L01). Utah PFA used MT heavily.
- **Installed (unverified):** about 100-150 MWe from Blundell, Cove Fort and Thermo, plus Cape Station Phase 1 coming online.
- **Data density: medium to high.** UGS is a strong data steward. There are many oil, gas and water wells in the Sevier and Black Rock basins, FORGE data, and Great Basin coverage in INGENIOUS and USGS ML maps.
- **Rights fit: good.** BLM plus SITLA state trust land (R05) plus private land. Zanskar won nothing in Utah (Z04).

### 2.2 Oregon (USGS mean undiscovered 1,893 MWe; 29 identified systems)
- **Two very different provinces.** (a) **Southeast Oregon Basin and Range** (Alvord, Crump, Neal Hot Springs, Vale). This is Nevada-like, and flagged in 2008 as under-explored (L05). (b) **The High Cascades and Newberry**, where a rain curtain scrubs the top 0.5-1 km (M23). Newberry has over 300 C at about 3 km (AltaRock EGS work, unverified figure).
- **Masking evidence.** For the whole US Cascade arc, Ingebritsen and Mariner measured only about 240 MW of advective heat in thermal springs, out of about 1,050 MW in total (M23). That is thermal heat, not electric power. Most of the arc's heat never surfaces.
- **Play fairway:** Oregon Cascades PFA (OSU, GDR 646) stopped at Phase 1 (M03).
- **Installed (unverified):** about 30-40 MWe (Neal Hot Springs about 22 MWe, plus small units at Klamath Falls and Paisley).
- **Data density: low to medium.** Southeast Oregon is partly inside the Great Basin rasters. The Cascades have sparse deep wells, and masking makes shallow data misleading. DOGAMI keeps a thermal-spring and well database (contents unverified).
- **Rights fit: good in the southeast.** There is BLM land and large private ranches. In the Cascades it is fair to poor: national forest, wilderness and tribal concerns.

### 2.3 Idaho and the Snake River Plain (USGS mean undiscovered 1,872 MWe; 36 identified systems)
- **Hidden potential.** The Snake River Plain is a hot-spot track with high heat flow (about 110-130 mW/m2 under the aquifer, M23). It is masked by a cold aquifer up to about 1 km thick on the eastern plain (M23). The **Project Hotspot MH-2 well at Mountain Home** (2012) hit an artesian flow of about 150 C at about 1.7 km under no surface sign. Source: Shervais et al. / Nielson et al., Stanford and GRC papers (unverified exact depth and temperature).
- **Play fairway:** Shervais et al. (2020, 2021), SRP PFA, GDR 1316. Camas-1 well (2023): about 70 C/km and 66-80 C at 533-618 m. That was a hit on seal and permeability, but too cool for power (M03, L01).
- **Installed (unverified):** about 15-18 MWe at Raft River.
- **Data density: low to medium.** Many shallow irrigation wells exist, but they mostly see the cold aquifer. There are few deep wells (INL holes, Kimberly, MH-2).
- **Rights fit: very good.** Southern Idaho is ranch and farm land with irrigation wells everywhere, which suits the ranch drop probe. The Idaho state endowment also holds land. Idaho Power is the buyer.

### 2.4 Cascades (Washington, northern California) and the Modoc Plateau
- **USGS:** Washington has 300 MWe mean undiscovered and 1 identified system. The northern California volcanic share is buried in CA's 11,340 MWe.
- **Classic blind proof: Medicine Lake (CA).** Fourmile Hill and Telephone Flat were drilled in the 1980s-90s and found high temperatures (over 230 C, unverified) under a volcano with almost no hot springs. This is the textbook rain-curtain blind system. It has stayed undeveloped for permitting reasons, not resource reasons (unverified).
- **Washington PFA** (Mt St Helens, Mt Baker, Wind River) reached Phase 2 (M03).
- **Data density: low.** There are few deep wells, deep water tables and strong masking.
- **Rights fit: poor.** Most of it is national forest and sacred-site land. The model should still score it, because the resource is real. But rights are slow.

### 2.5 Imperial Valley and Salton Trough (inside California's 11,340 MWe)
- **Why it matters: this is the best evidence that sediment-buried blind fields are common and large.** East Mesa, Heber and North Brawley have no strong hot springs. They were found by heat-flow and temperature-gradient drilling in the late 1960s and 1970s. OpenEI area pages: https://en.openei.org/wiki/East_Mesa_Geothermal_Area ; https://openei.org/wiki/North_Brawley_Geothermal_Area ; USGS OFR 81-834, https://pubs.usgs.gov/of/1981/0834/report.pdf (details unverified this run).
- **Size.** NREL's Salton Sea report (2023) put potential at about **1,800 MW** for the Salton Sea area (headline, Renewable Energy World, https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/solar/geothermal-visual-nrel-s-new-salton-sea-report-shows-1-800-mw-geothermal-potential/). A GRC conceptual-model paper by Kaspereit et al. gives a new reserve estimate for the Salton Sea field (Geothermal Resource Group, https://geothermalresourcegroup.com/publications/updated-conceptual-model-and-reserve-estimate-for-the-salton-sea-geothermal-field-imperial-valley-california; their number, about 2,950 MWe, is unverified). Installed in Imperial County is about 700+ MWe (unverified), with about 400 MWe at the Salton Sea field itself. Lithium brine is extra value (L12, A02).
- **Hidden upside.** The trough is a pull-apart rift filled with about 5-6 km of sediment. Its hidden fields sit under a flat, farmed surface. Spreading-center segments run south into Mexico (Cerro Prieto, Laguna Salada) and under the Salton Sea itself. Ground away from the known KGRAs (for example the west side and the southern border) is less tested (unverified).
- **Data density: high in the KGRAs, medium elsewhere.** There are many old gradient holes and CalGEM well records. Data is legible, so the edge is smaller.
- **Rights fit: medium.** It is private farmland plus BLM and IID, and the land is crowded with BHE, Ormat, CTR and EnergySource. Power prices are high and CA demand is firm.

### 2.6 Arizona (USGS mean undiscovered 1,043 MWe; 2 identified systems; ratio 40x)
- **Hidden potential.** Basin and Range in the south and southeast (San Bernardino Valley, Safford, Clifton, Willcox). Deep basins are full of alluvium, so heat is hidden in the same way as Nevada. The AZGS was a major NGDS data contributor. Its geothermal well and spring datasets are on the GDR and NGDS (D04).
- **Installed:** none for power (unverified).
- **Data density: very low** for deep temperature. There are many groundwater wells (ADWR) that have never been logged for temperature.
- **Rights fit: very good** for private ranch land and Arizona State Land Department trust land. BLM is also present.
- **Why it matters to us:** the 40x ratio means the USGS number here is almost pure prior. A few dozen ranch-well temperature logs would move it a lot, either up or down.

### 2.7 New Mexico (USGS mean undiscovered 1,484 MWe; 7 identified systems; ratio 8.7x)
- **Proof of blind.** Lightning Dock (Animas Valley) was found in 1948 when an irrigation well hit boiling water (unverified date detail). It now runs about 15 MWe and is owned by Zanskar, which raised output 140% with one deep well (Z02). Valles Caldera (Baca) is a high-temperature known system on federal preserve land.
- **Rio Grande rift.** LANL's "hydrogeologic windows" PFA and the Tularosa Basin PFA (Fort Bliss / McGregor Range) covered it (M03, L01).
- **Data density: low.** There are many oil and gas wells in the Permian (southeast) but few in the rift basins. NMBGMR holds a heat-flow and well-temperature archive (contents unverified).
- **Rights fit: good.** New Mexico State Land Office trust land, private ranches and BLM.

### 2.8 Colorado and Montana (1,105 and 771 MWe mean undiscovered)
- Not in the brief, but both have high ratios (37x, 13x). Colorado's San Luis Valley (Rio Grande rift) and its Sawatch "hot springs belt" are under-measured. Low data density. Good private land. Worth one probe campaign each.

### 2.9 Alaska (USGS mean undiscovered 1,788 MWe; 53 identified systems)
- **Hidden potential.** It is mostly magmatic and remote: the Aleutian arc (Makushin on Unalaska, Akutan) and the Wrangell and Cook Inlet volcanoes (Mt Spurr). USGS 2009 noted that the largest undiscovered magmatic upside is partly remote, for example the Aleutians (L05). AVO keeps the Makushin resource studies (https://avo.alaska.edu/explore/reference/1727 ; https://avo.alaska.edu/explore/reference/2495). Alaska DGGS IC 100 is the state's geothermal summary (https://dggs.alaska.gov/webpubs/dggs/ic/text/ic100.pdf).
- **Installed:** Chena Hot Springs, under 1 MWe (unverified).
- **Data density: very low.** There are few wells and almost no MT outside a few volcanoes.
- **Rights fit: poor for grid power.** The Railbelt grid is small, island loads are 5-30 MW, and the state leases competitively (unverified detail). It could be an option for future mine or data-centre loads. In a portfolio it is a long-dated option, not a first target.

### 2.10 Hawaii (USGS mean undiscovered 2,435 MWe; 1 identified system; ratio 13.5x)
- **Hidden potential.** This is the largest undiscovered number outside CA and NV, for the smallest land area. It is driven by the active rift zones on the Big Island (Kilauea, Mauna Loa, Hualalai) and older magmatic centres on Maui and Lana'i.
- **PFA.** Lautze et al. (2017) Phase 1 and Ito et al. (2017). In 2019 Lana'i Well 10 was deepened to 1,057 m and found 42 C/km and 66 C (L01, M03). The 2025 revision says the Phase 1 probabilities (under 10% over most of the state) were "too conservative" because groundwater masks heat (M03).
- **Installed (unverified):** Puna Geothermal Venture, 38 MWe, with plans to repower to about 46 MWe.
- **Data density: low**, except Puna. Strong freshwater-lens masking.
- **Rights fit: poor for the cheap-right mechanic.** Hawaii reserves geothermal resources to the state, and development needs a Geothermal Resource Subzone (HRS ch. 182 and 205; unverified detail). Value per MW is the highest in the US, because power costs about $0.35-0.45/kWh (unverified). So it is a farm-in or partnership market, not a rights-bank market.

### 2.11 Canada: BC and Yukon
- **Potential.** Grasby et al. (2012), *Geothermal Energy Resource Potential of Canada*, GSC Open File 6914, says Canada's in-place geothermal power exceeds one million times its electricity use. It flags BC, far-north Alberta, southeast Yukon and the NWT as having 150 C at 5 km or shallower (summary on https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power_in_Canada ; catalogue https://geothermalcanada.org/catalogue/geothermal-energy-resource-potential-of-canada). This is an in-place heat number, not an MWe estimate comparable to USGS. CanGEA and SFU favourability work put BC conventional potential in the low thousands of MWe (unverified figure).
- **Projects.** Mount Meager (BC, about 100 MW thought possible; Thompson 2010). Clarke Lake / Tu Deh-Kah (Fort Nelson First Nation, reusing gas-field data). Little Salmon Carmacks (Yukon, 3 MW Eavor closed loop). Canada has **no geothermal power plant online** (same source).
- **Data density: low** for the volcanic belts, **high** for northeast BC (gas wells, BCER data, R11's 2-year release rule).
- **Rights fit: poor.** BC uses points-scored tenders with no sale since 2014 and needs First Nations referral (R11). Yukon has no mature geothermal tenure regime (unverified). The best Canadian play is **northeast BC gas-field heat** using oil and gas data, which is the D-series and Terranox playbook. Volcanic-belt prospecting is not a good fit.

### 2.12 Mexico
- **Installed:** about 976 MWe at Cerro Prieto, Los Azufres, Los Humeros, Las Tres Vírgenes and Domo San Pedro (L19).
- **Potential.** Mexican reserve estimates (Gutiérrez-Negrín; Iglesias and co-authors) list thousands of thermal manifestations. Probable plus possible electric reserves are in the several-GWe range (unverified numbers; a Monte Carlo reserve paper is in *Natural Resources Research*, https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11053-016-9295-2, and could not be read this run). The **Baja California and Sonora part of the Salton Trough** (south of Cerro Prieto, Laguna Salada) is the same blind sediment-hosted play as Imperial.
- **Data density: low publicly.** CFE holds the well data, and it is not public.
- **Rights fit: poor.** It uses SENER concessions under the 2014 Geothermal Energy Law, CFE dominates, and offtake is state-controlled (L19). It is a later partnership market.

## 5. What dominates uncertainty

1. **Detection coverage outside the Great Basin.** No modern ML favorability map exists for the Rio Grande rift, Arizona, the Snake River Plain east of the GB edge, the Cascades, Hawaii, Alaska, Canada or Mexico. The USGS 2008 surface there is 18 years old.
2. **Masking strength.** In the SRP, Cascades and Hawaii, shallow data says "cold" over real deep heat (M23). The size of the unseen deep share sets whether these regions hold 0.5x or 3x their USGS mean.
3. **Size of hidden systems vs known ones.** USGS borrows sizes from known systems. In AZ, CO and UT there are only 2-6 known systems, so the size distribution is barely sampled.
4. **Basin-hosted sedimentary vs fault-hosted.** Black Rock, Imperial and Rio Grande rift resources may be broad, deep, sedimentary reservoirs. The point-system frame of the USGS may under- or over-count them.
5. **Recovery factor and temperature at depth** (0.08-0.25, M01). These matter everywhere but are least constrained where there are no deep wells.
6. **Non-US numbers are not comparable.** Canada gives in-place heat, and Mexico gives reserve categories. Neither is an undiscovered-MWe estimate.

## 6. What a sensor could fill

- **Arizona, New Mexico and Colorado ranch wells (highest value of information per dollar).** Thousands of registered groundwater wells (ADWR, NM OSE) have never been temperature-logged. The ranch drop probe (T/P/conductivity) turns each one into a gradient label and signs the heat option on the same visit. With a 40x undiscovered/identified ratio, even 50-100 logs would collapse the prior.
- **Snake River Plain and Cascades: a deep-enough sensor to see under the mask.** A 2 m probe will not work under a cold aquifer. What helps: (a) logging the few deep wells **below** the aquifer base (more than 1 km); (b) E-field MT to find the smectite clay cap (M23, M08); (c) stream chloride, boron and helium loggers (the M23 "watershed tree") to measure advected heat that never shows as warm water.
- **Imperial / Salton Trough:** the shallow ground is sediment with little water flow, so **2 m and 10-30 m gradient probes work well**. The old 1970s gradient-hole method can be redone densely and cheaply on farmland edges and the west side.
- **Hawaii:** basal-lens wells are deep and saline-capped. Here, logging temperature and conductivity profiles in existing water-supply wells, plus MT, is the fit.
- **NE BC:** no new sensor needed. Use existing gas-well BHT and DST data (BHT correction, M04).

## 7. Implications for a better model

1. **Build one model for the whole West, not a Nevada model.** Train on Great Basin labels, but add explicit **province features**: rift basin, hot-spot track, arc, sediment-filled pull-apart, oceanic hot spot. Let a hierarchical (multi-level) model share strength across provinces and widen uncertainty where a province has few labels.
2. **Replace the shared USGS multiplier with a per-region detection-coverage term.** The expected hidden count per cell should be favorability x (1 - P(already found)), where P(found) depends on well density, temperature-logged wells and PFA coverage. AZ, NM and CO will then show large, wide hidden numbers. Our sensor campaigns can shrink these numbers fastest.
3. **Add a masking-correction layer** (recharge, aquifer thickness, water-table depth) so the SRP, Cascades and Hawaii are not scored "cold" by shallow data (M23).
4. **Use Imperial Valley as the blind-basin training analogue.** It has a dense, legible history of gradient-hole discoveries in a buried basin. Transfer it to the Black Rock/Sevier basins, the Rio Grande rift basins and the Baja part of the Salton Trough.
5. **Rank regions by value of information times rights fit, not by MWe.** On that score, AZ, NM, SE OR and S ID beat HI and AK, even though HI has more paper MWe.
6. **Report results in USGS units** (mean MWe with F95/F5) per region, so a buyer can compare our updated numbers with the 2008 table. The headline metric is "how much our probes narrowed the F5/F95 band in region X".
