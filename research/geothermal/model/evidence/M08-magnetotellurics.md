# M08: Magnetotellurics (MT) as model input and as our own sensor

Date: 23 Oct 2026. This file builds on `../../evidence/L14-geophysics.md` (MT vs HTEM at East Hawthorne, BRIDGE funnel, ML feature importance), `../../../hardware/evidence/H03-mt-seismic-nodes.md` (E-node design, costs, cheapest first test) and `M02-ingenious.md` (Argenta Rise miss). It does not repeat them. It asks one question: **how should MT enter a better "how much hidden hydrothermal is there" model, and what MT sensor closes the biggest gap?** Web budget: 10 searches and 9 fetches. One fetch (the EarthScope page for the CONUS-MT-2023 model, https://data.earthscope.org/app/products/portal/emc_model_viewer.html?id=EMC-CONUS-MT-2023) was blocked, so the details of that model come from memory and are marked (unverified). Physics rules of thumb are textbook knowledge and are also marked (unverified) where no source was fetched.

## Bottom line

Public MT in the western US comes in two very different sizes. **Deep MT (USMTArray, about 1,711 stations on a 70 km grid, now complete across the lower 48)** has been inverted into 3D models of the whole country and the whole Great Basin. These models see crust and mantle conductors at 2 to 200 km depth. They are a proven regional ML feature ("bottom-up" heat and fluid source), but they are blind to clay caps. **Prospect MT (about 50 to 130 stations at 0.5 to 1 km spacing over one field)** sees the clay cap and the upflow, but it exists for only a few dozen Great Basin areas, and most of it is held privately by operators. A blind system's clay cap is often under 2 km² across. You need about three stations across a target to see it, so you need **spacing of 300 to 500 m or less**. Almost nobody pays for that, because contract MT costs $1,500 to $4,000 per station. That is the gap. A better model should do three things. (1) Treat the deep conductance as a prior on *where heat comes from*. (2) Treat shallow conductors (from the new Earth MRI airborne EM, about 400 m deep, plus all public prospect MT re-gathered and re-inverted) as a noisy likelihood, using an explicit resistivity-to-temperature physics model that also allows for false positives (basin clays, brines, fossil caps). (3) Use value-of-information maths to decide where to put dense, cheap MT. The sensor to build is the H03 **E-field-only node read against a few shared magnetic reference stations**. Synthetic 3D tests show that its "inter-site impedance" inverts as well as full MT (Kruglyakov and Kuvshinov 2019). At about $200 per site, a 250 m carpet over a whole valley-floor fault setting costs about the same as one contract MT survey. Every carpet that we later tie to a gradient-hole temperature becomes a private label. Labels pairing resistivity with measured temperature are the scarcest input in this whole problem.

## Findings

### F1. USMTArray is finished, and it is a 70 km grid
- 1,711 transportable stations were planned. As of 14 Apr 2024, 1,695 were archived, with 2 sites left, so CONUS was "99.94% complete". There are also 7 long-running "Backbone" stations and 21 stations added by USGS. Grid about 70 km. Funding ran about 18 years: NSF 2006-2018, NASA 2019-2020, USGS 2020-2024. Each station records 2 E and 3 H channels and gives an impedance tensor plus tipper. The stated purpose is geomagnetic hazard to the power grid, not geothermal (Schultz 2024, https://par.nsf.gov//servlets/purl/10513322).
- Long-period instruments are tuned for periods of about 10 to 10,000 s (unverified for each station). With the skin-depth rule δ ≈ 503·√(ρ·T) m, a 10 s period in 10 Ω·m ground already samples about 5 km. So USMTArray tells us nothing about the top 1 to 2 km where clay caps sit (physics, unverified).
- All transfer functions are free in EarthScope/IRIS SPUD EMTF (https://ds.iris.edu/spud/emtf). This repository also holds many denser USGS and university surveys.

### F2. The 3D models built from it are already public and already ranked as useful
- **Great Basin conductance maps (Peacock and Bedrosian, USGS 2022).** These come from a 3D conductivity model fit to **more than 800 public MT transfer functions** from SPUD. They are given as conductance over **five depth slices from 2 to 200 km**. CC0, DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU (https://www.usgs.gov/data/electrical-conductance-maps-great-basin-usa). The slices are already a layer in INGENIOUS.
- **CONUS 3D conductivity model (EMC-CONUS-MT-2023; Murphy, Kelbert, Bedrosian et al.).** This is the first lower-48 3D model, published for induction-hazard use. Cells are tens of km across (unverified) (https://www.usgs.gov/publications/first-3d-conductivity-model-contiguous-us-reflections-geologic-structure-and ; https://ds.iris.edu/ds/products/emc-conus-mt-2023). It is useful outside the Great Basin: Idaho, Oregon, the Rio Grande rift and the Salton Trough fringe.
- **Why it matters.** In Trainor-Guitton et al. (2025), mantle and upper-mantle MT conductivity appeared in all four feature-importance rankings, and surface and lower-crust conductivity were in the top ten (see L14 section 2.5). Peacock and Siler (2021, Gabbs Valley) and the USGS "bottom-up control" paper argue that deep conductors mark where fluids and melt feed the brittle-ductile transition (https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70250909). So deep MT is a **source prior**, not a reservoir detector.

### F3. Prospect-scale public MT exists, but it is patchy
- **Gabbs Valley NV (USGS 2020):** 59 wideband 5-component stations, good data from 0.007 to 2,048 s. DOI 10.5066/P9GZ9Z56 (https://www.usgs.gov/data/magnetotelluric-data-gabbs-valley-region-nevada-2020).
- **BRIDGE (Sandia):** raw MT for Gabbs Valley and others is on data.gov (https://catalog.data.gov/dataset/basin-and-range-investigation-for-developing-geothermal-energy-exploration-data-11fed/resource/12481abb-a39d-4f61-b287-0473b771e470). East Hawthorne had 61 stations at about 800 m spacing (L14).
- **Univ. of Utah / Wannamaker (DOE):** 3D MT at McGinness Hills, run with Ormat. It found a NW-SE conductive zone at about 1.2 km depth under the production and injection wells that "persists into the deep crust". Kumiva Valley 3D MT (131 sites, L14) found low-resistivity upwellings under the known San Emidio system and under the west flanks of the Blue Wing and Seven Troughs ranges. A Dixie Valley transect imaged a steep fluid-filled fault feeder from a deep zone under the Humboldt Range (https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2015/06/f23/9-Magnetotellurics_Soil%20Gas%20Geochemistry_U%20of%20U.pdf ; https://www1.eere.energy.gov/geothermal/pdfs/wannamaker_peer2013.pdf).
- **Others on GDR/ScienceBase:** Mountain Home ID (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1249), NV22 by Enthalpion for NREL (GDR 1514), and Pahute Mesa (USGS OFR 2015-1019, which is a good warning about how 3D inversions can be over-read; https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/ofr20151019). Operator MT at Brady, Dixie Valley, Patua, Blue Mountain and other fields is mostly proprietary (unverified for each field).
- **Plain count (my estimate, unverified):** perhaps 30 to 60 prospect-scale MT surveys are public in the Great Basin, covering well under 2% of the roughly 1,430 favorable structural settings in INGENIOUS. Most of the rest have no MT at all.

### F4. What resolution you need to see a clay cap or an upflow
- **Physics (textbook, unverified here).** Smectite clay is stable below about 180 °C and makes a 1 to 10 Ω·m cap. Mixed-layer clay and then illite/chlorite (above about 220 to 240 °C) make the reservoir more resistive, often 20 to 100+ Ω·m. The *base* of the conductor roughly tracks the 180 to 220 °C isotherm in volcanic-hosted systems. This is the standard Ussher et al. (WGC 2000) and Cumming (SGW 2009) model. Cumming warns that MT uncertainty, if not carried into the conceptual model, causes well-targeting failures (https://gen.usc.edu/files/2018/03/94081-rh4733.pdf).
- **Great Basin twist.** Many Basin-and-Range systems are fault-hosted, 130 to 180 °C and in sediment-filled valleys. There the "cap" can be thin or patchy. Saline basin water, lacustrine clay and playa sediment are just as conductive. So a shallow conductor in a valley is a **weak** signal by itself. The useful signals are *shape* (a conductive dome or a hole in it over a fault intersection), *connection* (a conductive path down to a deep conductor, as at McGinness) and *co-location* with 2-m temperature or chemistry anomalies.
- **Depth and frequency band.** A cap at 100 to 1,000 m depth in 3 to 30 Ω·m ground needs frequencies of about 1 Hz to 10 kHz (AMT/broadband). Upflow roots at 1 to 4 km need about 0.01 to 10 s. The 1 to 5 kHz "AMT dead band" and the 0.5 to 5 s band have weak natural signal, which long stacking helps (physics, unverified).
- **Lateral resolution.** Folsom et al. (2024): spacing above 1 km "can easily miss" alteration of under 2 km² (L14). Rule of thumb: you need 3 to 4 stations across a body, so a 1 to 2 km cap needs **spacing of 250 to 500 m**. A 100 to 200 m-wide fault upflow zone needs about 100 to 150 m spacing along profiles across the fault (unverified). Typical contract surveys (500 m to 1 km) sit right at the edge of resolving these.
- **Airborne EM complements MT.** USGS Earth MRI released 11,079 line-km of HELITEM airborne EM plus magnetics in Sep 2025. It covers western NV/eastern CA (Clayton Valley, Rhyolite Ridge), McDermitt and Railroad Valley, with a typical depth of investigation of about 400 m. DOI 10.5066/P1UKDJSM (https://www.usgs.gov/index.php/data/regional-airborne-electromagnetic-and-magnetic-surveys-basin-and-range-province-nevada-oregon). It was collected for critical minerals, but it maps the same top-400 m conductors. So for a growing part of Nevada, the shallow search layer is free, and MT's job moves to 0.4 to 3 km depth.

### F5. Cost per station and why E-only changes the maths
- Contract MT costs about $400 to $2,300 per station (OpenEI), or about $1,500 to $4,000 with western-US mobilization (unverified). A full 5-channel kit costs $60k to $120k (H03).
- **E-only is supported by theory.** Kruglyakov and Kuvshinov (2019, Earth Planets Space 71:4) ran 3D synthetic inversions on a 13 × 13 grid at 1 km spacing with 2% noise. They found that the **inter-site impedance Q (E at each site, H at one reference site) recovers structure as well as the full single-site impedance Z**. The telluric tensor T was slightly worse, the magnetic tensor M clearly worse, and the reference-site location had only a "marginal effect" (https://earth-planets-space.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40623-018-0972-8). This was a synthetic test, not a field test. Field matching against a commercial station is the H03 cheapest-first test.
- New work (EGU26-7264) adds inter-station magnetic transfer functions to reduce "speckle" artifacts in sparse 3D arrays. This suggests a hybrid layout: many E-nodes plus a handful of 3-component H sites, with all tensors inverted together (https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU26/EGU26-7264.html).
- **Cost of an E carpet (H03 numbers):** about $150 to $250 per site, or $3k to $8k per km² at 250 m spacing. A 25 km² valley-floor fault setting at 250 m is about 400 sites, roughly $60k to $100k all-in once the fleet exists. One contract survey of about 60 stations at 800 m costs about $100k to $250k (unverified). Same money, about **7 times the station density**.

### F6. Competitors
- Zanskar has public permits for **passive seismic** surveys on candidate lease parcels near Cove Fort, UT, on Fishlake National Forest (https://www.fs.usda.gov/r04/fishlake/projects/66691). No public sign of Zanskar running its own dense MT was found. This agrees with H03: dense MT looks like an open gap (no public evidence either way).

## What dominates uncertainty (MT slice)
1. **Station density at prospect scale.** Over 98% of candidate structural settings have no MT finer than 70 km. Where MT exists, spacing is usually at or over the edge of resolving a 1 to 2 km cap.
2. **Non-uniqueness of conductors.** Basin brine, lacustrine and playa clay, graphitic rock and *fossil* clay caps all look like an active cap. Without a temperature tie, a conductor is maybe a 2 to 3 times likelihood ratio, not proof (my estimate, unverified).
3. **Missing resistivity-to-temperature labels.** Very few public places have MT and a measured temperature profile at the same spot. That is why INGENIOUS and USGS can use only deep conductance as a feature, not a calibrated shallow-temperature predictor.
4. **Inversion choices.** Smoothing, static shift, mesh and the starting model change 3D results a lot (Pahute Mesa OFR 2015-1019). Most published models give no uncertainty.
5. **Data access.** The best prospect MT sits in operator files.

## What a sensor could fill
- **Dense E-field-only carpet (H03 E-node) at 100 to 250 m spacing**, read as inter-site impedance against 1 H reference per 5 to 10 km² plus a remote reference. It fills gaps 1 and 4 at about 10 times lower cost per site. Band: sample at up to 4 to 8 kHz in bursts for the 100 to 1,000 m cap, and 4 to 32 Hz continuous for weeks to stack the 1 to 4 km upflow band.
- **Add a few full 5-component H sites inside each carpet** (EGU26-7264 logic) to measure tipper and the magnetic transfer function. This cuts artifacts and gives hard evidence of a fault upflow along strike.
- **Co-locate with our 2-m probes and ranch water-well drop probes** (the prior hardware pick), so every conductor gets a temperature tie on the same visit. That fills gap 3, and it is the part competitors cannot download.
- **Time-lapse option.** Leaving the nodes for months makes repeat surveys almost free. That allows seasonal noise rejection and, later, reservoir monitoring as a paid service to buyers.

## Implications for a better model
1. **Use MT on two levels.** Deep conductance (2 to 200 km, plus derived features: conductance gradient, how close a deep conductor comes to the surface, and whether a conductive path links it to the upper crust under a mapped FSS) goes in the regional *source prior*. Shallow conductors (AEM to 400 m, public prospect MT to 3 km) go in a *local likelihood*. Never feed them into one feature vector.
2. **Build the "public prospect-MT cube".** Gather every public EDI (SPUD, GDR, ScienceBase, BRIDGE, Utah DOE) and re-invert it jointly in 3D with ModEM or SimPEG, merged with Earth MRI AEM. Publish uncertainty, using ensembles or a neural surrogate. Nobody has this, it costs only compute, and it shows where public MT is dense enough to trust.
3. **Use an explicit physics likelihood, not a raw resistivity feature.** Model resistivity as a function of temperature, clay type, porosity and salinity (Archie plus surface conduction plus smectite stability). Add explicit false-positive classes: basin brine, lacustrine clay and fossil cap. This turns a conductor into a likelihood ratio for "T at 1 km > 150 °C" that the Bayesian volume model can use, and it lets cold conductors become honest negative labels.
4. **Choose survey sites by value of information.** For each FSS, compute how much a 250 m E carpet would narrow the posterior on MWe, and how much that is worth given the right's option cost. Then survey first and option only the parcels over upflow. The carpet costs less than the options on most tracts.
5. **Every carpet plus a temperature tie is a label.** After about 20 to 30 carpets with gradient-hole ties, we would hold the only public-scale calibrated resistivity-to-temperature dataset in the Great Basin. That is the asset Zanskar's public-data model (GeoCore on INGENIOUS) lacks, and it lifts the whole basin-wide estimate, not only one prospect.
6. **Fast inversion is a compute problem, so throw compute at it.** Train a neural 3D inversion surrogate on synthetic Great Basin models (fault plus cap plus basin fill). Then a 400-node carpet becomes a model with uncertainty in hours, and the field crew can move nodes to fill gaps during the same deployment.
