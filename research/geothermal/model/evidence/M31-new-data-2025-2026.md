Date: 23 Oct 2026. ## Bottom line

From 2024 to 2026 the public data that matters for finding blind hydrothermal systems shifted from DOE's GDR to USGS ScienceBase. Of the 178 public GDR submissions numbered 1700 to 1892 (about January 2025 to October 2026), 108 (61%) are Utah FORGE EGS records. Only two are new field data packages from a hydrothermal system: Zanskar's Lightning Dock release (GDR 1856 and 1868). The hydrothermal-exploration flow into GDR has almost stopped. Over the same period, the USGS Great Basin team (Burns, DeAngelo, Mordensky, Caraccioli) put out a coherent stack that a model should ingest right away:

- a new 3D conductive temperature model (2024),
- geothermometer reservoir-depth predictions (2025),
- a 135 GWe provisional EGS assessment (May 2025),
- a **new list of 205 electric-grade hydrothermal systems with centred upflow points**: 28 with power plants, 78 known, and **99 newly flagged as "probable"** (Feb 2026),
- a 53-feature, 120-realization favorability release (2026),
- a Nevada ML release with **83 positive and 62 confirmed-negative training sites** and P95 minus P5 uncertainty maps (Sep 2026).

USGS says it will use these files in a conditional Monte Carlo that produces a new undiscovered-hydrothermal number for the Great Basin. That new official hidden-resource estimate has not been published yet. The pending number, plus the 99 "probable" systems, is both the biggest new input and the biggest risk: once it is out, everyone reads the same list on the same day. On the DOE side, the GTO web pages now return 404 inside a renamed "Hydrocarbons and Geothermal Energy Office". NREL is now the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR). The new NLR products are economic layers (reV supply curves, an EGS LCOE suitability map, LLM-extracted siting ordinances, a value-of-information app), not new subsurface measurements. **For our model, the gain is labels and uncertainty, not new physics.** The 99 probable systems and the 62 confirmed negatives give us the first public "contested" label set. The places where those labels disagree with each other and with the favorability maps are exactly where our own probe and E-field MT readings carry the most value.

## Findings

### F1. GDR has become a FORGE archive. Hydrothermal exploration data has nearly stopped arriving.
- I listed GDR IDs 1700 to 1892 one by one. There are 178 public records. 108 are Utah FORGE, 9 are Cape EGS or EGS Collab, and the rest are mostly heat pumps, district heating, drilling materials and webinars. Only **GDR 1856 and 1868 (Zanskar, Lightning Dock NM, Aug and Sep 2026)** are hydrothermal field packages. IDs 1893 to 1960 are placeholders marked "New Submission" (titles listed from https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1700 through /1892).
- The GDR home page today shows 764 TB, **1,426 datasets** (up from 1,423 on 21 Oct 2026 per D04), 146 providers and 73.7M downloads (https://gdr.openei.org/).
- Several important packages are held back for years after they are submitted. The Oxy-run DOE "GLADE" deep-basement drilling package in the DJ Basin, Colorado, has its final report "Available Apr 30, 2031" (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1858). Colorado School of Mines hyperspectral/LST deep-learning code (GDR 1694) is embargoed to **1 Sep 2029** (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1694). So DOE-funded results can stay dark for 3 to 5 years.

### F2. Zanskar's Lightning Dock release is the best public "full system" training example
- GDR 1856 (submitted 20 Aug 2026; contact Ghoulem Ifrene, Zanskar) covers **41 wells**: logs, drilling records, flow tests, PT surveys, and production and injection time series. It also has tracer tests from 2012 to 2019, MT, aeromagnetics, gravity, LiDAR, the **daily plant record from Jan 2014 to Aug 2026**, shallow groundwater monitoring and fluid and gas chemistry (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1856). GDR 1868 adds 1.34 GB of GIS data in 43 files (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1868). The GDR search for "GEOGYM" returns only these two records, so this looks like the first site of Zanskar's DOE GEOGYM benchmark (project EE0011649, from D04).
- Why it matters: Lightning Dock was found from a hot shallow well; it is not a classic blind system. Even so, it is the only public dataset where surface geophysics, shallow groundwater temperature and a producing reservoir all sit in one place. It is the right site to calibrate "what does a shallow reading look like above a real reservoir". It is also a signal: Zanskar is setting the public benchmark that it then gets to beat.

### F3. USGS ScienceBase: a new label set with 205 systems, 99 of them newly "probable"
- DeAngelo, Burns & Mordensky 2026, *Conventional Hydrothermal Power-Producing Systems of the Great Basin, USA*, USGS data release, https://doi.org/10.5066/P13AXSYL (ScienceBase https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/697a8b5fd4be0240120f25c6, Feb 2026). A system is "electric-grade" if a well shows **>85 °C plus evidence of convection**. Points within **10 km** are grouped into one system, and each system gets a single "throat of the upflow" centre point. Result: **28 systems with operating plants, 78 known electric-grade systems, and 99 new "probable" electric-grade systems** with confidence rankings. The release says USGS **"will"** run Monte Carlo estimates of discovered and undiscovered resource, conditioned on a favorability map. So the official update of the 2008 undiscovered-hydrothermal number is still to come.
- Why it matters: the 99 probable systems are a ready-made public target list. Some will already be under BLM lease. Some will sit on private ranch land. **Cross-checking the 99 against lease status is a one-day job and should be done first** (land status: see the R files).

### F4. USGS ML favorability: real negatives and uncertainty maps are now public
- Mordensky & DeAngelo 2026, *Feature, Label, Prediction, and Zone Data for Great Basin Zone Classification and Hydrothermal Favorability Modeling Using Supervised Machine Learning*, https://doi.org/10.5066/P14QM39R (ScienceBase item 6927454dd4be02716f722cb3). It has **53 public input features**. Labels are modeled departures from conductive heat flow at wells. It uses unsupervised "zones" to split the Great Basin into regimes and gives the median of **120 realizations**, as CSV and GeoTIFF.
- Mordensky & Caraccioli 2026 (data release dated 1 Sep 2026) for Caraccioli et al. 2026, *Geothermics* 103833, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2026.103833 (ScienceBase item 67b8dc25d34e1a2e835b8382). It has 13 features on the NVML grid (**n = 1,695,126 cells**), **83 positive and 62 confirmed-negative training sites**, P50 of 60 realizations, and **P95 minus P5 and P75 minus P25 uncertainty maps**. The paper shows that the choice of negative sites and features changes the map a lot.
- An earlier 2025 release goes with *Geothermics* 103450 (ScienceBase item 66e88690d34e0606a9db9b43; abstract not read, 502 error).
- Why it matters: this is the first public geothermal favorability work with an explicit **model-spread map**. The cells where the spread is high and the median is high are where one new measurement changes the answer most (M17 value of information). We can take those maps as they are, use them as a prior, and spend sensor time where P95 minus P5 is widest.

### F5. USGS thermal stack: a 3D temperature model, geothermometer depths and an EGS number
- Burns, DeAngelo & Williams 2024, *Three-dimensional temperature model of the Great Basin* v1.1 (Nov 2024), https://doi.org/10.5066/P149FR54. It is a conductive model using DeAngelo et al. 2023 heat flow, radiogenic heat production of 1.89 µW/m³ and bulk conductivity of 2.7 W/m/°C below the basins. The authors say near-surface conductive heat flow is the largest source of uncertainty.
- DeAngelo, Burns & Mordensky 2025, *Depth predictions of chemical geothermometers...*, https://doi.org/10.5066/P14B8XOH. It pairs spring geothermometer temperatures with the 3D model to estimate **how deep each spring's water circulated**. Deeper circulation lines up with higher strain rate and with basins. This is a new derived feature: "implied circulation depth" at every sampled spring.
- Burns, Williams & DeAngelo 2025, USGS Fact Sheet 2025-3027, https://doi.org/10.3133/fs20253027 (posted 22 May 2025): a provisional **135 GWe EGS** estimate for the upper 6 km of the Great Basin. The data release is at https://doi.org/10.5066/P14LU7WD.
- Why it matters: the conductive 3D model is the "no hydrothermal" baseline. A blind system is a **positive residual** against it. Every temperature reading we take (water wells, 2 m probes, TG holes) becomes a residual against a public baseline that anyone can check, so buyers can verify our claim.

### F6. NLR (formerly NREL) layers: economics and siting, not subsurface
- *United States Geothermal Supply Curves 2024* (GDR 1732, submitted Apr 2025, 627 MB). reV supply curves for EGS and hydrothermal at four depths, with MW, MWh, LCOE, LCOT (cost of transmission) and a composite siting-exclusion TIFF (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1732).
- *EGS Suitability for Contiguous United States* (GDR 1852, Jul 2026). Normalized all-in LCOE (including transmission) at the best depth from 1 to 7 km, with and without exclusions (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1852).
- *U.S. Geothermal Electricity Siting Regulation and Zoning Ordinances (2026)* (GDR 1877, Sep 2026). State and local ordinances extracted with LLMs by the INFRA-COMPASS tool: setbacks, drilling hours, noise, bonding and prohibitions (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1877).
- *Value of Information App* webinar (GDR 1878, Sep 2026). It models a "drill or walk away" decision from a prior. It also gives the value of imperfect information, using geophysical observations at known systems and at "negative" locations (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1878).
- *Priority Geothermal Leasing Areas* ReEDS results (GDR 1604, May 2024; DOI 10.15121/2516751). It sorts BLM and USFS land into near, mid and far deployment priority (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1604).
- *Alaska Geothermal Resource Data Gaps* with a 0 to 20 km Alaska thermal model (GDR 1810, in progress). FedGeo low-temperature studies in NY and GA (GDR 1748, 1880).
- Why it matters: these let us price each target (LCOE, transmission, ordinance friction) with no extra work. They also tell buyers where the federal government thinks leasing will go next. That points us toward the leftover parcels and private land **outside** the priority areas, which is where rights are cheap.

### F7. Older-but-recent field packages worth ingesting (2024)
- **BRIDGE** (Sandia, Geologica, US Navy GPO, Cumming, Sewell; GDR 1682, Dec 2024, DOI 10.15121/2513910). Hidden-system exploration data: helicopter TDEM, MT, **2 m temperatures**, gravity, aeromagnetics, geochemistry, mapping, LiDAR and 3D models, organized by prospect (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1682). It is already read in L14. It matters here because its prospects with both 2 m and MT data are the best public calibration set for our exact sensor pair.
- **Steptoe Valley NV** (Sandia, GDR 1568, Jan 2024, DOI 10.15121/2282349). A stratigraphic hydrothermal reservoir first found in oil wells, with CSEM, MT, spring chemistry, seismic and a Leapfrog model (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1568). It is a template for **basin-hosted blind systems**, which favorability models built on faults under-rank.
- **Stanford Thermal Earth Model** (GDR 1592, Mar 2024, DOI 10.15121/2324793). A physics-informed graph neural network giving temperature, heat flow and conductivity from 0 to 7 km for CONUS, at about 18 km² per cell, with an API (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1592). It is a second baseline to compare with USGS F5.
- **NGDS AASG node preserved** (GDR 1805, Jul 2026, University of Arizona). Republished well, heat-flow and aqueous-chemistry tables on Zenodo, plus a web map (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1805).

### F8. Programs: DOE reorganized, and philanthropy fills the map layer (partly unverified)

From memory: it is a free global layer stack (heat flow, temperature at depth, demand and infrastructure) launched in 2024 and extended in 2025 (unverified). - **GEODE** (the DOE-funded Geothermal Energy from Oil and gas Demonstrated Engineering consortium, around 2023 to 2025) worked on transferring oil-field know-how and data. Its outputs and status in 2026 are unverified.

## What dominates uncertainty (from this slice)

1. **The coming USGS undiscovered-hydrothermal number.** The 2026 releases say a conditional Monte Carlo "will" be run. Until it is published, the size of the hidden resource in the Great Basin rests on the 2008 assessment. How the 99 "probable" systems are treated (discovered or undiscovered) will move the headline a lot.
2. **Choice of negative labels.** Caraccioli et al. 2026 show that which "confirmed absent" sites you train on changes the favorability maps. There are only 62 public negatives against 1.7M cells.
3. **Near-surface conductive heat flow.** USGS names this as the largest error in its temperature-at-depth baseline (P149FR54 abstract). Every blind-system residual inherits it.
4. **Embargoed and missing data.** DOE packages under 3 to 5 year embargoes (GLADE to 2031, CSM code to 2029), FORGE dominating GDR, and the retirement of NGDS mean much new field data is not public for years.
5. **Program continuity.** The GTO reorganization and unclear FY2026 hydrothermal funding make future public releases less certain (unverified amounts).

## What a sensor could fill

- **Test the 99 "probable" systems quickly.** For each one, a water-well drop probe (temperature, pressure, conductivity) plus a 2 m probe line confirms convection at shallow depth or knocks the system down. That turns USGS "probable" into our private "confirmed" or "negative" before the Monte Carlo is published.
- **More negatives.** Each cold, honest reading near a high-favorability cell is a new negative label. There are only 62 public ones, so every few dozen of ours make a real difference to the model.
- **Target high-spread cells.** Put E-field MT nodes and probes where the USGS P95 minus P5 spread is widest and the median is high. That is where one reading moves the posterior the most (NLR's VOI App can score this directly).
- **Measure the near-surface heat flow that dominates baseline error.** Temperature profiles in existing water wells give conductive gradients that tighten the USGS 3D baseline locally, so our residuals are cleaner than anyone else's.
- **Calibrate the sensor-to-reservoir link at Lightning Dock and the BRIDGE prospects.** Run our probe and E-node kit where the public data already shows a reservoir, to fit the likelihood model before we use it on blind ground.

## Implications for a better model

1. **Ingest the USGS 2024 to 2026 stack as the prior.** Use the 3D temperature baseline, the circulation-depth feature, the 53-feature favorability realizations and the NVML spread maps. Do not rebuild them. Our edge is in the posterior, not the prior.
2. **Change the target.** Use "positive residual against the USGS conductive baseline" as a continuous target, with the 205-system list as hard positives. Keep the 99 probable systems as a separate, weighted class.
3. **Pool negatives.** Merge the 62 USGS negatives, the INGENIOUS cold holes (M02), BRIDGE and Argenta failures, and our own cold readings into a single negative set that tracks where each one came from. Train with label noise built in.
4. **Model choice of action, not only favorability.** Combine favorability, spread, NLR LCOE, transmission, ordinance friction (GDR 1877) and land status into an "expected value per dollar of measurement" score for each cell, using the VOI framework (GDR 1878, M17).
5. **Watch for releases as a race.** Set an automated watcher on ScienceBase (query by the Burns, DeAngelo, Mordensky authors) and on GDR IDs above 1892. The USGS undiscovered-resource release is the day the 99 probable systems become common knowledge. Our rights need to be locked before that day.
6. **Calibrate on Lightning Dock.** Use Zanskar's own public package to fit how a shallow signal relates to a reservoir. That is fair use of public data, and it beats Zanskar to using its own benchmark against blind ground.
7. **Add basin-hosted plays.** Steptoe-type stratigraphic systems are under-ranked by fault-based models. Add a sedimentary-aquifer branch fed by oil-well BHTs (M25).
