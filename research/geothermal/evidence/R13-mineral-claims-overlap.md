## 1. The federal statutory map

### What the Geothermal Steam Act covers
Under 30 USC 1001(c), "geothermal resources" means:
- "(i) all products of geothermal processes, embracing indigenous steam, hot water and hot brines";
- "(ii) steam and other gases, hot water and hot brines resulting from water, gas, or other fluids artificially introduced into geothermal formations";
- "(iii) heat or other associated energy found in geothermal formations";
- "(iv) any byproduct derived from them."

Under 30 USC 1001(d), a "byproduct" is "any mineral or minerals (exclusive of oil, hydrocarbon gas, and helium) which are found in solution or in association with geothermal steam and which have a value of less than 75 per centum of the value of the geothermal steam or are not, because of quantity, quality, or technical difficulties in extraction and production, of sufficient value to warrant extraction and production by themselves." The regulations repeat this definition (43 CFR 3200.1).

### Exclusivity
Under 30 USC 1022, rights to develop geothermal resources in federally owned lands may be obtained **only** under the Geothermal Steam Act.
- **Heat cannot be acquired through a mining claim.** A placer claim on the 1872 Mining Law gives no right to the thermal energy.

### Byproduct royalty
Under 30 USC 1004(a)(2), a byproduct royalty applies only to "any byproduct that is a mineral specified in the first section of the Mineral Leasing Act." That list (30 USC 181) is: coal, phosphate, sodium, potassium, oil, oil shale, gilsonite and gas.
- **Lithium is not on the list.**
- So lithium produced as a byproduct under a federal geothermal lease appears to carry **no federal byproduct royalty**. That is a reading of the text; BLM or ONRR has not been seen applying it (unverified).
- Sibling R02 reaches the same reading from 43 CFR 3211.19.

### Electricity royalty and rent
- **Statutory royalty band (30 USC 1004):** 1-2.5% of gross proceeds for the first 10 years, then 2-5%.
- **Regulatory rates (sibling R02, 43 CFR 3211.17):** 1.75% for the first 10 years, then 3.5%. The rate is 10% on an arm's-length sale of fluid to a generator.
- **Rent:** $1, or $2-3, per acre per year for the first 10 years, then $5/acre/yr.

### Mining claims and geothermal leases can overlap
- **Overlap is expected.** Under 30 USC 1005(e), a geothermal lessee whose lease overlies a mining claim with an approved plan of operations is exempt from annual work requirements while development would interfere with mining. The statute therefore anticipates a claim and a lease on the same ground.
- **Locatable minerals stay open, but only unassociated ones.** Under 30 USC 1005(i), minerals locatable under the mining laws in geothermal-lease lands, if they are "**not associated with the geothermal resources**" of those lands, remain locatable under the Multiple Mineral Development Act principles.
- **The implication:** lithium dissolved *in* the geothermal brine is plainly "associated." The text of 1005(i) therefore leaves it outside the mining-claim carve-out once a geothermal lease exists (my reading, unverified).

### Mining-claim mechanics
- **Placer claims** are capped at 20 acres per individual (30 USC 35) and 160 acres per association (30 USC 36).
- **Fees (43 CFR 3830.21):**
  - a one-time location fee of **$49**;
  - an annual maintenance fee of **$200 per 20 acres** (or part) of a placer claim, due by September 1;
  - plus a processing fee under 3000.12 (amount not fetched).
- **Cost per acre:** a full 160-acre association placer costs **$1,600/yr, about $10/acre/yr**. That is roughly **3-10x the per-acre holding cost of a BLM geothermal lease** in its first decade.
- **No royalty on locatable minerals:** the federal government still takes no royalty under the 1872 Law. Every reform bill (2007 H.R. 2262 at 4-8%, 2009, 2014, 2015 S. 2254 at 2-5%, 2017) died ([Wikipedia summary of bills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_Law_of_1872)).

### Legal reading: three regimes on federal land
**(a) Unleased federal land, lithium-only brine play** (closed-basin playa brine such as Clayton Valley, NV):
- Lithium is a locatable mineral, and industry stakes it with **placer claims** under free entry.
- That is how the Clayton Valley claim rush of 2016-2018 happened (unverified as to counts and dates; case law not retrieved).
- **The real chokepoint is water.** In Nevada, brine pumping needs a State Engineer water-right permit, and Clayton Valley groundwater is over-appropriated. Albemarle's Silver Peak senior rights have dominated the disputes (unverified; see R09).
- Silver Peak has produced since 1967. Albemarle bought it via Rockwood in 2014, reported at $6.2B for Rockwood as a whole ([Wikipedia: Silver Peak, NV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Peak,_Nevada)).

**(b) Federal land with a geothermal lease, hot lithium brine:**
- If lithium is worth under 75% of the steam (or not worth producing on its own), it is a **byproduct**. It belongs to the geothermal lessee, and no federal royalty applies because lithium is not an MLA mineral.
- If lithium is worth **75% or more** of the steam, it falls out of "byproduct" and out of "geothermal resources."
  - Is it then locatable by a stranger's placer claim? Section 1005(i) excludes minerals "associated with the geothermal resources," so the text leans **no**, and there is no clear doctrine.
  - **This is a genuine statutory gap** (unverified; no IBLA or court decision retrieved).
- Salton-grade brine (about 200 ppm Li, [Wikipedia: Lithium Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_Valley)) would plausibly cross the 75% line. Most Great Basin hydrothermal brines are much more dilute (generally under 10 ppm; unverified), so their lithium would remain a byproduct.

**(c) Federal land, geothermal brine but no lease yet:**
- A placer claim staked for lithium does **not** stop BLM from issuing a geothermal lease over it, because 1005(e) contemplates overlap.
- The claim confers no right to the heat (1022).
- At best it creates a co-owner of the dissolved lithium with whom the eventual geothermal lessee must deal.
- **Its value is nuisance or hold-up, not control.**

**Answer to the core question.** Lithium does **not** offer a clean free-entry route around geothermal leasing:
- You cannot get the heat that way.
- You can get a defensible lithium-only position in non-thermal playa brines.
- In hot brines, a claim gives you a contestable hold-up position, worth something mainly where lithium value exceeds steam value.

## 2. State lithium-royalty regimes (where the brine is not federal)

### Arkansas (Smackover)
- **The royalty order.** On 29 May 2025 the Arkansas Oil and Gas Commission **unanimously approved a 2.5% quarterly gross royalty** to brine owners. It covers Phase I of the South West Arkansas Project's Reynolds Unit (SWA Lithium LLC, the Standard Lithium/Equinor JV, "Smackover Lithium").
- **Bromine fee on top:** brine owners also receive an "in lieu bromine royalty" of **$65.05/acre/yr**.
- **All-in cost:** about **3%** of revenue at the prices then current.
- **A first:** this is the first AOGC-approved royalty rate for lithium from brine ([press release](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/smackover-lithiums-south-west-arkansas-project-receives-royalty-rate-approval-from-the-arkansas-oil-and-gas-commission)). The application had been filed on 6 May 2025.
- **How the regime works:** the commission **unitises** brine and **sets** the royalty administratively, much as it handles oil and gas pooling.
  - A small mineral owner cannot hold out.
  - A pre-positioned lessee of brine rights earns roughly the regulated rate, not a negotiated premium.
  - The statutory basis under the Arkansas Brine Resources Act (Ark. Code Title 15, Ch. 76) is unverified; the AOGC site returned 403.
- **The resource:** grades of about **582 mg/L Li**, peaking at 616 mg/L, in Lafayette County wells. The project was named a Priority Critical Mineral Project on 21 Apr 2025 and targets **22,500 t LCE/yr** ([Wikipedia: Smackover Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smackover_Formation); secondary).
- **Relevance:** Arkansas is a lithium-only play with low reservoir heat for power, so it is relevant to geothermal only as the template for a regulated brine royalty.

### California (Salton Sea, Imperial County)
- **Excise tax:** California's **lithium extraction excise tax** is levied per metric ton of LCE, tiered by the producer's lifetime cumulative production ([CDTFA rates](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/special-taxes-and-fees-tax-rates/)).

| Effective | 20,000 t cumulative or less | Over 20,000 to 30,000 t | Over 30,000 t |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Jan 2023 | $400 | $600 | $800 |
| 1 Jan 2025 | $413 | $620 | $826 |
| 1 Jan 2026 | $425 | $638 | $851 |

- **Inflation indexing:** rates are CPI-indexed each January.
- **Where the money goes:** revenue funds Salton Sea restoration, community grants and the affected counties (R&T Code 47100) ([CDTFA program page](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/lithium-extraction-excise-tax/)).
- **Change-of-ownership penalty:** on a change of permit ownership, the new holder is **deemed to have produced 30,000 t**, so it jumps straight to the top tier.
  - **That is a direct tax on flipping a lithium-producing asset**, and an anti-step-up feature to note.
- **Size of the tax:** at about $15-20k/t LCE, $425/t is roughly **2-3%** of revenue, comparable to Arkansas (price assumption unverified).
- **State lands:** state-owned geothermal lands are leased by the State Lands Commission with a royalty of **at least 10%** of gross on steam and brine and **at least 2%** on mineral by-products (PRC 6913; sibling R05).
- **Private lands:** most Salton Sea field acreage is private, Imperial Irrigation District or county land, and is not open to free entry (sibling L12).
- **The resource:** about 202 ppm Li, fluids at 250-380°C, 11 operating plants, and a CEC estimate of about 600,000 t LCE/yr potential ([Wikipedia: Lithium Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_Valley); secondary).

### Texas
- SB 785 (2023) gives geothermal energy to the **surface owner**. It expressly excludes "minerals dissolved or otherwise contained in groundwater, including in hot brines," which stay with the mineral estate (sibling L12/R07).
- **Heat and lithium are separate estates** in Texas.

### Nevada (state law)
- Nevada has almost no state trust land, about 345k acres (sibling R05).
- Brine lithium plays there are federal placer claims plus State Engineer water rights.
- A specific Nevada lithium-brine royalty or the Nevada net-proceeds-of-minerals tax rate as applied to lithium could not be verified; the Net Proceeds of Minerals tax is up to 5% (unverified).

## 3. What this means for "free entry"

1. **Free entry exists for lithium, not heat.** On open federal land, a lithium placer costs $49 plus about $10/acre/yr, with no federal royalty. This is the closest US analogue to Saskatchewan claim-staking, but it secures only the dissolved mineral, and in Nevada it is useless without water rights.
2. **In hot brine, the geothermal lease wins.** The lessee holds byproduct lithium royalty-free (federal), and 1005(i) leaves an associated mineral outside the mining-claim carve-out. A claim filed over a future lease area mostly buys a negotiating position.
3. **The 75% value test is the live wildcard.** For high-Li, high-salinity brines (Salton-type, some sedimentary basin brines), lithium may be "not a byproduct." Then ownership of the lithium on federal land is legally unsettled. A party holding *both* the geothermal lease and an overlapping placer claim removes the ambiguity cheaply. **Stacking rights is cheap insurance, not a bypass.**
4. **Regulated royalties cap the landowner's take** at about 2.5-3% (AR) or about $425/t (CA). The upside of pre-positioning in lithium brine comes from controlling acreage that operators need unitised, not from extracting a high royalty.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

- **Lithium is not the free-entry backdoor to geothermal.** Heat on federal land is obtainable only by geothermal lease (30 USC 1022).
  - The cheap, quiet, Saskatchewan-style acquisition route for heat remains the BLM noncompetitive and over-the-counter channels, state trust land and private fee geothermal rights (R01-R06).
  - A pitch claiming "we stake lithium claims to lock up geothermal" would be legally wrong.
- **What does work is a "dual-estate stack" on high-Li geothermal brines.** Hold the geothermal lease (heat plus royalty-free byproduct lithium), and also stake a placer claim to cover the case where lithium exceeds 75% of steam value.
  - Placer cost: about **$10/acre/yr**.
  - Geothermal lease cost: about **$1-3/acre/yr** rent.
  - For a few thousand acres per prospect, that is trivial money for an asset whose lithium alone could exceed the power value.
  - **Owning both estates is the step-up**, since a buyer (Fervo, Ormat, CTR-type co-producers, lithium majors) otherwise faces a title cloud.
- **Use ML to rank brine chemistry as well as temperature.** Old oil and gas produced-water analyses (USGS Produced Waters Database), geothermal fluid chemistry and assessment files can rank prospects for Li grade alongside heat.
  - That points the company at Texas and Gulf Coast (split estates: heat to the surface owner, Li to the mineral owner, so aggregate both), Arkansas-adjacent sedimentary basins, and Great Basin playas.
- **The gap vs Zanskar:**
  - Zanskar is a power developer and operator. Its public story is heat-for-electricity (Big Blind, Lightning Dock, a PPA). We found nothing showing it treats dissolved minerals as a separate ownable asset (unverified; see Z01-Z05).
  - A Terranox shape can be the **land-and-minerals owner** that assembles both estates before proof and sells the *combined* heat-plus-lithium right at a step-up. Buyers: developers for the heat, DLE players for the brine.
  - Zanskar is structurally unlikely to do this, and it cannot be a neutral seller to its own competitors.
- **Risks:**
  - The 75% rule and 1005(i) are untested for lithium (no IBLA or court decision found).
  - Nevada water rights can block brine projects outright.
  - California's 30,000-t deemed-production rule taxes flips of lithium-producing assets. **Sell before first production.**
  - Arkansas-style unitisation caps owner economics at about 3%.
  - Mining Law royalty reform (4-8% proposals) has repeatedly failed, but it remains a tail risk on placer-held lithium.
- **Scoring view:** lithium overlap is an **option-value kicker** on a geothermal land bank. It is not a standalone thesis and not a bypass. It strengthens the "own the asset" shape, especially in Texas, where heat and minerals are separate estates and both can be bought cheaply.
