## Why state trust land matters for a Terranox-shape company

Federal (BLM) geothermal leasing has been competitive-only since the 2005 Energy Policy Act. Nominated parcels go to a periodic auction, so the nominator reveals the target and can be outbid. State trust lands work differently. Each state sets its own process, and several of them still keep a **first-in-time application or a prospecting-permit-to-preference-right path**. Those paths are the closest US analogue to Saskatchewan's online staking of mineral claims: you file first, you hold the right, and no auction exposes your target.

## State-by-state

### Nevada: effectively nothing to lease
- The Nevada Division of State Lands holds about **345,000 acres, "less than 1% of the total land area of Nevada"** (https://lands.nv.gov/). Nevada sold most of its original school grant long ago (unverified history). The NDSL site does not mention any geothermal leasing program.
- In practice, Nevada geothermal rights are **BLM** (roughly 80%+ of the state is federal (unverified)) or **private fee/railroad-checkerboard** land. For Zanskar's home state, the non-BLM play is private mineral and geothermal rights, not state land.

### Utah: SITLA (Trust Lands Administration)
- Geothermal is a leasable trust-land mineral. R850-24-150 says mineral resources "include all hardrock minerals and building stone; coal; and geothermal resources" (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R850-24-150). The geothermal-specific rule **R850-27 is now marked [Repealed]** on Cornell LII (https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/school-and-institutional-trust-lands/title-R850/rule-R850-27). This suggests geothermal now runs under the general mineral-lease rule R850-25, but that inference is (unverified).
- **Issuance:** "The director may issue mineral leases competitively or non-competitively, or enter into joint ventures or other business arrangements" (R850-25-200, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R850-25-200).
- **Over-the-counter priority by time stamp:** applications are "stamped with the exact date and time of filing," and the time stamp sets priority. Identical, simultaneous filings go to a public drawing or an oral auction. Competitive offerings require at least 15 days' notice and sealed bids, with a minimum bid of the first year's rental (R850-25-700, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R850-25-700).
- **Terms:** primary term set by the director, **at most 10 years** unless an Other Business Arrangement (OBA) applies. Held by production, or by diligence plus a minimum royalty. Rental of **at least $1/acre/yr, with a $500 minimum per lease**. Royalty and minimum royalty are set when the lease is offered (R850-25-300, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R850-25-300).
- **Size:** at least a quarter-quarter section; **at most 2,560 acres (4 sections)** unless the director approves more (R850-25-200).
- **Preference right:** a lessee who discovers a mineral its lease does not cover gets a 60-day preference right, with notice due within 10 days (R850-24-400, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/utah/Utah-Admin-Code-R850-24-400). This could matter for a party already holding, say, a lithium or potash lease on hot ground.
- Context: SITLA manages about 3.3M surface acres and 4.4M mineral acres in a checkerboard of school sections across the Great Basin side of Utah, including the Milford/Cape Station and FORGE area (unverified). Utah is the one state here with both a strong geothermal resource and a rule framework that allows first-come leasing.

### Idaho: Department of Lands (IDAPA 20.03.15)
- **Auction only on conflict:** "a lease auction will be held" when more than one application covers the same parcel (20.03.15.021, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.021). A single applicant therefore gets the lease without competition.
- **Term: up to 49 years**, plus shut-in provisions (20.03.15.030, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.030). That is very long tenure.
- **Rental is set by the Land Board**, by bid, negotiation, fixed amount or formula (20.03.15.035, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.035).
- **Royalty guideline ranges for non-competitive leases:** direct-use heat or energy 5-20%; by-products including demineralized water 2-15%; **electric power 2-5% of gross receipts** (20.03.15.036, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.036).
- **No acreage cap.** "Geothermal leases are not limited in surface area." The Board sizes each lease to the likely reservoir (20.03.15.040, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.040).
- **Fees:** $250 per application; $150 per assignment (20.03.15.120, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/idaho/IDAPA-20.03.15.120).
- Idaho endowment lands total about 2.4M acres (unverified). The Snake River Plain and the Raft River area are the resource targets.

### New Mexico: State Land Office (19.2.7 NMAC; Geothermal Resources Act of 1967)
- Leasing is by application only (19.2.7.8). **The earlier properly filed application gets a preference right to lease** (19.2.7.15, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-mexico/N-M-Admin-Code-SS-19.2.7.15).
- **Known geothermal fields** are withdrawn and **leased only by public auction** (19.2.7.20, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-mexico/N-M-Admin-Code-SS-19.2.7.20). For simultaneous applications, the commissioner either takes amended high-bonus bids or rejects all applications and auctions the land (19.2.7.17). The commissioner may also reject any application and auction the land for the highest bonus on top of the minimum rental (19.2.7.19).
- **Holding cap: 51,200 acres per holder**, direct or indirect (19.2.7.28, https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/new-mexico/N-M-Admin-Code-SS-19.2.7.28).
- Rental, royalty and term are in the 1967 Act (NMSA 19-13). The statute site returned 403, so these figures are (unverified). NMSLO manages about 9M surface and 13M mineral acres (unverified). Lightning Dock, the plant Zanskar bought, is in the Animas Valley, Hidalgo County.

### Arizona: State Land Department (A.R.S. 27-668 to 27-677)
- **Applicant-triggered competitive auction.** After an application, the department publishes a call for bids and leases "to the highest and best bidder" on the first-year bonus (A.R.S. 27-670, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/27/00670.htm).
- **10-year primary term**, held by production, with a 2-year extension if drilling is underway. **Royalty at least 12.5% of gross wellhead value.** **Rental at least $1/acre/yr.** **2,560 acres maximum per lease** (A.R.S. 27-671, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/27/00671.htm).
- The department may designate KGRAs. Until it does, "all state lands shall be deemed not located within any known geothermal resource area" (A.R.S. 27-669, https://www.azleg.gov/ars/27/00669.htm).
- Arizona trust land totals about 9.2M acres (unverified), but its geothermal resource is modest. The bid process exposes the applicant's target, much like BLM.

### Oregon: Department of State Lands
- ORS 273.551 lets DSL lease state land and mineral or geothermal rights "upon conditions agreed upon." The statute **does not require competitive bidding**, and leases may run with no fixed time limit, subject to due-diligence cancellation (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_273.551). ORS 273.780(2) makes state geothermal rights subject to "exploration permit or lease" by DSL (https://oregon.public.law/statutes/ors_273.780).
- The minerals rules (OAR 141-071) have both an application-priority path and a competitive-bid path (https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_chapter_141_division_71). Whether those rules govern geothermal is (unverified). In short, Oregon is negotiated and bespoke, with a small rangeland estate in the Basin and Range southeast (unverified).

### California: State Lands Commission (PRC 6901-6925)
- **Competitive lands:** lands selected for competitive lease go "by competitive bid on the basis of a cash bonus, net profit, or other single biddable factor" (PRC 6911, https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/public-resources-code/prc-sect-6911/). A surface owner of state-reserved-mineral land may match the high bid within 30 days, or file within 4 months of another party's application (PRC 6912, https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/public-resources-code/prc-sect-6912/).
- **Prospecting permit, the most Terranox-like instrument in the West.** On lands not selected for competitive bid, the commission may grant **2 years of exclusive prospecting, extendable to 4 years total**. Rent is **$1/acre in year 1, rising to at most $5 in year 2 and $25 in year 3** until a well is drilled. **On a discovery in commercial quantities, the permittee is "entitled to a lease"** if the permit provides for it (PRC 6910, https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/public-resources-code/prc-sect-6910/).
- **Lease economics:** rental at least $1/acre/yr. **Royalty at least 10% of gross revenue** on steam, brine and gas, and **up to 16 2/3%** in competitive sales. At least 2% on mineral by-products such as Salton Sea lithium. Minimum royalty $2/acre after discovery. Renegotiation 20-30 years after commercial operation, then every 10 years (PRC 6913, https://codes.findlaw.com/ca/public-resources-code/prc-sect-6913/).
- CSLC holds about 458k acres of school lands plus about 790k acres of reserved mineral interests (unverified). Much of it is scattered desert sections, and a drilling obligation comes with the permit.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **State trust land is where the "file first, before proof" move still exists.** BLM auctions every geothermal parcel and publishes nominations ahead of each sale, so your ML target is visible to everyone. Utah (time-stamped over-the-counter), Idaho (no auction unless someone else applies), New Mexico (first-filer preference outside known fields) and California (prospecting permit with an entitlement to lease) all let a company hold a blind target at **about $1/acre/yr** without revealing it at auction. That is the closest US match to Saskatchewan claim staking.
2. **Holding costs are tiny compared with the step-up.** 2,560 acres in Utah at the floor rate costs about $2,560/yr (the $500 minimum is below that). A 4-year California prospecting permit costs about $1+$5+$25+$25 = **$56/acre over 4 years** (assuming year 4 is capped at $25), and only if no well is drilled. Royalties of 2-5% (Idaho power) to 10-16.7% (California) and 12.5% (Arizona) reduce what a buyer will pay. Even so, the right is optional and cheap to carry.
3. **Pick states with first-come rules and real heat.** Utah, the Great Basin side of Idaho, and New Mexico's Rio Grande rift and bootheel (where Zanskar's Lightning Dock is) are the targets. Skip Arizona, where every application triggers an auction. Nevada state land barely exists, so Nevada means BLM plus private rights.
4. **Checkerboard strategy.** Trust sections (2, 16, 32 and 36 per township in UT, NM and AZ) are spread across BLM-dominated basins. A blind system rarely sits wholly inside one section. The play is to lock the state sections first-come, which **creates blocking or unitization leverage** over the surrounding BLM parcels that someone must later win at auction. Every state here has unit and cooperative-development provisions (e.g., A.R.S. 27-672, NMAC 19.2.7.43, IDAPA 20.03.15.085), so a state lease can carry a share of a larger unit.
5. **The gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar is vertically integrated: it explores, drills, develops and owns plants (Lightning Dock). It mostly competes in BLM auctions and on private land in Nevada and Utah, holding resources for its own development. The open seam is a **land-bank / prospect-generator** that runs ML over well logs and temperature-gradient data, then quietly assembles **state-trust and private geothermal rights** in many basins. It would not drill to production. Instead it would de-risk to a temperature-gradient or slim-hole stage and sell or farm out to Fervo, Ormat, Zanskar itself, or data-centre developers, keeping an override. Cheap first-come state rights are the cost-efficient raw material for that model.
6. **Risks.** Utah's director, New Mexico's commissioner and Idaho's board all have broad discretion to reject an application and auction the land, so a blatant land rush may be converted into a sale. Royalty and rental terms are often set per lease, which leaves room to negotiate but makes them hard to underwrite in advance. Each state's total geothermal-prospective trust acreage is a bounded pool (unverified), so state land alone is probably too small. Combine it with private fee geothermal rights, especially Nevada railroad checkerboard and Utah private land, to get scale.
