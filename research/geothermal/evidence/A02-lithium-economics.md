# A02 — Economics of owning brine-lithium rights (Smackover, Salton Sea): is lithium the faster step-up?

Everything below comes from fetching primary pages directly: Standard Lithium/Smackover Lithium press releases, USGS, permitting.gov, CDTFA and Imperial County. Several numbers I wanted, such as Exxon's and Albemarle's $/acre, the Galvanic sale price, Chevron's acreage, and the California excise-tax rates, were behind 403/404 or paywalled pages. ## 1. The best documented case: Smackover Lithium (Standard Lithium 55% / Equinor 45%)

This is the cleanest public record of the full path from lease to proven asset in US brine lithium. It is also the closest analogue to a Terranox-style land play.

**South West Arkansas (SWA), DFS, 3 Sep 2025** ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/smackover-lithium-announces-positive-definitive-feasibility-study-results-for-its-south-west-arkansas-project)):
- About 30,000 acres of brine leases in total. The initial Reynolds Brine Unit covers **20,854 acres**.
- 22,500 tpa battery-quality Li2CO3. Capex **$1.45B** (Class III, 12.3% contingency). Cash opex **$4,516/t**. All-in cost **$5,924/t**, which includes royalties.
- Pre-tax unlevered NPV8 **$1.666B**, after-tax $1.275B. IRR 20.2% pre-tax, 18.2% after-tax. Price deck **$22,400/t** (Fastmarkets 20-year forward average).
- P&P reserves 709 kt LCE. Grade 481 mg/L average over the plant's life, 549 mg/L at start-up.
- Royalty: **2.5% gross plus a $65/acre/yr brine fee**, plus override fees on certain optioned leases.

**Derived value per acre:** $1.666B / 20,854 acres ≈ **$80,000 per unit acre** (pre-tax NPV8 at DFS stage, 100% basis). That value only exists once $1.45B of capex is committed. It is not a price anyone has paid for the land.

**Royalty decision, AOGC, 29 May 2025** ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/smackover-lithiums-south-west-arkansas-project-receives-royalty-rate-approval-from-the-arkansas-oil-and-gas-commission)):
- The Arkansas Oil and Gas Commission unanimously approved a **2.5% quarterly gross royalty** for the Reynolds Unit. It was the first lithium-brine royalty the state has ever set.
- The rate matched the operator's application exactly (filed 6 May 2025). With the $65.05/acre/yr "in lieu bromine royalty" fee added, the release puts total compensation at about **3%** at then-current prices.
- Implication: in Arkansas the state sets the royalty for a unitised brine pool. **Brine owners (landowners) get a low, fixed ~2.5–3% gross. The developer or lessee keeps the step-up.** For comparison, landowners commonly receive 12.5–25% in oil and gas (unverified, general industry knowledge). A company that owns the lease or working interest is on the winning side of this rule. A company that only holds a royalty is not.

**Federal path:** SWA was designated a Priority Critical Mineral Project on 21 Apr 2025. It finished federal permitting on 13 May 2026 under a 20-year operating plan, with first production expected in **2029** ([permitting.gov](https://www.permitting.gov/newsroom/press-releases/south-west-arkansas-project-completes-federal-permitting)). A **$225M DOE grant** closed on 16 Jan 2025 ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/standard-lithium-and-equinor-finalize-225-million-grant-from-the-us-department-of-energy-for-the-south-west-arkansas-project)). Binding offtakes were signed in March 2026 (first), August 2026 (LG Energy Solution) and September 2026 ("exceeds targeted volumes"). Two pre-FID contracts were awarded in May 2026, and FID had not been announced as of the press-release index ([index](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases)). FID was first targeted for end-2025, so it has slipped by more than 6 months.

**East Texas, Franklin Project:**
- Maiden inferred resource, 24 Sep 2025: **2.159 Mt LCE at 668 mg/L** (peak 806 mg/L, the highest reported in North America). Leasing began in **2022**: **617 separate brine leases** covering **46,287 gross acres** in a project area of about 80,200 acres ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/smackover-lithium-releases-maiden-inferred-resource-for-its-franklin-project-comprising-a-portion-of-significant-brine-position-in-east-texas)).
- PEA, 20 Oct 2026: acreage grew to **110,064 acres** (+37%). Pre-tax NPV8 **$5.9B**, IRR 25.7%, capital intensity **$49,945 per annual tonne**, 1.29 Mt Li2CO3 over 20 years ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/standard-lithium-files-positive-preliminary-economic-assessment-for-the-high-grade-70000-tonnes-per-year-franklin-project-in-east-texas)). That implies roughly 70 ktpa × $50k ≈ **$3.5B capex** (derived).
- Derived: $5.9B / 110,064 acres ≈ **$53,600 per acre** of PEA-stage NPV. The leases were taken one at a time from private owners, about 75 acres per lease (46,287 / 617). The terms were not disclosed.

**The real step-up data point: Equinor farm-in, 8 May 2024** ([source](https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/standard-lithium-and-equinor-form-partnership-to-develop-south-west-arkansas-and-east-texas-lithium-projects)):
- Equinor paid **$30M cash** plus a **$60M funded work programme**, of which $33M carries Standard Lithium. In return it took **45%** of both the SWA and ETX project companies, with up to $160M of total gross investment.
- Rough implied value (derived): ($30M + $33M carry) / 45% ≈ **$140M for 100%**, or about $200M if the whole $90M is counted. That is for a pre-DFS position of roughly 30k SWA acres plus an early-stage ETX lease book.
- Per acre, that is on the order of **$2,000–4,000 per acre at farm-in** (derived, depending on how many ETX acres had been leased by May 2024, which is not disclosed). This is the stage where the step-up happens without capex: leases taken in 2022–24 at bonus-plus-rental terms, then marked up through a strategic farm-out within about **2 years**.

## 2. Other Smackover players (mostly unverified because search was unavailable)

- **ExxonMobil**: in 2023 bought about **120,000 gross acres** in SW Arkansas from Galvanic Energy, reportedly for **more than $100M** (WSJ). That works out to about **$800+/acre** for an exploration-stage lithium brine lease book (unverified). Exxon set a 2027 first-production target under the "Mobil Lithium" brand (unverified). Galvanic is the clean "acquire rights early, prove grade with data, sell to a major" exit, and is the strongest precedent for a Terranox-style company in lithium.
- **Albemarle**: a long-standing bromine brine operator in Columbia/Union counties (the Magnolia operations). It already holds brine rights and units under the bromine regime that the $65/acre fee mirrors (unverified detail).
- **Chevron**: entered US lithium in mid-2025 by buying roughly **125,000 net acres** in NE Texas and SW Arkansas (unverified). It is a fourth major buyer in the basin.
- Typical bonuses for Texas/Arkansas brine leases in 2023–25 are said to range from low hundreds to about $1,000+/acre, with $/acre/yr rentals (unverified; no primary source fetched).

**Why ML on public data works here (USGS proof of concept):** In October 2024 USGS used **machine learning on the USGS National Produced Waters Geochemical Database** (1965–2018 historic samples plus 2022 sampling) to estimate **5.1–19.0 Mt of lithium in place** in Arkansas Smackover brines ([USGS release](https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/unlocking-arkansas-hidden-treasure-usgs-uses-machine-learning-show-large); [FS 2024-3052](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20243052/full)). The model resolution is about **1.5 mi²**, and USGS says predictions are "not suitable for specific locations". The maximum predicted concentration is about 399 mg/L, while industry wells now measure **549–806 mg/L**. USGS also notes that 1960s values above 500 mg/L "could not be verified".

This is the Terranox thesis in miniature. Old, illegible oilfield produced-water records hold the signal. The public model is too coarse to choose acreage. Operators kept finding higher grades than the public model predicted. The edge belongs to whoever builds a finer model at lease scale and leases first.

## 3. Salton Sea ("Lithium Valley"), the only true geothermal-brine lithium play

- The brine is at 250–380 °C, 1–3 km deep, with about **200 ppm Li** (±20%) and 24% TDS. The CEC cites 3.4 Mt of reserve and a potential 600 ktpa LCE ([Wikipedia summary citing LBNL/CEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_Valley); [LBNL report](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/4x8868mf)). Imperial County cites an LBNL 2023 estimate of **17+ Mt** of lithium in the regional brines ([Imperial County](https://lithiumvalley.imperialcounty.org/)).
- Existing operators include BHE Renewables (about 10 plants in the field), Controlled Thermal Resources (Hell's Kitchen, combined power and lithium), and EnergySource Minerals. The Lithium Valley Specific Plan and its PEIR were in draft as of the Dec 2025 version (same Imperial County source).
- **California lithium extraction excise tax:** tiered by cumulative LCE (≤20 kt, 20–30 kt, >30 kt) ([CDTFA](https://www.cdtfa.ca.gov/taxes-and-fees/lithium-extraction-excise-tax.htm)). The rates are **$400 / $600 / $800 per tonne** under SB 125 (2022) (rates unverified; the page did not list them). At $22k/t this is a 2–4% levy. It is a tax, not a royalty to landowners.
- Land: the key surface and mineral holders around the field include the Imperial Irrigation District, BHE, CTR's lease position, and federal and state land (unverified detail). The field has been fully leased and producing power since the 1980s. **There is no blind or unleased upside left for a newcomer.** It is a development play, not an exploration play.
- Other US geothermal brines (Nevada, Utah) typically carry far less lithium than Salton's ~200 ppm. Tens of mg/L is typical (unverified). So outside Salton, "geothermal lithium" is at best a by-product of a power project, not a way to value the land.

## 4. Is lithium brine the faster step-up?

**Arguments for:**
- The Galvanic → Exxon sale (2023) and the Standard Lithium → Equinor farm-in (2024) both happened **about 1–2 years after leasing began**. Neither required a power plant, a PPA or an interconnection.
- Grade from a sampled well is proof enough. One brine sample (at $ thousands) re-entering an existing oilfield well can change the value of thousands of acres. Geothermal proof needs a flow test or production well, typically $5–15M per well (unverified, see other evidence files).
- At least four deep-pocketed buyers compete in one basin: Exxon, Chevron, Equinor, and Albemarle/Koch/Oxy-type players (partly unverified). The DOE grant and priority-project designation show federal tailwinds.
- The royalty regime favours the lessee. Arkansas fixed landowner compensation at about 2.5–3%, so an early lessee captures nearly all of the NPV.

**Arguments against:**
- **Prices**: the economics rest on $22,400/t forward pricing. Spot lithium carbonate fell from about $70–80k/t (late 2022) to about $10k/t (2024–25) (unverified). Buyers' appetite for farm-ins swings with price. Geothermal power sold to data centres is a firmer, longer-dated price.
- **Capex intensity**: $1.45B for 22.5 ktpa, about $50k per annual tonne. The NPV per acre ($50–80k) is only realised by majors. A landholder exits at roughly $1–4k/acre, which is 2–5% of the DFS NPV.
- **Geographic concentration**: the prize is Smackover (AR/TX/LA/MS/AL) and Salton. Arkansas is already leased up by Exxon, Standard, Albemarle and Chevron. The remaining open frontier is the **Texas/Louisiana/Mississippi/Alabama Smackover**, where the produced-water and well-log data is public and illegible: exactly the Terranox input.
- **FID slippage**: even the leader has no FID 13+ months after its DFS. Lithium proves faster, but takes longer to cash flow.

**Verdict**: Lithium brine gives the **faster step-up from lease to farm-out** (1–2 years, roughly 3–10× on lease cost; the multiple is derived and unverified). But it is a **commodity-cyclical, basin-concentrated side-bet, not a substitute** for geothermal. It is not "too small": tens of billions of NPV across Smackover. It is "too correlated": one price cycle can freeze every buyer at once.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Same data, two products.** Oil and gas wells hold bottom-hole temperatures (heat) and produced-water chemistry (lithium, bromine, iodine) in the same files. USGS has already shown that ML on the Produced Waters Database works, and that its 1.5 mi² public output is too coarse to choose leases. A Terranox-style model that ingests old Gulf Coast logs can **rank acreage on heat and Li-mg/L at once**, and lease the rights that score on either.
2. **Lease the full stack.** In Texas and Louisiana, brine and geothermal rights on private land can be leased together (the legal treatment varies by state; unverified, see D01-texas-rrc.md). Owning both lets the company sell lithium to Exxon/Chevron/Equinor-type buyers on a 1–2 year cycle. That funds the slower geothermal step-up, sold to Fervo, Ormat or utilities on a 3–6 year cycle. Galvanic (about 120k acres to Exxon, more than $100M, unverified) is the precedent to copy for the exit.
3. **Royalty versus working interest.** Arkansas shows the state can cap the landowner's take at about 2.5–3%. The company must **hold the lessee or working-interest position**, not a royalty, to own the step-up. This matches the "own the asset" rule.
4. **Gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar's public footprint is Basin and Range hydrothermal (Nevada/Utah/New Mexico, Big Blind, Lightning Dock). It is power-only, on BLM-style lease geography, with a developer/IPP posture. It has no publicly visible brine-mineral or Gulf Coast sedimentary position (from what is publicly visible). The open gap: **Gulf Coast/Smackover sedimentary basins on private land, where heat and lithium co-occur in decades of public oilfield records.** Here a pure rights-acquisition company can monetise through lithium early and keep geothermal optionality, which Zanskar's model does not target.
5. **Risk to flag in the application.** Lithium is the faster cash event but cyclical. Pitch it as the **first monetisation of the same data engine**, not as the core. Keep geothermal (firm power for data centres) as the long-duration asset.
