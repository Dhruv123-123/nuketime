## 1. Why royalty and mineral companies are the right valuation template

Royalty and mineral owners do not drill, operate, or spend capex. Third-party operators develop their land, and the owner collects a share of gross revenue. The result is the highest-margin, highest-multiple shape in natural resources.

**Takeaways:**
1. **Royalty owners earn roughly twice an operator's margin.** Their margins are 83–92%, against Ormat's 44%. On the same revenue, a royalty dollar is worth more than an operator dollar.
2. **Owning land outright beats owning a thin royalty strip.** TPL trades at ~32x EBITDA, against ~9–10x for Viper, BSM and KRP. The premium reflects that TPL owns the *surface*, not only the mineral right, and sells many things off it. In Q2-26, **$100.5M of $246.1M revenue (41%) was non-royalty**: water sales $39.7M, produced-water royalties $37.1M, and easements and other surface income $23.7M. TPL is also signing land and brackish-water deals for data centres ("Project Kilby") and spent $110.2M buying more land in the quarter. A geothermal land owner near load and transmission has the same optionality on water, surface, easements and data-centre siting.
3. **Mining royalties are valued higher than oil and gas royalties.** FNV trades at ~22x and RGLD at ~16x, against ~9–10x for oil and gas minerals, because mining royalties last longer and decline more slowly. A geothermal royalty is a 30+ year, baseload, non-depleting-price stream (reservoirs do decline, but PPAs are fixed-price), so it should price closer to mining royalties or infrastructure than to shale minerals (unverified, analyst judgement).

## 2. How mineral aggregators got big: consolidation and active management

- **Viper–Sitio (2025):** Viper bought Sitio Royalties in an all-equity deal worth **~$4.1B including ~$1.1B net debt**. The exchange ratio was 0.4855. Sitio held **~34,300 net royalty acres** producing 42.1 Mboe/d. That works out to about **$120k per net royalty acre** (calc). Closing on 19 Aug 2025 lifted Viper's Q3 guidance by 18 Mboe/d. Viper has kept buying since: the Riverbend acquisition (closed 1 Jul 2026), 933 NRA from Diamondback for ~3.7M OpCo units, and $103M of cash acquisitions in Q2-26. Sitio itself was a roll-up of fragmented private minerals (unverified history detail). **Aggregating fragmented rights is itself a path to a multi-billion-dollar company.**
- **Black Stone is the closest analogue to a geothermal resource bank.** BSM does not wait for operators to show up. Its "engineering and geotechnical staff ... build prospects to offer to industry" and its land team "brings outside development capital onto its acreage." It signs **development agreements** with minimum drilling commitments:
  - Revenant: 270,000 gross acres, minimum 6 wells in 2026, ramping to 25 per year by 2030. A May 2026 amendment cut Year 1 to 4 wells and released ~40,000 gross acres.
  - Caturus: 220,000 gross acres, with minimum annual lateral-foot requirements, ramping to ~12 gross wells per year by 2031.
  - Adamas (formerly Aethon): 14 wells spud in the program year ended 30 Jun 2026.

  The model is: own the rights, do the geoscience in-house, and then contract operators to drill on a schedule while you keep the royalty. **This is the template a Terranox-geo would copy with Fervo, Ormat or Invenergy as the "operators".** It also shows the risk: operators can renegotiate commitments down (Revenant).
- **TPL is the extreme long-hold outcome.** A bankruptcy-liquidation land trust from 1888 became a **$23.75B** company. Revenue went from $66M in 2016 to $798M in 2025 (Wikipedia). Value came from geology that others later developed, plus adjacent surface businesses.

## 3. Mining prospect generators: the closest risk-stage analogue, and a warning

Prospect generators acquire ground cheaply, add geoscience, then option or sell to majors while keeping a royalty. EMX Royalty is the type example. Its exit valuation was modest: **~$444M market cap on ~$30M TTM revenue** when it merged into Elemental Altus in Nov 2025 (StockAnalysis). The big winners were **royalty owners whose royalties reached production at scale** (FNV $47B, RGLD $20.5B), not the project-flippers. **Lesson: flipping alone gives a company worth hundreds of millions. A multi-billion outcome requires keeping a royalty or carry on what you sell, plus the land itself, so the portfolio compounds into TPL- or FNV-type cash flow.**

## 4. Geothermal royalty mechanics (what exists today)

**Federal (BLM) leases issued on or after 8 Aug 2005** (43 CFR 3211.17):
- Lessee generates and sells electricity: **1.75% of gross proceeds for the first 10 years of production, then 3.5%.**
- Lessee sells the steam or brine at arm's length to a generator: **10%**.
- ONRR computes royalty on gross proceeds from the electricity sale, with limited deductions for Class I leases only (30 CFR 1206.352).
- Ormat's 10-K describes the band as "royalty rates are fixed between 1.0%-2.5% of the gross proceeds from the sale of electricity" in the first ten years.

**BLM rents** (43 CFR 3211.11):
- Competitive leases: $2/ac in year 1, $3/ac in years 2–10.
- Noncompetitive leases: $1/ac in years 1–10.
- Both: $5/ac after year 10.

**Private leases:** Ormat's 10-K says the lessee pays "a certain specified percentage of the value" measured at the wellhead, on gross proceeds or on gross revenues. Primary terms run "five to 30 years". Typical private royalties are **2–5%**, with bonuses of $1–30/ac and rent of ~$1–3/ac/yr (via R06/B01, unverified individually).

**State examples:**
- Texas GLO: 10% gross royalty and $3/ac delay rental (via L10).
- Arkansas brine unit: 2.5% gross royalty (via L12).

**Non-landowner override precedent:** in the Fervo Cape Phase I financing, Centaurus provided $75M of preferred equity and keeps a **$5/MWh royalty** after its priority return (Fervo 424B4, via B01/B02/R04). At ~7,900 MWh per MW-yr that is ~$39k/MW-yr, or **~$0.4M/MW PV** (8%, 25 yr, calc).

**Exploration and drilling cost context (Ormat FY2025 10-K):**
- Initial evaluation: ~$10k per prospect in the US.
- Exploration before a full-size well: $1.0–5.0M per site.
- Production and injection wells: $2.1–13.0M each, average ~$4.3M in the US.
- New PPAs are now priced "above $100 per MWh", against $60–80 historically.

## 5. Sizing: is a geothermal royalty or land aggregator big enough?

**Royalties alone on today's hydrothermal base: too small.**
- US geothermal output is roughly 16–17 TWh/yr (unverified, EIA order of magnitude).
- A blended 3% royalty on ~$90/MWh is ~$2.7/MWh, so the *entire* existing US geothermal royalty pool is only **~$45M/yr** (calc).
- For scale: BLM-land plants total 2.65 GW across 51 plants (BLM).
- Even owning all of it at an RGLD-like 16x would be < $1B. **Under the "too small kills" rule, a pure aggregator of existing geothermal royalties fails.**

**Royalties on new capacity: big, if the build-out happens.**
- Per GW (calc):
  - 1 GW at 90% capacity factor is ~7.9 TWh/yr.
  - At a $5/MWh override that is **~$39M/yr**, or ~$0.6B at 16x.
  - At a 3.5% royalty on $100/MWh it is ~$28M/yr.
- To support a **$5–10B** royalty company (FNV/RGLD/TPL tier) you need roughly **10–20 GW under override**.
- That is plausible only in an EGS or next-generation scale-up:
  - Fervo alone has a 3 GW Google framework (via Z05).
  - NREL GeoVision-type scenarios run to tens of GW (via L06).
  - Sibling B04 computes $5/MWh on 90 GW ≈ **$3.5B/yr** of royalty cash flow.
- On optimistic-scope scoring this passes, but **only if the company holds rights on the next-gen resource (EGS-grade heat, sedimentary basins), not just blind hydrothermal**.

**Land and step-up adds a second engine.**
- BLM acreage that cleared at $2/ac now clears at **$232–412/ac** on hot trends (via R01/Z04/B01).
- Fervo's IPO implies **~$12.6–12.9k per leased acre** once the land sits inside a contracted developer (calc, via B01/Z09).
- A Terranox-geo that buys at $2–20/ac and sells a portion at the developer stage while retaining a $5/MWh override captures both:
  - the step-up, which is cash today;
  - the royalty, which compounds into long-dated, high-multiple cash flow.
- That is the TPL and BSM structure.

**Tax wrapper:** the 2025 reconciliation law (OBBBA, §70524) added income from "geothermal energy" to MLP qualifying income (via B05). Geothermal royalties can now sit in the same yield vehicle as BSM and KRP (both MLP or partnership structures), which widens the buyer base for a royalty portfolio at exit.

## 6. Solar and wind land aggregators (thin evidence this pass)

Ground-lease aggregators for solar and wind exist. Landmark Dividend, later owned by DigitalBridge, is one example; its deal values are (unverified) because no primary source was fetched. They trade as infrastructure or real-estate yield vehicles at low single-digit cap rates (unverified). Their rents are fixed $/acre, not a share of revenue, so they capture none of a resource step-up. The surface-easement line at TPL ($23.7M/quarter, which includes renewables and power easements, composition unverified) is the public proof that renewables land rents are a meaningful *add-on* for a large landholder. They are not a standalone thesis. Geothermal differs from solar and wind in a way that favours the Terranox shape: **the resource is scarce and site-specific**, like oil and gas, rather than ubiquitous like sunlight. That scarcity is what justifies mineral-style royalties rather than flat rents.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The right comps are royalty owners, not developers.** Royalty and mineral companies earn 83–92% EBITDA margins against Ormat's 44%, and are valued at 9.5x (Viper, BSM, KRP), 16–22x (RGLD, FNV) and 32x (TPL). A Terranox-geo should be pitched as "**the Black Stone or TPL of heat**": own the rights, do the geoscience, and contract developers to drill under development agreements.
2. **A pure royalty aggregator on existing geothermal is too small.** The whole US pool is ~$45M/yr (calc). The company must combine three things:
   - (a) **buy-before-proof land** at $2–20/ac, then sell part of it at the developer step-up ($232–412/ac at auction, ~$12.9k/ac inside Fervo);
   - (b) a **retained override**, Centaurus-style $5/MWh or 2–5% gross, on everything sold or farmed out;
   - (c) **surface, water and data-centre-site optionality**, as at TPL, where 41% of revenue is non-royalty.

   Reaching $5–10B needs ~10–20 GW under override, which means holding **EGS- and sedimentary-grade acreage**, not only blind hydrothermal.
3. **Flipping alone caps out in the hundreds of millions** (EMX ~$444M). Never sell clean: every sale should keep a carry or override, so the portfolio becomes a long-lived royalty book.
4. **The gap vs Zanskar.**
   - Zanskar is a developer-owner. It finds a system (Big Blind), then builds and operates it (Lightning Dock, six 20 MW plants, a 100 MW NV Energy PPA). That puts it at Ormat's ~44%-margin, capex-heavy point in the chain. Its auction footprint is small: ~18 NV parcels and ~50k acres (via Z04/R01).
   - Because it competes with Ormat and Fervo, it **cannot be the neutral rights-holder** that every developer contracts with.
   - The open position is the Black Stone role: a multi-state, ML-screened rights bank that holds hundreds of thousands of acres of federal, state, private and split-estate rights. It would sign minimum-commitment development agreements with Fervo, Ormat, Invenergy, XGS, Sage, utilities, hyperscaler developers, and even Zanskar, and keep royalties.
   - Nobody occupies this role today. Fervo self-sources acreage at ~$4/ac. The landmen (Buffalo River, Percheron, Cordillera) speculate on acreage without exploration or royalty structuring (via R01/Z04).
5. **Risks:**
   - Royalty value depends on someone else building. BSM's Revenant cut its commitments, and BLM's 10-year primary term forces a flip or development within ~5–8 years.
   - Federal royalties go to the government, not to the lessee. On BLM land the Terranox-geo earns only an *override* it negotiates, not the 1.75–3.5% statutory royalty.
   - Developers with fresh capital (Fervo ~$1.9B IPO) may prefer to buy land outright rather than accept overrides. Expect a price concession for override structures.
   - Market multiples here come from a third-party aggregator and move with commodity prices.
