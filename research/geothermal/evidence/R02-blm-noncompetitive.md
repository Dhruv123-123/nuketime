# R02 — BLM noncompetitive geothermal leasing (43 CFR 3200): terms, costs, transferability, who uses it

Research note, 2026-10-21. Primary sources are the eCFR text of 43 CFR Part 3200, retrieved through the eCFR versioner API; 30 U.S.C. §1003; BLM Public Land Statistics FY2024 and FY2025; and BLM Nevada State Office sale-result PDFs (2022 to 2026). The shared WebSearch budget ran out partway through, so the remaining items were pulled straight from primary documents. Anything not verified that way is marked (unverified).

## 1. How the noncompetitive route works

- **Statute.** 30 U.S.C. §1003(c): "The Secretary shall make available for a period of 2 years for noncompetitive leasing any tract for which a competitive lease sale is held, but for which the Secretary does not receive any bids." Competitive sales must be held "at least once every 2 years" in any state with nominations pending (§1003(b)(2)). Anyone qualified can nominate land at any time (§1003(a)).
- **The four noncompetitive doors** (43 CFR 3203.5(b), 3204.5):
  1. Lands that drew no bid at a competitive sale. This is the main one.
  2. Direct-use leases (non-electric heat use only, the resource may not be sold, and there must be no competitive interest; Subpart 3205).
  3. Mining-claim holders who have an *approved plan of operations* (3204.12).
  4. Applications pending on 8 Aug 2005 (legacy).

  The statute adds a fifth: **an oil and gas lessee with a producing well can get a noncompetitive geothermal lease for co-production** (§1003(b)(4)).
- **Mechanics** (3204.11):
  - *Day after the sale:* every application received on the first business day after the sale counts as filed at the same time. Each applicant may file one application per parcel, and **BLM picks the winner at random**.
  - *Days 1 to 30:* applications are accepted only for parcels as configured in the sale notice.
  - *After day 30:* applicants may reconfigure any available lands from the sale.
  - *Rest of the window:* **first qualified applicant wins**, and simultaneous overlapping filings go to a random draw.
- **Payment with the application** (3204.10): the noncompetitive processing fee plus **$1/acre advance rent**. The rent is refunded if the application is rejected. An application is rejected if the rent paid is under 90% of the correct amount.
- **Who can hold a lease** (3202.10): US citizens aged 18 or older, associations of US citizens, US/state/DC corporations, or domestic governmental units. Proof of qualification is not needed at filing (3202.11).

## 3. Rent, royalty, term, diligence

**Rent** (3211.11, for leases issued after EPAct 2005):
- **Noncompetitive:** $1/acre/yr for years 1 to 10.
- **Competitive:** $2/acre in year 1 and $3/acre in years 2 to 10.
- **Both, from year 11:** $5/acre/yr.
- Rent is always payable, including during production (3211.14). It can be credited against royalty under the ONRR rules (3211.15).

**Royalty** (3211.17):
- When the lessee or an affiliate sells electricity: **1.75% of gross proceeds for the first 10 years of production, then 3.5%.**
- When the lessee sells the geothermal fluid arm's-length to a generator: **10%** of gross proceeds.
- Byproducts are charged at Mineral Leasing Act rates for listed minerals. Lithium is *not* a listed MLA mineral, so no royalty would be due on it (3211.19(a)(2); applying this to lithium is my reading, unverified).
- No minimum royalty applies to post-2005 leases (3211.21).
- Revenue is split 50% to the state, 25% to the county and 25% to Treasury (BLM Nevada page).

**Lease size and caps** (3206.12–3206.14):
- Each lease is 640 to **5,120 acres**.
- **No one may hold more than 51,200 acres per state, directly or indirectly.** Holding more than 10% of the stock of a corporation that holds leases counts toward the cap pro rata.

**Term** (3207.5–3207.16):
- **10-year primary term.**
- First extension of 5 years. It requires **$40/acre of qualifying work by the end of year 10**, or a cash payment in lieu, or proof of commercial production.
- To keep the first extension: **$15/acre/yr in years 11 to 14**. To earn the second 5-year extension: **$15/acre in year 15**. To keep it: **$25/acre/yr in years 16 to 19**.
- Qualifying work includes temperature-gradient holes, geophysics, geochemistry, core drilling, "geologic investigation and analysis" and other activities BLM approves (3207.11(a)).
- These dollar amounts are **indexed every 3 years** to the GDP implicit price deflator (3207.12(i)), so today's figures are probably above the base numbers (unverified).
- If drilling is under way at expiry, a 5-year drilling extension applies (3207.14).
- With commercial production, a production extension of up to 35 years applies, then a preferential renewal of up to 55 years (3207.15–16).
- BLM may also limit how many years it accepts payment in lieu of work (3207.12(d)).

**Bonding** (3214.10): a bond is required **before exploration, drilling or utilization operations**, not just to hold a lease. Minimum amounts are set by activity type (exploration under 3251.15, drilling under 3261.18).

## 4. Transferability: yes, a fixed-fee assignable right with ministerial approval

- Record title and operating rights can both be transferred, but **BLM must approve before the transfer takes effect** (3216.10). Approval is effective **on the first day of the month after BLM approves** (3216.21).
- BLM refuses a transfer **only if** (3216.22): (a) the lease account is not in good standing; (b) the transferee is not qualified; or (c) there is no adequate bond. This is a short checklist, not a discretionary public-interest review.
- **Paperwork** (3216.14–3216.15): forms 3000-3 (record title) and 3000-3(a) (operating rights), the $115 fee per lease, and filing within 90 days of signing.
- **Partial assignments** must generally cover 640 acres or more (3216.19). Assigning 100% of a portion segregates it into a new lease with the same terms (3216.20).
- **Liabilities after transfer** (3216.12–3216.13): the transferee takes on obligations accruing after the transfer. The assignor keeps pre-transfer rent and royalty, and keeps the plugging liability for wells drilled while it held the lease.
- **Corporate mergers and name changes** need only a list of leases (no form). Selling the *company* that holds leases is the cleanest exit.
- **Conclusion:** a BLM geothermal lease is a fixed-cost, freely tradable property-like right. Approval is close to automatic for a qualified, bonded buyer, and the lease does not reset when it changes hands. Pre-proof flips to Ormat, Fervo or utilities are legally simple.

## 6. Cost model: holding a 5,120-acre lease for 10 years

- **Noncompetitive:** about $520 fee + $1 × 5,120 × 10 = **about $51.7k**.
- **Competitive at the $2 minimum bid:** $10,240 bonus + $10,240 (year 1) + $3 × 5,120 × 9 = **about $158.7k**.
- **Either path, to extend past year 10:** $40/ac = **$204.8k** of work. Ordinary exploration work counts toward it, including "geologic investigation and analysis", which plausibly covers the work an ML-plus-geophysics firm would do anyway (unverified that BLM credits desk ML).
- **At the 51,200 ac/state cap:** about $517k (noncompetitive) or about $1.6M (competitive at minimum) of rent over 10 years per state, plus about $2.0M of work obligation by year 10.
- These are trivially small numbers next to the value of a proven 20 to 50 MW resource (asset value is unverified here; see other R-files).

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The right is cheap, fixed-price and assignable.** BLM leases match the Terranox thesis as a legal object:
   - $1 to $3/ac/yr rent and $2/ac minimum bonus.
   - A 10-year option on the resource with no royalty until production.
   - Transfer approval is ministerial: good standing, a qualified buyer, a bond.
   - Royalty is only 1.75% then 3.5% of electricity revenue, so the buyer keeps most of the step-up.

   This is close to Saskatchewan claim staking, and arguably better: a fixed price per acre and no competing claim once issued.
2. **Noncompetitive is the gap, not the core.** You can only get land noncompetitively that *nobody bid on*.
   - Nominations become public sale notices, so your own nominations broadcast your targets to Ormat, Invenergy, Zanskar and speculators.
   - The day-after lottery and first-come filing mean anyone can grab unsold parcels.
   - The proprietary edge: run ML continuously on **every** unsold parcel, *including other people's nominations* (about 97k ac unsold in Oct 2025 alone, with 53.7k ac still open in Sep 2026), and file at $1/ac on the ones the model rates highly. You do not reveal yourself in an auction, and the cost is about $5k per 5,000 ac.
   - In Oct 2025, 26 of 27 unsold parcels drew no day-after filings. The market is not screening the leftovers.
3. **Competitive land is still cheap.** Nevada averages about $8 to $34/ac, and even the "hot" parcels top out at $200 to $410/ac. A Terranox-shape company can simply outbid on its high-conviction targets: a 5,120-ac lease at $100/ac is $512k, against a proven-resource value in the tens of millions (unverified).
4. **The binding constraint is the 51,200 ac/state cap,** not cost. It is about 10 maximum-size leases per state, and it counts indirect ownership above 10% stock, so ring-fenced SPVs do not trivially escape it.
   - So the portfolio must be *selective*: high-precision ML matters more than breadth.
   - Go **multi-state** (NV, UT, ID, OR, NM, CA, plus WA, AZ and CO), using 3216 assignments to recycle capacity by selling proven or rejected leases.
   - Pair BLM leases with **state trust land and private fee geothermal rights**, which have no federal cap (see other R-files).
5. **Oil and gas co-production and mining-claim side doors** give a Terranox-specific data play. §1003(b)(4) lets producing oil and gas lessees get noncompetitive geothermal leases. A firm that mines old oil-well bottom-hole temperatures could partner with or option from those lessees, which auction-only competitors do not touch.
6. **Gap vs Zanskar.**
   - Zanskar is in the same auctions: 7 Nevada wins in 2024, 2 in 2025, at least 1 in 2023, mostly at $14 to $61/ac. It is a modest buyer of rights, not a dominant one.
   - Its stated model is exploration plus development and ownership of plants (e.g., Lightning Dock). That ties up capital in drilling and power plants, and with the per-state cap its acreage is confined to its own pipeline.
   - The opening for a Terranox shape is the **prospect generator / royalty-and-flip model**: amass the best-ranked leases across states, prove them with cheap work-commitment-eligible activity (thermal-gradient holes, magnetotellurics, ML), then assign them under 3216 to Ormat, Invenergy or Fervo, or retain an overriding royalty. Doing only exploration work does not raise the plugging and abandonment risk the way production drilling does.
   - Today the buyer universe (Ormat, Invenergy) bids for land itself. Pre-derisked inventory is the step-up product.
   - **Risks:**
     - The 14% producing rate means most leases never produce.
     - The $40/ac year-10 work bar forces real spend.
     - Auction visibility.
     - Speculator LLCs (Buffalo River, Skyladder, Cordillera) already follow this cheap-option strategy without ML, so the edge must be *picking*, not *access*.
