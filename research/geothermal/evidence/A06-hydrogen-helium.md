## 1. Why this topic matters for a geothermal "Terranox"

Geothermal, natural (geologic) hydrogen and helium share inputs.
- **Same data:** legacy oil/gas wells, mud-gas logs, bottom-hole temperatures, basement-penetrating wells, gravity and magnetic data, fault and structure maps.
- **Same geology:** rift and basement structures, deep fluid pathways, faults with high permeability, and seals.
- **Overlapping rights questions:** who owns a non-hydrocarbon gas or the earth's heat under fee, state or federal land.

If one ML/data stack can rank prospects for heat, H2 and He at the same time, a land-acquisition company could buy a multi-commodity option on each parcel. That raises the expected value per acre bought and widens the buyer universe.

## 2. Natural hydrogen: state of the field (hard numbers)

**USGS prospectivity map (Jan 2025).** Gelman, Hearon and Ellis, *Prospectivity Mapping for Geologic Hydrogen*, USGS Professional Paper 1900 (posted 16 Jan 2025, v1.2 22 Jan 2025).
- It is described as the first public prospectivity map of geologic-H2 accumulations in the conterminous US.
- Method: a play-based source + reservoir + seal approach.
- **The midcontinent and the central California coast rank high.** The offshore eastern seaboard is flagged if long-distance lateral migration occurs.
- https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/pp1900
- Methodologically this is the same as geothermal play-fairway analysis (heat + permeability + fluid). An ML company that builds play-fairway models for geothermal is already building the H2 model's skeleton.

**USGS global resource framing.** USGS (Ellis) says a conservative model mean could meet projected global H2 demand for "thousands of years". Most of that is inaccessible, but recovering a small fraction could cover demand "for hundreds of years".
- USGS points out that hydrogen and helium molecules are about the same diameter, so they may be trapped by similar seals. Helium accumulations persist for up to about 100 Myr.
- The central-US focus is the **Midcontinent Rift** (Lake Superior, Iowa, Minnesota, Michigan).
- https://www.usgs.gov/news/featured-story/potential-geologic-hydrogen-next-generation-energy
- The underlying paper is Ellis & Gelman 2024, *Science Advances*, doi:10.1126/sciadv.ado0955 (fetch blocked, 403). The widely reported model mean of about 5.6 trillion tonnes H2 in place is **(unverified this run)**.

**Only proven producer.** Bourakebougou, Mali (Hydroma).
- About 1,400 m³ of gas per day feeds a village generator.
- H2 is reported above 95%.
- Pressure rose slightly after 11 years of production.
- Sources: Maiga et al. 2023, *Scientific Reports*, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-023-38977-y; Hydroma history, https://hydroma.ca/about-us-our-history/.
- **The commercial proof base is one village-scale well.** Hydrogen is far less de-risked than hydrothermal geothermal, which has more than 3.7 GW operating in the US (general knowledge).

## 3. Leasing and ownership regimes: where the rights sit

The legal picture decides whether a "subsurface rights" bundle can actually be bought.

| Regime | Geothermal | Helium | Natural H2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Federal (BLM)** | Geothermal Steam Act lease (30 USC 1001 et seq.). The definition of "byproduct" **explicitly excludes oil, hydrocarbon gas and helium** (30 USC 1001(d); 43 CFR 3200.1, sourced in sibling R13). | Leased with oil and gas under the Mineral Leasing Act. The federal helium reserve was sold, to Messer in 2024 **(unverified)**. | **No explicit federal leasing authority.** Natural H2 is not a listed MLA mineral, and bills to add it have been introduced but not enacted as of the last confirmation **(unverified)**. A Senate ENR hearing on geologic H2 took place in 2025 (listed on koloma.com). |
| **Nevada (fee)** | NRS 534A.050: the surface owner owns geothermal unless it was severed. NRS 534A.010 **excludes hydrocarbons and helium** from the geothermal resource (https://www.leg.state.nv.us/NRS/NRS-534A.html, sibling R06). | Separate estate | Not addressed **(unverified)** |
| **Kansas (fee-dominant)** | No major geothermal statute (unverified). | Treated as part of the "gas" mineral estate. Kansas courts have historically addressed helium ownership under gas leases (*Navajo Tribe*-era and Kansas helium cases, unverified). | **Ambiguous.** K.S.A. 55-150 (fetched, https://www.ksrevisor.gov/statutes/chapters/ch55/055_001_0050.html) defines oil and gas regulatory terms **without mentioning hydrogen or helium**. Operators such as HyTerra lease from fee mineral owners using oil and gas-style leases that name H2 and He explicitly (unverified). |
| **Nebraska** | n/a | Gas estate | Nebraska has considered or enacted legislation treating natural hydrogen as a mineral and placing it under the Oil and Gas Conservation Commission. This was driven by NH2E's Hoarty well **(unverified; bill number not retrieved)**. |
| **Minnesota** | n/a | Minn. Stat. 93.514 defines "gas" to include **hydrocarbon and nonhydrocarbon gases** and gives the DNR rulemaking authority over pooling, spacing and the like (fetched, https://www.revisor.mn.gov/statutes/cite/93.514). This regime was built for Pulsar helium. | Probably covered as a "nonhydrocarbon gas" (inference) |
| **Saskatchewan** | No stakeable geothermal right (sibling R11) | First-come application | (unverified) |

**Reading across the table:**
1. **Geothermal, helium and hydrogen are separate estates almost everywhere.** A BLM geothermal lease does **not** carry helium. A Nevada fee geothermal right excludes helium. A Kansas oil and gas lease may or may not carry H2. A "subsurface rights" company therefore has to assemble the bundle on purpose. Doing that is itself a value-add (title curation, unitizing fragmented estates) and an arbitrage: owners of severed fee minerals in the Midcontinent often hold the "gas" estate dating from 1920s-1950s deeds, and those deeds are ambiguous about H2.
2. **Legal ambiguity cuts both ways.** If a state later rules that H2 belongs to the gas estate, the people who leased the gas estate early win. That is a Terranox-style "buy before legibility" trade, but on legal legibility rather than geological legibility.
3. **Federal land is closed for H2.** Without MLA coverage, a Great Basin multi-commodity play on BLM land can only bundle geothermal plus minerals (lithium via mining claims, see sibling R13) plus helium via an oil and gas lease. H2 is only a fee-land play for now.

## 4. Does a multi-commodity play broaden the market?

**For it:**
- **Shared inputs.** Mud-gas logs, gas analyses and drill-stem tests in state well databases (Kansas KGS/KCC, Oklahoma OCC, Texas RRC; see siblings D01 and D02) record H2, He and BHT together. One digitization and ML pipeline yields three prospect layers, so the marginal cost of the second and third commodity is close to zero.
- **More buyers.**
  - Geothermal: Fervo, Ormat, utilities, hyperscalers.
  - Helium: industrial-gas majors (Air Products, Linde, Messer), He-3 buyers (Interlune, Bluefors).
  - Hydrogen: Koloma, HyTerra/Fortescue, ammonia producers (Koch, CVR and CF in Kansas and Oklahoma), and Japanese utilities (JERA, Osaka Gas and MHI are already in via Koloma).
- **Venture appetite.** Koloma's financing (above $350M, unverified) and JERA/MHI/Osaka/Fortescue strategic money show that well-capitalized buyers of H2 and He acreage exist.
- **Helium is a real near-term cash commodity.** The 0.3% cut-off is met by the Kansas Sue-Duroche historic 3%, Hoarty at 13% and Topaz at 5.6-8.1%. He-3 adds a premium tail.
- **Midcontinent sedimentary geothermal + H2 + He overlap geographically** (Kansas/Nebraska/Oklahoma basement highs). Low-temperature sedimentary geothermal for direct use or data-centre cooling and heat (sibling L10, L23) stacks onto the same parcels.

**Against it:**
- **Different geography for the core geothermal asset.** High-enthalpy blind hydrothermal (Zanskar's game) sits in the Great Basin on BLM land, where H2 cannot be leased and helium is weak. The best H2/He ground (the Midcontinent) is mediocre geothermal. **The overlap is strongest for sedimentary/low-temperature geothermal, not for power-grade hydrothermal.**
- **Hydrogen is commercially unproven.** One village-scale producer exists, and H2 flow rates and recharge are open questions. Under the standing rules this does not kill the idea (score on plausibility), but H2 should be valued as a free option, not as the core.
- **Focus risk.** A three-commodity pitch can look unfocused to YC and investors. The cleaner framing is: *"we buy the deep-subsurface estate; geothermal is the anchor, helium and hydrogen are embedded options."*

**Net:** a multi-commodity bundle **widens the buyer universe and raises value per acre on fee land in the Midcontinent and Gulf/Permian sedimentary basins.** It adds little in the Great Basin core. The market is broader, but mainly in a second geography.

## 5. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Zanskar is single-commodity and Great Basin-focused.** It lands and develops blind hydrothermal for power (Big Blind, Lightning Dock). Nothing in its public materials shows it bundling helium or hydrogen rights (sibling Z files; unverified for absence). Koloma is the hydrogen equivalent and is also a vertically integrated developer. **No visible player is running "ML over legacy well archives → buy the deep subsurface estate (heat + He + H2) cheaply → sell to whichever commodity buyer values it most."** That is the gap.
2. **The Terranox analogy is strongest here.** Kansas, Oklahoma and Nebraska well files are public but illegible: scanned logs, mud-gas reports, gas analyses. Fee mineral owners are fragmented and price acreage as depleted oil and gas ground. Interest in H2 and He is new, so the step-up after a discovery like Hoarty at 44% H2 / 13% He, or a helium flow test, is large. The play is buy first, prove (or have a buyer prove), then sell.
3. **Recommended shape:** keep geothermal (BLM + Nevada fee) as the anchor thesis that answers "too small". Add a **Midcontinent multi-commodity land bank** in which every lease explicitly conveys "geothermal resources, helium, hydrogen and all non-hydrocarbon gases". The marginal cost of that clause is near zero, and it captures legal-ambiguity upside.
4. **Exit buyers per parcel:**
   - Geothermal: Fervo, Ormat, utilities, data centres.
   - Helium: Pulsar-type juniors, industrial-gas majors.
   - Hydrogen: Koloma, HyTerra/Fortescue, JERA, ammonia producers.
   - Multiple bidders shorten time to monetization, which is the main weakness of geothermal-only land flipping.
5. **Risks to underwrite:**
   - State law could assign H2 to surface owners rather than gas-estate owners. Mitigate by taking leases from both where they are severed.
   - There is no federal H2 leasing.
   - Helium price volatility.
   - Hydrogen remains unproven at commercial scale.
   - None of these kills the idea; they change how much each parcel is worth.
6. **Pitch line:** *"Zanskar finds and builds geothermal plants. Koloma finds and builds hydrogen wells. We own the subsurface estate before anyone knows what it's worth, across heat, helium and hydrogen, and sell it to whichever of them needs it."*
