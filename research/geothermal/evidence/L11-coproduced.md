# L11 — Co-produced geothermal from oil & gas fields, and repurposing abandoned/orphaned wells

Deep literature review. Scope covers resource-potential estimates, pilots (RMOTC, UND/Continental, Huabei, Swan Hills, Blackburn, San Ardo, Pleasant Bayou, Wells of Opportunity), economics, conversion studies, and orphan-well counts. Research note: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through, so a few items (Texas and Alberta geothermal-tenure law, NREL's Blackburn paper body) are marked (unverified) and need a follow-up pass.

---

## 1. Core primary sources, summarised

### 1.1 Augustine & Falkenstern (2014), SPE Journal 19(3):530–541 (NREL): near-term co-production potential
- They combined location, depth and water-production data for **2.5 million oil/gas wells, about 0.5 million of them active**, with geothermal resource maps.
- Their **near-term market potential is about 300 MWe** from active-well co-produced water. That is "less than suggested in previous studies". A Texas-report summary calls the economics "marginal".
- This is the main sober counterweight to the older "3 GW" and "5,000 MW" claims (see 1.2).
- Source: https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/estimate-of-the-near-term-electricity-generation-potential-of-cop/

### 1.2 DOE GTP co-production factsheet (c. 2008–2010) and the Enzi/Ormat claim
- DOE says water produced annually by US oil and gas fields "could generate up to **3 GW**" with binary units.
- Sen. Enzi, quoted in Ormat's 2008 release, put US oilfield potential at **5,000 MW**. Another review gives at least 15,000 MW.
- These are theoretical upper bounds. The 300 MWe NREL number is the realistic "bolt-on to active wells" figure.
- Sources: https://www1.eere.energy.gov/geothermal/pdfs/gtp_coproduction_factsheet.pdf ; https://investor.ormat.com/news-events/news/news-details/2008/Geothermal-Energy-Improves-US-Oil-Recovery/default.aspx

### 1.3 RMOTC / Ormat ORC at NPR-3 (Teapot Dome), Wyoming (2008–09)
- DOE–Ormat CRADA signed January 2007. The 12-month test started September 2008.
- Water at about **190–200°F (88–93°C)** fed an air-cooled isopentane ORC. Output was **150–250 kW gross**, with about 220 kW often quoted.
- RMOTC described it as "for the first time in the world electricity is generated from hot water co-produced in an operating oilfield."
- I found no final 12-month net-performance report (unverified).
- Sources: Ormat press release above; https://www.smu.edu/News/2008/david-blackwell-20oct2008 ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/co-production-geothermal-energy-from-oil-wells/?amp=1

### 1.4 Gosnold, Mann et al., UND, "Electric Power Generation from Co-Produced and Other Oil Field Fluids" (DOE DE-EE0002731/2854; 2013 Peer Review; NDIC final report, 15 April 2017)
This is the best-documented US pilot, and it carries a cautionary tale.

**Resource characterisation (2013 peer review)**
- Cedar Creek / Cedar Hills field, Bowman County, ND: waterflood EOR, **210°F (99°C), 875 gpm**, low-TDS Lodgepole water. Total available water was 1,750 gpm.
- Eland-Lodgepole: 210°F, 400 gpm, high-TDS.
- Corrected-BHT formation averages: Bakken **122°C**, Red River **130°C**, Madison **105°C**.
- Basin-wide extractable power from produced water, cooling to 70°C: Bakken **~10.9 MWe**, Red River **~2.0 MWe**, Madison **~4.0 MWe**. That is about 17 MWe for the whole Williston produced-water stream, which is tiny.

**Key finding**
- The top Madison oil wells give only **73–110 kW in co-production**. The same wells produced for water alone would give **1.6–2.2 MW at moderate rates and 14.6–22 MW at high rates**.
- The authors write: "Power production from co-produced fluids is feasible and would be economic in **unitized fields**". Also: "The petroleum industry has a **low level of interest**"; "the driver ... should be the electrical power industry."

**Final report (2017)**
- Two 125 kW Access Energy/Calnetix ORCs were installed on 98°C water at 51 L/s at Continental's Davis Water Injection Plant.
- The south unit hit **124 kW**, meeting design, but failed shortly after startup. The cooling system was undersized.
- Bakken-boom service costs blew up the site bid to **$285,410**, and Continental dropped a **$500k cost-share** offer.
- Installed cost was **$870k ($3,480/kW)**. CREST modelling gave **7.25¢/kWh** LCOE.
- A later 2020 UND thesis found repurposing Bakken directional wells for ORC "uneconomical", with actual co-produced power "several orders of magnitude less" than early estimates.
- Sources: https://www1.eere.energy.gov/geothermal/pdfs/coproduced_demoprojects_peerreview2013.pdf ; https://ndic.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/Renewable-Energy-Program/Grant-Rounds--Final-Reports/Approved-Projects/Grant-Rounds-29-20/FR-R-21-30.pdf ; https://commons.und.edu/theses/3087

### 1.5 Xin, Liang, Hu & Li (2012), GRC Transactions 36:219–223: 400 kW generator on Huabei oilfield co-produced fluids (China)
- A **400 kW** co-produced-fluid unit near Beijing. I have the citation only; I did not read the full text.
- Yang et al. (2017, *Energy* 140:633–645) built a low-temperature ORC on abandoned Huabei wells. They measured **78.5% turbine efficiency but only 5.33% cycle efficiency**, which illustrates how weak conversion is at low ΔT.
- Sources: https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.202182013 ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/energy/v140y2017ip1p633-645.html

### 1.6 Caulk & Tomac (2017), *Renewable Energy* 112:388–397: reuse of abandoned California wells
- Screened abandoned wells for EGS and deep borehole heat exchangers (BHE).
- Best counties: Santa Clara, Monterey and Santa Barbara, with heat flow of **75–100 mW/m²**.
- A typical 1 km well reaches **40–73°C**, which suits direct use only.
- BHE outlet is above 40°C only where the gradient exceeds 7°C/100 m.
- Fracturing poorly consolidated sediments limits the EGS route.
- Source: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v112y2017icp388-397.html

### 1.7 Nian & Cheng (2018), *Renewable & Sustainable Energy Reviews*: "Insights into geothermal utilization of abandoned oil and gas wells"
- A review of single-well closed-loop (coaxial/U-tube) retrofits.
- The wider literature agrees that closed-loop retrofits yield **tens to a few hundred kWth per well**, suitable for heat, not utility power. I could not open the full text, so this is (unverified as to exact figures).
- Related: Bu, Ma & Li (2012), *Renewable Energy* 41:80–85. They found output depends mainly on flow rate and geothermal gradient.
- Sources: https://faculty.ustc.edu.cn/nianyongle/en/lwcg/59164/content/13525.htm ; https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/renene/v41y2012icp80-85.html

### 1.8 Santos & Dahi Taleghani (2023, SPE; Penn State): challenges for repurposing O&G wells
- Repurposing for community heating can be cost-effective. Even after workover for wellbore integrity, it is cheaper than drilling new wells.
- Low reservoir temperatures usually limit power generation.
- Source: https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/challenges-for-repurposing-oil-and-gas-wells-for-geothermal-appli/

### 1.9 Boutot, Peltz, McVay & Kang (2022), *Environ. Sci. Technol.* 56(20):14228–14236: documented US orphaned wells
- **81,857** documented orphaned wells (Sept 2021) and **123,318** (April 2022). That is only 2–3% of all estimated abandoned US wells.
- About **56%** are in OH, PA, OK and KY (17/15/13/12%). These are mostly shallow, cool Appalachian and Mid-Continent wells.
- Only **49%** have depth data and about 16% a last-production date.
- EPA counts about **2 million unplugged abandoned wells**. EDF puts undocumented orphans at "hundreds of thousands to several million."
- Federal plugging money: about **$4.7B (IIJA)**, of which about $4.2B is state grants. It was paused and revised in 2025, and Phase 3 formula guidance was issued 29 May 2026.
- Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9583604 ; https://www.edf.org/orphanwellmap ; https://www.doi.gov/pressreleases/orphaned-wells-program-news ; https://www.eenews.net/articles/interior-slow-walks-4-7b-biden-era-effort-to-plug-old-oil-wells

### 1.10 CanGEA (2017), Alberta co-production / repurposing database
- Filtered about **600,000 AER wells** (geoSCOUT BHT, log and DST temperatures).
- **60,935 wells have BHT >60°C, 7,702 >90°C, and only ~500 >120°C**, which is the power-grade threshold.
- Sources: https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/repurposing-oil-gas-wells-for-geothermal-development-a-new-database-for-alberta-canada/ ; https://cangea.ca/reportanddashboards.html

### 1.11 Razor Energy / FutEra, Swan Hills, Alberta: Canada's first co-produced geothermal power
- A hybrid of a **21 MW nameplate** gas turbine and an ORC running on hot produced fluids from existing wells.
- Up to **30% of output is claimed as "clean"**. The geothermal ORC share is small; my figure of about 1.3 MW is unverified.
- The gas turbine started 8 Sept 2022 and the ORC on 29 Jan 2023. Final cost was about **C$49M**, with grants of C$7.75M (NRCan), C$10M (ERA) and C$2M (Alberta Innovates).
- Power is sold merchant into the Alberta pool.
- **Razor filed NOI on 30 Jan 2024, converted to CCAA on 28 Feb 2024, and its assets went to Texcal via a reverse vesting order in Dec 2024. Texcal was then put in receivership.**
- In 2023 Razor had given up 70% of FutEra to AIMCo to settle C$64M of debt. Even the flagship co-production plant could not save a marginal oil producer.
- Sources: https://www.energy-pedia.com/news/canada/canada%E2%80%99s-first-co-produced-geothermal-power-project-is-operational-190949 ; https://cfcanada.fticonsulting.com/Razor-Blade/default.htm ; https://globalrestructuringreview.com/article/canadian-court-appoints-receiver-over-failed-oil-producer-following-rvo-last-year

### 1.12 Pleasant Bayou geopressured-geothermal hybrid plant (DOE, Brazoria County TX, 1989–90)
- About a **1 MW hybrid** plant on brine plus dissolved methane from the Frio Fm at roughly 14,700 ft and about **300°F (149°C)**.
- The well produced about **12 million bbl brine and 231 MMcf gas**. Program sites were Pleasant Bayou, Gladys McCall and Willis Hulin.
- It was technically successful but uneconomic at 1990 gas prices.
- Sources: https://www.beg.utexas.edu/files/publications/contract-reports/CR1990-Kreitler-1.pdf ; https://www.osti.gov/biblio/893428 ; https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/html/2007/gcags/abstracts/erdlac02.htm ; https://www1.eere.energy.gov/geothermal/pdfs/20100713_lowtemp_blackwell.pdf

### 1.13 DOE Wells of Opportunity: ReAmplify (Jan 2022), and follow-ons
- Up to **$8.4M** for 4 projects:
  - Transitional Energy (now Gradient Geothermal) at Blackburn field, NV: **$2.5M**, target ≤1 MW.
  - ICE Thermal Harvesting on 11 Elk Hills wells, CA: **$1.7M** plus $472k cost share.
  - Geothermix in TX.
  - University of Oklahoma at Tuttle, OK: school direct use.
- The success bar is about **1 MW for one year**. I found no public measured results (unverified).
- NREL/CP-5700-90827 (2024) on Blackburn: co-production is "viable for oil reservoirs producing large water cuts with elevated wellhead temperatures," and well reuse "significantly reduces initial investment."
- Transitional's 2022 privately funded pilot generated power from a Grant Canyon well.
- Sources: https://www.energy.gov/node/4816509 ; https://jpt.spe.org/doe-program-seeks-to-reuse-abandoned-oil-wells-for-geothermal-production ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/transitional-energy-generates-geothermal-energy-from-oil-and-gas-well/ ; https://nrel.gov/docs/fy24osti/86947.pdf

### 1.14 Chevron / Baseload Capital / Orcan, San Ardo field, California (July 2022)
- A waste-heat-to-power pilot on produced fluids. Chevron joined Baseload's **$25M** round in 2021.
- No performance data has been published.
- Chevron's later geothermal ventures (e.g. Esmeralda County, NV, with Baseload) moved to conventional hydrothermal plays rather than co-production. That is a revealed preference.
- Sources: https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/baseload-and-orcan-energy-commission-oilfield-waste-heat-project/ ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/chevron-and-baseload-capital-to-collaborate-on-u-s-geothermal-projects/

### 1.15 Texas context ("Future of Geothermal in Texas", 2023; Baker Hughes abstract)
- **Hundreds of thousands** of public Texas borehole records. Electricity is "generally feasible where temperatures exceed **120°C within 4 km**."
- West Texas co-produces about 100 bbl water per bbl oil (SMU via trade press, unverified).
- Sources: https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/handle/2152/117189 ; https://www.searchanddiscovery.com/abstracts/pdf/2023/91204meosgeo/abstracts/ndx_mccarthy02.pdf

## 2. Synthesis

1. **Co-produced power from active wells is a niche.** The realistic US near-term figure is about 300 MWe (NREL 2014), and the Williston Basin's whole produced-water stream is about 17 MWe. Pilots are 0.1–0.4 MW (RMOTC, UND, Huabei) or about 1 MW (Pleasant Bayou, WoO targets). Fluid temperatures of 90–130°C give ORC efficiencies of about 5–14%.
2. **Water-only conversion of watered-out or unitized fields changes the numbers.** UND found the same wells give 10–200x more power when produced for water instead of oil, and Pleasant Bayou-type geopressured brines reach about 150°C. The value sits in **picking the right field**, not in bolting an ORC onto every pumpjack.
3. **Economics.** Avoiding drilling saves the 30–60% of geothermal capex that goes to wells (generic industry figure, unverified here). Small ORCs still cost about **$3,500/kW installed** for about **7¢/kWh** (UND 2017). Pilots died from service-cost inflation, cooling shortfalls and oil-operator indifference.
4. **Orphan wells are mostly the wrong wells.** About 123k documented, 56% of them in shallow, cool Appalachian and Mid-Continent basins, and half with no depth data. Hot, deep candidates are a small subset: Alberta has only about 500 of 600k wells above 120°C. The subset is findable from public logs, though.
5. **The data substrate is exactly Terranox-like.** Millions of public BHTs, DSTs, water-cut and production records sit in state commissions and AER files, uncorrected and heterogeneous. BHT correction is a known ML and physics problem (Horner/Harrison corrections), and only about 49% of orphan records have even depth.
6. **Incumbents have shown little interest.** UND says oil operators show low interest, Chevron pivoted to hydrothermal, and Razor went insolvent. Nobody is systematically aggregating the hot, watered-out, cheaply acquirable fields.

## 3. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company, and the gap vs Zanskar

**What maps well**
- The Terranox playbook fits: public-but-illegible archives (state O&G commission well files, AER geoSCOUT, BHT/DST logs, water-cut histories) plus ML to rank fields, then **acquire cheaply before proof**.
- Here the acquirable asset is distressed, watered-out or end-of-life oil fields and their wellbores, unitized waterfloods, and the geothermal/brine rights under or beside them. That includes BLM geothermal leases overlapping federal O&G units and private geothermal rights.
- Distressed sellers really exist. Razor went from CCAA to RVO to receivership, the Bakken has marginal operators, and plugging liabilities are pushing operators out.
- The "proven state" step-up is real: a reservoir with decades of production history, known permeability and existing injection infrastructure is a de-risked geothermal reservoir, not a wildcat.
- Exit buyers: ORC developers (Ormat, Baseload, Gradient), utilities and co-ops (the UND project drew three co-ops), and data centres wanting baseload in Texas, Oklahoma, Wyoming and Alberta.
- Optionality: lithium in brines and avoided plugging liabilities (avoided P&A in the US is about $100k+ per well, unverified).

**What does not map, and the "too small" risk**
- Bolt-on co-production at active wells is too small: about 300 MWe nationally and 0.1–0.4 MW per site. Run as a service to oil operators, it is a low-scoring tool shape.
- The orphan-well count (123k) is a headline, not a resource. Most are shallow and cool, so they suit direct use at best.
- The idea survives only if it targets **hot (>120°C), high-permeability sedimentary reservoirs where the wells are already drilled and the field can be bought**, such as the Gulf Coast geopressured Frio/Wilcox, the deep Williston Red River/Madison, Alberta Swan Hills/Leduc, Nevada Basin-and-Range oil fields (Blackburn, Grant Canyon), and the San Joaquin Elk Hills. That links to the sedimentary-geothermal and EGS-in-sediments thesis (Fervo's Cape Station is in a sedimentary basin with O&G-style drilling).

**Gap vs Zanskar**
- Zanskar's focus is **blind hydrothermal systems in the Great Basin**, found with physics-plus-ML on geophysics, heat flow and temperature-gradient holes. It drills new wells and owns plants (Lightning Dock, NM; Big Blind, NV).
- Zanskar does not appear to work on **oil-and-gas-derived datasets or acquire existing oilfield wellbores and fields** (unverified; no public evidence found).
- The open niche is a "petroleum-data geothermal roll-up":
  1. Ingest about 2.5M+ US and 600k Alberta well records.
  2. ML-correct BHTs and identify hot, high-water-cut, end-of-life fields.
  3. Buy fields or wellbores, plus geothermal and brine rights, from distressed operators. Plugging liability can become a negotiating asset.
  4. Prove flow and temperature with cheap workovers instead of $5–10M exploration wells.
  5. Sell or develop at a step-up.
- This is complementary to Zanskar geographically (sedimentary basins vs Great Basin hydrothermal) and in its data (petroleum archives vs geophysics).
- Key risks:
  - Each site has limited MW unless reservoirs are produced for water-only at high rates.
  - Legal ownership of geothermal rights in O&G states is unclear. Texas legislated in 2023 that geothermal belongs to the surface estate (unverified), and Alberta has the 2021 Geothermal Resource Development Act tenure (unverified).
  - The pilot track record is poor (UND unit failure, Razor insolvency, no public WoO results).
- Plausibility under the standing rules: **medium**. The data advantage is highly Terranox-like, and asset ownership is feasible through distressed-field acquisition. Score it on the hot-sedimentary-field roll-up, not on generic co-production or orphan-well repurposing, which would fail the "too small" test.
