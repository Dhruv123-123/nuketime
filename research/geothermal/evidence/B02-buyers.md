# B02: Who buys proven geothermal resources and projects in the US, and how much they want (2024-2026)

*Research note, 2026-10-21. Method: the shared WebSearch budget for this turn was already used up when this agent started, so no new web searches ran. The new facts below come from direct WebFetch of primary documents: Ormat SEC 8-K exhibits (Feb 2026, Mar 2026, Aug 2026), Ormat's FY2025 10-K, Fervo's 424B4 IPO prospectus (May 2026), Meta's newsroom, and Zanskar's press releases. Other facts are reused from sibling evidence files (Z05, Z08, Z09, L23, R04, R10) and are cited by the primary URL those files used. Anything I could not trace to a URL is marked (unverified). Fetches of Constellation's Calpine closing release, Google's CTT blog and Business Wire (XGS-Meta) failed with 404 or 403.*

## 1. Bottom line

The US buyer pool for geothermal has changed from one thin strategic buyer (Ormat) to a layered, cash-rich stack:

- **Strategic owner-operators** buy *operating* assets at roughly $1.8-6M/MW:
  - Ormat;
  - Cyrq, Calpine/Constellation and Open Mountain Energy, mostly as sellers.
- **Next-generation IPPs** that now hold large public or venture balance sheets:
  - Fervo, with about $1.9-2.2B of IPO proceeds;
  - Sage, XGS, Quaise and Mazama.
- **Utilities buying through PPAs and build-then-transfer structures:**
  - NV Energy, which signed 365 MW across three geothermal deals in 2024-26;
  - PNM, the CCAs and CPUC/DWR.
- **Hyperscalers paying a firm-clean premium through utility tariffs:**
  - Google: 115 MW (Fervo), 150 MW (Ormat), and a 3 GW framework with Fervo;
  - Meta: 2 × 150 MW, with Sage and XGS.

PPA prices have moved from **$60-80/MWh to above $100/MWh** (Ormat 10-K).

**The gap.** No public, arm's-length market for *proven-but-unbuilt* geothermal resources was found. Buyers pay for MW that are built and contracted, or they buy land at auction. The middle of the chain, a confirmed resource with no plant, has no published comps. That gap is the opening for a Terranox-shape company.

## 2. Buyer by buyer

### 2.1 Ormat Technologies (NYSE: ORA): incumbent strategic buyer

**Size**
- Total portfolio is 1,850 MW: 1,355 MW of geothermal and solar plus 495 MW of storage.
- 202 MW of generation is under construction or in development, "all backed by long-term PPAs."
- Q2 2026 revenue was $258.8M and adjusted EBITDA $143.9M. FY2026 EBITDA guidance is $630-650M.
- Source: Q2 2026 8-K Ex. 99.1, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774926025961/ex_999557.htm

**Stated growth target**
- Ormat expects to "add 310MW to 410MW of generating capacity in the Electricity Segment," reaching about **1.65-1.75 GW of Electricity capacity by 2028**.
- It also targets 950-1,050 MW / 2,500-2,900 MWh of storage by year-end 2028.
- Source: FY2025 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000129644526000006/ora-20251231.htm

**Pricing**
- Recent US geothermal PPAs are priced "at prices above $100 per MWh." PPAs from the prior five years were generally $60-80/MWh.
- The 10-K cites hyperscaler demand as a driver of higher prices.
- Ormat says it will evaluate in-front-of-the-meter deals with hyperscalers and data-centre developers (FY2025 10-K).

**Acquisition appetite.** The 10-K says Ormat intends to grow through "strategic global acquisitions, including geothermal assets." Recent geothermal purchases:

| Deal | Date | Price | $/MW | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Terra-Gen TG Geothermal (Dixie Valley + Beowawe, ~76 MW) | 2021 | $171M cash + ~$258M assumed, ~$429M EV | ~$5.6M/MW | FY2021 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774922004440/ora20211231_10k.htm |
| Enel Green Power NA portfolio (~150 MW incl. Cove Fort, Stillwater, Salt Wells and solar, plus 2 greenfield assets) | announced Oct 2023, closed 2024 | $271M announced; $274.6M per 10-K | ~$1.8M/MW; ~11.3x EBITDA | https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774923028865/ex_585361.htm |
| Blue Mountain, NV (20 MW, from Cyrq) | Jun 2025 | $88.7M | ~$4.4M/MW | FY2025 10-K (via Z08) |

**Offtake for hyperscalers**
- On 17 Feb 2026, Ormat signed a portfolio PPA with NV Energy for **up to 150 MW of *new* geothermal** to serve Google, under NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff (CTT).
- Projects reach COD between 2028 and 2030. The term runs 15 years past the final project's COD.
- PUCN approval was expected in H2 2026. Price is undisclosed.
- Ormat's CEO tied the deal to "recent exploration and drilling work."
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774926004369/ex_921781.htm
- **Ormat has to find roughly 150 MW of new Nevada resource within about 2-4 years.** That makes it the most obvious near-term buyer of confirmed resources.

**Other signals**
- Ormat put $25M into Sage Geosystems' $97M Series B (Jan 2026) and received development rights. https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/sage-geosystems-closes-97-million-series-b-funding-round-to-support-pressure-geothermal-deployment/
- It runs an EGS program with SLB at Desert Peak, with drilling planned for Q4 2026.
- It is "pursuing additional land, water rights, and interconnections for EGS development" (Q2 2026 8-K).
- It raised **$750M base (up to $862.5M) of 2031 convertible notes** in Mar 2026. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000121390026032467/ea028270301ex99-1.htm
- Its 10-K notes that a party that outbids Ormat for a lease "may still become a customer of Ormat's Product Segment." Ormat therefore sells equipment to third-party resource holders (Z08).
- It is a heavy auction bidder: about $3.3M of NV/UT bonus bids in 2022-25, at about $133/acre on average in NV 2024 (Z08, from BLM all-bids PDFs).

### 2.2 Fervo Energy (NASDAQ: FRVO): next-generation IPP with a public currency

**Capital and contracts**
- IPO in May 2026 at $27/share, with about $1.77B of net proceeds (424B4). Other reports give about $1.9B gross and about $2.2B with the greenshoe, at a roughly $7.5B valuation (Z09/ThinkGeoEnergy).
- The $462M Series E (Dec 2025) included Google's first equity in Fervo. https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/google-joins-usd-462m-funding-round-for-fervo-energy/
- **658 MW of binding PPAs**, about **$7.2B of revenue backlog**. Counterparties are SCE, Shell, Clean Power Alliance, Desert Community Energy, Google/NV Energy and an unnamed hyperscaler.
- **3 GW Google Geothermal Framework Agreement**, dated 19 Mar 2026. It "does not obligate Google to purchase power."
- Rystad pricing for EGS-type clean firm power is **$100-130/MWh**.
- First-of-a-kind cost is about $7,000/kW, against an NOAK target of $3,000/kW.
- Source: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm

**Land**
- About 595,900 acres, assembled at about **$4/acre in 2019-21**.
- It contrasts that with 2025 BLM maximum bids of **$344/acre (UT) and $410/acre (NV)**.
- It warns that others "paid hundreds of dollars per acre in 2025" for positions that fail its standards (424B4).
- About 256k acres are idle "Prospects."
- Fervo is therefore a buyer of *screened* quality land and a possible farm-out source for land it will not develop.
- **Royalty precedent:** Centaurus provided $75M of project preferred equity in Cape Phase I and keeps a **$5/MWh royalty** after its priority return (424B4, via R04).

### 2.3 Utilities

**NV Energy (Berkshire Hathaway Energy)** is the single most active buyer.
- **115 MW Fervo "Corsac Station" for Google** under the CTT, the first CTT project.
  - The CTT lets customers of 5 MW or more fund dedicated clean resources at their own premium.
  - Source: R10/L23, LBNL *Speed to Power* and Latitude Media.
- **Up to 150 MW from Ormat for Google** (Feb 2026), also under the CTT.
- **100 MW, 20-year PPA with Zanskar** (May 2026), delivering by 2030.
  - Zanskar will build and operate the projects, then **sell them as a portfolio to NV Energy**.
  - The deal is contingent on PUCN approval of the 2026 IRP.
  - The end customer is not named in the release. A sibling file's claim that Amazon is behind it is (unverified).
  - Source: https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-inks-100-mw-power-purchase
- **Total: about 365 MW of new geothermal committed by one utility in about 2 years**, about 265 MW of it fronting for Google.
- The Zanskar deal shows that a utility will **take ownership of built projects** (build-transfer), not only buy power.

**PNM (New Mexico)**
- Buys from Zanskar's Lightning Dock (about 15 MW).
- The Meta-XGS 150 MW deal is in New Mexico (L23, from the NLR 2025 report). Utility structure is (unverified).

**PacifiCorp**
- No 2024-26 geothermal procurement was found in the sources read (unverified).
- R10 notes it moved to cluster interconnection studies early.
- Utah's Cape Station sells to California load (SCE and the CCAs) rather than to PacifiCorp (424B4 counterparties).

**California** is the largest structural buyer.
- The CPUC 2021 mid-term reliability order required ≥1 GW of firm clean power by 2026, which has produced **≥616 MW of geothermal PPAs**.
- In Aug 2024 the CPUC directed DWR to procure centrally about **1 GW more long-lead geothermal for 2031-37**.
- The CCAs (CPA, DCE, and CC Power with its 9 members) are active buyers.
- CC Power signed a GEODE prospect-for-offtake framework with Zanskar in Apr 2026.
- Source: NLR/DOE GTO market report, https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3014949 ; https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-and-cc-power-sign-geode-agreement

**Calpine (The Geysers, about 725 MW (unverified))**
- Calpine sells power rather than buying resource: SMUD at **$99/MWh** for 100 MW over 10 years (2022), and Port of Oakland at $70/MWh (NLR report).
- Constellation's acquisition of Calpine was reported closing in early 2026 (unverified; the closing release returned 404).
- Calpine has not appeared in any BLM auction from 2022 to 2025 (Z08).
- Treat it as a deep-pocketed potential buyer of The Geysers and Northern California adjacencies, not an active acquirer.

**Read**
- Google is the anchor buyer: about 265 MW contracted, a 3 GW framework, and equity in Fervo.
- Meta has bought 300 MW from next-generation technologies outside the classic Great Basin hydrothermal fairway.
- Hyperscalers buy **power plus the developer relationship**. None has been disclosed buying a resource or lease directly. Meta's newsroom notes more than 12 GW of renewables contracted, but sets no numeric geothermal target.
- The NLR market report counts **26 geothermal PPAs totalling 1,642 MWe** from 2021 to June 2025, with **60% from next-generation developers** (984 MWe) (https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3014949).

### 2.5 Infrastructure and financial capital

- **Centaurus Capital:** $75M of project preferred equity in Fervo Cape Phase I, with a $5/MWh royalty.
- **Project Granite:** a $421.4M, nine-lender construction and term facility for Fervo (424B4).
- **Mercuria:** a $100M term loan plus an $80M LC facility for Fervo (424B4).
- **Zanskar:** a $40M non-recourse development capital facility, designed to upsize to $100M, from Just Climate and Spring Lane (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-closes-40m-in-novel-development).
- **Monarch Private Capital:** tax equity for Open Mountain Energy (Z08).
- **Invenergy**, a utility-scale IPP backed by infrastructure investors (unverified), entered geothermal auctions in 2024. It won about $2.8M of NV/UT bonus bids from 2022 to 2025 (Z08).
- **Specialist land-bank LLCs** (Buffalo River Minerals, Percheron, FLHN) took about **52% of 2022-25 NV/UT auction dollars**, paying up to about $408/acre (Z08). Their principals are unidentified. Their existence shows that financial buyers will pay for pre-proof rights in hot, obvious fairways.
- **Federal tax credits:** 45Y/48E remain available to geothermal for construction starting through 2033. Geothermal survived the OBBBA phase-outs (NLR 2025; Ormat 8-K Feb 2026).
- No infrastructure fund (Brookfield, KKR, BlackRock/GIP and similar) was found buying US geothermal *development* resources in 2024-26 (unverified absence).

### 2.6 Sellers and how deep the market is

- **Cyrq** is a net seller: Lightning Dock to Zanskar (2024, price undisclosed) and Blue Mountain to Ormat (2025, $88.7M).
- **Terra-Gen** exited geothermal to Ormat (2021).
- **Enel** sold its US geothermal to Ormat (2023-24).
- Pattern: operating plants change hands at **$1.8-5.6M/MW**, and **Ormat is the buyer in most disclosed deals**.
- The buy side for operating assets is concentrated in Ormat, Zanskar and Open Mountain Energy.

## 3. The value ladder from the buyer's side

| Stage | Who pays | Price evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Raw lease, unscreened | land banks, Ormat, Invenergy, at BLM auction | $2-4/acre floor; $100-410/acre for hot, obvious ground (BLM 2024-25) |
| Lease beside a public discovery | fast followers | $232/acre at the Pumpernickel step-out, one month after Zanskar's PR (Z04) |
| Confirmed resource, no PPA | **no public comps** | about $0.1-0.5M/MW-equivalent implied (R04, unverified) |
| Contracted, under construction | public markets | Fervo at about $11.4M per binding PPA MW (Z09 arithmetic) |
| Operating plant | Ormat | $1.8-5.6M/MW |
| Contracted power | utilities and hyperscalers | above $100/MWh new; $100-130/MWh for EGS; about $10M/MW PV at $100/MWh over 20 years (L23 arithmetic) |

## 4. Appetite in GW: demand versus supply

Disclosed demand, 2024-26:
- Google's 3 GW framework with Fervo.
- About 1 GW of CPUC/DWR central procurement for 2031-37.
- Ormat's growth of 310-410 MW of Electricity capacity by 2028 (part of it solar).
- NV Energy's 365 MW.
- Meta's 300 MW.
- Taken together, US buyers are asking for **about 4-5 GW of new geothermal by the mid-2030s**.

Supply:
- US installed geothermal is about 3.7-3.9 GW (unverified this session).
- The NLR count of 1.64 GW of PPAs signed in 2021-25 is the actual signing pace, about 0.4 GW/yr.
- Demand is running ahead of *confirmed resource*. Ormat has to fill 150 MW of new Nevada supply for Google. Fervo says land is scarce and costly.
- The binding constraint buyers name is **sites plus interconnection** ("Interconnection is the new PPA," Fervo; "Interconnection is our bottleneck," Zanskar).

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Buyers exist and are cash-rich, but they buy two things: land and MW.**
   - Ormat (about $630-650M EBITDA and $750M+ of fresh converts), Fervo (about $1.8-2.2B of IPO cash and a 3 GW Google framework), NV Energy (a build-transfer buyer) and Google/Meta (premium tariffs) all need *new confirmed resource* to fill contracts already signed.
   - Ormat's 150 MW Google portfolio, due 2028-30, is the sharpest near-term need.
   - The step from land to MW, a confirmed resource with an interconnection path, has **no public price**. A Terranox-shape rights owner would sell exactly that and set the first comps.

2. **The step-up is large on any reasonable assumption.**
   - Entry costs $2-20/acre plus temperature-gradient or slim-hole confirmation. Ormat's own figure is $1-5M per site for exploration before a full-size well.
   - Exit is either a resource sale (implied $0.1-0.5M/MW, unverified), a $/MWh royalty (the Centaurus $5/MWh is worth about $0.4M/MW PV), or a build-transfer to NV Energy-type utilities.
   - Ormat pays $4.4-5.6M/MW for operating plants, so the headroom above a confirmed-resource sale is wide.

3. **Sell to everyone, which Zanskar cannot do.**
   - Zanskar is a developer-owner: it has Lightning Dock, a 100 MW NV Energy build-transfer and $180M of equity.
   - It competes with Ormat at auctions, and with Fervo, Sage and XGS for hyperscaler offtake, so it cannot be their neutral supplier.
   - Its only originate-for-customer deal (GEODE with CC Power) still routes development through Zanskar.
   - A rights bank can sell to Ormat, Fervo, Sage, XGS, Quaise, Mazama, Invenergy, NV Energy, PNM, the CCAs, *and* Zanskar.

4. **Follow the hyperscaler geography, which is outside Zanskar's footprint.**
   - Meta's 300 MW is with Sage (east of the Rockies) and XGS (New Mexico). Next-generation technologies have 60% of new PPAs and are geology-flexible.
   - They want hot, shallow sedimentary heat near load and interconnection: TX, NM, CO, OK/KS and the Gulf Coast.
   - That heat is mapped by **oil-and-gas bottom-hole temperatures and well logs**, which is the direct analogue of the Saskatchewan assessment files.
   - Zanskar's disclosed land is 100% Nevada BLM plus one New Mexico brownfield, and its method is tuned to blind hydrothermal systems.

5. **Sell sites and power packages to hyperscalers through utility tariffs.**
   - The NV Energy CTT is a direct pipe from a large-load customer to a specific resource.
   - A rights owner that holds a resource, water rights and a queue position in a single-purpose SPV can sell a "Google-ready" package to a developer or co-develop it with the utility.
   - Interconnection positions transfer with the site (R10).

6. **Risks**
   - Buyers may keep self-exploring: Fervo holds 596k acres, and Ormat holds 434k acres and 34 US prospects.
   - The confirmed-but-unbuilt market may stay a private farm-in market at low prices (unverified).
   - Land-bank LLCs move fast once anything is disclosed.
   - Mitigations:
     - sign one or two anchor buyers, with Ormat's Google portfolio as the obvious first target;
     - keep royalties and carries rather than selling clean;
     - work off-auction, through private, state, split-estate and oilfield land.

7. **Scoring view.** This is an asset-owning shape that sells into a demand pool of about 4-5 GW. It is not too small: 200 MW-equivalent sold at even $0.25-0.5M/MW, or held as $5/MWh royalties, is worth about $50-100M on a land budget of a few million dollars. The gap against Zanskar is *where in the chain it sells* (mid-chain, to every developer) and *which data and land it works* (oilfield BHT and sedimentary basins, plus private and state tenure). The gap is not "AI finds blind hydrothermal systems," which Zanskar already does.
