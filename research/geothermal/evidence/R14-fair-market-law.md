# R14: Does BLM reprice geothermal rights to market? Fees, re-tender, 2024–2026 rule changes, and cheap leases that turned valuable

**Method.** The shared WebSearch budget for this run was used up before my first query, so I did no web searches. I fetched primary documents directly:

- 43 CFR 3211.11 (Cornell LII);
- 30 U.S.C. §1003;
- the enrolled text of P.L. 119-21 (govinfo; I searched the full text for "geothermal");
- GAO-26-108040 (Sept 2026; full PDF, text extracted);
- BLM IM 2026-004;
- EO 14156 and EO 14154 (whitehouse.gov).

Sale-level numbers come from sibling files R01, R02, Z04, L16 and L24. Each of those parsed BLM sale PDFs, BLM Public Land Statistics and SEC filings, and the URLs are listed below. Anything I could not open is marked **(unverified)**.

---

## 1. Bottom line

- **The "public bodies reprice to market" law holds for geothermal only at the auction moment. After that it does not apply.** BLM uses competitive bonus bidding to find the price of *contested* parcels. It is now finding very high prices there: $412/ac in Idaho in 2025, then $701/ac in New Mexico and $1,207/ac in Utah in 2026.
- **Every other price term is a fixed statutory or regulatory number** that BLM has not changed since 2005: the $2/ac minimum bid, $1–3/ac rent, and the 1.75% royalty, rising to 3.5%. Uncontested land therefore still clears at the floor (37% of Nevada parcels in 2025), or goes noncompetitively at $1/ac with no bonus.
- **Once a lease is issued, BLM never re-tenders or re-bases it.** It does not re-appraise and does not escalate rent with market value. Any step-up in value belongs to the lessee for up to 10 + 5 + 5 years, then for a production term of up to 35 years plus a renewal of up to 55 years. Land comes back to auction only if a lease **lapses or is relinquished**.
- **Policy in 2025–26 increased supply and speed, not price.** It made geothermal part of the "national energy emergency", required annual sales, and banned deferrals. Congress *did* move wind and solar right-of-way rent to market-indexed formulas in the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA). It left the Geothermal Steam Act untouched.

## 2. Is the fee schedule below market? Yes, in every fixed component

| Term | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Rent, noncompetitive lease | **$1/ac/yr** for years 1–10; $5/ac after that | 43 CFR 3211.11 |
| Rent, competitive lease | **$2/ac** in year 1, **$3/ac** in years 2–10, $5/ac after that | 43 CFR 3211.11 |
| Minimum bonus bid | **$2/ac** | BLM-NM sale notice, 2026 |
| Noncompetitive application fee | $505 (FY2024); about $520 in FY2026 | 43 CFR 3000.12 via R02 |
| Royalty, lessee generates electricity | **1.75% of gross proceeds** for the first 10 years of production, **3.5%** after that | 43 CFR 3211.17 via R02 |
| Royalty, arm's-length fluid sale | 10% | 43 CFR 3211.17 via R02 |
| Minimum royalty (post-2005 leases) | None | 43 CFR 3211.21 via R02 |
| Acreage cap | 51,200 ac per state | 43 CFR 3206.12–14 via R02 |

Points to note:

- **There is no appraisal or fair-market-value test anywhere in the noncompetitive route.** The statute only says unbid tracts "shall [be made] available for a period of 2 years for noncompetitive leasing" (30 U.S.C. §1003(c)). GAO describes this as "available noncompetitively for a fee for 2 years… on a first-come, first-served basis."
- **The rent dollars are not indexed.** 3211.11 states fixed amounts. Only the diligence-work amounts ($40/ac by year 10, then $15–25/ac/yr) are indexed every 3 years to the GDP deflator (3207.12(i), via R02). In real terms the $1 and $3 rents have lost roughly 40% of their value since 2005 (calc from CPI, unverified).
- **Contrast with wind and solar.** OBBBA §50302 (codified at 43 U.S.C. 3007) sets wind and solar right-of-way rent as follows:
  - rent = the 5-year average of the NASS **pastureland cash rent** for the state × an encumbrance factor (100% for solar, at least 10% for wind) × 1.03^year;
  - plus a capacity fee once generation starts.

  Congress showed in 2025 that it knows how to tie federal land rents to market, and it chose to do so for wind and solar only. "Renewable energy project" is defined as "wind or solar" (§50302(a)(7)). A full-text search of P.L. 119-21 finds the word "geothermal" only in tax provisions (heat pumps, and the tax-credit phase-down that spares nuclear, hydro and geothermal). There is **no amendment to the Geothermal Steam Act.**
- **What "market" is worth.** On contested ground the market now pays 100–600× the floor in bonus, which shows how far the statutory floor sits below market:
  - NV 2025 maximum: $410/ac. ID Sept 2025 maximum: $412/ac.
  - The NM Jun 2026 sale averaged about $109/ac and took in $16.6M.
  - UT 2026 maximum: $1,207/ac.
  - Fervo's IPO implies about $12.6k per leased acre (Z09 calc).

## 3. Does BLM reprice through auctions? Yes, and increasingly so, but the market is bimodal

- **Revenue.** GAO counts 19 competitive sales from 2020 to 2025 that raised $39.4M, with **$24M from six sales in 2025**. Two 2026 sales (NM and UT) raised **more than $41M, more than all of 2020–25 combined**. Active leases rose from 599 to 749 between March 2025 and March 2026, up 25%.
- **Nominations.** Nominated acreage went from about 25,800 ac (2020) to **more than 1M ac (2025)**. Of the 1.245M ac offered from 2019 to 2025, about 87% was leased.
- **Nevada receipts.** $148k (2020) → $1.6M (2021) → $3.4M (2022) → $1.0M (2023) → $7.9M (2024) → **$9.45M (2025)** → $4.8M (2026). (R01, from BLM PDFs.)
- **The floor still matters.** In NV 2025 the median winning bid was **$3.50/ac**, 37% of parcels cleared at the $2 floor, and 27 parcels (97,120 ac) got no bid at all. Only **one** noncompetitive day-after filing followed, covering 4,890 ac. As of 10 Sep 2026 about **53,686 ac were still open over the counter at $1/ac** until about Oct 2027 (R02).

**Verdict.** The auction is a working repricing mechanism, but only for parcels the crowd already recognises. Blind ground is still priced at the statutory floor.

## 4. Does BLM ever re-tender?

- **Unbid parcels.** These are open noncompetitively for 2 years. After that the land must be **re-nominated** and go back through a competitive sale (30 U.S.C. §1003(a)–(c)). That is the only "re-tender".
- **Issued leases.** BLM never re-tenders, re-auctions or re-bases them:
  - Rent and royalty are fixed for the life of the lease.
  - BLM can approve an assignment only after checking good standing, the buyer's qualification and the bond. It cannot reprice the lease on transfer (3216.22, via R02).
  - Royalty does not rise when a resource is proven.
- **The only recycling route is lapse.** A lease ends at year 10 unless it is held by production or extended with about $40/ac of qualifying work or a payment in lieu (3207.10–.16, via R02).
- **The boom-era cohort shows the scale of lapse.** Leases in effect went from 785 (FY2010) to 568 (FY2024), so hundreds of 2007–10 leases lapsed and their land can be renominated (L24, from BLM Public Land Statistics). This is the geothermal equivalent of Saskatchewan's lapsed-claim pipeline. However, the land comes back through **auction**, not first-come staking.
- **No market re-basing at renewal.** I found no regulatory or statutory mechanism that resets rent to market at a production extension or renewal. Renewal terms are set by regulation, not by appraisal (3207.15–16; unverified in detail).

## 5. Rule changes, 2024–2026

| Date | Action | Effect on price and supply |
|---|---|---|
| 20 Jan 2025 | **EO 14156, Declaring a National Energy Emergency.** Sec. 8(a) defines energy resources to include "**geothermal heat**". Sec. 2(a) directs emergency authorities to "facilitate the identification, **leasing**, siting, production… including… on Federal lands". Secs. 4–5 direct use of emergency Army Corps permits and emergency Endangered Species Act consultation. | Puts geothermal inside the emergency-permitting perimeter. Does not affect price. |
| 20 Jan 2025 | **EO 14154, Unleashing American Energy.** Promotes federal land production. Its "particular attention" list omits geothermal. | Indirect only. |
| 2025 | BLM–Forest Service MOU WO 300-2025-05 on leasing and permitting | Addresses Forest Service consent delays: GAO finds an average of 1,098 days to sale on Forest Service and Reclamation land, against 455 on BLM land. |
| 4 Jul 2025 | **OBBBA (P.L. 119-21).** Restores noncompetitive and quarterly sales for *oil and gas* (§50101). Market-indexes *wind and solar* rights-of-way (§50302). **Makes no geothermal leasing change.** | Geothermal stays at fixed 2005 terms. |
| 16 Dec 2025 | **BLM IM 2026-004, Promoting Annual Competitive Geothermal Lease Sales.** Requires **annual** sales, up from the statutory "at least once every two years". At least 90% of timely nominated BLM parcels must be offered in the next annual sale, and at least 80% of parcels on other agencies' land within 2 years. **No deferral** for pending land-use plan amendments unless required by law. Cites EO 14154, EO 14156, SO 3417 and SO 3418. | **Increases supply and cadence.** Utah offered about 73,000 previously deferred acres in Aug 2026 as a result. |
| Feb 2026 | New Interior NEPA procedures (516 DM 1; amended 43 CFR Part 46), per GAO | Faster review. Details unverified. |
| Sep 2026 | **GAO-26-108040.** Recommends BLM finalise its geothermal handbooks, which have been in draft since at least 2022, track permit status, and set permit-time goals. Interior did not comment. | Confirms the bottleneck is staffing and permitting, not price. One Nevada office is "60 percent vacant" with "one of six geologist positions" filled. Nevada nomination review slipped from about 11 to about 15 months. |

**Pending bills (unverified).** The search budget ran out, so I could not confirm the bill numbers or status of 119th-Congress geothermal bills. Recalled names include a "GEO Act", the HEATS Act, and bills to expand categorical exclusions, mandate annual or quarterly geothermal sales, or extend noncompetitive leasing to land next to existing leases. Cornell's annotation of 30 U.S.C. §1003 lists only the 2005 and 2020 amendments, which suggests none had been enacted. **No evidence found of any proposal to raise geothermal rents or royalties to market.** Every visible federal action from 2025 to 2026 pushes toward more and faster leasing at the same prices.

## 6. Cheap leases that were later proven valuable (the analogue of free-entry staking)

1. **Zanskar's Pumpernickel block (Humboldt County, NV).**
   - The core parcel NV-2022-08-1859 was won at **$41/ac** in 2022.
   - Five step-out parcels covering about 12,900 ac were won at **$2/ac** in Nov 2023, each with only Zanskar bidding.
   - Zanskar announced the Pumpernickel discovery in Sep 2025. A month later, adjacent parcel NV-2025-10-2143 cleared at **$232/ac** (Lithos48 at $232 against Zanskar at $231). That is a **roughly 116× step-up before any MW was sold** (Z04, from BLM All-Bids PDFs).
2. **Big Blind (NV).**
   - Zanskar says it found the anomaly in late 2023 and secured "BLM leases at auction".
   - The likely parcels were bought at **$2–4/ac** (by inference).
   - The discovery well found about 250°F at about 2,700 ft, and the site now sits inside a **100 MW NV Energy PPA portfolio** (Z04).
3. **Blue Mountain (NV).**
   - Gold exploration holes in the early 1990s hit hot water. The mining company took **noncompetitive** BLM leases and **sold the leases plus data** to a geothermal developer (L16, citing NREL/Levine et al. 2022).
   - The 49.5 MW plant followed.
   - **Ormat bought the plant for $88.7M in Jun 2025** (Ormat 10-Q, via R04).
4. **Don A. Campbell, NV (42 MW).** Mining-hole temperatures, with no surface expression. Ormat nominated the land in 2001 and won it at auction in 2007 (L16).
5. **McGinness Hills, NV (about 140 MW).** Legacy mining holes found 190°F water in 2004. Ormat won the leases at auction in Aug 2007 (L16).

**Base-rate caution.** Only 88 of roughly 610 federal leases (about 14%) produce. At the 2010 peak, about 92% of leases and about 96% of leased acres produced nothing. Raser held 250,000+ acres and its equity was wiped out in 2011 (L24, R04). Cheap rights are an option, not an asset, until a resource is proven.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The legal object is the right shape.** A BLM geothermal lease has:
   - a fixed price;
   - no fair-market re-appraisal, no re-tender, and no royalty step-up on proof;
   - ministerial transfer;
   - royalty of only 1.75–3.5% of electricity revenue.

   The step-up belongs to the holder. This is *better* than many mining tenures that re-tender. The "public bodies reprice" risk applies **only at entry**. Policy since 2025 is pushing entry prices down in practice, through more supply and annual sales, not up.
2. **The edge is the information gap at the auction, not cheap paper.** Contested land already reprices: 22–36 registered bidders, $400–1,200/ac tops, and landman LLCs (Buffalo River, Percheron, Lithos48) land-banking. The arbitrage survives only on parcels the crowd does not recognise: 37% at the $2 floor, about 92k unbid acres, about 54k acres still available at $1/ac.

   The ML job is to score **every** offered and unbid parcel. That includes rivals' nominations, which reveals nothing of your own targets. File the $1/ac leftovers, and bid with conviction on the few blind targets before disclosure.
3. **Lapse is the lapsed-claim pipeline.** Hundreds of boom-era leases have lapsed (785 → 568). Many sit over drilled and logged ground. Re-nominating them after re-reading their old data is the closest analogue to Terranox's Saskatchewan workflow. Unlike Saskatchewan, it is gated by auction.
4. **Disclosure discipline is critical.** Zanskar's $2 → $232 Pumpernickel episode shows that a public discovery reprices neighbouring land within weeks, and that the discoverer can lose it by $1/ac. A Terranox-shape company should lock up the whole fairway, using federal, state-trust and private fee rights together, *before* any well result or press release.
5. **Gap vs Zanskar.**
   - Zanskar's auction position is about 50k acres, bought for about $764k, all in Nevada.
   - Its acreage additions fell to 872 ac in 2025, and it is now a plant developer: Lightning Dock, a 100 MW PPA, $180M of equity and a project-finance facility.
   - It does not appear in Utah, Idaho, New Mexico, California or Oregon awards, or in visible noncompetitive filings.
   - It cannot easily be a neutral seller of proven land to Ormat, Invenergy or Fervo.

   **The open lane is a multi-state, ML-selected, asset-owning lease bank** that proves leases cheaply and sells or farms them out at the step-up. It would use temperature-gradient holes and geophysics that also count toward the $40/ac diligence requirement, and would keep overriding royalties.
6. **Risks.**
   - The 51,200-acre per-state cap forces selectivity.
   - The year-10 diligence requirement forces a monetisation clock of about 5–8 years.
   - BLM staffing limits throughput, with 455-day average nomination-to-sale times.
   - Congress could someday apply the wind/solar §50302 market-indexing logic to geothermal (no evidence of this yet).

   None of these kills the idea. Scale is ample: 245M acres open to leasing, more than 1M acres nominated per year, and $24–41M per year of bonus revenue showing that buyers pay for de-risked land.
