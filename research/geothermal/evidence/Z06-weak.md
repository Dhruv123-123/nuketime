# Z06: Zanskar's weaknesses, absences and what it explicitly does not do

*Research note, 2026-10-21.*

**Method.** The shared WebSearch budget was used up before this agent's first query, so no searches ran. This note rests on three kinds of source:
- direct WebFetch of Zanskar's own pages and Substack releases;
- trade press (TechCrunch, Latitude Media, MIT Technology Review, Heatmap, ASME);
- cross-checks against sibling evidence files Z01, Z04, L03, L10, L12, L19 and L21, which themselves cite BLM, NREL and USGS primary documents.

Anything not tied to a URL is marked (unverified). I found **no published independent critique of Zanskar**: every press piece retrieved quotes only Zanskar's founders, and that absence is itself a finding (Section 7).

## 1. Geographic focus: one basin, one auction state

- **What the company says.** Exploration covers "millions of acres across the West." The approach is "applied globally," but no international project is named (https://www.zanskar.com/about ; https://www.zanskar.com/news).
- **Assets disclosed:**
  - Nevada: Pumpernickel (Humboldt Co.) and Big Blind (western NV), both Great Basin.
  - New Mexico: Lightning Dock (Hidalgo Co.), bought as an operating plant, not discovered.
  - California: a prospecting agreement (GEODE) with CC Power, Apr 2026. It covers CAISO, but no land position is disclosed (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-and-cc-power-sign-geode-agreement).
- **BLM auction record (Z04, from BLM all-bids PDFs):**
  - Zanskar has won **18 parcels, about 50,022 acres, all in Nevada**, for about **$764k** in bonus bids (about $15/acre).
  - It registered for the Utah sales in Jan 2023 and Apr 2025 and **won nothing**. Utah land near Fervo's Cape Station cleared at up to **$344/acre** in Apr 2025.
  - Its pace collapsed in 2025: only **872 acres** were added.
  - It lost a Pumpernickel step-out parcel (NV-2025-10-2143) by **$1/acre** ($231 vs $232) to Lithos48 Inc.
  - Source: https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2025-10/NV_Geot_20251021_Bidding_Results.pdf
- **CEO framing.** Hoiland says the US West "has the most potential" (https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/zanskar-thinks-1-tw-of-geothermal-power-is-being-overlooked/).
- **Absences:**
  - No Canada, Latin America, Africa or Asia projects.
  - No Gulf Coast or sedimentary-basin activity.
  - No state trust land or private fee-land acquisitions disclosed.
- **Verdict.** Zanskar is in practice a Great Basin hydrothermal company with one bought plant in New Mexico and a prospecting MOU in California.

## 2. Resource type: conventional hydrothermal, with a small EGS hedge

- **The core bet is conventional.** TechCrunch says Zanskar does not pursue EGS. It is betting that "conventional" hydrothermal resources are underestimated, against DOE's 60 GW-by-2050 figure, which leans on EGS (TechCrunch, above).
- **It is deliberate about avoiding technology risk.** Edwards: "We're avoiding the first-of-a-kind risk label, so that we can access cheaper capital sooner." Zanskar targets sites that fit conventional development methods lenders already understand (https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/armed-with-115-million-geothermal-startup-zanskar-gets-ready-to-build/).
- **The hedge.** In Sep 2026 Zanskar was selected for two DOE projects; award amounts are undisclosed and still under negotiation.
  - One would "design, drill and stimulate a short horizontal injection well" at Lightning Dock. That is a hybrid of hydrothermal and stimulation.
  - The other covers deep directional wells at an undrilled Nevada site.
  - Source: https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-selected-for-two-us-department
  - Latitude notes "enhanced geothermal potential" exists at many of its sites, so EGS is **optionality on its own land, not a line of business**.
- **Resources are moderate in temperature:**

  | Site | Temperature | Depth | Source |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Big Blind | ~121 °C (250 °F) | ~823 m (2,700 ft) | https://magazine.asme.org/issues/may-2026/news-hidden-geothermal-goldmines |
  | Pumpernickel | ~137 °C (279 °F) | ~762 m (2,500 ft) | same |

  - Both plants "will use binary cycle technology" (ASME).
  - NREL ATB 2024 gives **binary hydrothermal overnight capital cost of about $9,483/kW**, against **$4,350/kW for flash** (https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/geothermal, via L21).
  - Low-temperature binary is the most capital-intensive per kW of the conventional options.
- **Not done, on the evidence found:**
  - Superhot rock: no evidence found (L08).
  - Closed-loop: no evidence found.
  - Sedimentary or hot-sedimentary-aquifer plays: no source found (L10).
  - Oil and gas co-produced fluids or repurposed wellbores: no evidence found (L11).
  - Low-temperature direct use, district heat and data-centre cooling heat: none in news titles (https://www.zanskar.com/news).

## 3. No lithium or minerals, despite the corporate name

- The bidding entity on BLM documents is **"Zanskar Geothermal & Minerals Inc."** (Z04).
- Yet none of the ten news items from Sep 2025 to Sep 2026 mention minerals, lithium, brine or DLE (https://www.zanskar.com/news).
- Great Basin brines are mostly **below 20–25 mg/kg Li**, according to NREL (cited in L12). Mineral co-production is therefore geologically unavailable in Zanskar's home play.
- BLM geothermal leases also do not cleanly convey the lithium in the brine (L12).
- **Verdict:** heat-plus-lithium value stacking, in Smackover, Williston, Saskatchewan and Salton Sea brines, is entirely outside Zanskar's footprint.

## 4. Capital intensity of the build-own-operate model

- **Stated model.** Zanskar discovers resources, then "develops and operates the power plants." Its Series C funds are to "build, own, and operate high-grade geothermal assets" (https://www.zanskar.com/about ; https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-raises-115m-series-c-following).
- **Pipeline:**
  - Six 20 MW plants, with commercial operation in 3–4 years.
  - About 700 MW of sites under "full or partial control."
  - Zanskar needs **at least 10 confirmed sites** before project-finance investors engage (Latitude; TechCrunch).
- **Capital raised:**
  - **$180M** of equity in total.
  - A $40M non-recourse development facility, structured to scale to $100M (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-closes-40m-in-novel-development).
- **Rough capex math (unverified estimate):**
  - Six 20 MW binary plants at about $9.5k/kW (ATB average) comes to about **$1.1B of capex**. That is roughly 4–6× the equity plus facility raised so far.
  - Hitting the 100 MW NV Energy PPA by 2030 alone implies about $0.5–1B.
- **Timeline risk:**
  - Pumpernickel's first power is targeted for **2028** (https://heatmap.news/climate-tech/ai-geothermal-discovery), about three years after the discovery well.
  - Heatmap notes **no greenfield geothermal plant had been built in the US in nine years**.
- **Interconnection.** Edwards: "Interconnection is our bottleneck." The 20 MW plant size is chosen to fit small-generator interconnection rules (Latitude). That caps plant scale and makes it slow to fill a 700 MW portfolio.
- **Revealed preference: Zanskar already exits to utilities.** The 100 MW, 20-year NV Energy PPA (running through 2050) covers "multiple projects to be constructed and operated by Zanskar and sold in a portfolio to NV Energy." It is conditional on PUCN approval of the 2026 IRP, and the price is undisclosed (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-inks-100-mw-power-purchase). The step-up is captured, but only after Zanskar carries construction risk.

## 5. Operating-asset risk: Lightning Dock

- **What Zanskar bought** (Jun 2024):
  - A ~15 MW gross plant whose brine had cooled about 50 °F in five years, a decline 5–10× normal.
  - Inlet was ~255 °F against a ~310 °F design.
  - The plant "struggled to generate net power on hot summer afternoons."
  - Source: https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/one-year-at-lightning-dock
  - The prior operator is widely reported as Cyrq Energy (unverified).
- **What one deep well achieved.** Well 44-7, drilled to ~8,000 ft, delivered about 75,000 MWh in its first year, about 140% above the legacy-well counterfactual.
- **Zanskar's own caveats:**
  - The result rests on one year of data, and "only time can answer" whether the well will last.
  - Winter output is curtailed by a **transmission cap**.
  - Summer output is curtailed by the plant's heat-rejection limits.
  - No capex is disclosed.
- **Verdict.** The asset still carries reservoir-sustainability, transmission and plant-equipment risk. This is the kind of downside a rights-only owner never holds.

## 7. Critiques and unverified claims

No independent industry critic of Zanskar was found. The MIT Technology Review, Heatmap, Latitude and ASME pieces all quote only the co-founders. MIT TR's only outside voice, John McLennan of Utah FORGE, is supportive.

The weak points that are on the record:

- **No peer-reviewed validation.** No paper reports Zanskar's model accuracy. Its validation is company-reported drilling: "3 of 3 sites" successful (TechCrunch; L03). Heatmap: "no success rate, track record, or accuracy data."
- **Scale claims are opinion:**
  - The **1 TW** figure is Hoiland's view that undiscovered resources are underestimated "by an order of magnitude or more."
  - "Over 100 MWe" is a claim about *analogous* systems, not a booked resource at Big Blind.
  - Edwards: "We think these systems can be a lot bigger than expected."
  - Sources: TechCrunch; https://magazine.asme.org/issues/may-2026/news-hidden-geothermal-goldmines
- **The founders' own doubts:**
  - Edwards: "What does it look like when you try 10?"
  - TechCrunch says Hoiland is "not convinced" Zanskar has solved resource-finding.
- **Big Blind is unproven as a producer.** Long-term flow testing, permits, grid connection and financing are all still outstanding (https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/04/1128763/ai-geothermal-zanskar/).
- **Competition for land:**
  - Land-banking LLCs (Buffalo River Minerals, Lithos48, Skyladder, Cordillera) and Invenergy now crowd the Nevada auctions.
  - The top bid reached **$410/acre** in Oct 2025, up from Zanskar's $2/acre entries in 2023 (Z04).
  - Zanskar's public discovery announcements reprice adjacent land, and it has lost close bids on its own trends.
- **The sector-wide caution applies.** Heatmap's coverage of the Morpheus short report on Fervo shows investor scepticism about next-generation geothermal economics (https://heatmap.news/climate-tech/ai-geothermal-discovery). It is not aimed at Zanskar.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Zanskar has validated the Terranox step-up but chosen not to harvest it as a land owner.**
   - Pumpernickel step-out acreage cost **$2/acre** in 2023. After disclosure, adjacent land cleared at **$232/acre**, about 116×.
   - Zanskar's whole Nevada auction book cost under $1M.
   - Yet its business is spending about $1B to build 20 MW binary plants. Capital and management attention go to construction, interconnection and O&M rather than breadth of rights.
   - A rights-first company that holds hundreds of cheap positions and sells or farms out the proven state for a carry or royalty is a different, unoccupied shape.
2. **The largest whitespace is outside Zanskar's resource type and data source.**
   - Sedimentary basins with dense oil and gas BHT and well-log archives: Gulf Coast, Permian, Williston, Alberta/Saskatchewan.
   - Heat plus lithium brines, which Zanskar's geology and tenure cannot reach.
   - EGS-grade conductive hot rock near load and transmission, where rights can be sold ahead of Fervo-style developers.
   - All three map directly onto Terranox's "public-but-illegible archives" competence and its Saskatchewan rights know-how.
3. **Geographic gaps:**
   - Utah, Idaho, Oregon, New Mexico and California BLM, where Zanskar has 0 auction wins;
   - noncompetitive day-after BLM filings (about 92k acres left unclaimed in NV-2025, per Z04);
   - state trust land and private fee or split-estate rights;
   - Canada.
   Zanskar is visibly absent from all of these.
4. **Neutrality is an advantage Zanskar cannot offer.** Zanskar competes with Ormat, Fervo and Invenergy for the same PPAs, so it cannot be their land supplier. A neutral rights bank can sell to all of them, Zanskar included, and to data-centre developers.
5. **Risks to price in:**
   - Zanskar could add a land-flip or prospect-partner arm; GEODE already looks like prospect-for-offtaker.
   - Land-banking LLCs already compete in Nevada auctions.
   - Permeability, not temperature, is what decides hydrothermal value. A rights bank therefore needs cheap confirmation, such as temperature-gradient or slim holes, or plays where temperature and archive data carry most of the value: sedimentary, EGS-grade and lithium.
6. **Scoring.** On these facts the gap is real, large and asset-owning, not a service. It is not "too small": the international market alone is about 10.9 GW installed (L19), and the lithium-brine and EGS land markets sit on top of the US hydrothermal market. The entrant should avoid head-on competition for Great Basin blind-hydrothermal leases.
