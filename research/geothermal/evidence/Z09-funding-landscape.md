### Market-level totals

### DOE and public money

- **The Liftoff report (Mar 2024)** says the earliest capital is the scarcest:
  - About **$450M of equity** is needed to characterise and build a first-of-a-kind 30 MW EGS project (at $15,000/kW).
  - "most capital providers will find the resource or technology risk too high to engage at this early stage".
  - It calls this "the single largest barrier".
  - It endorses a **"farm-down" model**: the developer spends equity on subsurface work, demonstrates de-risked operation, then sells a stake. That cuts up-front equity to **~$180M per 30 MW**.
  - LPO had **$132B of remaining loan authority** applicable to geothermal (Jan 2024).
  - DOE's past cost-shared drilling programme (**GRED, 1982-2012**) "identified much of the geothermal capacity that is currently online in the U.S."
- **No LPO loan to a next-generation geothermal startup was found** in the 2021-26 sources read. The Fervo S-1 debt is commercial. Historical LPO geothermal deals exist (Liftoff cites energy.gov/lpo/blue-mountain; Ormat's ~$350M 2011 partial guarantee is unverified here).
- DOE grants in 2026:
  - Quaise: up to $25M.
  - Zanskar: selected for two DOE projects (Sep 2026), amounts undisclosed.
  - Mazama: Project Ceres has DOE support.

## 2. How investors value land and resources: the price ladder

| Stage | Hard data point | $/acre or $/MW |
|---|---|---|
| Unproven BLM lease, cold or blind ground | Zanskar's NV wins; 6 of 18 at the $2 statutory floor (Z04). Fervo's 2019-21 average was ~$4 (S-1) | **$2-15/acre** |
| Lease beside a disclosed discovery or play | Pumpernickel step-out cleared at **$232/acre** in Oct 2025, a month after Zanskar's discovery PR (Z04). BLM 2025 maxima were $344 (UT) and $410 (NV) (S-1). Utah reached $1,207/acre in 2026 (L21) | **$100-1,200/acre** |
| Discovery cost, explorer-reported | Zanskar Big Blind: **<$15k/MW**, against >$100k/MW historically (Z02/Z04) | **<$0.015M/MW** |
| Equity required, FOAK EGS | DOE: $450M per 30 MW all-equity, or $180M per 30 MW with farm-down | $6-15M/MW |
| Operating hydrothermal portfolio (M&A) | **Ormat bought Enel Green Power NA's 150 MW (Cove Fort, Stillwater, Salt Wells, plus solar) for $271M** in Oct 2023. That is **~11.3x** the ~$24M average 2020-22 EBITDA. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774923028865/ex_585361.htm | **~$1.8M/MW** for aged, partly solar assets |
| Contracted firm power value | ~$10M PV per MW at $100/MWh, 20 years, 6% (L23) | ~$10M/MW |
| Public-market developer (Fervo IPO) | ~$7.5B implied at the range | **~$12.6k per leased acre**; **~$11.4M per MW of binding PPA**; **~$2.9M/MW** on the 2.6 GW advanced pipeline (all my arithmetic) |

**Reading the ladder.** Value climbs about **100x** from $2-4/acre to $232-410/acre on *disclosure alone*, before any MW is proven. It climbs another **~30x** (to ~$12.6k per acre) once land sits inside a contracted, financed, public developer. Operating aged plants trade at **~$1.8M/MW**. Contracted new firm power is worth **~$10M/MW**.

No arm's-length sale of a *proven-but-undeveloped* resource ($/MW at confirmation stage) appeared in the sources read. That data point is missing. Historically such deals are private farm-ins (unverified).

Strategics are already buying early technology and resource exposure:
- Ormat's $25M into Sage, with development rights attached.
- Google's equity in Fervo.
- Devon in Fervo; Nabors in Quaise and Sage; ConocoPhillips and Shell in Mazama.
- Meta has two 150 MW deals and Amazon is behind the Zanskar PPA.

## 3. What investors are rewarding

1. **Owning the asset and the PPA.** Fervo's valuation is driven by its "roughly 600,000-acre land position" and the Google framework (RBC, via ThinkGeoEnergy). An IPO priced at a premium to Ormat proves "there is a viable public market exit path" (RBC's Jonathan Sisto).
2. **Bridging early-stage development capital.** Zanskar's $40M→$100M facility is explicitly meant to fix "the critical bottleneck of early-stage project financing" (CEO Carl Hoiland). Lenders will now fund pre-construction work on AI-discovered greenfield sites.
3. **Avoiding the FOAK label.** Zanskar builds conventional binary plants "so that we can access cheaper capital sooner".
4. **Interconnection.** Zanskar sizes projects at 20 MW to fit Western small-generator interconnection rules, and calls interconnection "our bottleneck".

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The step-up is real, measured, and big enough to fund a company on.** A disclosed geothermal signal moves land from $2-4/acre to $232-1,207/acre within weeks to years. Public developers then capitalise it at ~$12.6k/acre (Fervo). A rights bank of 100k acres bought at ~$5/acre costs ~$0.5M plus ~$0.2-0.3M/yr in rentals. Re-rating even 10% of it to the $300/acre band returns ~6x on land cost. Selling one confirmed 20-50 MW prospect into a developer at even $0.1-0.3M/MW (deep below the ~$1.8M/MW operating comp) is $2-15M per prospect, against TG-hole confirmation costs of a few hundred thousand dollars (L13). All of this is my arithmetic, and the confirmed-resource $/MW is unverified.
2. **The buyers exist and are now cash-rich.** Fervo (~$1.9B of IPO proceeds plus $462M Series E), Ormat (strategic buyer, and an investor in Sage), Mazama, Quaise and Sage (with >$400M raised in 2026) all need specific top-tier parcels. Hyperscalers (Meta, Google, Amazon) are funding offtake. There was no liquid buyer pool for proven-resource rights in 2021 ($68M of sector VC). There is one in 2026.
3. **DOE says the capital gap sits exactly at the stage a Terranox shape occupies.** The Liftoff report calls early resource-characterisation equity "the single largest barrier" and endorses farm-down. A company that carries resources only to the confirmed state, then farms down or sells, needs **one or two orders of magnitude less capital** than the $180-450M per 30 MW that a developer needs. That fits a seed/Series A budget. DOE grants (GRED-style cost share) can fund confirmation holes, but they usually require public data disclosure. The company must lock up acreage *before* taking grant money.
4. **The gap vs Zanskar is a capital-structure gap, not a technology gap.**
   - Zanskar raised ~$180M of equity plus a $40-100M facility, and is spending it to become an IPP: six 20 MW plants, Lightning Dock, the NV Energy/Amazon PPA.
   - Its auction position is only about 50k acres, bought for under $1M (Z04).
   - Its acquisition pace collapsed in 2025: 872 acres, after it lost the Pumpernickel step-out by $1/acre. Its own disclosures repriced its neighbourhood.
   - At roughly $5-10M/MW of capex, each MW Zanskar builds absorbs the capital that could have optioned thousands of acres.
   - Zanskar captures the full stack on a handful of sites. It structurally *cannot* be the high-throughput, multi-basin, multi-tenure (BLM, state, private, O&G brownfield) rights originator that sells into Fervo, Ormat, Sage, Quaise, Mazama, Eavor, XGS **and** Zanskar itself.
   - That role, the Terranox shape, is open.
5. **The pitch can borrow Zanskar's and Fervo's numbers:**
   - discovery at <$15k/MW (Zanskar);
   - land at $4/acre becoming a ~$7.5B company (Fervo);
   - land repricing ~100x on disclosure (BLM 2025);
   - DOE naming early-stage resource capital as the binding constraint.
6. **Risks surfaced by the funding data:**
   - (a) Disclosure leakage reprices neighbouring land at once, so buy whole fairways quietly before any press or grant.
   - (b) Well-funded buyers like Fervo already hold 596k acres and may prefer to self-explore. The best customers are then the technology-first developers (Quaise, Mazama, Sage, XGS, Eavor) and hyperscaler-backed IPPs, which raised equity for drilling, not for land origination.
   - (c) There is no public comp for a proven-but-undeveloped $/MW sale yet (unverified gap). Establishing the first one is itself a milestone.
