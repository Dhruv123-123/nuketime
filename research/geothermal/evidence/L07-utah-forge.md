# L07: Utah FORGE (2018-2026) and Fervo Project Red / Cape Station: what EGS results imply for which land becomes valuable

Status as of 2026-10-21. Some sources could not be opened in full during this run, because the Stanford pangea server returned 503 errors and TLS failures. Those sources are summarised from their abstracts, search snippets or secondary coverage, and each one says so. Anything without a source is marked (unverified).

## Bottom line

1. **EGS has moved from science to commercial operation in under 4 years.** Fervo's Cape Station GeoBlock 1 synced to the grid on 24 Sep 2026 and declared commercial operation on 30 Sep 2026, at **33 MW net**. Phase I is about 100 MW, from 3 x 33 MW GeoBlocks, and Phase II is 400 MW, from 8 x 50 MW GeoBlocks, due in 2028.
2. **Per-well output is real but contested.** Fervo's headline numbers are about 3.5 MW gross per producer at Project Red (2023) and more than 10 MW gross per producer at Cape (2024, 107 kg/s peak). Independent critics compute about 1.4 MW net (Red) and about 2 MW net per well (Cape, 24 h). Utah FORGE's doublet, analysed with conventional reservoir engineering, comes out at **1.7 MWe net (P90 0.9 MWe)**.
3. **Drilling is no longer the binding constraint.** Cape drilling time fell to **21 days per well**, a 70% reduction from 2022. Cost per well fell from **$9.4M to $4.8M** across the first four horizontals. Learning rate was **35%**, against 18% planned. By 2026 Fervo drilled a 19,450 ft MD / 7,500 ft lateral well at 460°F in 21 days.
4. **The land window Fervo actually uses is about 300-625°F at no more than about 13,000 ft vertical depth**, preferably next to existing deep wells that show high shallow temperatures, and near transmission. Those are the criteria in Fervo's S-1.
5. **The Terranox mechanism already happened, to Fervo.** Fervo assembled its leases in 2019-2021 at a **weighted average of about $4/acre**. 2025 BLM auctions cleared at a maximum of **$344/acre (Utah) and $410/acre (Nevada)**. A 2026 Utah sale in Millard and Beaver counties, which is the Cape/FORGE neighbourhood, set a record **$1,207/acre** and **$24.7M** in total high bids. That is a step-up of roughly 100-300x on the right itself, before any MW is proven.

## Literature and primary-source review

### 1. Norbeck, J. & Latimer, T. (2023). "Commercial-scale demonstration of a first-of-a-kind enhanced geothermal system" (EarthArXiv preprint; Fervo). Related DOE GDR dataset DOI 10.15121/1999428.
- Project Red, Nevada: a horizontal doublet at about 7,500-8,000 ft TVD with about 3,250 ft laterals. The gradient is about 75°C/km, and the target is about 350°F (S-1).
- 30-day test: **63 L/s at high temperature, 2-3.5 MW gross**. The 3.5 MW figure assumes a supercritical ORC. Drilling and stimulation ran from Jan 2022 to Mar 2023, and testing ran from Apr to May 2023.
- This was the first proof that plug-and-perf, multi-stage, oilfield-style horizontal completions can make a commercially relevant doublet. (I located the preprint itself only through secondary coverage and the GDR record.)
- Critique: Yearsley (GeoExpro, Jul 2026) argues that extended testing showed **about 1.4 MW net**, with thermal decline and a need for makeup water.

### 2. Fervo team (2024). Stanford Geothermal Workshop paper on Cape drilling performance (12 Feb 2024; author list not verified, file name suggests "Elsadi"). Press release via DCVC.
- Cape drilling began in June 2023. The team drilled 1 vertical and 6 horizontal wells in about 6 months.
- Fastest well took **21 days**, 70% less time than the first Red horizontal. Average rate of penetration was **70 ft/h** on the 4th horizontal, which beats NREL's 2035 "moderate" projection.
- Cost per well fell **from $9.4M to $4.8M**. The realised learning rate was **35%**.
- Cape wells are more than 2,100 ft deeper than Red's and hotter.

### 3. Fervo Energy (Sept 2024). Cape Station 30-day well test announcement (BusinessWire; covered by ThinkGeoEnergy and JPT).
- **107 kg/s peak flow**, which Fervo says equals **more than 10 MW gross from one producer**, about 3x a Red well. Fervo said the test beat productivity levels that NREL's ATB did not expect until 2035.
- 15 wells had been drilled at Cape by then, with up to 29 planned. Expected producer rates are 50,000-100,000 BWPD at about 400°F (JPT).
- Counterpoint (Yearsley 2026): about 6 MW net after 24 h from 3 wells, or **roughly 2 MW net per well**, with the plot cut at 24 h while still declining. He also argues NREL's assumed productivity index of 38 kg/s/bar is about 40x too high.

### 4. Fercho, S., Norbeck, J., et al. (2025). "Update on the Geology, Temperature, Fracturing, and Resource Potential at the Cape Geothermal Project," SGW 2025.
- New horizontal EGS wells on the Gold and Bearskin pads, north of the original Frisco pad and west of FORGE in Milford Valley.
- They extend the geological and temperature characterisation of the granitic/metamorphic basement under the basin. (I saw the abstract only; quantitative figures are unverified here.)
- Significance: Cape and FORGE sit on the same thermal anomaly, a buried, conductively heated basement beneath basin fill. This is the archetypal "invisible from the surface, legible from old deep wells" EGS target.

### 5. Utah FORGE, Moore, J., McLennan, J., et al. (University of Utah / EGI, DOE GTO). Program reports 2018-2026 and SGW "Current Activities" papers.
- Site: Milford, Utah, granitic basement. DOE selected it in 2018 (unverified detail).
- Injector 16A(78)-32 was drilled in 2020 to **8,559 ft TVD / 10,897 ft MD**. Producer 16B(78)-32 was drilled in 2023. Both laterals are at **65° from vertical, 300 ft apart**. Toe temperature is about 228-230°C (unverified, commonly cited).
- April 2024 stimulation: **8 stages in 16A** (re-fracturing 3 old intervals plus new ones) and 4 in 16B, with proppant, monitored by fibre and microseismic. Maximum induced seismicity was **M1.9**.
- 9-hour test (Apr 2024): injection up to **15 bpm (630 gpm)** and production about **8 bpm (344 gpm), about 70% recovery**. Outflow temperature reached about 139°C.
- 30-day test (Aug-Sep 2024): steady **10 bpm (420 gpm, about 26.5 kg/s)** injection, **more than 90% recovery**, production temperature about **370°F (188°C)**.
- Aug 2026: an extended circulation test lasting months was started (DOE). It ramps to 10 bpm and is meant to measure thermal decline and water loss.

### 6. Xing, P., England, K., Moore, J., McLennan, J., et al. (2024, 2025). SGW papers on FORGE circulation tests.
- 2024 paper: the July 2023 pre-stimulation connectivity tests. They showed poor recovery and connectivity before the proppant-laden plug-and-perf treatment.
- 2025 paper: the 2024 tests showed a **significant improvement in conductivity and fluid recovery after commercial-scale hydraulic stimulation**. This is the clearest controlled before/after evidence that modern completions, not the site, make the reservoir. (Abstract-level access only.)

### 7. McClure, M. (ResFrac) (2025). "Preliminary analysis" SGW 2025.
- Plug-and-perf with proppant "has delivered dramatically improved EGS performance relative to conventional EGS designs." **Stimulations without proppant have been ineffective.**
- FORGE: **26-28 kg/s sustained from about 1,000 ft of stimulated lateral**. That implies per-well rates scale with lateral length and stage count. Fervo's 5,000 ft (Cape Phase I) and 7,500 ft (Sawtooth) laterals sit at the high end of that logic.

### 8. Holt, R. (2025). "EGS Well Test Analysis from the Perspective of Conventional Geothermal Reservoir Engineering," SGW 2025.
- The FORGE doublet as-is has a **1.7 MWe net** generation capacity, with **P90 of 0.9 MWe net** over 10 years.
- This is a sceptical, bankability-style reading: today's FORGE well pair would not by itself be a commercial asset.
- Dotel & Podgorney (SGW 2026) report a highly localised flow, with **more than 60% of injected fluid recovered from the first 3 fracture zones**.
- Leggett et al. (SGW 2026, fibre in 16B) found injection concentrated in 3 heel stages and production efficiency rising from 25% to 65% during a 1-week test. There is clear headroom from better flow distribution.

### 9. Horne, R., Genter, A., McClure, M., Ellsworth, W., Norbeck, J., Schill, E. (2025). "Enhanced geothermal systems for clean firm energy generation." *Nature Reviews Clean Technology* 1, 148-160.
- Peer-reviewed review. Improved drilling methods are lowering costs and increasing scale. **US EGS could reach market-competitive costs by 2027.** With seismicity management, EGS can give baseload and potentially dispatchable power.

### 10. Aljubran, M. & Horne, R. (2024). "Thermal Earth model for the conterminous United States..." *Scientific Reports* 14 (doi 10.1038/s41598-024-68580-8). Also SGW 2024.
- A graph neural network temperature model of the contiguous US at 0-7 km depth, on 18 km² cells, with **MAE 4.8°C**.
- EGS that beats conventional hydrothermal on LCOE: about **83,000 GW** under the baseline case, and up to 184,000 GW with flexible dispatch. EGS that beats solar PV: only **0.65 GW** under the baseline case, and up to 44.66 GW with flexibility.
- Average LCOE was $86.1/MWh under baseline drilling rates and **$63.4/MWh** under advanced rates. **78% of gross potential is at 6-7 km.** A **4 km depth is optimal on only 0.27% of US land, mostly in the West.**
- This is the key land-scarcity number. The shallow-hot cells where EGS is cheapest are rare, and that is where rights will reprice first.

### 11. Ricks, W., Voller, K., Galban, G., Norbeck, J., Jenkins, J. (2024/2025). "The role of flexible geothermal power in decarbonized electricity systems." *Nature Energy*.
- Load-following plus in-reservoir storage (59-93% round-trip efficiency) raises EGS's least-cost share. Combined with lower drilling costs, it supports **more than 100 GW in the western US**.
- Earlier work: Ricks, Norbeck & Jenkins (2022), *Applied Energy* 313, on in-reservoir storage.

### 12. US DOE (2024). *Pathways to Commercial Liftoff: Next-Generation Geothermal Power.*
- **90 GW or more by 2050**, up to 300 GW. Liftoff needs $20-25B through 2030. The Enhanced Geothermal Shot target is **$45/MWh by 2035**.

### 13. Fervo Energy Co. (2026). Form S-1, Q2 2026 results, and COD release.
- **Wells:** Red is about 7,500 ft TVD with a ~3,000 ft lateral at about 350°F. Cape Phase I is about 9,000 ft TVD / 14,000 ft MD with a ~5,000 ft lateral at about 400°F. A GeoBlock uses 8-10 wells. More than 25 wells have been drilled.
- **Drilling trends, 2022 to 2025:** drilling time fell about 75% and cost per foot fell about 70%. Sawtooth 7 ("Fervo 3.0") reached 19,450 ft MD with a 7,500 ft lateral at 460°F in 21 days.
- **Capital cost:** about **$7,000/kW** for Cape Phase I, **$5,500/kW** targeted for Phase II, and **$3,000/kW** as the long-term (Nth-of-a-kind) target.
- **Site-selection screen:** **reservoir temperature about 300°F to 625°F; vertical depth no more than about 13,000 ft**. Fervo looks for existing deep wells confirming high shallow temperatures, transmission, market and permitting.
- **Land:** **about 595,900 acres** as of 31 Dec 2025, of which about 256,000 acres are prospects. Leases were assembled 2019-2021 at a **weighted average of about $4/acre**, mostly on 10-year terms.
- **Capacity:** Cape is permitted for about 4.3 GW total. The pipeline is 2.6 GW advanced and more than 38-50 GW early-stage across 10 GeoClusters. A subset of reviewed leases holds more than 40 GW of heat in place.
- **Offtake:** 658 MW of binding PPAs (more than 1 GW by Oct 2026) worth about $7.2B in backlog. EGS PPAs run about **$100-130/MWh** (Rystad). Google has a non-binding 3 GW framework.
- **Finance:** IPO in May 2026 at $27/share, raising about $1.9-2.2B. Valuation above $10B (GeoExpro). Phase I has a $421M project credit facility.

## What the results imply about the temperature and depth envelope

- **Commercial EGS today:** about **175-240°C at 2.3-3.0 km TVD**, in granitic or metamorphic basement beneath basins. Red is 350°F at about 2.3 km, Cape is about 400°F at about 2.7 km, and FORGE is about 228°C at about 2.6 km. That corresponds to gradients of **about 60-80°C/km**, roughly 2-3x the continental average.
- **Near-term (2026-2030):** Fervo's own screen runs to 625°F and about 4 km TVD, and Sawtooth shows a 460°F, 19.5 kft MD well drilled in 21 days. The economic frontier is pushing toward **250°C+ at no more than 4 km**. The superhot regime (>375°C) remains R&D.
- **Productivity scales with lateral length and stage count once proppant is used** (McClure 2025). Land value therefore depends on **(a) temperature at no more than 4 km, (b) a thick, homogeneous, drillable basement with a stress field favourable to horizontal fracs, (c) water, (d) transmission, and (e) no induced-seismicity receptors.** It does not depend on natural permeability. That is the key difference from hydrothermal, which is Zanskar's game.
- **Scarcity:** Aljubran & Horne find that 4 km is optimal on only **0.27% of US land**. The hot-shallow-basement cells are a small, mappable set, mostly in the Great Basin, plus the Snake River Plain, Imperial Valley, and parts of NM/CO/OR (unverified for specific regions beyond the "mostly in the West" finding).
- **Contested risk:** net MW per well, thermal decline (Red, FORGE localisation), and water make-up. Proving a site therefore needs a **30-day-plus doublet test**, so the step-up from "right" to "proven reservoir" is large and lumpy.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The arbitrage is proven by the incumbent's own S-1.** Fervo bought about 596k acres at about $4/acre. BLM bids near its sites now clear at $344-1,207/acre before any new data. A Terranox-shape company that identifies EGS-grade land *before* auction pricing catches up captures the same step-up, at about $1-50/acre noncompetitive or private-rights cost (unverified range), against $300-1,200+/acre once a site is recognised.
2. **The screen is explicit and data-driven.** The criteria are 300-625°F at no more than 13,000 ft, plus deep wells confirming shallow heat. That is exactly what old oil and gas BHTs, temperature-gradient holes and state archives encode. They are public but illegible: BHTs need thermal corrections, logs sit in scanned TIFFs, and state databases are fragmented. ML-corrected BHT plus basement-depth plus stress mapping across the West is a direct Terranox analogue to the Saskatchewan assessment files.
3. **The gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar's models hunt *blind hydrothermal* systems, which need natural permeability. Big Blind is 121°C at 823 m, where a convective upflow is the target. Its value comes from exploration success on a binary yes/no reservoir, and it then develops plants itself, as at Lightning Dock. Zanskar is not optimising for **conductive, hot, deep basement with no natural permeability**, which is the EGS land class. After Cape, that class is bankable, it reprices fastest, and an EGS developer (Fervo, plus followers and oil majors) needs it at GW scale. The class is also roughly 100x larger by resource count than hydrothermal (Aljubran & Horne). The gap is an **"EGS land bank"**: a company that maps, acquires and de-risks EGS-grade rights, and sells or JVs them to Fervo-like developers, utilities and hyperscalers. Owning the rights is the asset; it is not a service.
4. **De-risking ladder (own the step-up):** rights at about $4-100/acre, then a corrected-BHT thermal model, then one cheap temperature-gradient hole, then a vertical slim-hole to basement. That makes a "Fervo-screen-qualified" parcel, which comparables price at about $300-1,200/acre at auction, and much more for a proven doublet. Note that Fervo's Phase I runs about $7,000/kW, so a 50 MW GeoBlock's land and resource value is a small but non-trivial share (unverified quantification).
5. **Scale check (not too small):** 38-50 GW in Fervo's pipeline and a 90-300 GW DOE target imply tens of millions of acres of demand. Comparable EGS-grade land in the West outside Fervo's 596k acres is the addressable inventory.
6. **Risks:** contested net MW per well could slow the bid for land. BLM noncompetitive windows are narrowing as competition rises. Private and state geothermal rights vary by state. Induced seismicity and water rights add further risk. Mitigation: a dual-use screen, so parcels that also score for blind hydrothermal keep their value either way.
