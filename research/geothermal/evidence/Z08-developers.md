# Z08: Geothermal developers and land holders in the US West, and who wins the lease auctions

**Scope.** This file covers Ormat, Fervo, Cyrq, Open Mountain Energy (OME), Controlled Thermal Resources (CTR), Calpine, Terra-Gen, Gradient, and the 2022–2025 BLM lease-sale winners. It asks two questions: who holds the land, and who dominates the lease auctions.

**Method.** The shared web-search budget was exhausted when this task started, so no new searches ran. The file instead rests on primary documents downloaded directly:
- BLM Nevada and Utah all-bids reports, sale notices and registered-bidder lists. I parsed these to compute who won which parcels, acreage and bonus dollars.
- SEC EDGAR filings: Ormat's 10-Ks for FY2019, FY2021, FY2022, FY2024 and FY2025, and Fervo's IPO prospectus (424B4, May 2026).
- Company websites, read directly.

**Caveats.**
- Per-parcel bonus is computed as high bid per acre × acres rounded up, so it is ±1% versus BLM billing.
- Non-competitive, state and private leases do not appear in auction data.
- Facts I could not re-verify this session are marked (unverified).

**Pipeline and prospect counts.**

*Ormat (FY2025 10-K):*
- 34 US prospects: 23 in Nevada, 5 in California, 2 in Oregon, 3 in Utah, 1 in New Mexico. It also has 16 international prospects.
- The US count has grown: 28 (FY2022) → 31 (FY2024) → 34 (FY2025).
- Ormat says an initial desk evaluation costs **~$10k per US prospect**. Exploration before a full-size well costs **$1–5M per site**, excluding land.
- Write-offs of unsuccessful exploration were small: **$1.4M in 2025 and $3.9M in 2024**.

*Fervo (424B4):*
- 658 MW of binding PPAs, with SCE, Shell, Clean Power Alliance, Desert Community Energy and Google/NV Energy.
- A 3 GW framework agreement with Google (GFA, 19 Mar 2026).
- ~500 MW under construction and 550 MW "Ready to Build".
- More than 40 GW of pipeline: 2.6 GW in Advanced Development and more than 38 GW in Early Development across ten "GeoClusters".
- ~256,000 acres are pure "Prospects" with no development work started.
- The IPO priced at **$27/share** with **~$1.77B** in net proceeds.
- Cape Station (Milford, UT) is cited at ~4.3 GW of potential, with the first ~100 MW expected to reach the grid by early 2027.

**Fervo's own framing of the arbitrage (direct quote, 424B4).** The ~$4/acre position stands "in sharp contrast to current U.S. Bureau of Land Management lease sales in Utah and Nevada, where maximum bids reached **$344 and $410 per acre**, respectively, in 2025." The prospectus also cites Bloomberg: "certain geothermal developers in Utah and Nevada have paid hundreds of dollars per acre in 2025 for geothermal positions that failed to meet our development standards."

This is the clearest public statement of the Terranox thesis in geothermal. The leading EGS developer is telling public-market investors that its moat is land bought before the market knew what it was worth.

### Price regime change
- The Sep 2019 NV sale averaged ~$4.17/acre with a top bid of $20 (see L01).
- 2022 averaged ~$15/acre.
- 2025 tops reached **$408–410/acre in NV and $344 in UT**.
- Floor-price ($2/acre) parcels still clear every year. In 2025 Durin, Cordillera, Navram and Skyladder each won many parcels at $2–4/acre.
- In NV 2025 only **1 of 27** unsold parcels (4,890 of 97,120 acres) drew a noncompetitive day-after offer.

## 3. Developer-by-developer notes

### Ormat
- Ormat is the vertically integrated incumbent: plants, OEC equipment and EPC.
- It describes its "primary competitors" as "smaller, pure-play developers focused on resource identification and development". It also notes that venture-backed EGS newcomers are "competing for geothermal leases".
- A key line from the 10-K: if Ormat loses a lease, "the acquiring entity may still become a customer of Ormat's Product Segment." Ormat is therefore structurally a buyer and supplier for third-party resource holders.

**Acquisitions (Ormat's buy-side prices):**
- **Blue Mountain (NV) from Cyrq, June 2025:** 20 MW for **$88.7M**, about **$4.4M/MW** for an operating plant that also carries 51 MW of interconnection.
- **Terra-Gen's TG Geothermal Portfolio, July 2021:** **$171M cash plus ~$258M assumed debt and finance obligation**, about $429M total, for Dixie Valley (56 MW then, 64 MW now) and Beowawe (~20 MW).
- **Enel Green Power North America portfolio, Jan 2024:** **$274.6M**. It included two contracted geothermal plants, the Stillwater triple hybrid and two greenfield development assets.

**Demand side:** a Feb 2026 portfolio PPA for **up to 150 MW of new Nevada geothermal for Google** via NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff, with deliveries in 2028–2030. Meeting it requires new resource, which is exactly what a prospect generator would sell.

### Fervo
- Fervo holds the largest acreage: ~595,900 acres bought at ~$4/acre.
- It is an EGS developer and builds and operates its own plants. Its prospectus says land cost is rising and that "there is no assurance that we will be able to obtain additional lease rights … at the cost we anticipate." That makes Fervo a natural buyer of de-risked, well-located acreage.
- A further 256k acres sit idle as "Prospects", so Fervo is also a potential seller or farm-out partner for land it will not develop.

### Cyrq Energy
- Cyrq's website lists Hudson Ranch (Salton Sea, CA), Patua (Hazen, NV), Soda Lake (Fallon, NV) and Thermo 1 (Minersville, UT).
- It sold Blue Mountain to Ormat in 2025 for $88.7M.
- Lightning Dock (NM), which Zanskar bought in June 2024, is widely reported as an ex-Cyrq plant (unverified).
- Cyrq acts as a seller of mid-size operating assets.

### Open Mountain Energy (Lehi UT / Reno NV)
- OME acquires, develops, owns and operates plants.
- Published figures: 57.5 MW of guaranteed PPA capacity, **$767.7M in total PPA contract value**, 95.8 MW of contracted interconnection, 23,477 leased acres, 4,203 fee acres, and 64 wells including monitor and exploration wells.
- It closed a tax-equity deal with Monarch Private Capital in Feb 2023.
- OM Geothermal LLC (Dallas, TX), a 2022 NV winner, is a different entity as far as registration addresses show (link unverified).

### Controlled Thermal Resources (CTR)
- Hell's Kitchen, Salton Sea, CA: geothermal power plus lithium.
- A "500 MW Geothermal Project" with Baker Hughes (Sep 2025), FAST-41 covered status (Jun 2025), and a business combination agreement with SPAC Plum Acquisition Corp IV (Mar 2026).
- Its resource is private and Imperial County land, not BLM auctions. Acreage is not disclosed.

### Calpine
- Operates The Geysers (CA), the largest geothermal complex in the world, at ~725 MW net (unverified this session).
- Its land is legacy steam-field leasehold.
- Constellation's acquisition of Calpine was reported as closing in early 2026 (unverified).
- Not active in recent BLM auctions.

### Terra-Gen
- Its homepage now lists only 4.2 GW of wind, solar and storage.
- It exited Nevada geothermal by selling Dixie Valley and Beowawe to Ormat in 2021.
- Shows that non-core owners divest geothermal to Ormat at roughly $5–6M per operating MW (computed: ~$429M EV for ~76 MW).

### Gradient Geothermal
- A co-produced, oilfield-brine modular-power startup (unverified this session; see L11).
- Holds no material BLM land.

### Other auction players
- Invenergy (a utility-scale IPP entering geothermal in 2024).
- Raser Power Systems (the Thermo-area heritage, in Utah).
- Rodatherm (Salt Lake City; closed-loop/novel developer).
- TLS Geothermics (New York; buys cheap at ~$4–5/acre).
- Norte Geothermal (Carson City).
- XGS Energy (registered 2025).
- Baseload Capital.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The model is proven by Fervo, not Zanskar.** Fervo's S-1/424B4 shows a public geothermal company whose stated moat is ~596k acres bought at ~$4/acre in 2019–21, now set against $344–410/acre clearing prices. That is a ~100× mark-up on land alone, before any MW. Zanskar's ~50k acres at ~$15/acre is the same trade at 1/12 the scale. The "buy the right before proof" leg is validated. The open question is who does it next with a data edge.

2. **Who dominates now: capital-rich land banks, not explorers.** Anonymous LLCs (Buffalo River, Percheron, FLHN) took ~52% of 2022–25 NV+UT bonus dollars. Ormat took ~13%, Invenergy ~11% and Zanskar ~3%. These land banks pay $100–400/acre on hot, obvious ground near Cape Station and known fields. That validates resale demand for well-located rights. It also means competing head-on on hot, obvious ground is a losing trade.

3. **The open lane is blind and cheap ground, plus the off-auction channels.**
   - $2–4/acre parcels still clear every sale.
   - ~92k acres in NV 2025 went unclaimed in the noncompetitive round.
   - Large incumbents hold idle acreage that could be farmed in: Fervo's 256k "Prospects" acres and Ormat's ~434k acres.
   - Private land is a big share of the major holdings: 27% of Fervo's acreage and 18% of Ormat's.
   An ML-ranked portfolio bought at the floor and through private/state deals is the Terranox analogue. The auction data says that lane is still open.

4. **The buyers are identifiable and paying.**
   - **Ormat** pays ~$4.4–6M per operating MW (Blue Mountain, Dixie Valley). It needs new Nevada resource to fill a 150 MW Google PPA. Its 10-K explicitly frames third-party resource holders as Product-segment customers.
   - **Fervo** says publicly that new leases are scarce and costly.
   - **Invenergy** and the land banks show capital waiting for de-risked rights.
   The exit market for "proven-state" rights is real.

5. **The gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar is now a capital-heavy developer: Lightning Dock, a 100 MW NV Energy PPA, a $115M Series C and project-finance facilities. Its auction pace collapsed in 2025: 872 acres added, and it lost Pumpernickel step-outs by $1/acre. Zanskar cannot be a neutral seller to Ormat, Fervo or Invenergy, because they are its competitors. A Terranox-shape company that owns and resells, or farms out with a retained royalty, could:
   - (a) cover all seven western states, since Zanskar's auction wins are 100% Nevada;
   - (b) run the noncompetitive, private, state and split-estate channels;
   - (c) sell to every developer;
   - (d) avoid drilling and plant capex beyond cheap TG holes ($1–5M per site per Ormat's figure).

6. **The main risk is the information leak.** Nominations and discovery announcements reprice adjacent land within weeks: Zanskar's $2 → $232/acre at Pumpernickel. Land-bank LLCs are fast followers. Execution has to prioritize stealth: nominee entities (which the incumbents already use), floor bids spread across many basins, and locking up whole fairways before any drilling result is public.

7. **Scale check.** Fervo alone claims more than 40 GW of pipeline on ~596k acres. Ormat holds 34 US prospects. Operating plants change hands at $4–6M/MW. A 100–200 MW-equivalent resource portfolio sold at even $0.5–1M/MW for de-risked, pre-construction rights (unverified multiple) is a $50–200M asset value. That is a venture-scale outcome on a land budget of a few million dollars.
