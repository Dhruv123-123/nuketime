### Reading the ladder

1. **Land step-up (stage 0 → 1): 25–100x, measured, fast.**
   - The cleanest numbers: $4 → $344–410/ac (Fervo's own comparison), and $2 → $232/ac next to Pumpernickel within 23 months.
   - The market is bimodal. In NV 2025 the median parcel still cleared at $3.50/ac, and 37% of parcels went at the floor. Only a handful of tracts that consensus has identified get bid up (R01).
   - So the step-up is paid for **information**, not for acreage.
2. **Per-MW conversion of land value: still small.**
   - A hydrothermal plant's productive core is tiny compared with the lease block. At San Emidio, USG held **17,846 acres**, but the unitised participating area is **583.68 acres (3.3%)**, for ~10 MW (USG 10-K via L24: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1172136/000106299317001274/form10k.htm).
   - At $300/ac on a 10,000-acre block for a ~20 MW system, the land is worth ~$3M, or **~$0.15M/MW** (calc). That is only 3–4% of the $4–5M/MW that operating plants trade at.
   - Land is therefore cheap to the buyer, which is why buyers pay up. It is also why selling land alone caps the absolute exit.
3. **The missing middle (stage 1 → 3).**
   - No arm's-length, priced sale of a **proven-but-unbuilt** US geothermal resource appeared in any filing read here or in the sibling files.
   - Where such assets did change hands, they were either:
     - valued near zero inside a larger deal (USG's $10M liquidation value, Enel's unpriced greenfields), or
     - structured as farm-ins/JVs (Crump Geyser).
   - The USG proxy shows why. Bidders discounted development assets because there was **no PPA and transmission costs were rising**. Development projects were discounted at **14–16%**, against 8–10% for operating ones (DEFM14A, ROTH NPV analysis).
   - Value jumps at **PPA plus interconnection**, not at the temperature hole.
4. **The operating benchmark is steady at ~$4–5.5M/MW** (Blue Mountain 2025, USG 2018, Terra-Gen 2021). Note that Blue Mountain cost NGP far more to build (~$180M, unverified) than the $88.7M Ormat paid in 2025. **The step-down for distressed builders is as real as the step-up for early land buyers.**

## 2. Who the buyers are and what they say

- **Ormat** (the incumbent consolidator) holds **434,430 US acres**: 78% federal, 18% private, 4% owned. It has **34 US prospects and 50 in total**.
  - Its own costs are about $10k–$50k per prospect for initial evaluation and **$1–5M per site of exploration before a full-size well**, excluding land.
  - It notes that venture-backed entrants are "actively competing for geothermal leases".
  - It buys operating assets at ~8x EBITDA and states a strategy of "Acquisition of Geothermal Assets".
  - It has a Google/NV Energy portfolio PPA for **up to 150 MW of new Nevada geothermal**, deliveries 2028–30 (Z08). That PPA needs *new resource*, which is exactly what a prospect generator sells.
  - Source: Ormat FY2025 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000129644526000006/ora-20251231.htm
- **Fervo** says: "There is no assurance that we will be able to obtain additional lease rights … at the cost we anticipate."
  - It warns that "super majors … can bid more aggressively".
  - It claims that others "paid hundreds of dollars per acre in 2025 for geothermal positions that failed to meet our development standards." Buyers are therefore paying for **quality screening**, and a screened, de-risked parcel is what commands the premium.
  - Fervo's Centaurus financing leaves Centaurus a **$5.0/MWh royalty** after its priority returns. That is a live precedent for a non-operator holding a perpetual per-MWh override on a geothermal project.
  - Source: Fervo 424B4
- **Cyrq Energy** (the Raser successor) is a **net seller**. It sold Lightning Dock to Zanskar (2024) and Blue Mountain to Ormat (2025).
- **Land-bank LLCs:**
  - Buffalo River Minerals, Percheron, FLHN, Cordillera, Skyladder and Navram took **~52% of NV/UT bonus dollars** in 2025.
  - Invenergy is also a buyer.
  - Raser Power Systems still bids (R01/Z08).
  - **Speculative intermediaries already exist, but they bid without disclosed data or ML** (R01, Z08).

## 3. Is there a step-up, and how big?

**Yes. It is large at the land layer and very large at the asset layer, but the middle rung is illiquid.**

| Transition | Multiple | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Floor lease → recognised play | **25–100x** ($4 → $100–410/ac); ~300x at the $1,207 outlier | BLM sales, Fervo 424B4 |
| Lease next to disclosed discovery | **~116x** in under 2 years | Zanskar Pumpernickel (Z04) |
| Discovery cost → operating value | <$15k/MW (Zanskar claim) → $4–5.5M/MW: **~300x**, but this needs ~$5–7M/MW of capex in between (unverified capex range) | Zanskar; Blue Mountain/USG |
| Confirmed, no PPA → operating | $0.1M/MW → $4.4M/MW. The spread is consumed by drilling, plant, PPA and interconnection. | USG proxy; Ormat 10-Q |
| Royalty alternative | $5/MWh × ~7,900 MWh/MW-yr ≈ **$39k/MW-yr**, about **$0.4M/MW PV** (8%, 25 yr, calc) | Centaurus/Fervo precedent |

So a rights owner that stops at "TG-confirmed lease" can realistically sell at **$0.1–0.5M/MW-equivalent**, or **$300–1,500/acre**, against an entry cost of **$2–20/acre**. That is a 25–100x+ gross multiple on land plus confirmation spend. The bigger prize, $0.4M+/MW, comes from **retaining an override/royalty** or carrying the asset to a PPA-ready state, where buyers like Ormat pay $4M+/MW.

## 4. Risks surfaced by the transaction record

- **The exit market at the proven-but-unbuilt stage is thin.** Buyers in the USG process walked away from development assets.
- **The boom-bust record is unforgiving.** Raser held 250k acres and lost 100% of its equity. U.S. Geothermal returned roughly its paid-in capital (~$122M) after ~15 years (L24). Land banks without a monetisation path die.
- **The auction market is now public and fast.** Any disclosure reprices neighbouring land within one sale cycle (Pumpernickel), so the rights must be secured *before* any data is published.
- **The size of the land-only exit is bounded.** NV federal bonus receipts were $9.4M in 2025, and all-state receipts are roughly $35–40M/yr (R01). The total market for raw leases is small, so **the business must sell resources, not acres**.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The Terranox arbitrage exists in geothermal, and the incumbents' own filings document it.**
   - Fervo writes the $4 → $344/$410 per acre step-up into its prospectus.
   - Zanskar's Pumpernickel neighbours repriced $2 → $232/ac.
   - Entry is trivial: about $2–4/ac bonus plus $1–3/ac/yr rental. Holding 100,000 acres costs about $0.3M/yr.
   - Floor-priced and unbid acreage is still available every year (37% of NV 2025 parcels at the floor; ~92k acres left unbid).
2. **Sell resources, not acres.**
   - Land repriced to $300/ac is still only ~$0.15M/MW, a few percent of plant value.
   - The venture-scale outcome needs either:
     - (a) packaging leases plus TG/slim-hole confirmation into "Fervo/Ormat-screen-qualified" resources sold at $0.1–0.5M/MW, or
     - (b) retaining a **$/MWh override** on every sale. The Centaurus $5/MWh precedent is worth ~$0.4M/MW PV.
   - The mining prospect-generator model (EMX, Franco-Nevada) is the right analogue. Sell with a carry or royalty, never clean.
3. **Fill the missing middle.**
   - No priced, arm's-length market exists for confirmed-but-unbuilt US geothermal. Creating it, and setting the first comps, is the opportunity.
   - The known buyers have explicit unmet resource needs:
     - Ormat's 150 MW Google portfolio PPA;
     - Fervo's lease-cost warning;
     - Invenergy and hyperscaler developers bidding at auction.
4. **Avoid the Raser/USG failure modes.**
   - Do not build plants, and do not carry ~$565M of stranded development capex (USG).
   - Do not skip slim holes.
   - Monetise at the stage the market can price.
5. **The gap vs Zanskar.**
   - Zanskar validates the front half of the loop. It bought Big Blind and Pumpernickel leases at ~$2/ac before drilling, then confirmed them with two holes.
   - It then **converts every win into a developer/IPP project**: Lightning Dock (bought from Cyrq, price undisclosed), six 20 MW plants, and a 100 MW NV Energy PPA.
   - Its auction position is small: ~18 NV parcels, ~50k acres, under $1M of bonus (Z04/R01). It **lost its own step-outs at $232/ac**.
   - Because it competes with Ormat and Fervo as a developer, it **cannot be a neutral seller** of resources to them.
   - A Terranox-geo can be the neutral, high-throughput resource originator, which Zanskar is not:
     - lease far more acreage per dollar;
     - confirm cheaply;
     - sell or farm out to Ormat, Fervo, Invenergy, utilities, data-centre developers, or even Zanskar, with a retained royalty.
   - Distressed-asset buying (the Lightning Dock and Blue Mountain pattern) is a secondary lane. Cyrq-type sellers and the 1970s–80s exploration leases are inventory.
