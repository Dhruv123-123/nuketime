# L08 — Superhot Rock Geothermal (>374 °C): Literature Review

Scope: CATF reports, Iceland IDDP-1/2, Kakkonda, Larderello, US supercritical wells, Quaise, Mazama, resource maps of where superhot rock is shallow (<10 km), and what this means for land and rights. Sources were read directly where possible. Anything I could not confirm is marked (unverified).

## 1. Primary sources reviewed

### 1.1 Reinsch, Dobson, Asanuma, Huenges, Poletto, Sanjuan (2017). "Utilizing supercritical geothermal systems: a review of past ventures and ongoing research activities." *Geothermal Energy* 5:16. doi:10.1186/s40517-017-0075-y
This is the reference catalogue. I read the full text. Supercritical means above 374 °C and 221 bar for pure water, or above 406 °C and 298 bar for seawater. More than 25 wells worldwide have gone past 374 °C. Table 1 lists them, and almost all are **shallower than 4.7 km**:
- **Italy, Larderello:** San Pompeo 2 (>400 °C at 2,966 m), Carboli 11 (427 °C at 3,328 m), San Vito 1 (419 °C).
- **Iceland:** NJ-11 (>380 °C at 2,265 m), IDDP-1, IDDP-2.
- **Japan:** Kakkonda WD-1a (500 °C at 3,729 m).
- **Mexico:** Los Humeros H-8, H-11 and H-12 (>380 °C, about 2.3–3.0 km).
- **Kenya:** Menengai MW-01 (391 °C at 2,198 m).
- **US examples (most relevant here):**
  - Geysers **Prati-32**: 400 °C at 3,352 m, with a 10.6 kg/s steam entry. Rate of penetration was only 3 m/h and the last roller-cone bit lasted 30 m.
  - Geysers **Wilson No. 1** (Mt Hannah, 1981): about 400 °C from fluid inclusions. The casing collapsed.
  - Salton Sea **IID-14** (Red Hill, 1990): 390 °C at 2,073 m. It was plugged because of overpressure. Salton brines are 20–30% salinity, so these fluids only become supercritical above about 550 °C.
  - Puna, Hawaii: **KS-2** (supercritical conditions extrapolated at 2,440 m) and **KS-13** (hit dacite magma at 2,488 m, about 1,050 °C). **Lanipuna-1** reached ≥363 °C.
- **Newberry, Oregon:** about 320 °C at 3,000 m, and temperatures "very high" are expected at less than 5 km.
- **New Zealand, Taupō Volcanic Zone:** estimated >400 °C at 5–7 km and **10 GWe** of potential (Bignall 2010).

The recurring failures are corrosion from HCl, HF and H2S, silica scaling, casing collapse, total lost circulation, overpressure, and tools rated only to about 450 °C for about 8 hours.

### 1.2 Friðleifsson, Elders et al. (2014), *Geothermics* vol. 49 special issue on IDDP-1 (Krafla)
Source: summarised via Reinsch 2017 Table 1 and coverage of the special issue. In 2009 the well hit rhyolitic magma at **2,104 m** and was completed at 2,072 m. Flow tests produced **10–12 kg/s of superheated steam at 450 °C** with wellhead pressure up to 140 bar and enthalpy of about 3,200 kJ/kg. It was shut in July 2012 after acid-gas corrosion and a valve failure.
- The widely quoted "**~35–36 MW**" (Power Magazine, WEF) is not consistent across sources. My own arithmetic: 11 kg/s × 3.2 MJ/kg ≈ 35 MW, which is a *thermal* power figure. So the electric output was probably materially lower. Treat "35 MWe per well" as **(unverified)**.
- For comparison, a typical conventional well is 3–5 MWe.

### 1.3 Friðleifsson et al. (2017). "The Iceland Deep Drilling Project 4.5 km deep well, IDDP-2, in the seawater-recharged Reykjanes geothermal field." *Scientific Drilling* 23:1–12
Operator: HS Orka. The team deepened existing well RN-15 from 2.5 km and drilled a slant hole to **4,659 m measured depth (about 4.5 km vertical)**. Results:
- **426 °C and 34 MPa** at the bottom after 6 days of heating, which is supercritical for seawater. Alteration minerals indicate formation temperatures above 450 °C.
- Total circulation was lost below about 2.5–3 km. Zones below 3.4 km took less than 5% of injected fluid, so deep permeability was poor.
- The authors say it is "too early" to judge the well's energy potential or economics.
- **Key lesson:** permeability, not temperature, is the binding constraint. The economics are not yet demonstrated.

### 1.4 Muraoka et al. (1998), *Geothermics* 27:507–534; Ikeuchi et al. (1998), *Geothermics* 27:591–607. Kakkonda WD-1a, Japan
The well reached **>500 °C at 3,729 m**, confirmed with melting tablets. It entered Quaternary Kakkonda granite (0.07–0.3 Ma) at 2,860 m. Below about 3.1 km (about 380 °C) the gradient is conductive, about 32 °C per 100 m, which marks the brittle-ductile transition (BDT). There was **no permeability** in the ductile section. Kakkonda shows that 500 °C can sit at less than 4 km under young intrusions, but that this rock needs EGS-style stimulation to produce.

### 1.5 Bertani et al. (2018), Stanford Geothermal Workshop. DESCRAMBLE / Venelle-2, Larderello (EU H2020)
Enel deepened a dry well from 2.2 km and 350 °C to about **2.9 km**. Temperatures were reported at **>450 °C** (Tenaris document). A 507 °C figure circulates but is **(unverified)**. No major fractures or fluid entries were found. The well was drilled for the drilling-technology test, not to produce power. (The Stanford PDF returned HTTP 503, so this rests on CNR and Tenaris summaries.)

### 1.6 Scott, Driesner, Weis (2015). "Geologic controls on supercritical geothermal resources above magmatic intrusions." *Nature Communications* 6:7837
These are the first numerical models of how supercritical resources form. Fluids above **400 °C and 3 MJ/kg** develop above shallow intrusions in host rock whose BDT is above 450 °C, such as basalt. The models reproduce Krafla. The authors argue that such resources "may be widespread below conventional high-enthalpy systems." **Implication:** the best exploration targets are directly *beneath known hydrothermal fields*, which already have wells, data and leases.

### 1.7 Watanabe, Numakura, Sakaguchi, Saishu, Okamoto, Ingebritsen, Tsuchiya (2017). "Potentially exploitable supercritical geothermal resources in the ductile crust." *Nature Geoscience* 10:140–144
Lab work on fractured granite at **350–500 °C** found no abrupt loss of permeability at the BDT. Exploitable reservoirs above 450 °C may form at **2–6 km depth even in granite**. This widens the target set beyond basalt and volcanic arcs.

### 1.8 CATF / LucidCatalyst & HERO (Nov 2022). "A Preliminary Techno-Economic Model of Superhot Rock Energy"
I read the full PDF. The modelled Nth-of-a-kind LCOE is **$20–35/MWh**. Base inputs:
- 250 MW plant, wells to 5 km
- 55 kg/s per well (range 30–100 kg/s) × 0.3095 MW/(kg/s) = **17 MW per production well**
- WACC 8%, 25% learning from the 1st to the 10th well, O&M at 1% of CapEx

The headline finding is that **MW per well is the dominant LCOE driver.** Drilling cost is a small share of CapEx, so "dramatic reductions in drilling cost do not significantly reduce CapEx or LCOE." That undercuts a pure drilling-tech thesis like Quaise's and favours *finding* rock that is shallow, hot and permeable. The model also says no superhot plant exists yet, commercialisation is expected in the 2030s, Portland cement fails above about 275 °C, and hydrogen could cost under $1.50/kg.

### 1.9 CATF + University of Twente (ITC) (Mar 2024). Global "Heat Beneath Our Feet" superhot map
- **Method:** a steady-state conductive lithosphere model giving depth to the **450 °C** isotherm. It combines Lucazeau (2019) heat flow on a **0.5° grid** with LithoRef18 (Afonso et al. 2019) on a **2° grid**.
- **Headline:** 1% of the global resource is **63 TW**; 1% of the US resource is **4.3 TW**.
- **Caveats CATF states:** the work is "preliminary" and has no hard depth constraints.
- CATF has also published regional assessments, including one for 14 US states. Its Middle East report puts superhot rock under about 40% of the region's land at 3–12.5 km.
- **Key point:** cells of 0.5°–2° are about 50–200 km across. That is far too coarse to pick leases. **The map is an invitation, not a targeting tool.**

### 1.10 Company and government primary releases
- **Mazama Energy** (Newberry, OR; incubated by Khosla; builds on AltaRock and Blade):
  - Built a **331 °C** EGS in 2025, the hottest ever.
  - The **Athena** well reached 10,350 ft in 15 days, about 80% faster than the 2025 well, and is heading to about 15,500 ft and >400 °C.
  - **Project Ceres** (DOE-supported) targets 15 MWe per horizontal well, with power in 2027.
  - Claims **>10 GWe** at Newberry, pending independent certification.
  - Raised a **$135M Series B** (Sept 2026; Centaurus, Doerr, ConocoPhillips, Shell Ventures).
- **Quaise Energy:**
  - Its millimetre-wave system drilled **>100 m of granite** in Texas in 2025 and is "approaching 1 km" in 2026.
  - Series B initial close of **$134M** (July 2026), about $230M raised in total.
  - **Project Obsidian:** **1,334 acres won at a BLM geothermal lease sale** just south of Newberry National Volcanic Monument, where nearby wells show a gradient of about 100 °C/km.
  - Phase I is 50 MW by about 2030, Phase II is 250 MW. A PPA is signed with an undisclosed buyer, reportedly a hyperscaler **(unverified)**.
  - **Its first reservoirs target 315 °C and 365 °C with conventional drilling.** That is *sub-critical*. Millimetre-wave drilling comes later at the earliest.
- **New Zealand:** up to **NZ$60M** from the Regional Infrastructure Fund for three supercritical exploration wells, the first at Rotokawa with Mercury NZ. Separately, NZ$10M went to DeepHeat.
- **Newberry leasing history:** in 2014 there were **62 active BLM geothermal leases covering about 53,000 acres** on the volcano's flanks (KTVZ). BLM offered 8 parcels and 6,193 acres south of Newberry in a Sept 2022 sale; I could not find the bid results.
- **Oregon lease prices for comparison:** in July 2025 BLM sold 5,235 acres in Malheur to Ormat at a high bid of **$89/acre**. In 2020 a parcel went for $2/acre.

## 2. Where superhot rock is shallow (<10 km), US-focused

Measured or near-measured >374 °C in the US within 2–4 km:
- The Geysers, NW high-temperature zone: 400 °C at 3.35 km
- Salton Sea: 390 °C at 2.1 km
- Puna, Hawaii: supercritical conditions at 2.4 km, and magma
- Newberry: 331 °C reservoir now, 400 °C targeted at about 4.7 km

Likely shallow candidates are the Cascade arc volcanoes (Newberry, Medicine Lake, Lassen), Long Valley and Coso, the Yellowstone margins (largely off-limits), the Salton Trough, Hawaii and the Alaska Aleutians. Most of these are **already-known hydrothermal provinces**. That is consistent with Scott et al. (2015), and it means the "hidden" share of superhot sites is smaller than for blind 150–200 °C hydrothermal systems. Away from volcanic provinces, the 450 °C isotherm in the stable US interior generally lies beyond 10–15 km and needs Quaise-type drilling (unverified depth range; CATF does not publish point depths).

## 3. Land and rights implications
- **Rights are cheap but concentrated and contested.** BLM competitive sales clear at about $2–$130/acre in Oregon. Quaise bought its Obsidian position at auction. Leases near volcanoes sit beside national monuments, parks and Forest Service land:
  - Newberry National Volcanic Monument excludes the caldera.
  - The Geothermal Steam Act closes the Yellowstone and other National Park System thermal areas to leasing.
  - Lands near Native American cultural sites need consultation.
  - Development of Hawaii's Puna has been politically fraught (unverified for current status).
- **Incumbents control the shallowest superhot rock.** Calpine holds the Geysers, CalEnergy/BHE and Controlled Thermal Resources hold Salton Sea, Ormat holds Puna, and Enel and HS Orka hold their own fields abroad. A newcomer's best superhot rights are therefore (a) *deep rights beneath or beside existing fields*, which needs a deal with the surface or field holder, or (b) volcano-flank BLM and Forest Service parcels before a discovery reprices them.
- **Lease terms favour holding.** BLM geothermal leases run 10 years with extensions, rent is cheap, and diligence or work obligations apply. A rights-owner can sit on acreage while drillers like Mazama and Quaise de-risk the play next door. The Newberry analogy: Mazama's claim of >10 GWe, once certified, should reprice the 53,000 acres of flank leases.
- **"Superhot" is a deep-rights question.** In most western states geothermal rights go with the surface estate unless severed (unverified per state), so title work under private land matters. On federal land, one lease covers all depths.

## 4. Assessment
- **Physics: strong.** At least 25 wells have exceeded 374 °C, and 400–500 °C at 2–4 km has been reached in five or more fields.
- **Engineering: unproven.** No superhot well has produced power commercially. IDDP-1 lasted about 2 years before corrosion and valve failure. IDDP-2 had poor deep permeability. Kakkonda and Venelle-2 were essentially impermeable.
- **Economics:** the CATF $20–35/MWh is a modelled Nth-of-a-kind figure. Its own sensitivity analysis says power per well matters far more than drilling cost.
- **Timing:** Mazama's first superhot power is about 2027, Quaise's about 2030, and the field generally points to the 2030s.
- **Signal:** even the "superhot" companies are starting their commercial projects at 315–365 °C.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Zanskar does not play here.** I found no evidence of superhot work by Zanskar. Its known finds are blind, moderate-temperature hydrothermal systems, for example Big Blind at about 823 m and 121 °C. The superhot developers (Mazama, Quaise) are **drilling-tech and EGS-engineering companies that bought land as a means to an end**. Nobody runs a pure "find and own superhot or hot-EGS acreage" strategy.

2. **The data gap fits Terranox.** The best public map (CATF/Twente) uses 50–200 km cells and has no well constraints. Meanwhile decades of deep-well records exist in state archives, but they are illegible as they stand:
   - Geysers, Salton Sea and Coso wells (California DOGGR, now CalGEM)
   - Newberry temperature-gradient holes
   - Oregon DOGAMI and USGS heat-flow data
   - Unocal and Phillips exploration wells
   - Oil and gas bottom-hole temperatures
   
   ML that predicts the depth to 400 °C *at lease scale* (1 km) across volcanic provinces, combined with magnetotelluric and seismic data, is a direct analogue of the Saskatchewan Mineral Assessment Database play.

3. **There is a step-up catalyst, and others pay for it.** Mazama's certification of more than 10 GWe at Newberry and Quaise's Obsidian results (2026–2027) should reprice adjacent BLM acreage that currently clears at $2–130/acre. A Terranox-shape company could build positions on Cascade, Great Basin margin and Salton-adjacent parcels *now*, then sell or farm out to superhot developers, Fervo, Ormat or hyperscaler-backed developers, as Terranox does with uranium rights.

4. **Hedge with a temperature ladder.** The same parcels hold value at 200–300 °C for Fervo-style EGS, at 315–365 °C as near-superhot, which is what Quaise is actually building, and at more than 400 °C as an option on future tech. That gives a plausible owned-asset story that does not depend on superhot technology arriving. Per CATF, MW per well drives value, so "hottest and shallowest per acre" is the right thing to rank on.

5. **Risks:**
   - **Concentration:** the shallowest superhot rock is under incumbents (Calpine, BHE, Ormat) or under closed land such as parks and monuments.
   - **Permitting:** volcano-flank sites near Forest Service land and monuments are hard to permit.
   - **Lease rules:** diligence requirements limit indefinite holding.
   - **Size:** the "superhot-only" market is not too small given Newberry's >10 GWe claim and New Zealand's Taupō 10 GWe estimate. But superhot alone is a long-dated option. It works best as the **upside tier of a broader rights-acquisition company**, not as the whole thesis.
