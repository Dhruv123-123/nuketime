# Z02 — Zanskar: what it has actually drilled and found

Scope: Zanskar drilling and discoveries. Covers Big Blind (NV, 2025), Pumpernickel (NV, 2025), the Lightning Dock (NM) acquisition and its well 44-7, success-rate claims, MW, temperatures, depths and costs.

## 1. Corporate frame (as of Oct 2026)

- Founded 2019. Co-founders are Carl Hoiland (CEO) and Joel Edwards (CTO). It describes itself as the "largest U.S. geothermal exploration company" and "the first AI-native geothermal energy company". Its model is to **"develop, build, own, and operate"** geothermal assets. [Big Blind PR]
- Funding: a **$115M Series C** (Jan 2026) led by Spring Lane, taking total equity to **$180M**. Series B was led by Obvious; Series A was co-led by USV and Lowercarbon. Investors also include Munich Re Ventures, StepStone and others. Just Climate added more later. [Series C post]
- A **$40M revolving, non-recourse development capital facility**, built to grow to $100M, co-led by Just Climate and Spring Lane (Apr 2026). It pays for pre-construction work and bridges projects to bank project finance. [Dev capital post]
- A **100 MW, 20-year PPA with NV Energy** (May 2026) for delivery by 2030, running to 2050. It depends on approval of NV Energy's 2026 IRP. The PPA covers several projects that Zanskar will "build and operate, then sell as a portfolio to NV Energy". Price is not disclosed. [PPA post]
- A GEODE prospecting agreement with CC Power (California CCAs), Apr 2026. [About page]
- **Two DOE awards** (Sep 2026), now in negotiation. One is a short horizontal stimulated injection well at Lightning Dock. The other is deep directional wells at an unnamed Nevada site "never previously drilled to reservoir depth". [DOE post]
- Customers listed: PNM and NV Energy. Partners listed: DOE, "U.S. Department of War" and Freeport-McMoRan. [Who-we-work-with page]
- Exploration footprint: "millions of acres across the West". This is not stated as acres under lease. [About page]

## 2. Lightning Dock, New Mexico (brownfield acquire-and-fix)

**History before Zanskar** (NREL OpenEI):
- Farmers' wells hit boiling water in 1948 at depths as shallow as about 26.5 m. The area was designated a KGRA in 1974.
- AMAX Exploration drilled about 53 shallow temperature-gradient holes, 4 intermediate holes and deep test 55-7 (7,001 ft, 1984). It also ran over 500 gravity/magnetic stations, about 50 line-miles of seismic and 2,000 line-km of aeromagnetic survey.
- **AMAX's proprietary data became public when Lightning Dock Geothermal Inc. took the lease in 1986.** This is a direct example of the "data opened when the right changes hands" pattern that Terranox exploits.
- Several small power attempts failed (1982, 1995, 2008). Cyrq Energy (formerly Raser) commissioned a 4 MW plant in Dec 2013 with a long-term PNM PPA and planned a further 6 MW. Cyrq's wells from 2010–12 were 2,349–4,441 ft deep.
- Measured temperatures: producing wells about 99–113 °C; TG52-7 bottom-hole about 137 °C; about 165 °C at about 2 km. Geothermometry suggests up to 250 °C at depth.

**Zanskar era** ("One year at Lightning Dock", Jul 2026):
- Acquired **June 2024**. Price not disclosed. Heatmap describes it only as "an underperforming geothermal power plant".
- Zanskar gives the plant as **about 15 MW gross**. At takeover, the two legacy production wells produced from zones **shallower than about 2,500 ft**. Brine temperature had fallen **about 50 °F in 5 years**, which Zanskar puts at 5–10x the normal 1–2 °F/yr. Plant inlet was **about 255 °F against a design value of about 310 °F**.
- Six months of reservoir modelling with stochastic models and modern imaging concluded that the legacy wells only tapped the top of the reservoir.
- **New production well 44-7**: drilled to **about 8,000 ft**, 3–4x the depth of the legacy wells. It used oil-and-gas PDC bits and directional drilling and was about 35% faster than the fastest legacy well. It flows **more than 4,000 gpm** with a **productivity index of about 50 gpm/psi**. By June 2025 it matched both legacy wells combined and **raised plant inlet temperature by about 60 °F** to about 10 °F above the original design inlet. Zanskar calls it "the most productive pumped geothermal well in the U.S." Its flow is about 3–5x that of the best EGS wells and about 50x "easier" per psi.
- After one year: **no measurable thermal decline**, and net power was back to nameplate. About **75,000 MWh** was delivered to PNM, which is **more than 44,000 MWh (more than 140%) above** a modelled counterfactual for the legacy wells (R² ≈ 0.9). Output is curtailed by a winter transmission cap and summer heat rejection, so the resource now exceeds the plant.
- Well cost: not disclosed.
- Next step: a DOE-backed short horizontal stimulated injection well.

Takeaway: Zanskar's first "discovery" was **re-interpreting a known, depleted field from old public data and deepening it**. A step-up came from one well, at an asset bought cheaply while distressed (price unverified).

## 3. Pumpernickel, Humboldt County, Nevada (greenfield at a "failed" site)

- Announced 18 Sep 2025 as Zanskar's "second major discovery". It was previously known as Hot Springs Ranch / Tipton Ranch. **Oil and gas companies and the federal government drilled exploratory wells there on and off since the 1970s, and none found commercial temperatures** (Heatmap). Nevada Geothermal Power filed a BLM 2-m probe survey there in 2008 (OpenEI, NEPA NVN-086287).
- Campaign: **two exploratory holes**. Hole 1 did not reach target because of hot, unstable ground. **Hole 2 measured a maximum equilibrated ~280 °F at ~2,500 ft MD**, with several total-loss-circulation and artesian zones and correlated damage zones more than 60 ft thick.
- The target before drilling was >230 °F at <3,000 ft. The stochastic model's mean predicted about 240 °F at about 3,000 ft. **The result beat the model's mean.** For comparison, the median Nevada production well inlet is about 292 °F (NDOM 2024).
- Plan: **Phase I is 20 MWe**, which Zanskar calls the minimum economic size, with production wells under 8,000 ft. Heatmap reports a plant targeted for **2028**, and the press release says power within 3 years.
- Zanskar's context claim: in the last 10 years only 1–3 U.S. greenfield sites have had discovery wells of this quality, and only one greenfield plant (Tungsten Mountain) was built.
- Cost: no figures. Zanskar claims it "avoids tens of millions in exploratory drilling costs" and cut discovery cost per MW "by an order of magnitude".

## 4. Big Blind, western Nevada (true blind greenfield)

- Announced 4 Dec 2025 as "the first blind geothermal system discovered by industry in the U.S. in over 30 years" and Zanskar's **third deep discovery in about 12 months**.
- **No surface signs, no earlier geothermal exploration and no earlier well data.** Zanskar spotted the anomaly in **late 2023** with its prospecting toolkit (regional heat-flow models), then **bought the land as BLM leases at competitive auction**. Acreage and price are not disclosed. Next it collected high-resolution data (types unnamed) and permitted two intermediate-depth exploration wells, spudded **July–August 2025**.
- The discovery well, **BGS-67(61-32)-29** (the second hole), reached TD 2,668.5 ft. Maximum equilibrated temperature was **about 250 °F at about 2,700 ft**, with total lost circulation. It showed a 300 ft zone above 225 °F with a gradient of about 100 °C/km, above a 70 ft brecciated and altered zone at about 240 °F, and open fractures confirmed by core and image logs.
- Zanskar says background regional conditions would need well fields around 10,000 ft deep plus stimulation to reach similar temperatures. It also says the system continues deeper to higher temperatures.
- MW: **no MW estimate for Big Blind.** The release only says that "similar natural systems can produce >100 MWe each". The MW figure still needs more exploration wells, full-size wells and long-term flow tests.
- Cost claim: **estimated discovery cost below $15k/MW, against more than $100k/MW** for blind discoveries in the late 1970s and early 1980s. The method is not disclosed. It is presumably total exploration spend divided by expected MW (unverified).
- Pipeline: "dozens of additional hotspots" with similar MW-scale heat-flow signatures.

## 5. Success rate: what the record supports

| Site | Type | Holes disclosed | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lightning Dock 44-7 | Brownfield, deepening | 1 full-size production well | More than 4,000 gpm; inlet up about 60 °F; no decline in 1 year |
| Pumpernickel | Greenfield, previously "failed" | 2 exploration holes (1 did not reach TD) | ~280 °F at ~2,500 ft, permeable |
| Big Blind | Blind greenfield | 2 intermediate holes | ~250 °F at ~2,700 ft, permeable |

- Zanskar publishes **no formal hit rate**. Publicly, it is **3 sites drilled and 3 announced discoveries**, with about 5 disclosed holes. That count could be survivorship: undisclosed dry prospects or TG-hole failures are not public (unverified). Note that hole 1 at Pumpernickel failed mechanically, not geologically.
- Heatmap: test wells have been drilled in only about 5% of the geothermally prospective western U.S. Historical exploratory-well success is described only as "low". For a quantified benchmark, see the IFC/ESMAP data in other evidence files: about 50–60% for first exploration wells globally (unverified here).
- **No discovery has yet been flow-tested at commercial scale or turned into MW on the grid.** The only MWh Zanskar produces are at Lightning Dock (about 15 MW gross). The 100 MW PPA and the "multi-GW" pipeline are still forward-looking.
- Temperatures found so far, about 250–280 °F (121–138 °C), are **moderate**. They suit binary plants and are below the Nevada median production inlet of about 292 °F. Commercial viability depends on hotter fluid at depth, which Zanskar's models predict but which is not yet drilled.

## 6. Costs (what is and is not public)

- Disclosed: under $15k/MW discovery cost (Big Blind estimate), an "order of magnitude" cheaper than historical, and 44-7 drilled about 35% faster than legacy wells.
- Not disclosed: lease bonus bids and acres, cost per hole, the Lightning Dock purchase price, and capex per MW for Phase I.
- Context (unverified, industry rule of thumb): a full-size geothermal well in Nevada costs about $5–10M, and binary capex is about $4–6k/kW. So a 20 MW Phase I is roughly $100–150M. That explains the $40M → $100M development facility and the non-recourse structure.

## 7. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Zanskar already runs the Terranox loop, end to end.** It (a) models regional heat flow from public data, (b) **buys BLM leases at auction before drilling** (Big Blind, late 2023 to 2025), (c) drills cheap 2,500–2,700 ft holes to prove temperature and permeability, and (d) owns and develops, with PPA sell-down to NV Energy. It also bought a distressed brownfield (Lightning Dock) whose public legacy data (AMAX TG holes, deep 55-7, opened in 1986) it re-read. "ML + buy the right cheaply + own the step-up" is therefore **validated, not open**. It is also competed: Zanskar holds $180M of equity, a $100M dev facility, DOE awards and "millions of acres" of exploration footprint.
2. **Where Zanskar is not playing (the gap):**
   - **Asset churn, not build-out.** Zanskar is becoming an IPP. It takes every discovery to a 20 MWe+ plant, needs $100M+ per project, and has a 2028–2030 COD. A Terranox shape could **stop at the proven-resource stage**: sell discovery-grade leases (two holes, ~250–280 °F, permeability shown) to Ormat, Fervo or utilities at a step-up, or keep a royalty or carry. This is closer to a prospect-generator / royalty business in mining. It is capital-light, faster to cycle and avoids project finance. Zanskar's own <$15k/MW discovery cost against the value of a proven, permitted resource shows how large the arbitrage is.
   - **Non-BLM rights.** Zanskar's disclosed discoveries are on BLM leases (Big Blind) or acquisitions. **Private and state geothermal rights** (Texas, Utah and Nevada state lands, and severed private mineral and geothermal estates), plus **oil and gas brownfields** with millions of BHT logs, are a different, more illegible data and title problem. That suits a data-plus-title-diligence company.
   - **Brownfield roll-up of tired small plants.** Lightning Dock shows the playbook: one deep well brought more than 140% extra MWh. The U.S. has other small, declining binary plants and dormant leases from the 1970s–80s exploration wave. A focused acquirer of distressed geothermal assets and leases could repeat this without greenfield risk (inventory of candidates unverified).
   - **Geographies outside the Great Basin.** Zanskar's track record is NV and NM, with a CA prospecting deal. Sedimentary basins (TX, LA, ND, Cheyenne/Denver), the Rio Grande rift and Canada (BC/Alberta/Saskatchewan, where Terranox's assessment-file instincts transfer directly) are under-covered by Zanskar's disclosed activity.
3. **Risks the record shows.** The temperatures discovered are moderate. No greenfield discovery has yet proved commercial flow or MW. Hit rate is unpublished. A Terranox shape that sells at the "discovery" stage must find buyers willing to pay for resources that are not yet flow-tested. That market is thin today (unverified), so a sell-down strategy needs one or two anchor buyers, such as Ormat or Fervo, or data-centre developers seeking sites.
4. **Pitch framing:** "Zanskar proved that AI + cheap leases + two holes finds blind systems at <$15k/MW. They chose to be a vertically integrated utility-scale developer. We are the high-throughput **resource originator**: more prospects, more rights types (private, state, O&G brownfield), sold or farmed out at the proven stage, with Terranox's mineral-rights acquisition engine."
