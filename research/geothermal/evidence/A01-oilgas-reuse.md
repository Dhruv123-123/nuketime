## 1. Who from O&G entered geothermal, and what form the entry took

Pattern: almost every O&G entrant bought **equity in a technology developer** (Fervo, Eavor, Baseload, Quaise, Sage) or sold **services** (rigs, subsurface workflows). None of them visibly runs a systematic programme to **acquire geothermal rights** using its own well data. That gap is the main finding.

## 2. Do O&G lessees already hold the geothermal rights?

### 2.1 Texas: no, by default. Heat follows the **surface** estate (SB 785, 2023)
Enrolled SB 785 (88R) added **Tex. Nat. Res. Code §141.004**:
> "(a) Except as otherwise expressly provided by a conveyance, contract, deed, reservation, exception, limitation, lease, or other binding obligation, the geothermal energy and associated resources below the surface of land are owned as real property by: (1) the landowner; or (2) if the surface estate and the mineral estate of the land have been severed, the owner of the surface estate of the land."

- §141.004(c) says the section does not "change existing law regarding (A) oil, gas, or mineral extraction regardless of its heat or energy potential; (B) the rights of the dominant and servient estates; or (C) the ownership and use of groundwater". It also does not apply to minerals dissolved in hot brines.
- It amended the "by-product" definition to exclude minerals and oil and gas.
- It let title insurers except geothermal from coverage.
- Votes: Senate 30-1, House 140-0 ([SB 785 enrolled](https://capitol.texas.gov/tlodocs/88R/billtext/html/SB00785F.htm); see also R06/R07).

**What it means for lease wording.** A typical Texas O&G lease grants "oil, gas and other minerals" or "oil and gas". Unless the lease or a deed *expressly* conveys geothermal energy or heat, the O&G lessee **does not** hold the geothermal right. It sits with the surface owner. But the mineral estate stays **dominant**, so a geothermal operator must accommodate the O&G lessee's reasonable surface use (§141.004(c)(2)(B)). Co-produced hot fluid from an O&G well stays with the oil/gas or groundwater regime, not geothermal (§141.004(c)(1)-(2)). Net effect: Texas O&G companies own the **data** (logs, BHTs) but generally not the **heat right**. The heat right belongs to about 400k scattered surface owners (R07 estimate) with no price discovery.

### 2.2 North Dakota: statute silent on ownership
- **NDCC ch. 38-19** (Geothermal Resource Development Regulation) defines "geothermal resource" as "the recoverable stored heat of the earth". It assigns permitting to the Industrial Commission and requires logs and cores to be filed with the state geologist. It speaks of protecting "the correlative rights of all owners" but **does not say who owns the heat**.
- §38-19-03 states the chapter "does not apply to any activity regulated under chapters 38-08 [oil and gas]...", so co-produced heat from Bakken/Williston O&G wells falls under O&G jurisdiction ([NDCC 38-19](https://www.ndlegis.gov/cencode/t38c19.pdf)).
- *Inference:* ownership on severed estates is an open question that would be settled by common law and by the wording of each deed and lease. Mineral owners would argue heat is a "mineral", surface owners would argue it is not. No ND case on point was found **(unverified)**.

### 2.3 Oklahoma: not verified
- No primary statute text on geothermal ownership was fetched. Sibling file D02 flags Oklahoma's ownership law as the critical open diligence item. Whether a recent statute assigns heat to the surface or mineral estate is **(unverified)**.
- The default risk is the same as in ND: "oil, gas and other minerals" grant clauses invite mineral-estate claims, so leasing **both** the surface and mineral owners may be needed.

### 2.4 Federal land overlay (relevant in NM, ND, and federal minerals in OK)
- Under **30 U.S.C. §1003(b)(4)**, the holder of a Mineral Leasing Act **oil and gas lease** with an approved permit and active production may get a **noncompetitive geothermal lease**, but only for geothermal energy **co-produced** from an O&G well ([30 USC 1003](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/30/1003)).
- Otherwise federal geothermal is leased **competitively** (§1003(b)(1)). Tracts with no bid open for noncompetitive leasing for 2 years (§1003(c)).
- So on federal land an O&G lessee has a **co-production preference only**. It does not hold standalone EGS or hydrothermal rights.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The O&G majors bought tech equity, not rights.** Devon put in ≥$10M plus a lead role in a $244M round, and BP, Chevron, OMV and Nabors invested in developers. SLB and H&P sell services. None publicly runs "mine our own well archive → lock up geothermal rights → sell the proven asset". The obvious acquirers of a data-to-rights shop (Devon, SLB/Ormat, Fervo, now a public company with acquisition currency) are not doing this step themselves.
2. **In Texas, the data and the right sit with different owners.** O&G operators and the RRC archive hold the logs and BHTs. Since SB 785 the **heat right sits with the surface owner** unless a lease expressly says otherwise. That is the Saskatchewan-assessment-file pattern: public-but-illegible data and a cheap, unpriced, fragmented right held by non-specialists. A Terranox-shape company can lease or option surface geothermal estates from ranchers before proof, and the O&G lessee is not a competing claimant for the heat. The lessee does keep dominant-estate surface accommodation rights, which matters for siting.
3. **Gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar's edge is blind hydrothermal targets in the **data-sparse, federal, Great Basin** setting ("no prior well data" at Big Blind). There, rights come through **public BLM nominations and competitive auctions**, which leak signal. The open lane is the **data-rich, private-rights, sedimentary basins** (Permian, Anadarko, Gulf Coast, Williston), where:
   - (a) there are hundreds of thousands of O&G wells with BHTs to ML-correct;
   - (b) rights are bought privately and quietly (TX surface estates; OK/ND surface + mineral);
   - (c) the buyers are EGS developers (Fervo, Sage, SLB-Ormat), whose economics depend on picking the hottest, most drillable sedimentary or basement sites close to load, Texas data centres in particular.
4. **Ways to monetise the step-up:**
   - (a) sell or JV ranked, leased TX/OK acreage to EGS developers;
   - (b) partner with O&G operators that hold data but not heat rights. Devon is the natural first call given its Fervo tie and its "adjacencies" language;
   - (c) on federal minerals, use the §1003(b)(4) co-production route through O&G partners.
5. **Risks to score on plausibility, not as kills:**
   - ND and OK ownership law is unresolved, so leases may need both estates and title insurers may except geothermal (SB 785 explicitly allows this);
   - sedimentary-basin gradients are lower than the Great Basin's, so the value case depends on EGS;
   - O&G operators could wake up and lease surface estates themselves. Today they show no sign of it.
6. **Diligence to-do:** verify Oklahoma's statute; confirm the Oxy, Equinor, Baker Hughes and Chevron pilot details; sample real Permian and Anadarko lease forms for "heat/geothermal" language; and check whether any Texas operator has begun taking geothermal top-leases from surface owners since 2023.
