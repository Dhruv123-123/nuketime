# R11 — Geothermal tenure in Canada (Alberta, BC, Saskatchewan)

**Question:** Can a Terranox-shape company (ML on public data, then acquire the right cheaply before proving it, then sell the proven state at a step-up) get geothermal rights in western Canada? Is any regime first-come, fixed-fee like a mineral claim?

**Method note:** The shared web-search budget ran out before this agent's first query, so no search was possible. Everything below comes from fetching primary government sources directly: statutes, regulations, information bulletins, sale-result PDFs and the official lease list. Claims not checked against a source are marked (unverified).

---

## 1. Alberta: first-in, first-out, fixed fee. The closest thing to a mineral claim for geothermal

**Framework.** The *Geothermal Resource Development Act*, the Geothermal Resource Tenure Regulation (AR 251/2021) and the Geothermal Resource Development Regulation (AR 250/2021) took effect **January 1, 2022**. The Alberta Energy Regulator (AER) regulates operations under Directive 089. Tenure is administered by Alberta Energy through the Electronic Transfer System (ETS). (https://www.alberta.ca/geothermal-resource-development)

**How you get a lease.** It is **by application, not public offering.** Information Bulletin 2023-02 says: "Applications for a geothermal lease will be handled on a **first-in, first-out basis** and incomplete applications will be rejected. If multiple applications are received for the same rights and locations… the department will work with the proponents of subsequent applications to amend… the original application." There is no auction and no bonus bid. (IB 2023-02: https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/cbf251cf-d1c5-420e-b104-f476c3dc6601/resource/c23dbf89-95d4-4fb8-9779-ddbb4f0cca5d/download/em-mineral-rights-information-bulletin-2023-02.pdf)

**Cost and size (IB 2023-02):**
- Application fee: **$625**, non-refundable.
- Rental: **$3.50 per hectare per year**, with the first year paid on application.
- Maximum lease size: **nine sections**, adjoining laterally or diagonally. That is about 9 × 259 ha ≈ **2,330 ha**, so a full-size lease rents for about **CAD 8,200 a year**. Holding one for the whole 5-year initial term costs about **CAD 41,000** plus the $625 fee.
- Any depth slice of Crown geothermal rights can be requested: surface to basement, to a named zone, from a zone to basement, or specific zones. Only resources below the base of groundwater protection are included.
- Applications must include a project overview, the technology, the target formation, any conflicting agreements (mineral, CCS, etc.) with mitigation, and lessee details. A project narrative is required, but there is no technical-capability scoring.

**Term structure and "use it or lose it":**
- **Initial term: 5 years.** To move into the 5-year intermediate term you need a Geothermal Assessment Report showing **onsite physical work**. The bulletin says explicitly that "desktop studies; re-interpretation of public geoscience or technical studies" do **not** count. Activities that do count include drilling, re-completing or deepening existing wells, testing, logging, surface infrastructure, and fluid sampling with bottom-hole temperatures.
- **Intermediate term: 5 years.** You must show the lease is *productive* (actually generating heat or power) to get an **indefinite continued term**.
- Extensions are discretionary and limited to "extenuating circumstances". Business reasons such as a late farm-in do not qualify.
- **Transfers are allowed**: total, partial, division and consolidation under the Mines and Minerals Administration Regulation. Surrender is allowed at any time.
- Alberta Energy can see non-confidential AER well, production and test data. It treats Geothermal Assessment Reports as confidential.

**Uptake (official lease list XLSX, fetched 2026-10-21):** only **31 geothermal leases** exist province-wide, held by **6 designated representatives**:
- Renewable Geo Resources Ltd.: 16 leases, "to base Leduc", west of Edmonton
- Sprocket Energy Corp.: 8, "to base Beaverhill Lake"
- 1212843 B.C. Ltd.: 3, "below base Wabamun", Twp 67–70 W6
- Novus Earth Energy: 2
- Zest Resources: 1
- Summation Energy Technology: 1

At most about 72,000 ha is leased in total. (https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/energy-list-of-geothermal-leases.xlsx; map: https://gis.energy.gov.ab.ca/GeoView/geothermal)

**Read-through:** Alberta is a fixed-price, first-come land grab, almost untouched. Leases cost about CAD 3.50/ha/yr. One holder has already tried a Terranox-style move by chaining 16 nine-section leases along the Leduc reef trend. The catch is the 5-year physical-work test, which caps how long anyone can sit on cheap paper. Re-entering or logging an existing oil and gas well is listed as qualifying work, though. Alberta has a very large inventory of legacy wells; a count near 450–600k is (unverified). So the "work" can be cheap: re-log or flow-test an orphan or suspended well rather than drill a new one.

**Open points.** Under the Mines and Minerals Act amendments, the Crown appears to own geothermal resources in some form. How this interacts with freehold mineral title, roughly 19% of Alberta's mineral rights, is (unverified) and needs the Act text. The pace of ETS processing is also (unverified).

## 2. British Columbia: sealed-bid tender with experience scoring. Not first-come, and nearly dormant

**Framework.** *Geothermal Resources Act*, RSBC 1996 c.171 (https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/96171_01). The Act covers resources above 80°C produced at surface through a well.
- s.2: all geothermal resources are vested in the government.
- **s.5(5): the minister "must not issue a permit except by public tender"**, with BC Gazette notice at least two weeks before.
- Permits last **1 year**, renewable up to **7 times** (8 years in total).
- Maximum permit size is one "block" (s.6).
- A **20-year lease** (s.8) goes only to a permit holder who has **drilled a geothermal well** and filed a satisfactory development plan.

**Process.** (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/electricity-alternative-energy/renewable-energy/geothermal-energy/exploration-and-sales)
1. Anyone can *request* a parcel.
2. The Ministry runs a **pre-tenure referral** to agencies, local governments and **First Nations**, and the results can become tenure caveats.
3. The parcel is offered by **sealed-bid public competition**.

**Bids are not cash-only.** Geothermal Information Letter GIL-13-01 sets out a points system. (https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/farming-natural-resources-and-industry/electricity-alternative-energy/geothermal/geothermal_bids_information_letter.pdf)
- **Suitability: 6 points, 50% weight.** Covers the draft exploration plan, P.Geo/P.Eng certification, and applicant experience. Up to 3 points require experience with an *operating* geothermal development or multiple drilling programs.
- **Prior exploration work on the parcel: 3 points, 25%.** One point each for remote sensing, field work, and lab analysis of water, rock or biological samples.
- **Bid amount: 3 points, 25%**, scored pro-rata to the highest bid.

A new entrant with ML but no drilling track record therefore starts behind incumbents, though the remote-sensing point does reward desktop data work.

**Holding costs** (Geothermal Resources General Regulation, BC Reg 39/2017: https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/39_2017):
- Permit: $500 fee plus **$500 a year rent**.
- Work requirement: **$5/ha in year 1, rising to $25/ha in year 8.** Cash in lieu is allowed with ministerial approval.
- Lease: $200 fee plus **$10/ha a year**.
- **Data release (s.11):**
  - Geothermal reports become public **2 years after a permit terminates**, or 5 years after conversion to a lease.
  - Well data and cores become public **2 years after rig release**.

  This is a BC version of Saskatchewan's mandatory-disclosure rule that Terranox relies on: failed permits feed a public archive.

**Actual tender results** (official sale-result PDFs; index: https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/electricity-alternative-energy/renewable-energy/geothermal-energy/exploration-and-sales/geothermal-permit-sales):

| Sale | Parcels | Hectares | Price/ha | Bonus total | Buyers |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oct 26, 2010 | 3 | 11,848 | avg $2.67 | $31,655 | Borealis GeoPower |
| Jul 26, 2011 | 3 | 8,056 | $3.05 / $4.05 / **$65.00** (avg $27.25) | $219,552 | Salal Geothermal; 2149749 Ontario Inc. ($202,410 for 3,114 ha) |
| Jan 21, 2014 | 1 | 2,865 | $33.73 | $96,635 (total $100,000) | LL Geothermal Inc. |

The government page lists only **seven sales ever** (2002, 2002, 2004, 2010, 2010, 2011, 2014), with **none after January 2014**. Whether sales happened after 2014 under a different process, or the page is simply stale, is (unverified). The newest information letter, GIL-24-01 (2024), deals only with delegation of ministerial authority.

**Read-through:** BC prices were very low ($1.80–$65/ha in bonus), but tenure is lumpy, discretionary, gated by First Nations referral, and scored on experience. It is not a land-grab venue, and the volcanic-belt targets (Meager, Garibaldi, Lakelse/Kitselas) are known and politically sensitive.

## 3. Saskatchewan: no dedicated geothermal tenure found. Geothermal is a project approval plus water rights

- The **Subsurface Mineral Tenure Regulations (C-50.2 Reg 30, 2015 consolidation)** contain **no mention of geothermal**. They govern potash, salt, brine minerals and helium.
  - Permit rents: $2/ha a year for years 1–5, then $5/ha.
  - Lease rents: $10/ha initial, $20/ha on renewal.
  - Fees: lease application $500, transfer $250.
  - (https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/72797/formats/81163/download)
- Geothermal goes through **IRIS as a "Geothermal Project" application under Storage**, introduced March 2018, to get a Ministry authorization before a well licence. (https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/89268/formats/106087/download)
- The application form requires:
  - maps of surface and **mineral owners** and operators;
  - **mineral owner consents** where injecting onto others' mineral lands;
  - a **Ground Water Investigation Permit or Water Rights Licence** from the Water Security Agency for "using the ground water for geothermal energy extraction".
  - (Form: https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/89312/formats/106134/download)
- In practice, the "right" in Saskatchewan is **water rights plus land access plus regulatory approval**, not a Crown disposition you can stake. Whether a dedicated geothermal tenure was created after 2019 is (unverified); none appeared in the publications catalogue, where only the 2018/2019 project-application items mention geothermal.
- **Adjacent tenure is relevant for co-production:**
  - **Brine minerals (lithium)** go by **sealed-bid public offering**, three a year. The 2026 offerings are Feb 9, Jul 13 and Nov 23 (https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/126699/formats/148790/download).
  - **Helium** goes by **application** (first-come).
  - Since Oct 16, 2023, helium and brine tenure can overlap in the same horizon (Bulletin BT2023-11: https://publications.saskatchewan.ca/api/v1/products/122226/formats/141797/download).
  - A Williston-basin geothermal developer such as DEEP Earth Energy (Estevan area) therefore sits among lithium and helium holders. DEEP's own rights structure is (unverified).

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Alberta is the only Canadian venue where Terranox's playbook transfers directly.** Tenure is first-come and fixed-price at CAD 3.50/ha/yr, with no auction to bid away the information edge. That is cheaper than BLM non-competitive leasing, at about USD 1–3/acre/yr, which is roughly CAD 3.50–10/ha (BLM figures unverified, see the BLM evidence files). A model that ranks Alberta's legacy-well bottom-hole temperatures could, in principle, lock up the 10–30 best nine-section blocks for under CAD 300k a year. Only 31 leases and 6 holders exist today, so the land is open.

2. **The constraint is the 5-year physical-work test, not price.** Desktop re-interpretation explicitly does not count. A Terranox-style holder must therefore do one of the following within 5 years:
   - (a) sell or farm-out the lease, since transfers are allowed and the buyer inherits the clock;
   - (b) do cheap qualifying work, such as re-entering or logging existing wells or flow-testing with bottom-hole temperatures (Alberta's orphan and suspended well stock is a cost-free source of boreholes, unverified count);
   - (c) partner with a well owner.

   This suits "acquire → de-risk with one cheap physical step → sell", but it rules out passive land banking.

3. **The resource is different from Zanskar's.** Alberta and Saskatchewan geothermal is mostly **sedimentary-basin, moderate temperature**: Leduc/Wabamun/Beaverhill Lake carbonates and the Deadwood/Winnipeg in the Williston, around 60–130°C (range unverified). It suits direct-use heat, low-temperature ORC power, and **co-produced lithium brine**. Zanskar's edge is blind *hydrothermal* systems in the Great Basin, with high-enthalpy power sold under US PPAs. **The gap Zanskar is not pursuing** (as far as public information shows, unverified) is the *legacy-oil-well-data* basin play in Canada. That play has dense, mandatory-disclosed well logs and bottom-hole temperatures, first-come tenure, and stacked value from heat plus lithium plus (in SK) helium. It is the most Terranox-like data situation available: public, illegible, abundant.

4. **Scale caveat ("too small" test).** Alberta sedimentary geothermal is real but lower-grade. Exit buyers would be few for now: Eavor (Alberta closed-loop), Novus Earth, Alberta No. 1 and the oil and gas operators repurposing wells (names from general knowledge, unverified), not Fervo or Ormat. Upside comes from heat offtake (greenhouses, data centres, district heat) and from lithium optionality. On its own, Canada is probably a **secondary geography** to a US BLM-led company, valuable as a cheap, uncontested land bank and a data moat, not as the main market.

5. **BC and Saskatchewan are poor fits.**
   - **BC:** tender scoring rewards drilling experience, processes are dormant (no sale since 2014), and First Nations referral is required. BC's **2-year post-termination release of geothermal reports and well data** does create a Terranox-style archive (Meager Creek and similar), but the land is hard to buy.
   - **Saskatchewan:** there is no stakeable geothermal right. Control comes through water licences, land access and adjacent lithium or helium tenure. Helium is first-come, and brine is auctioned three times a year.

**Bottom line:** Alberta offers a genuine first-come, fixed-fee, transferable geothermal lease at about CAD 3.50/ha/yr, and almost nobody holds one. It is the cleanest Terranox analogue in Canada, but the 5-year physical-work rule forces a flip or cheap-work strategy. Canada works best as a complementary, low-competition sedimentary-basin, legacy-well-data play alongside a US hydrothermal core, which is a niche Zanskar does not appear to target.
