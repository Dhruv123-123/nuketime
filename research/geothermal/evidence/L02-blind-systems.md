# L02 — Blind / hidden hydrothermal systems in the Great Basin: how many, how found, what share is blind

Literature review for "Terranox for geothermal". Question: is there a large stock of undiscovered conventional hydrothermal systems in the Great Basin, and has it historically been found by accident in public or semi-public drilling data? Both matter for a find-it-then-lock-the-land model.

---

## 1. Primary sources reviewed

### S1. Williams, Reed, Mariner, DeAngelo & Galanis (2008). *Assessment of Moderate- and High-Temperature Geothermal Resources of the United States.* USGS Fact Sheet 2008-3082.
This is the standard national number. Probabilistic volumetric assessment of 248 identified systems in 13 states, plus GIS weights-of-evidence favorability models (28 spatial models) for undiscovered resources. Table 1:

| | F95 | F50 | Mean | F5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Identified, all US (N=248) | 3,675 | 8,356 | **9,057 MWe** | 16,457 |
| Undiscovered, all US | 7,917 | 23,739 | **30,033 MWe** | 73,286 |
| EGS, all US | 345,100 | 507,000 | 517,800 | 727,900 |
| Nevada identified (N=56) | 515 | 1,216 | 1,391 | 2,551 |
| Nevada undiscovered | 996 | 3,243 | **4,364** | 11,507 |
| California undiscovered | 3,256 | 9,532 | 11,340 | 25,439 |
| Idaho undiscovered | 427 | 1,391 | 1,872 | 4,937 |
| Oregon undiscovered | 432 | 1,406 | 1,893 | 4,991 |
| Utah undiscovered | 334 | 1,088 | 1,464 | 3,860 |

Key results:
- Undiscovered resources are about 3.3 times the identified ones.
- "A larger fraction of the undiscovered geothermal resources are located outside California." The Geysers inflates California's identified total, and states other than California and Nevada have had little exploration.
- Regions with high favorability but few identified systems are northeastern Nevada, western Utah, southern Idaho, eastern Oregon, and parts of New Mexico and Colorado. These are greenfield areas.
- The estimate covers "private and accessible public lands" only and is valued over 30 years of production.
- Nevada alone: about 4.4 GWe mean undiscovered against about 1.4 GWe identified, so roughly 3.1 times.
Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2008/3082 (PDF: https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2008/3082/pdf/fs2008-3082.pdf)

### S2. Williams, Reed, DeAngelo & Galanis (2009). "Quantifying the undiscovered geothermal resources of the United States." *GRC Transactions* v.33.
This is the methods paper behind S1's 30,033 MWe. The undiscovered estimate rests on four things: how similar undiscovered systems are to identified ones, the history and spatial coverage of exploration, the probability that standard techniques would succeed, and limits on how systems form. The ratio method assumes poorly explored ground holds systems like the explored ground does. Without saying so directly, this is an argument that the undiscovered stock depends on exploration coverage, and better data coverage finds more of it.
Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70158979

### S3. Coolbaugh, Raines, Zehner, Shevenell & Williams (2006). "Prediction and discovery of new geothermal resources in the Great Basin: Multiple evidence of a large undiscovered resource base." *GRC Transactions* v.30, 867-873.
This is the most direct count of undiscovered systems.
- Method: a weights-of-evidence model of known system locations, combined with a fuzzy-logic "degree of exploration" model. The exploration model is built from water-table depth, the carbonate aquifer, and the location, depth and type of drill holes.
- Result for Nevada: **157 undiscovered systems at ≥~100 °C, which is 3.2 times the 69 known systems**. At that time only 9 of the 69 known systems produced power. The model projects that **59 known plus undiscovered systems could produce electricity economically, more than 6 times the producing count then.**
- Supporting discoveries: at least 2-3 new systems at ≥~150 °C on the Pyramid Lake Paiute Reservation; 3 blind systems near the borate playas at Rhodes, Teels and Columbus Marshes; and **at least 4 new discoveries from gold-exploration drilling, including McGinness Hills (~200 °C).**
- Relevance: the degree-of-exploration model uses drill-hole location, depth and type, so existing drilling archives were already treated as the key covariate. This is the closest academic analogue to the Terranox method.
Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70028672

### S4. Faulds, Hinz, Coolbaugh, Ayling, Glen, Craig, et al. (2019/2020). *Discovering Blind Geothermal Systems in the Great Basin Region: An Integrated Geologic and Geophysical Approach for Establishing Geothermal Play Fairways — All Phases.* DOE final report DE-EE0006731 (OSTI 1724080).
This is the flagship DOE play-fairway study. Hard numbers from the report text:
- "~39% of the **known** systems are blind (Faulds and Hinz, 2015), and estimates suggest that **~75% of all** geothermal resources in the region are blind (Coolbaugh et al., 2007)."
- "**Nearly 90%** of the known geothermal systems in the region are hosted by only four major structural settings": horse-tailing fault terminations, step-overs or relay ramps, fault intersections, and accommodation zones.
- At the time, the Great Basin hosted about 24 power plants with more than 550 MW of capacity, set against an estimated potential of about 30,000 MW (Faulds et al., 2010). Nameplate capacity was under 600 MW.
- "**Most of the known blind systems were discovered** through regional gradient drilling programs (e.g., Desert Peak) **or by accident** during the drilling of agricultural wells (e.g., Stillwater) **or mineral exploration holes (e.g., McGinness Hills, Tungsten Mountain, Blue Mountain).**"
- The study area was 96,000 km² (400 × 240 km). Ten parameters were combined into a fairway map and tested against **34 benchmark systems ≥130 °C**. The map contains about 375 favorable structural settings, covering about 12% of the area (over 11,000 km²). Phase 2 picked 24 prospects and studied 5 in detail. Phase 3 drilled temperature-gradient (TG) holes at 2 sites.
- Data audit: the well database for the transect held **47,263 well records, and only about 16-17% report both temperature and depth**. The SMU heat-flow set was 59% complete; the Nevada State Engineer water-well records were only 10% complete.
- "More than 50% of the Great Basin region consists of Neogene basins," and the basin fill hides structures. Basin systems, such as those in the Carson Sink, are the most under-explored.
- Permeability, more than heat, is the binding constraint: it "can vary significantly over distances of 100-200 m."
Source: https://gdr.openei.org/files/756/Faulds-DE-EE0006731-Report-v2.pdf ; USGS record https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70221765

### S5. Faulds et al. (2017). DOE GTO Peer Review slides, "Discovering Blind Geothermal Systems in the Great Basin Region."
- "Hidden or blind systems most common." Hot springs sitting directly above an upflow are uncommon. Most springs mark outflow, and "drilling [on them] usually results in non-productive well."
- Gabbs Valley was "new discovery — blind system, no previous exploration." It had a 124-station 2 m temperature survey and geothermometry of about 135 °C.
- The industry partners were Ormat (Gabbs and Granite Springs) and U.S. Geothermal (Crescent Valley, cost-share). One line bears directly on the gap: **"Full cost-sharing on drilling difficult for industry to justify without secure leases."**
- McGinness Hills is cited as having "88 MW new power plants" and as evidence of "more McGinness Hills discoveries" to come.
Source: https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2017/12/f46/3_2_Play%20Fairway_Nevada%20Bureau%20of%20Mines%20and%20Geology_Discovering%20Blind%20Geothermal_Presentation.pdf

### S6. Faulds, Craig, Hinz, Coolbaugh, Glen, Earney, Schermerhorn, Peacock, Deoreo & Siler (2018). "Discovery of a blind geothermal system in southern Gabbs Valley, western Nevada, through application of the play fairway analysis at multiple scales." *GRC Transactions* v.42.
- The target was a set of fault intersections in a displacement-transfer zone just north of the Petrified Springs dextral fault, identified from gravity, magnetics and MT.
- **Six TG holes reached up to 124 °C at 152 m. The system is blind, with no hot springs, fumaroles or paleo-sinter.** The companion site in Granite Springs Valley produced 6 TG holes at about 96 °C at about 250 m and is interpreted as a hidden system.
- Phase 3 cost about $1.5M in DOE money. Faulds called the two successes "of perhaps hundreds of potential sites" (UNR Nevada Today, 2019).
- Takeaway: public-data favorability plus cheap shallow TG holes found 2 blind systems in 2 attempts. No public record shows anyone advancing them to production, which is consistent with the lease problem in S5. (Current lease status of Gabbs Valley and Granite Springs: unverified.)
Sources: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70201803 ; https://www.unr.edu/nevada-today/news/2019/geothermal-success ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/?p=32198

### S7. Faulds et al. (2012), New Zealand Geothermal Workshop / DOE structural-controls work, and Faulds et al. (2017) Stanford Geothermal Workshop paper.
- Inventory of **250+ Great Basin geothermal fields**. Step-overs and relay ramps are the most common favorable setting at **~32%**; normal/strike-slip fault intersections and normal-fault tip-lines are about **22% each**.
- The 2017 Stanford paper: ">85%" of systems, especially those over 130 °C, sit in fault-interaction zones.
- This gives a compact, learnable target. Hot systems cluster in a small number of geometric settings, so structural features derived from public fault maps, LiDAR, gravity and earthquake catalogs should carry real predictive weight.
Sources: https://gdr.openei.org/files/383/Faulds%20et%20al%202012%20GeoNZ%20Paper.pdf ; https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2017/Faulds.pdf ; https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2014/02/f7/tracers_faulds_structural_controls.pdf

### S8. Mordensky, Burns, Lipor & DeAngelo (2025). "Favorability mapping for hydrothermal power resource assessments of the Great Basin, USA." *Geothermics* (doi:10.1016/j.geothermics.2025.103450).
This is the USGS machine-learning update toward replacing the 2008 assessment. It uses 16 input features and Monte Carlo cross-validation (n=1200).
- Benchmark: **28 power-producing systems in the Great Basin, 9 of them hidden (32%) and 19 exposed.**
- **15 of 28 fall in the 99th percentile of favorability, and 26 of 28 (93%) fall above the 80th percentile.** Earlier maps put only about half above the 80th.
- It predicts hidden and exposed systems "equally well": 5 of 9 hidden and 10 of 19 exposed land in the top percentile.
- Implication: public-data ML can already rank blind power-grade systems near the top of favorability. Public USGS maps, and the data release (doi:10.5066/P14EET2C), are commoditising regional screening. The edge has to come from finer, proprietary data plus acting on land faster.
Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70270953

### S9. USGS (2025). *Enhanced Geothermal Systems Electric-Resource Assessment for the Great Basin.* Fact Sheet 2025-3027.
- The best estimate is **135 GWe of EGS in the upper 6 km**, which is only about 0.5% of the accessible electric-grade resource base. This is EGS, not hydrothermal; the hydrothermal update is still pending as of these sources.
- It matters as context. It sets the outer bound and shows where the Fervo-type buyers are headed. It also shows that USGS has not yet revised the 30 GWe undiscovered-hydrothermal figure.
Source: https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20253027

### S10. Discovery case histories (company filings and press)
- **McGinness Hills (Ormat, Lander County, NV).** Ormat acquired the federal lease in Aug 2007. "Prior to that, no geothermal leasing had occurred in the area, and all historical exploration drilling was related to metallurgical mineral interests" (Ormat 10-K). There are no modern thermal manifestations, only about 0.25 sq mi of relict sinter. The reservoir covers at least 3 sq mi at an average of 335 °F, with production from 2,000-5,000 ft. Capacity grew from 72 MW (2012/2015) to 138 MW (Phase 3, Dec 2018) to about 160 MW (2021), which Ormat calls the largest geothermal complex in Nevada. **This is the canonical Terranox-shape case: gold-exploration holes revealed heat, the lease was picked up cheaply as the first-ever geothermal lease in the area, and the field became about 160 MW.** Sources: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000143774916026139/ora20151231_10k.htm ; https://investor.ormat.com/news-events/news/news-details/2021/Ormat-Completes-McGinness-Hills-Complex-Expansion/default.aspx ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/48-mw-mcginness-hills-phase-3-geothermal-plant-starts-operation-in-nevada/
- **Tungsten Mountain (Ormat, Churchill County).** A greenfield 24 MW single-OEC plant reached COD on 1 Dec 2017 under a 26-year PPA with SCPPA and LADWP. It was later described as 26 MW. S4 lists it as found through mineral exploration holes. Source: https://investor.ormat.com/news-events/news/news-details/2017/24-MW-Tungsten-Mountain-Geothermal-Power-Plant-in-Nevada-Begins-Commercial-Operation/default.aspx
- **Blue Mountain (Humboldt County).** Nassau Ltd gold exploration ran from 1984 to 1990, and mineral holes hit hot water in the early 1990s. Noramex then took geothermal leases in the mid-1990s. DOE cost-shared Deep Blue No.1 in 2002, which reached 144.7 °C at 672 m. The plant is 49.5 MW nameplate and came online in 2009. **This is a second clean case of a junior acquiring the right after hot mineral holes and before proof.** Source: https://openei.org/wiki/Blue_Mountain_Geothermal_Area ; https://www.osti.gov/biblio/810407
- **Desert Peak** was found by regional gradient drilling; **Stillwater** by agricultural wells (S4).
- **Patua (Cyrq, 48 MW, 2012).** I found no primary source calling Patua blind, and Hazen-area hot springs lie nearby (unverified). Do not cite it as a blind discovery.
- **Big Blind (Zanskar, western NV, announced 4 Dec 2025).** No surface manifestations, no prior geothermal exploration or well data. Two intermediate-depth wells drilled Jul-Aug 2025 found a permeable reservoir at about 250 °F (121 °C) at about 2,700 ft. Capacity is unconfirmed; Zanskar says analogous systems produce "over 100 MWe." Zanskar calls it the first blind system found and confirmed commercial by industry in the US in over 30 years. The claim rests on a strict definition, since McGinness Hills (lease 2007) and Tungsten Mountain had mineral-hole hints. Source: https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-reveals-big-blind-the-discovery ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/zanskar-announces-discovery-of-big-blind-geothermal-site-in-western-nevada-usa/
- **Zanskar context.** Series C of $115M led by Spring Lane, bringing total equity to $180M. The pipeline is six 20 MW plants targeted for COD in 3-4 years. The company owns Lightning Dock in NM and discovered Pumpernickel in NV in Sep 2025. Co-founder Hoiland thinks the true undiscovered resource is at least 10 times USGS's 30 GW. Faulds told CNN that more than three-quarters of US geothermal resources are blind and that better methods could unlock "many tens and perhaps hundreds of gigawatts in the western US." Sources: https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/armed-with-115-million-geothermal-startup-zanskar-gets-ready-to-build/ ; https://ktvz.com/cnn-other/2025/12/12/a-startup-discovered-a-hidden-source-of-abundant-clean-energy-and-did-it-in-an-unusual-way/

## 2. Synthesis: how big is the blind stock?

| Metric | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| US undiscovered hydrothermal, mean | 30.0 GWe (F95 7.9, F5 73.3) | S1 |
| Undiscovered : identified (US) | 3.3 : 1 | S1/S2 |
| Nevada undiscovered, mean | 4.4 GWe (F5 11.5) | S1 |
| Nevada undiscovered systems ≥100 °C | 157, against 69 known (3.2x) | S3 |
| Share of known GB systems that are blind | ~39% | S4 citing Faulds & Hinz 2015 |
| Share of GB power-producing systems that are hidden | 9 of 28 = 32% | S8 |
| Share of all GB resources estimated blind | ~75% | S4 citing Coolbaugh 2007 |
| Known systems in four structural settings | ~90% | S4 |
| Known GB fields catalogued | 250+ | S7 |
| GB installed capacity versus potential | ~550-600 MW against ~30,000 MW | S4 |
| Well records with usable temperature and depth | ~16-17% of 47,263 | S4 |

The literature agrees on three things:
1. Most of the remaining resource is blind, roughly 3 undiscovered systems for every known one.
2. Most blind systems found so far were accidents of non-geothermal drilling (mineral, agricultural, gradient). Those holes sit in public or semi-public archives that are poorly digitised.
3. The physics is constrained: about 90% of systems sit in 4 structural settings, which a model can learn.

The figure that would make or break the business, how many blind systems are ≥130 °C and ≥20 MWe, is not pinned down. S3's estimate of "59 economic systems" in Nevada, against 9 producing in 2006, is the best primary anchor. That implies roughly 30-50 economic blind systems remain in Nevada alone. This is my inference from S3, not a published figure.

## 3. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The asset stock is large enough.** About 30 GWe of undiscovered US hydrothermal resource (mean) lies mostly in blind Great Basin systems. Nevada alone holds 4.4 GWe and about 157 systems. At an illustrative $1-3M per proven MW in a sale to Ormat or Fervo (unverified; covered by other evidence files), even a 1-2% capture means hundreds of MW and nine-figure asset value. "Too small" does not kill the idea.
2. **The historical discovery mechanism is exactly Terranox's.** McGinness Hills (about 160 MW), Tungsten Mountain (24-26 MW) and Blue Mountain (49.5 MW) all started from **mineral-exploration holes**. In each case a geothermal player then picked up the lease cheaply before proof: Ormat's first-ever lease in the McGinness area in 2007, and Noramex's leases at Blue Mountain. Coolbaugh (2006) counts at least 4 gold-drilling discoveries. Nevada's mining drill-hole archives (NBMG, BLM mining claim and Notice/Plan filings, company assessment-type data) are the direct counterpart to the Saskatchewan assessment database. Turning those archives into usable form is the wedge: only about 17% of 47k well records even pair temperature with depth.
3. **The gap the literature itself names is land, not science.** DOE play fairway found 2 blind systems in 2 tries for about $1.5M of TG drilling, yet the slides record that industry would not cost-share "without secure leases." Science identified the prospects, but nobody owned them. A company whose core competency is **systematically locking BLM, state and private geothermal rights ahead of disclosure** fills that hole. That is an ownership business, not a tool.
4. **Versus Zanskar.** Zanskar is vertically integrated: it raised $180M, owns Lightning Dock, and is building six 20 MW plants. That makes it a developer and operator that drills its own wells and builds plants, and its capital is tied up in a few sites. It does not pursue three things:
   - (a) A broad, cheap **land bank** across hundreds of ML-ranked cells.
   - (b) **Selling proven-state rights** to Ormat, Fervo, Cyrq or data-centre offtakers instead of building.
   - (c) A focus on **mining-archive data**, rather than regional heat-flow and geophysics models.
   The Terranox shape is "prospect generator / royalty-and-land company," the model of mining juniors such as Franco-Nevada and EMX applied to geothermal. Zanskar's "dozens to hundreds of new sites" thesis implies more sites than one developer can build, so a land-first company could even sell to Zanskar.
5. **Risks the literature flags.**
   - USGS's ML favorability (S8) is public and already ranks hidden systems in the 99th percentile, so the edge from regional screening is shrinking. The moat must be proprietary data plus speed on land.
   - Permeability, not heat, is the binding risk: it changes over 100-200 m. Shallow TG holes prove heat cheaply but not flow. Step-up value before a flow test may be modest; Big Blind and Gabbs Valley reached 96-124 °C at under 1 km.
   - BLM competitive lease sales are public, so nominating parcels can reveal targets (to be checked in a separate file).
   - Several of the most-cited "blind share" figures (39%, 75%) trace back to conference papers I could not retrieve.
