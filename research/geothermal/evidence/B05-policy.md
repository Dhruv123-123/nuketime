## 1. Bottom line

1. **Yes: geothermal kept 45Y/48E about six years longer than wind and solar.** OBBBA ends 45Y and 48E for wind and solar placed in service after 31 Dec 2027, unless construction began by 4 Jul 2026. For everything else, geothermal included, it fixes the 45Y/48E "applicable year" at **2032**. Under the existing 45Y(d)(2) schedule that gives:
   - **100% credit for construction begun through end-2033**;
   - 75% for 2034;
   - 50% for 2035;
   - **0% from 2036**.

   There is no placed-in-service deadline, which matters for geothermal's 7–10-year build cycle. OBBBA also added geothermal income to the publicly traded partnership (MLP) qualifying-income list (§70524). That is a new financing route for owned geothermal assets.
2. **Washington now treats geothermal as "energy" alongside oil, gas and uranium.** EO 14156 lists "geothermal heat" as an energy resource for the national energy emergency. Interior's emergency NEPA alternative arrangements therefore cover geothermal: an EA in about 14 days and an EIS in about 28 days. BLM now must hold **annual** lease sales, offer at least 90% of nominations and grant no deferrals (IM 2026-004). New CXs also cover geothermal exploration and resource-confirmation drilling up to 20 acres. Meanwhile wind and solar lost their credits.
3. **DOE geothermal R&D was protected, not cut.** The FY2026 request raised Geothermal Technologies from **$118M (FY2024 enacted) to $150M (+27%)**. Over the same request, EERE was cut 74% and solar and wind went to **$0**. Geothermal moved into a new "Hydrocarbons and Geothermal Energy Office" (HGEO), alongside fossil energy. In September 2026 DOE selected 21 projects (**up to $99M**) for EGS field tests and exploration drilling. It also launched a **$300k GEMS Prize** for algorithms that find "hidden geothermal systems" from fault datasets.
4. **The real risk is capacity, not policy.** GAO (Sept 2026) found:
   - nominations rose from about 25,800 acres (2020) to **more than 1M acres (2025)**;
   - drilling-permit approvals rose from **7 (2019) to 68 (2024)**;
   - BLM has no permit-tracking system, and its handbooks have been in draft since 2022.

   Per R14's reading of the full report, one Nevada office is "60 percent vacant". The bottleneck has moved from statute to staff. Other risks: the credit cliff in 2034–36, foreign-entity-of-concern (FEOC) material-assistance rules, and a much more crowded, expensive auction market.

## 2. Federal tax credits after OBBBA (P.L. 119-21, signed 4 Jul 2025)

**Primary text (govinfo, parsed):**

- **§70512(a), 45Y.** It adds 45Y(d)(4): "This section shall not apply with respect to any applicable facility placed in service after December 31, 2027." An "applicable facility" is one that uses **wind** (§45(d)(1)) or **solar** (§45(d)(4)). The same section sets "applicable year" to "**calendar year 2032**."
- **§70512(l)(4).** The wind and solar termination applies only to facilities whose construction begins **more than 12 months after enactment**, that is, after 4 Jul 2026.
- **§70513(a), 48E.** It sets the same wind/solar termination for property placed in service after 31 Dec 2027, with an exception for storage.
- **The geothermal reading, from the NLR 2025 Market Report:**
  - 45Y: "geothermal projects utilizing Section 45Y can have a later placed-in service deadline, effectively extending the PTC for geothermal facilities through 2036 … The full credit is available for facilities that begin construction by the end of 2033, with a phased down credit beginning in 2034 and is repealed in 2036."
  - 48E: "the credit will also be available for facilities with construction commencing by the end of 2033; it will be phased down beginning in 2034 and repealed in 2036."
- **Credit size (NLR, 26 USC 45Y/48E).**
  - The PTC is 0.3 ¢/kWh base and **1.5 ¢/kWh** with prevailing wage and apprenticeship, before bonuses.
  - The ITC is **30%**, plus domestic-content and energy-community adders.
- **Begin-construction rules.** IRS Notice 2025-42 (Aug 2025, implementing EO 14315) tightened begin-construction tests and removed the 5% safe harbor. It is titled and scoped to "**Applicable Wind and Solar Facilities**", and its 5% safe-harbor carve-back covers only solar of 1.5 MW or less. Geothermal is not an "applicable facility", so it keeps the pre-existing Physical Work Test and the 5% safe harbor (my reading of the notice's scope; confirm with tax counsel).
- **FEOC restrictions (§70512(b), §70513(b)).** For construction beginning after **31 Dec 2025**, a facility is not a qualified facility if it includes "material assistance from a prohibited foreign entity". Taxpayers that are specified foreign entities or foreign-influenced entities get no credit. There is also a 10-year recapture for "applicable payments" to prohibited foreign entities (§50(a)(4)).
  - The material-assistance cost ratio thresholds for qualified facilities step up from 40% (2026) to 45% (2027), 50% (2028), 55% (2029) and **60% from 2030** (§70512, parsed).
  - Geothermal's main imports are binary ORC turbines (Ormat makes them in Reno and Israel, unverified), pumps and steel casing. Its FEOC exposure is much lower than solar's or batteries' (qualitative; unverified).
- **MLP status (§70524).** It amends §7704(d)(1)(E) to add income from "geothermal energy" to MLP qualifying income. Owned geothermal assets can therefore sit in a tax-advantaged, yield-oriented vehicle the way oil and gas royalties do.
- **Cost recovery (§70509).** It strikes 168(e)(3)(B)(vi)(I), the 5-year MACRS route tied to §48 energy property, for construction beginning after 2024. Property qualifying under 45Y/48E keeps the IRA-added 5-year class via the re-designated subclause, as I read it (**unverified; tax-counsel item**). §70301 separately restores full expensing (bonus depreciation) (title parsed; scope not analysed).
- **Other incentives unchanged.** Transferability (§6418) was preserved, with FEOC limits on transferees (Sidley, cited in the sibling nuclear file). Intangible drilling cost (IDC) expensing under §263(c) has always covered geothermal wells; OBBBA §70523 only changes how IDC enters the corporate AMT (title parsed).

**Practical meaning.** A geothermal prospect that reaches "begin construction" by **31 Dec 2033** locks in the full credit and has no placed-in-service deadline. The value of a confirmed geothermal right rises because the buyer's credit is still alive. Wind and solar competitors for the same "clean MWh" lose credits from 2028, which raises the relative price of firm clean power.

**Baseline timelines (DOE Liftoff, Mar 2024):**

| Step | Typical time |
|---|---|
| Exploration under a CX | 2–4 months |
| Exploration-drilling EA | 6–12 months |
| Resource-confirmation EA | 1–1.5 years |
| Utilization EA | 1–1.5 years |
| Lease sale | "can take over 4 years" |
| **Total on public land** | **7–10 years**; reforms could cut this "by up to three years" to 4–7 |

Several of those reforms have now happened: the confirmation-drilling CX, emergency EA and EIS clocks, and annual sales.

**Legislation (unverified status).** The House passed the SPEED Act (NEPA reform) in Dec 2025, and it stalled in the Senate (sibling nuclear file, unverified). Geothermal-specific bills from the 119th Congress could not be confirmed: CX expansion, mandated sales and noncompetitive leasing adjacent to existing leases (R14). OBBBA itself made **no Geothermal Steam Act changes**. Rents stay at $1–3/acre, the minimum bid at $2/acre and the royalty at 1.75%→3.5% (R14).

**Asymmetric treatment against wind and solar.**

- OBBBA §50302 moved wind and solar right-of-way rents to market-indexed formulas. Geothermal leasing terms were left alone (R14, from P.L. 119-21).
- A mid-2025 Interior memo reportedly required Secretary-level sign-off for wind and solar actions on federal land (**unverified**; not fetched).

Federal land policy is now tilted in favour of geothermal relative to other renewables.

## 5. State-level pull (from B02 and R10, primary URLs listed below)

**California (CPUC).**

- **D.21-06-035** requires at least 1,000 MW of firm zero-emission resources by 2026. In practice that means geothermal, and it has produced at least 616 MW of geothermal PPAs.
- **D.24-02-047** (2023 Preferred System Plan) models new geothermal of 0.8 GW (2026), 1.1 GW (2028), 1.5 GW (2030) and 2.0 GW (2033–45).
- AB 1373 central procurement by DWR covers about 1 GW of long-lead geothermal for 2031–37 (D.24-08-064; unverified in B02).

**Nevada.** The NV Energy **Clean Transition Tariff** lets customers of 5 MW or more fund dedicated clean resources. Deals so far:

- Ormat–Google: up to 150 MW of new geothermal (Feb 2026);
- the Fervo/Google arrangement.

**Texas.** SB 785 (2023) vests geothermal in the surface owner, which creates a cheap private-rights market (R06/R07).

**Accelerator states.** These 15 states are drafting goals and rules (DOE, Jul 2026).

## 6. Risks

1. **The credit cliff.** Full credits require construction to begin by end-2033 (75% for 2034, 50% for 2035, zero from 2036). A prospect acquired in 2027 has about 6 years to reach begin-construction. That fits the post-reform 4–7-year timeline but not the 7–10-year legacy one. After 2033 the step-up from "proven" depends on PPA price alone.
2. **Political reversal.** Credits and emergency NEPA rest on a statute that could be amended and on EOs that could be rescinded. Emergency alternative arrangements are legally untested in court for geothermal (unverified).
3. **BLM capacity.** One Nevada office is 60% vacant and nomination review slipped from about 11 to about 15 months (R14/GAO). Annual sales mandated without staff can mean slower permits after the lease.
4. **Crowding and price.** Policy raised supply but not the fixed price terms. The auction market is now hot: NV $410/acre maximum (2025), UT $1,207/acre (2026), and NM's June 2026 sale took $16.6M (R14). The federal "cheap before proof" window is narrowing on recognised ground.
5. **Public de-risking erodes information edge.** DOE now funds 16 exploration-drilling projects and a hidden-systems fault-mapping prize. INGENIOUS TGHs at Lund North are public. Government-funded discovery data will become public and compress buy-before-proof margins in the targeted regions (L04).
6. **FEOC compliance** for construction after 2025. Exposure is low but administratively costly.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Policy strongly supports "own the asset, sell the proven state".** The buyers' credits (Ormat, Fervo, utilities, hyperscaler-backed SPVs) last through 2033 construction starts, with no placed-in-service deadline. Wind and solar lose credits from 2028. MLP eligibility (§70524) gives owned producing geothermal a yield-vehicle exit. A confirmed right sold in 2028–2032 is worth the most it ever will be. The window is time-boxed, which favours fast flips over slow self-development.
2. **Permitting reforms cut the cost of "proving" a right.** TGH programmes now clear on CXs (Lund North, Aug 2026). Confirmation drilling on 20 acres or less has a CX (Jan 2025). Emergency EAs run in about 14 days. Proving a blind resource on a federal lease used to take 2–3 years before the first full-diameter well and can now take months. Terranox's Saskatchewan step-up comes from cheap proof on a cheaply acquired right, and this reproduces it.
3. **Federal acquisition is the policy-favoured lane, but it is also the crowded lane where Zanskar plays.** Annual sales, 90% offering and no deferrals all increase supply on BLM land. That is a tailwind for anyone, Zanskar included, and Zanskar already competes there at scale. Fixed rents and royalties mean the step-up stays with the lessee once a lease is won. But bonus bids on recognised ground are now hundreds to over a thousand dollars per acre, so the edge must come from identifying ground the crowd prices at the $2/acre floor. In NV 2025, 37% of parcels cleared at the floor.
4. **The gap Zanskar is leaving, and where policy now helps a newcomer:**
   - **(a) Private, fee and state rights** outside BLM auctions. Texas SB 785 surface-owner geothermal, Utah and Nevada surface-estate rules, and state trust land are not touched by federal crowding. Federal credits apply equally there, and private land avoids BLM staff bottlenecks and Forest Service consent delays (1,098 days on average).
   - **(b) Non-Great-Basin, sedimentary and accelerator states** (LA, PA, WV, CO, MT, AZ) where state goals are being written now. First-mover rights there come before state rules and procurement pull arrive.
   - **(c) Post-2033-aware asset selection.** Target prospects that can reach begin-construction quickly: shallow, near transmission, on CX-eligible pad sizes. Choose them because the credit cliff penalises slow assets.
   - **(d) Selling into the CPUC/DWR (about 1 GW for 2031–37) and NV CTT buyers** who need *sites*, not technology.

   Zanskar's model is to build plants itself (Lightning Dock, Big Blind) on federal leases bought at public auction. That leaves room for a pure rights-aggregator and flipper working off-auction, which is the closest analogue to Terranox's Saskatchewan claim-staking.
5. **DOE is building a free public competitor to the ML layer.** The GEMS prize for "hidden geothermal systems" algorithms and 16 DOE-funded exploration-drilling projects mean the model itself will not be the moat. **Speed of tenure acquisition** will be: lock up rights before public de-risking data lands. This matches the Terranox lesson that the data is public and the edge is acting on it first.
