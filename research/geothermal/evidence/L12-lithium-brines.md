# L12 — Lithium and mineral co-production from geothermal brines

Covers the Salton Sea (Hell's Kitchen/CTR, BHE/TerraLithium), the Smackover (Arkansas/East Texas) and the Upper Rhine (Vulcan): concentrations, value per MW-equivalent, rights (who owns lithium in brine), and the literature.

**Method note.** The shared WebSearch budget ran out after 2 queries. Everything else was fetched directly from primary pages: OSTI records, USGS, the US Code, Texas Legislature Online, CDTFA, company releases, and ThinkGeoEnergy for news. Paywalled or full-text versions of some papers could not be read. Those are summarised from their abstract or record only, and this is flagged on each one.

## 1. Literature review (primary sources)

**1. Dobson, Araya, Brounce, … Stringfellow, White (22 authors), 2023. *Characterizing the Geothermal Lithium Resource at the Salton Sea*. LBNL-2001557, DOI 10.2172/2222403.**
The DOE-GTO-funded flagship resource study.
- Average brine Li is about 198 ppm.
- The well-characterised reservoir holds about 4.1 Mt LCE. The probable extent is up to 18 Mt LCE.
- The brine produced by the existing about 400 MW of plants is about 120 Mt/yr. It carries about 127,000 t/yr LCE.
- Regional potential is up to 2,950 MW.
- The authors caveat that the estimate assumes access to the whole reservoir and full extraction.
- (Numbers via the DOE/ThinkGeoEnergy summary of the report. The PDF was over 10 MB and was not parsed directly.)
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2222403 ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/?p=58697

**2. Humphreys, Brounce, McKibben, Dobson, Planavsky, Kalderon-Asael, 2025. "Characterization of Li in the Salton Sea Geothermal Field." *Economic Geology* 120(5), DOI 10.5382/econgeo.5161.**
- The brine reservoir inventory is 4–18 Mt LCE.
- Chlorite holds 270–580 ppm Li at about 2,358 m but only 70–100 ppm at about 2,882 m.
- Li behaviour changes at about 2,500 m (about 325 °C). The system is open, not closed.
- Implication: alteration minerals act as a Li sink or source, so the rock may recharge the brine. That matters for long-run grade.
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3008172

**3. Knierim, Blondes, Masterson, Freeman, McDevitt, … Ausbrooks, Chenault, 2024. "Evaluation of the lithium resource in the Smackover Formation brines of southern Arkansas using machine learning." *Science Advances* 10(39) eadp8149.**
- A random-forest model, trained on legacy and new brine samples with geologic, geochemical and temperature predictors, maps Li across southern Arkansas.
- It estimates 5.1–19 Mt Li in place, equal to 35–136% of the total US lithium resource.
- Brines exceed 400 mg/L.
- About 5,000 t of dissolved Li per year already comes to surface in oil, gas and bromine waste streams (2022).
- Historic 1960s values above 500 mg/L could not be verified.
- **This is the closest published analogue to the Terranox method: ML over legacy industry fluid data producing a resource map.**
https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70259385 ; https://www.usgs.gov/news/national-news-release/unlocking-arkansas-hidden-treasure-usgs-uses-machine-learning-show-large

**4. Warren, I., 2021. *Techno-Economic Analysis of Lithium Extraction from Geothermal Brines*. NREL/TP-5700-79178.** (Full text read.)
- Lithium passing through Salton Sea plants is more than 24,000 t Li/yr (2019).
- Geothermal brines elsewhere in the US are mostly under 20 mg/kg. Salton Sea reaches 400 mg/kg. Roosevelt Hot Springs is about 25 mg/kg.
- Salton post-flash brine analyses show about 200–265 ppm Li, about 30,000–40,000 ppm Ca, about 1,300–1,950 ppm Mn and about 440–615 ppm Zn.
- DLE economics table:
  - SRI (Salton): 20,000 t/yr at $3,845/t, on a 50 MW plant with 6,000 gpm.
  - Vulcan (Upper Rhine): 181 mg/kg, 40,000 t/yr LHM, capex $1.29B, $3,217/t.
  - Standard Lithium (Smackover): 168 mg/L at $4,319/t.
- History:
  - CalEnergy's zinc plant (2002–04) shut down on economics.
  - Simbol piloted 95% Li capture but collapsed in 2015.
  - CTR was then planning 49.9 MW plus 20,000 t/yr LiOH.
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1782801 ; https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy21osti/79178.pdf

**5. Stringfellow & Dobson, 2021. "Technology for the Recovery of Lithium from Geothermal Brines." *Energies* 14(20):6805.**
- A review of DLE: inorganic ion-sieve sorbents (Li-Al LDH) are nearest to commercial.
- The Salton Sea is the most significant US Li brine.
- Technical feasibility is shown. Economically and environmentally sustainable large-scale production is "unresolved."
- (Abstract only.)
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/1826637

**6. Wesselkaemper, Renaud, Araya, Dekkers, Popineau, Riffault, O'Sullivan, Haddad, 2026. "Pathways to cost competitive and viable lithium production from Salton Sea geothermal brines." *Nature Communications* 17, DOI 10.1038/s41467-026-75389-8.**
- Large-scale cost is about $10,000/t. With higher-certainty brine modelling it rises to as much as $22,000/t.
- Selling pre-treatment by-products (Mn, Zn, Fe and others) cuts the break-even by about $5,000/t.
- Cutting capex 20% lowers the break-even by about 10%.
- This is far above the 2021 PEA-era $3–4k/t figures. **Reservoir and brine uncertainty is the cost driver**, which favours whoever understands the reservoir best.
- (Abstract only.)
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3439691

**7. Busse, Stokes-Draut, Camarillo, Millstein, Slattery, McKibben, Dobson, Stringfellow, 2023. "Environmental Impact Assessment of Lithium Recovery from Geothermal Brines in the SS-KGRA." *GRC Transactions*.**
- Salton Sea geothermal expands from about 400 MWe to 920 MWe within 3–4 years. Potential is about 2,950 MWe.
- Assesses water, air and solid-waste impacts.
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2305350

**8. Stringfellow & Camarillo, 2025. "Solid Wastes from Geothermal Energy Production and Implications for DLE." *Energies* 18(6):1359.**
- Salton plants produce about 79,800 t/yr of wet solid waste, about 28 t/GWh. About 15% of it is hazardous.
- DLE pre-treatment (Si, Fe and metal removal) could add up to about 6,800 mg of solids per kg of brine, against about 500 mg/kg today. That is more than ten times the waste.
- Calcium handling dominates landfill volume.
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2565901

**9. Huang, Neil, Ahmmed, Mehana (LANL), Bhattacharya, Nicot, Elliott (UT-BEG), 2026. "Critical review of lithium recovery from geothermal brines with implications for Smackover Formation, USA." *Energy Conversion and Management: X* 30:101671.**
- Positions the Gulf Coast Smackover (AR, TX, LA, MS, AL) as a combined geothermal-plus-Li play.
- Explicitly calls for ML-based resource targeting.
- (Abstract only.)
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3375266

**10. Darvari, Nicot, Scanlon, Kyle, Elliott, Uhlman, 2024. "Controls on lithium content of oilfield waters in Texas and neighboring states." *J. Geochem. Explor.* 257:107363.**
Also **Gerardo & Song, 2025, *Environ. Sci.: Water Res. Technol.* 11(3), DOI 10.1039/D4EW00422A.**
- Darvari et al. is a regional oilfield-water Li dataset.
- Gerardo & Song argue that siting depends on geochemistry, end-users and workforce.
- (Records only. No abstracts were available, so their numbers are not used.)
https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2576668 ; https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2500981

## 3. Value per MW-equivalent (my arithmetic, from sourced inputs)

- **Contained Li per MW, Salton Sea.** LBNL gives 127,000 t LCE/yr in the brine from about 400 MW, which is about **320 t LCE per MW-yr**. As a cross-check, BHE moves 50,000 gpm (about 3,150 kg/s) for 345 MW. At about 200 mg/kg that is about 20,000 t Li/yr, or about 106,000 t LCE/yr, about **305 t LCE per MW-yr**. The two estimates agree.
- **Revenue per MW.** At 90% recovery and $10–15k/t LCE, lithium is worth about **$2.7–4.3M per MW-yr**. Power at 92% capacity factor and $80–110/MWh is about **$0.65–0.9M per MW-yr**.
  - **On the Salton Sea, lithium is worth about 3–5× the electricity from the same brine.**
  - At Wesselkaemper's $10–22k/t cost, the margin is thin or negative at today's prices (spot price unverified).
- **Smackover.** One unit (SWA) at about 168 kbbl/d yields 22,500 tpa (NPV about $1.3B). Brines there are typically about 100–140 °C (unverified), so power is minor and **lithium dominates value**.
- **Upper Rhine.** About 181 mg/kg at about 90–125 L/s per well (Vulcan well test). Power and heat roughly pay for operations, and Li is the equity story.

## 4. Rights: who owns lithium in the brine?

- **Federal (BLM geothermal leases).** The Geothermal Steam Act (30 USC 1001) puts "any byproduct" inside "geothermal resources." But it defines a byproduct as a mineral worth **less than 75% of the value of the geothermal steam**, or one not worth extracting on its own. Byproduct royalty (§1004) applies only to minerals listed in the Mineral Leasing Act.
  - Salton-grade lithium is worth more than the steam, so lithium arguably falls *outside* the byproduct definition, and therefore outside the geothermal lease.
  - It is unclear whether federal Li-in-brine is then leasable, locatable, or neither (my legal reading, unverified).
  - **A geothermal lease is not a clean lithium right.**
- **Texas.** SB 785 (effective 18 Jun 2023) gives geothermal energy to the **surface owner** but expressly excludes "minerals dissolved or otherwise contained in groundwater, including in hot brines." **Heat and lithium belong to different estates**, so both must be aggregated (and are aggregated cheaply in fragmented tracts).
- **Arkansas.** The AOGC unitises brine and set a **2.5% gross royalty** plus a **$65.05/acre/yr** in-lieu bromine fee for the SWA Reynolds Unit, about 3% all-in. That is a cheap, predictable framework.
- **California.** The rights are private, Imperial Irrigation District or county land (Hell's Kitchen sits in the Lithium Valley Specific Plan, 4,000 acres). There is a state **lithium extraction excise tax of $425/$638/$851 per t LCE** (2026 tiers by cumulative tonnage), and CEQA litigation risk (CTR remand).
- **Germany.** Lithium is state-owned/"bergfrei" under the Bundesberggesetz and licensed by the state (unverified; the statute page did not load). There is no private right to buy.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Lithium is where the step-up is.** On Li-rich brines, mineral value per MW is 3–5× power value. Smackover projects carry billion-dollar NPVs (SWA $1.3B after-tax, Franklin $5.9B pre-tax). An asset-owner that ties up **both** the heat and the brine-mineral rights before proof captures a far bigger step-up than a power-only lease.
2. **The method is proven to work on this data.** USGS (Knierim 2024) already ran ML over legacy oilfield brine data to map Smackover Li. LANL and UT-BEG (Huang 2026) call for more of it. Legacy produced-water chemistry, bottom-hole temperatures and well logs are exactly the "public but illegible" dataset Terranox reads. One model ranking **temperature × permeability × Li/K/Br grade × rights fragmentation** is a direct transplant.
3. **Rights fragmentation is the arbitrage.** The Texas split (surface owns heat, mineral estate owns lithium), the federal 75%-byproduct ambiguity, and Arkansas unitisation all create mispriced, separable rights. **T5 is the existence proof:** it bought cheap land, permitted a geothermal well, found about 800 ppm Li plus heat, and signed Glencore. Buy-before-proof, then sell to Standard Lithium/Equinor, Exxon, Chevron, Oxy/TerraLithium, Glencore or LGES, or to Fervo/Ormat for the heat.
4. **The gap vs Zanskar.** Zanskar's public strategy is blind *hydrothermal power* systems in the Great Basin (Big Blind, Lightning Dock) on BLM leases. Great Basin brines are mostly under 20–25 mg/kg Li (NREL), so mineral co-production plays no part in Zanskar's thesis. Its tenure (BLM geothermal leases) is exactly the instrument that does **not** cleanly convey high-value lithium. **The white space is sedimentary Gulf Coast, Williston and Saskatchewan brines, where heat plus lithium is valued together, on private and state mineral estates acquired before proof.** Zanskar is not playing there.
5. **Risks, scored on plausibility rather than as kills.**
   - DLE cost uncertainty: $10–22k/t (Nat. Comm. 2026). Simbol failed in 2015 and CalEnergy's zinc plant shut in 2004.
   - Solid waste can rise more than tenfold.
   - Lithium price cyclicality (unverified current spot price).
   - CEQA and water litigation at the Salton Sea.
   - Majors (Exxon, Chevron, Equinor) are already leasing Smackover acreage (unverified), so the cheap-entry window is closing in AR and East TX. That pushes the play to second-tier basins (Louisiana/Mississippi/Alabama Smackover, Williston, Saskatchewan).
   - None of this is "too small": Smackover alone is 5–19 Mt Li in place.
