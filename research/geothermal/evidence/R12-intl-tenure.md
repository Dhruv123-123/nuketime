## 2. Jurisdiction detail

### Turkey: the clearest "free-entry, fixed-fee, own-the-step-up" geothermal regime found (primary source, verified)
Law No. 5686 on Geothermal Resources and Natural Mineral Waters (2007, amended by 7257/2020) works like a mining claim system:
- **Art. 5(1):** Anyone applies with 1/25,000 sheet coordinates and an exploration project, for **≤5,000 hectares**. "**Priority right is the basis for applications**" (first in time wins). If several applications for the same place arrive at the same time, the project offering the fastest and largest investment wins.
- **Art. 5(2):** Overlaps with earlier applications are carved out (MAPEG registry). This is a cadastre-style system.
- **Art. 5(3):** **3-year term**, plus one 1-year extension with a revised project. Test production is allowed (Art. 5(4)).
- **Art. 6(1):** If the holder files an operating project before the last day of the exploration term, "**an operating licence is granted**". This is conversion **by right**, not by competition. The operating licence runs **30 years**, extendable in 10-year blocks (Art. 6(5)).
- **Art. 10(1)(a):** "**Exploration and operating licences are transferable.**" Art. 10(1)(b) sets up a **public registry**. Rights can be pledged and mortgaged, and lease contracts over the fluid are registered.
- **Art. 10(1)(ç):** Exploration licence fee is **1,000 TL** for geothermal (operating licence 4x). **Art. 10(1)(d):** deposit is 1% of the fee per hectare, **minimum 15,000 TL**, all revalued annually. Even revalued to 2026, this is in the low thousands of US dollars per licence (unverified FX/revaluation).
- **Royalty ("idare payı"):** **1% of gross revenue** for power, greenhouse and district heating (Art. 10(1)(e)).
- **Art. 10(1)(c):** Lapsed or abandoned licences are **re-tendered**, and if nobody bids they **reopen to applications**. That creates a recurring "dropped-claim" pipeline, the same mechanic Terranox exploits in Saskatchewan.
- **Art. 16:** MTA (the state geological survey) explores fee-free. **Where MTA proves a resource, it auctions the field under State Tender Law 2886.** The proceeds after costs are split between MTA and the province, and payment can be in up to 6 annual instalments. This is direct legal proof that a **proven geothermal field sells at a step-up over an unproven licence**: the Turkish state itself runs the Terranox business model.
- Context (unverified): Turkey has ~1.7 GW installed (4th globally), mostly in the Büyük Menderes and Gediz grabens. Hundreds of licences are held, many by small holders and speculators. Licences change hands in a secondary market. Much of the western Anatolian graben is already licensed, so the white space is in central and eastern Anatolia and in lapsed licences.

### Mexico: application-based but heavy (primary source, verified)
The new *Ley de Geotermia* (DOF 18-03-2025) requires an **Exploration Permit** before any work (Art. 8):
- Applicants must prove legal, technical and financial capacity and **experience in geothermal** (Art. 9; Art. 16 VI). They must also show **rights to use the surface** (Art. 16 VII) and "social, technical and financial feasibility" (Art. 16 VIII).
- **≤150 km², 4 years + one 4-year extension**. The extension requires ≥50% execution of the technical and financial schedules (Art. 12). Annual reports are mandatory (Art. 13).
- **The holder must drill and complete 1 to 5 exploratory wells** (Art. 10). This kills the "cheap option" economics.
- Exclusive rights during the term (Art. 11). Only the permit holder can apply for the **30-year concession** (Arts. 20, 22), which can be assigned with SENER approval (Art. 26). There is a **reassignment** procedure for idle concessions (Art. 39–40).
- Verdict: you can hold a geothermal right without a tender, but you cannot sit on it cheaply. Mexico is a developer's regime, not an option-holder's regime.

### Philippines (partially verified)
RA 9513 sets the fiscal terms. The **government share is 1% of gross income, except indigenous geothermal at 1.5%** (verified from statute text). Since the October 2020 IRR change, large-scale geothermal projects (initial investment > US$50M) can be **100% foreign-owned** via FTAA-type agreements (Rappler, cited by Wikipedia). DOE awards service contracts via OCSP for offered blocks. The pre-development stage runs 5 yr; the contract runs 25 yr + 25 yr (unverified). Incumbents EDC (Lopez group) and AP Renewables (Aboitiz) dominate. Installed capacity is ~1,984 MW at end-2024 (ThinkGeoEnergy via Wikipedia).

### Indonesia (mostly unverified)
Law 21/2014 moved geothermal out of the "mining" category, which unlocks conservation forests where ~80% of reserves sit. It also moved tendering to the central government (Jakarta Post, 27 Aug 2014). WKPs are tendered. The **PSP/PSPE route** (unverified): a private party funds a preliminary survey, plus exploration under PSPE, at its own cost and in return gets a **right to match** the winning tender bid. This is the only Asian route where proprietary data creates a priority claim. Installed capacity is ~2.65–2.74 GW (2024–25), 2nd globally, against an often-quoted ~24–28 GW potential. State-backed actors (PGE, Geo Dipa, and the government drilling program via PT SMI/GREM) crowd the high-quality targets (unverified).

### Kenya (mostly unverified)
GDC (100% state-owned, formed 2008) explores, drills and **sells steam** to KenGen and private IPPs (GDC website). The state therefore owns the de-risking step and its value uplift. Private licences exist, but Kenya is not a cheap-option jurisdiction. Kenya has ~890 MW installed (2023).

### Chile (unverified)
Law 19.657 (2000) separates **exploration** from **exploitation** concessions. Any Chilean person or Chile-incorporated company may apply (verified via Wikipedia summary). From memory (unverified): a competing application for the same area triggers a public tender; exploration lasts 2 + 2 years; exploitation concessions are indefinite and carry a preferential right for the explorer. Chile granted dozens of exploration concessions in the 2000s–2010s, and many lapsed. Only Cerro Pabellón (Enel/ENAP, 48 MW + 33 MW unit 3) has been built. Chile is a plausible first-come land bank for the high Andes, but grid, altitude and indigenous-consultation (ILO 169) constraints apply.

### Australia (unverified)
South Australia's GEL system gave out several hundred exploration licences by application during the 2000s hot-rock boom (Geodynamics/Habanero, Petratherm, Green Rock). Fees were modest, but work-program commitments applied. Most licences were surrendered after 2012–2016. Free entry exists, but the resource class (deep EGS-like hot granite far from load) did not pay off. This is a warning that **cheap tenure ≠ value** if no buyer exists.

### New Zealand (unverified)
There is no Crown geothermal title to stake. Use rights come from regional council consents under the RMA, and the land is often held by Māori trusts (e.g. Tuaropaki Trust with Mercury at Mokai; Tauhara North No. 2 Trust with Contact). The NZ-equivalent strategy is a JV with landowners, not a land grab.

## 3. Which jurisdictions offer free-entry or fixed-fee geothermal exploration rights?
- **Clear yes (primary-verified):** **Turkey**. First-in-time priority, fixed nominal fee, 5,000-ha blocks, by-right conversion to a 30-yr operating licence, freely transferable, public registry, 1% gross royalty. A lapsed-licence recycling mechanism exists, and the state itself auctions proven fields.
- **Likely yes (unverified):** **Chile** (application, tender only on conflict), **South Australia** and other Australian states (application GELs), **Peru** (application authorizations).
- **Application-based but obligation-heavy (verified):** **Mexico** (must drill 1–5 wells, prove experience and surface rights).
- **No, state-tendered or state-led:** **Indonesia** (WKP tender; PSPE right-to-match is the exception), **Philippines** (DOE OCSP service contracts), **Kenya** (GDC steam model).
- **No title to stake:** **New Zealand** (RMA consent plus Māori land).
- For comparison (see R01/R02): the US BLM uses competitive auctions for nominated parcels and noncompetitive leasing only for parcels left unsold. Private fee geothermal rights in NV/UT/CA/ID can be bought directly from landowners.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **Turkey is the closest international analogue to Saskatchewan's mineral disposition system.** It is first-come, fixed-fee, transferable, registered, convertible by right, and has a dropped-licence pipeline. It also shows a market price for proven fields: MTA auctions proven sites under State Tender Law 2886. A Terranox-shape company could (a) ML-screen MTA well data, old oil and gas wells in SE Anatolia, and thermal-spring geochemistry; (b) file applications on blind targets in under-licensed central and eastern Anatolian grabens and on lapsed licences; and (c) sell licences, or JV them, with the many Turkish IPPs (Zorlu, Enerjisa, Kipaş, Gürmat etc.; unverified). Risk: the best western Anatolia acreage is already licensed. Turkey has also had feed-in-tariff cuts (YEKDEM ended 2020 and was replaced with lower TL-based tariffs; unverified), which shrinks the buyer's willingness to pay.
2. **Chile and South Australia are secondary land-bank options.** But the SA hot-rock bust shows that cheap tenure only pays if a buyer exists for the proven state. Score these on the presence of offtake and buyers, not on tenure cost.
3. **Asia and Africa (Indonesia, Philippines, Kenya) are state-tendered or state-led.** You cannot buy the option before proof. The best you can do is Indonesia's PSPE route, where your survey earns a right to match. So the right there is earned with data, which fits an ML company, but it is not cheap free entry. Mexico has free entry with a drilling obligation, which is not an option shape.
4. **Gap vs Zanskar:** Zanskar is US-focused. It runs AI-driven exploration, then **develops and operates** plants (Lightning Dock NM; Big Blind NV discovery), taking capital-intensive developer risk on a few assets. The gap is to be the **portfolio option-holder** instead of the developer: many cheap rights acquired before proof, de-risked cheaply with data, then sold or JV'd at the step-up to developers such as Fervo, Ormat and Zanskar itself. The step-up can be large (unverified magnitude). Internationally, Zanskar has no visible presence (unverified). **Turkey (and possibly Chile) is where a Terranox-shape company could build a non-US portfolio of first-come geothermal titles Zanskar is not targeting.** That gives the "not too small" argument a second leg beyond BLM leases.
5. **Scope check:** Turkey alone has ~1.7 GW installed and a dense registry-based licence market (unverified size). US BLM plus private fee rights remain the core. International first-come regimes are a credible expansion story, not the beachhead.
