# R01 — BLM competitive geothermal lease sales, 2014–2026: actual results

**Method.** I downloaded the BLM's own sale-result PDFs (summary sheets, and for Nevada 2021–2025 and Idaho Sep-2025 the "All Bids" reports) and parsed them. Every number in the tables comes from those PDFs or from BLM press releases, except where marked "(calc)", which is my arithmetic on the BLM figures. The shared WebSearch budget for this run was used up, so I could not find the 2007–2013 post-EPAct sales. Their figures are marked (unverified) below. A follow-up search pass could fill them in.

## 1. Rules of the game (from the June 2026 BLM-NM sale notice)

- Online English auction on the National Fluids Lease Sale System. Each parcel is open for one hour, bids go up in $1/acre steps, and the winner is the highest bid per acre.
- **Minimum bid: $2/acre.** On sale day the winner pays 20% of the bonus, the first-year rental of $2/ac and a $205 admin fee. The rest of the bonus is due later.
- **Primary term: 10 years.** Rental is $2/ac in year 1, $3/ac in years 2–10, and $5/ac after that. The term can be extended under 43 CFR 3207.10.
- **Unbid parcels can be leased noncompetitively.** For 30 days after the sale (and for up to 2 years under EPAct 2005), anyone can file for an unbid parcel with **no bonus**: a $450 fee plus $1/ac first-year rental.
- Source: https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2026-05/geothermal-lease-sale-notic-blm-nm-050126.pdf

So holding 10,000 acres costs about **$30k/yr** in rental. That is a very cheap option on a resource, and it is the same economics as Terranox's Saskatchewan claims.

**What the Nevada numbers show.** Before 2021 nearly everything cleared at the $2 floor, and in 2019 74% of parcels got no bid at all. From 2021 receipts rose about **60x**: $148k in 2020, $9.45M in 2025. That tracks the Fervo/EGS, data-centre and IRA cycle. But the market is **bimodal**. In 2025 the median parcel still cleared at only $3.50/ac and 37% went at the floor, while a handful of parcels went for $150–410/ac. Most acreage is still undifferentiated. A small number of tracts that the "smart money" agrees on get bid up 100–200x. In 2025, 27 parcels (97,120 ac) got no bid, and only one noncompetitive offer (4,890 ac) followed, so about 92,000 acres stayed open to anyone at $1/ac.

What is missing is an asset owner that picks parcels with data rather than by sitting next to known fields.

## Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The "buy before proof" entry ticket is still cheap.** Even in the 2025 peak, the median Nevada parcel cleared at $3.50/ac, 37% cleared at the $2 floor, 24% got no bid, and about 92k acres were left for $1/ac noncompetitive filing. On a ~3,000-ac parcel, a position costs about $6k–$60k in bonus plus about $9k/yr in rental. A 100,000-acre portfolio across 30+ targets would cost **$0.5–3M upfront and ~$0.3M/yr** (calc). That is Terranox's Saskatchewan math almost exactly.
2. **The step-up is visible and real.** The best parcels now clear at $200–$412/ac, against $2 five years earlier. Single parcels have sold for $1.35M (UT), $1.49M (ID) and $2.10M (NV). Total federal geothermal bonus receipts in 2025–26 are on the order of **$35–40M/yr** across NV, NM, UT, ID, CA and OR (calc). A buyer who identifies a "blind" parcel at $2–20 before the consensus catches up captures 10–100x on the lease value alone. The bigger prize is the much larger step-up once a resource is proven (MW-scale valuations).
3. **Zanskar is a modest lease buyer, not a land aggregator.** It won 3 (2022), 6 (2023, all at $2), 7 (2024, median $14) and 2 (2025) Nevada parcels: about 18 competitive parcels in four years. Its model is explore-drill-develop on a concentrated portfolio (Lightning Dock, Big Blind). Zanskar's private-land and noncompetitive holdings are not visible in these PDFs (unverified). Meanwhile Buffalo River, Percheron, FLHN, Invenergy and Cordillera are buying 10–28 parcels per sale with no visible exploration thesis.
4. **The gap.** Nobody is running a **data-driven, multi-state, asset-owning lease aggregator** that:
   (a) screens every parcel in every sale, plus all unbid acreage and private/state rights, with ML;
   (b) buys broadly at $2–20 and noncompetitively at $1/ac;
   (c) de-risks cheaply, using public data and temperature-gradient holes;
   (d) flips or JVs proven tracts to Fervo, Ormat, Invenergy, XGS and data-centre offtakers.
   Zanskar concentrates on drilling. The landmen speculate without data. Terranox-geo would sit between them, and bring the Saskatchewan playbook of "read the public archive better than anyone, own the right before the step-up."
5. **Risks visible in the data.** (a) Competition is rising, with 22–36 registered bidders per NV sale, so the edge must come from parcels the crowd ignores, not from contested ones. (b) BLM chooses what to offer, through nominations, NEPA and EAs, and New Mexico added 68 parcels in one go. Nominating parcels yourself tips off rivals, because nominations become public in the sale notice. (c) Lease value is only realized if someone develops: the 10-year primary term needs diligence or production to extend, so the flip has to happen within about 5–8 years.
6. **Scale check.** This is not "too small". About 1M+ federal acres are offered per year across these states (2025–26), the top tracts are worth $1–2M each before any drilling, and proven-resource transactions are an order of magnitude larger again.
