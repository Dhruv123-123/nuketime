## 1. Primary sources reviewed

### S1. GAO (2006), *Renewable Energy: Increased Geothermal Development Will Depend on Overcoming Many Challenges*, GAO-06-629
This is the federal baseline at the start of the 2005-2012 boom.
- **Producing leases:** as of January 2006, **50 federal geothermal leases produced electricity** (48 BLM, 2 Forest Service). Twelve of them are at The Geysers. US output was about 1,275 MW from federal land, and 26 of 54 plants were on BLM land.
- **Exploration risk:** the Geothermal Energy Association (GEA) puts wells at **$2-5M each**. Only about **25% of initial test wells find commercial fields**, and confirmation-phase failure rates run "as high as 60 percent".
- **Costs:** a typical **25 MW plant costs about $75M**. Exploration and confirmation are about 10% of that, field wells 23%, the plant 54% and gathering 7%.
- **Financing:** "few companies, including venture capitalists, are willing to provide funding... particularly for the initial phases of exploration and confirmation". Funders would back only "fully confirmed" projects.
- **Leasing:**
  - Before EPAct 2005, land outside KGRAs (Known Geothermal Resource Areas) was leased **non-competitively to the first qualified applicant**.
  - Leases run 10 years, with a rental of at least $1/acre until production.
  - The backlog was 31 California applications averaging **7.4 years old** and 136 in Nevada.
- **Speculation, directly relevant to us:** BLM officials report developers saying that "**speculators often lease geothermal resources not for development purposes but rather to resell the leases at a significant profit**, running up the cost of the project". A fragmented "patchwork of lease ownership" made consolidation hard.
- URL: https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-06-629.pdf

### S2. BLM, *Public Land Statistics* FY2005, FY2010, FY2013, FY2024 (Tables 3-13, 3-14 and 3-17)
These tables answer "what share of leases ever produce". Totals below are competitive plus noncompetitive leases. The ratios are my own arithmetic.

| As of 30 Sep | Leases in effect | Acres | Producing leases | Producing acres | % leases producing | % acres producing |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FY2005 | 354 (132 comp + 222 noncomp) | 483,964 | ~50 (GAO, Jan 2006) | n/a (data system outage) | ~14% | n/a |
| FY2010 | **785** (442 + 343) | **1,574,545** | **62** | 66,539 | **7.9%** | **4.2%** |
| FY2013 | 680 (403 + 277) | 1,263,159 | 71 | 83,706 | 10.4% | 6.6% |
| FY2024 | 568 (368 + 200) | 1,201,122 | 88 | 102,278 | 15.5% | 8.5% |

**Boom signature:**
- The lease count **more than doubled from FY2005 to FY2010** (354 to 785) and acreage tripled.
- In FY2010 alone BLM issued **102 competitive leases on 294,997 acres for $2.76M in bonus bids (about $9.36/acre)**, plus 26 noncompetitive leases. Nevada accounted for 75 of the competitive leases and $2.33M of the bonus.
- In that same year only **37 geothermal drilling permits were approved and 16 wells started** across all federal land.

**Bust signature:**
- In FY2013 BLM issued just **5 competitive leases on 7,056 acres for $14,116, about $2/acre**, which is the statutory floor.
- Lease count then fell to 568 by FY2024 even with new issuance, so hundreds of boom-era leases were relinquished or expired.
- Pre-EPAct legacy leases fell from 122 (FY2010) to 69 (FY2024).

**Bottom line:** at the 2010 peak, **about 92% of federal geothermal leases and about 96% of leased acres were not producing**. Fourteen years later, producing leases rose only from 62 to 88.

### S3. US DOE Geothermal Technologies Program (2010), *A History of Geothermal Energy Research and Development in the United States: Exploration 1976-2006*
- **The 1970s-80s boom was oil-major-led.** Leasing and exploration in the Great Basin were concentrated "beginning in the 1970s". **Chevron and Phillips alone evaluated more than 75 prospects**, and industry evaluated more than 100 sites in total.
- **Industry-Coupled Drilling Program (1977/78):**
  - DOE paid part of the cost of confirmation drilling in exchange for **company data packages released to the public**.
  - Of **14 areas explored, 8 were later developed, producing 137 MWe**: Roosevelt, Cove Fort, Beowawe, San Emidio, Soda Lake, Stillwater, Dixie Valley and Desert Peak.
  - That is a **57% area-level conversion on surface-expressed, pre-selected prospects**.
  - Output included 15 deep wells, more than 50 reports and 25 drilling histories, all archived at EGI (Energy & Geoscience Institute, Utah).
- **The 1980s bust:**
  - Through much of the 1980s DOE dropped exploration as a stand-alone program.
  - **The Geysers fell from nearly 2,000 MWe in 1987 to about 1,000 MWe by 1995** because it had been overdeveloped and the reservoir pressure ran down.
- **GRED I-III (2000-2007), the precursor to the 2005-12 boom:**
  - GRED I funded 7 projects; 4 completed drilling, with about 100 MWe postulated.
  - Blue Mountain (49.5 MW) and Raft River came out of GRED.
- URL: https://www1.eere.energy.gov/geothermal/pdfs/geothermal_history_1_exploration.pdf

### S4. Muffler, L.J.P. (ed.) (1979), *Assessment of Geothermal Resources of the United States, 1978*, USGS Circular 790
- This was the top-of-cycle resource narrative. Geothermal exploration "since the petroleum crisis of 1973... [took] a dramatic upswing". World capacity growth was projected at about 19%/yr for 1977-83.
- Identified high-temperature hydrothermal systems held a **mean of 23,000 MWe for 30 years** (90% CI 17,400-28,000).
- **Actual US capacity is still only about 2.7-3.9 GW nearly 50 years later.** BLM reports 51 plants and 2.65 GW on BLM land. The "identified" estimate was never realised; conversion was the bottleneck, not resource.
- URLs: https://pubs.usgs.gov/circ/1979/0790/report.pdf ; https://www.blm.gov/programs/energy-and-minerals/renewable-energy/geothermal-energy

### S5. Raser Technologies, FY2010 Form 10-K (filed 15 Apr 2011) and Form 8-K Item 1.03 (filed 3 May 2011)
This is the canonical ARRA-era failure.
- **Land:** "Our geothermal resources portfolio consists of **over 250,000 acres in the United States**" plus about 100,000 acres in Indonesia. Raser had initiated **8 projects but built 1** (Thermo No. 1, Beaver County, Utah).
- **Fatal shortcut:** Raser got "a high degree of confidence" from consultants before the final resource report. On that basis, "**we omitted drilling slim holes and began drilling larger diameter production and reinjection wells**". It started construction while the well results were still inconclusive.
- **Outcome at Thermo No. 1:**
  - The PPA (power purchase agreement) with the City of Anaheim was for up to 11 MW.
  - The plant used 50 modular PureCycle units and delivered about **8 MW gross / about 6 MW net**.
  - Capitalised cost was **$54.6M of well field plus $68.9M of plant, $123.5M in total, or about $20M per net MW**.
- **Write-downs and funding:**
  - Impairments were **$52.2M (Q2 2010) plus $15.4M (Q4 2010)**, leaving a fair value of **$14.6M**.
  - Raser received a **$33.0M Treasury Section 1603 grant** (30% of qualifying cost) and $31.2M of Prudential project debt.
  - Accumulated deficit was **$215.4M against $6.6M of revenue over 3 years**.
- **End:** Raser filed **Chapter 11 on 29 April 2011** (D. Del. 11-11315). **All existing equity was cancelled.** Its Lightning Dock (NM) project was being funded by Evergreen-FE. Lightning Dock is the plant Zanskar later acquired; I did not trace the intermediate owner (possibly Cyrq Energy, unverified).
- URLs: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1103078/000114420411022542/v218751_10k.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1103078/000118143111026647/rrd310171.htm

### S6. Nevada Geothermal Power Inc. (NGP), FY2011 Form 20-F (filed Dec 2011) and 6-K press releases (Mar-Jun 2012)
- **The asset:** NGP owned the **49.5 MW Faulkner 1 plant at Blue Mountain, NV**, which came out of GRED and started in 2009.
- **The resource disappointed:**
  - "Since start-up the Company discovered the Blue Mountain water/brine **temperature is declining**", faster than the 2.5%/yr forecast.
  - The John Hancock loan forced NGP to nominate only PPA-minimum output: **35 MW in 2011 and 33.95 MW in 2012**.
  - Q1 2012 net generation was **65,784 MWh, about 30 MW average**, or about 61% of nameplate (my arithmetic). That was 18% below Q1 2011.
- **Financial distress:**
  - NGP **breached its EIG Global Energy Partners loan on 31 Dec 2011**.
  - At 31 Mar 2012, **total liabilities were $157.6M against total assets of $130.3M**.
  - Canaccord was hired to restructure. NGP let its East and South Brawley leases lapse and deregistered from the SEC in June 2012.
  - It received a $7.9M ARRA grant for injection drilling.
- **The rest of the portfolio:** NGP kept leasehold at **Pumpernickel Valley**, Edna Mountain, North Valley, New Truckhaven (Mannvit P50 of 60 MW) and Crump Geyser (50/50 JV with Ormat, with Ormat funding).
- **Pumpernickel resurfaces:** Zanskar announced a **Pumpernickel** discovery in Sep 2025, at about 137 °C at about 762 m (per sibling file L03). I have not checked that the parcels are the same (unverified). The prospect name matches a lease held by a distressed boom-era junior.
- **Afterwards:** NGP was later absorbed by Alterra Power (date and price unverified). Alterra itself went to Innergex in Feb 2018 (Wikipedia).
- **Blue Mountain origin (from sibling files L02 and L16, citing OSTI 810407 and OpenEI):**
  - Gold exploration holes hit hot water in the early 1990s.
  - Noramex took BLM leases non-competitively and **sold the leases plus data** before proof.
  - **The land-and-data holder exited; the builder later failed.**
- URLs: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177440/000117625611000946/ngp20f110630.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177440/000127956912000738/ex991.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177440/000127956912000864/ex991.htm

### S7. U.S. Geothermal Inc. (USG), FY2016 Form 10-K and DEFM14A merger proxy (Mar 2018)
This is the boom-era survivor that sold at about book value.
- **Plants:** Raft River (13 MW, 2008), San Emidio (10 MW, 2012) and Neal Hot Springs (22 MW, 2012), about **45 MW net in total**.
- **Pipeline:** WGP Geysers (30 MW, about $150M), San Emidio II ($145M), Crescent Valley ($130M) and El Ceibillo ($140M). Each was "TBD" on a PPA, so **about $565M of capex was stranded waiting on offtake**.
- **Land footprint versus land held:**
  - At San Emidio, USG held **17,846 acres** of leases, but the unitised **Participating Area is 583.68 acres (3.3%)**.
  - Neal Hot Springs (22 MW) sits on about **521 acres** of geothermal rights.
  - **The productive core is a small fraction of the acreage held.**
- **The exit:**
  - Ormat paid **$5.45/share cash**, a 28.5% premium and **5.4x EV/2018E revenue**.
  - An earlier 2017 bidder proposed a $120M equity valuation.
  - Against **$121.9M of paid-in capital** (Dec 2016), equity holders got back roughly what they had put in after about 15 years.
- **Western GeoPower link:** the "WGP Geysers" project is the old **Western GeoPower** Geysers project. Western GeoPower's last EDGAR item is a 2008 Form D. Its reported 2009 merger into Ram Power and the later transfer to USG are unverified here. The project was **still unbuilt in 2017**, more than a decade after Western GeoPower started it.
- URLs: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1172136/000106299317001274/form10k.htm ; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1172136/000106299318001265/defm14a.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Geothermal

### S8. Exploration success-rate literature (via sibling file L13)
- **IFC (2013):** first-well success in fields that were later developed was 50%.

## 2. Boom-bust narrative (synthesis)

**1. 1970-1985: oil-major boom.**
- The Geothermal Steam Act (1970), the 1973 oil shock and DOE cost-share programs drew Phillips, Chevron, Union/Unocal, Getty, AMAX and others into Great Basin leasing.
- Data was proprietary. DOE paid to make it public (S3).
- Majors held land non-competitively outside KGRAs and drilled more than 100 prospects.
- The surface-expressed winners (Dixie Valley, Roosevelt, Coso, Beowawe and others) were developed.

**2. Late 1980s-1990s: bust.**
- Oil companies exited geothermal. The cause is unverified as a single factor; low oil and gas prices are the usual explanation.
- The Geysers' output halved (S3) and DOE exploration funding shrank.
- Legacy temperature-gradient (TG) holes, logs and reports were left in archives (EGI, the USGS digitisation, SMU). That is the Terranox-shape data substrate.

**3. 2005-2012: policy boom.**
- Drivers were EPAct 2005 (full PTC eligibility, competitive leasing, royalty reform), state RPS mandates (renewable portfolio standards) and ARRA (1603 cash grants of 30%, DOE loan guarantees and GTO grants; GTO ARRA total unverified).
- Leases doubled (S2). Juniors raised TSX-V and AMEX money: NGP, Raser, USG, Western GeoPower, Ram Power, Magma/Alterra and Polaris.
- Most were **vertically integrated "explore + build + own" developers carrying project debt**.

**4. 2011-2013: bust.**
- Raser went bankrupt (Apr 2011) and NGP breached its covenants (Dec 2011).
- Competitive lease issuance collapsed to 5 leases in FY2013 at the $2/acre floor (S2).
- **Survivors were sold to the consolidators** (Ormat, Alterra/Innergex, Cyrq) at roughly the capital invested.

## 3. Why the companies failed

| Company | Primary failure mode | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Raser | Skipped slim-hole confirmation, built before the resource was proven, overbuilt modular units; equity wiped | S5: "omitted drilling slim holes"; $123.5M for 6 MW net; Ch. 11 |
| NGP | Resource overestimated (temperature decline); output about 61% of nameplate; project debt covenants | S6: liabilities exceed assets; EIG breach |
| USG | Did not fail, but could not get PPAs for about $565M of pipeline; sold near book | S7 |
| Western GeoPower / Ram Power | Geysers project stalled for more than 10 years (details unverified; SEDAR not accessed) | S7 |

**Common thread:**
- The land was cheap and was never what killed anyone.
- The killers were **(a) moving from lease to full-size wells and plant without staged, cheap confirmation, (b) project leverage sized to nameplate rather than proven deliverability, and (c) PPA and offtake dependence**.
- Each junior also held **far more acreage than it could ever drill**: Raser had 250,000 acres for 8 projects and built 1. Boom capital went into steel, not into upgrading the land bank's information.

## 4. Lessons for a land-first strategy

1. **The base rate is brutal, and that is the opportunity.** Only 8-15% of federal leases produce (S2), and leases are priced as if all are equal (about $2-9/acre). Even a modest ML-driven improvement in the hit rate is a large gain in value per acre.
2. **Separate the option from the steel.** The documented winners in this history are the information and land holders who sold before building: Noramex at Blue Mountain, the mining-data-to-Ormat paths at McGinness and Don A. Campbell (L02, L16), and data packages bought by DOE (S3). The losers integrated downstream with debt.
3. **Stage confirmation cheaply.** The Raser lesson is to never skip slim holes or TG holes. The step-up should be priced at the "TG-confirmed" or "slim-hole-confirmed" stage, before full-size wells.
4. **Sell data with the land.** GAO records developer resentment of resale "speculators" (S1). A credible package of lease plus data plus TG results turns a "flipper" into a "prospect generator" that buyers will pay up for, as with Noramex and the Industry-Coupled data packages.
5. **Acreage discipline.** The productive core is about 500-600 acres per 10-22 MW (San Emidio, Neal). Hold many small, well-chosen blocks, not 250,000-acre portfolios. Carrying cost is rental only (post-2005 schedule about $2-3/acre/yr, unverified), so a 100-block × 2,000-acre bank costs well under $1M/yr.
6. **Busts recycle land.** Boom-era leases lapse in busts: the FY2013 floor price, NGP dropping Brawley, and Pumpernickel resurfacing at Zanskar. A counter-cyclical land-first buyer gets the prospects that failed developers gave up.
7. **Exit buyers exist, and they consolidate.** Ormat bought USG, Innergex bought Alterra, Zanskar bought Lightning Dock. Now Fervo, utilities and data-centre offtakers have joined them.

## 5. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

- **History supports the land-first shape.** The 2005-12 boom destroyed capital at the *build* layer, not the *right* layer.
  - Raser lost all its equity after about $215M of losses.
  - NGP ended up with liabilities above assets.
  - USG returned roughly its paid-in capital.
  - Meanwhile land cost about $2-9/acre and about 90% of it sat unconverted.
- **A company that owns hundreds of cheap rights and sells "TG-confirmed" states at a step-up avoids every documented failure mode:** no plant capex, no project debt, no PPA dependence, and staged confirmation.
- **The Terranox analogy is close.** Saskatchewan's opened assessment files map onto the Industry-Coupled data, the EGI archive and mining holes in Nevada (S3, L16). Buy-before-proof maps onto Noramex at Blue Mountain.
- **Zanskar is repeating the 2005-12 developer path, with better science and much more capital.**
  - It acquired Lightning Dock, a Raser-era project.
  - It is building plants: six 20 MW plants, a $115M Series C and a 100 MW NV Energy PPA (L02, L14).
  - Its constraint is rig time and capex per prospect. It must concentrate on the few prospects it can build.
- **The gap:**
  1. Zanskar cannot hold or prove the long tail of second-tier anomalies. A land bank can.
  2. Zanskar is structurally a *buyer* of de-risked prospects, so a Terranox-shape company could sell to it.
  3. Busts will strand Zanskar-style developers' acreage again. A capital-light holder with low carrying cost survives the cycle and buys lapsed leases.
- **Key risks to price in:**
  - Public lease sales reveal nominations.
  - BLM is hostile to "speculators" (S1); mitigate by selling data with the land.
  - Lease terms (10 years, with diligence requirements) cap how long an option can be held.
  - Exit liquidity is concentrated in a few buyers. In the last bust, those buyers paid about book.
