# B01: Step-up evidence. What buyers pay for geothermal assets at each stage

**Question.** What is a geothermal right worth at each stage: bare lease, recognised play, exploration-confirmed (discovery well), permitted/contracted, operating? And what is the multiple from lease to proven resource?

**Method note.** The shared WebSearch budget for this run was used up before this file started. I could not run new searches. I verified the core transactions by fetching the primary SEC filings directly (USG 8-K and DEFM14A, Ormat Q2-2021 earnings release, Ormat Q2-2025 10-Q, Ormat-Enel 8-K, Fervo 424B4). Lease-auction figures come from sibling files R01, R04, Z08 and Z09, which parsed the BLM sale PDFs. Where I correct a sibling figure from primary text, I say so. Anything I could not fetch is marked (unverified).

## 2. Operating-asset comps: verified detail

### 2.1 Ormat / U.S. Geothermal (announced Jan 2018, closed 2018)
- **Price:** $5.45/share cash, a ~28.5% premium to the $4.24 close on 23 Jan 2018 (USG 8-K Ex.99.1; DEFM14A).
- **Shares:** 19,494,566 at the record date, so equity is ~$106M (calc). The commonly cited "~$110M" includes options. ROTH's premium analysis puts the **transaction value at $193.6M**. The termination fee was ~$3.2M (3%).
- **Capacity:** ~45 MW designed net output (Neal Hot Springs OR, San Emidio NV, Raft River ID).
- **$/MW:** $193.6M / 45 MW ≈ **$4.3M/MW EV** (calc).
- **Development assets valued near zero.** The board's stand-alone review used "a liquidation value of approximately $10 million" for the four development projects in aggregate. The most important stage-2 data point: bidder **Party G withdrew on 3 Aug 2017** because the development assets were not what it wanted, citing San Emidio 2, the Geysers and El Ceibillo specifically. An earlier bidder, "Party A" (a foreign strategic), had proposed a nonbinding $5.30–5.93/share in Nov 2016.
- **The ROTH DCF was below the deal price** ($3.56–4.25/share after a correction). So Ormat paid a strategic premium for operating MW plus the platform, and **not** for undeveloped resource.

### 2.2 Ormat / Terra-Gen TG Geothermal Portfolio (closed Jul 2021)
- From Ormat's Q2-2021 earnings release: Ormat paid **$171M in cash** for 100% of equity and **assumed debt and lease obligations of about $206M** (book value at 30 Jun 2021).
  - Correction: sibling files Z08 and R04 cite ~$258M assumed. The primary release says ~$206M.
- **Assets:** Dixie Valley (56 MW net) and Beowawe (11.5 MW). The deal also included rights to **Coyote Canyon, a greenfield development asset**, and **a 230 kV transmission line able to carry 300–400 MW**.
- **$/MW:** ~$377M / 67.5 MW ≈ **$5.6M/MW** (calc). The greenfield resource and the transmission line came bundled and were not separately priced.
- Ormat's FY2021 10-K shows **$65.4M of goodwill** added by the Terra-Gen transaction.

### 2.3 Ormat / Cyrq Blue Mountain (closed 18 Jun 2025)
- **$88.7M** total consideration for 100% of a **20 MW** plant in Humboldt County NV, with an NV Energy PPA expiring end-2029 (Ormat Q2-2025 10-Q).
- **Allocation:** PP&E/CIP $86.2M; a **$16.8M liability for the below-market PPA**; ARO $3.7M; goodwill $16.1M.
- **$/MW:** **$4.4M/MW** (calc).
- Sibling R10 notes the plant holds **51 MW of interconnection**. Part of the price is therefore interconnection headroom. In Sep 2026, Sage Geosystems picked Blue Mountain for its next commercial EGS facility (R07).

### 2.4 Ormat / Enel Green Power North America (announced Oct 2023)
- **$271M** for a 150 MW portfolio: Cove Fort UT, Stillwater NV (a triple hybrid, ~43 MW total generation including 20 MW PV), Salt Wells NV, plus 40 MW of solar (Ormat 8-K Ex.99.1).
- **Multiple:** "about 8x EV/EBITDA" including planned enhancements. The enhancements add ~17 MW and ~$15M of EBITDA for ~$55M of capex.
- **Two greenfield assets** were included and **not separately priced**: one next to Cove Fort, one in a "high-potential" California area.
- **$/MW:** ~$1.8M/MW blended (calc).

### 2.5 Other transactions (price undisclosed)
- **Zanskar / Cyrq Lightning Dock (NM).** Completed 29 May 2024, price undisclosed (ThinkGeoEnergy).
- **Calpine.** Calpine owns the Geysers (~725 MW net, unverified). Constellation's acquisition of Calpine, reported at about $16.4B of equity plus ~$12.7B of net debt, is (unverified, not fetched). Calpine is mostly gas, so it gives no usable geothermal $/MW.
- **Cyrq.** A net seller (Lightning Dock 2024, Blue Mountain 2025) and the successor to bankrupt Raser. Raser held 250k+ acres, and **100% of its equity was cancelled** in its 2011 Ch. 11 (R04). Land banks with no monetisation path go to zero.

**Takeaway from §2.** Operating US hydrothermal trades at **~$4.3–5.6M/MW**. Aged or partly solar portfolios trade at ~$1.8M/MW (~8–11x EBITDA). That is roughly **replacement cost**: FOAK EGS runs ~$7,000/kW (Fervo), and conventional binary is typically ~$4,000–6,000/kW (unverified). **The equity step-up is not in building plants.** It sits upstream, in land and discovery, and downstream, in contracted PPAs marked by public markets.

## 3. Upstream: lease and recognised-play prices

Siblings R01, Z04 and Z08 parsed the BLM All-Bids PDFs. I did not re-fetch them.
- **Floor.** The minimum bid is $2/ac. NV 2019: 102,403 acres at an **average of $4.17/ac**, with 74% of parcels unbid.
- **2025.** Max bids were $410/ac (NV), $412/ac (ID) and $344/ac (UT). Single parcels went for $1.35M (UT), $1.49M (ID) and $2.10M (NV). Federal geothermal bonus receipts are ~$35–40M/yr across six states (calc, R01).
- **Who pays up.** Mostly capital-rich land banks: Buffalo River Minerals, Percheron, FLHN, Invenergy, Cordillera. Together they took ~52% of 2022–25 NV+UT bonus dollars (Z08).
  - Ormat paid a median of $126/ac in NV 2024.
  - Zanskar is a modest buyer: ~18 competitive NV parcels in 2022–25, many at $2–14/ac.
- **Fervo states the arbitrage in its own IPO prospectus.** Its ~$4/ac position stands "in sharp contrast" to the $344 and $410/ac 2025 max bids. It also cites Bloomberg that some developers "paid hundreds of dollars per acre in 2025 for geothermal positions that failed to meet our development standards." **The market is paying for screening quality, not acreage.**
- **Private leases are cheap** (R06):
  - historic bonuses of $1–30/ac and ~$1–3/ac/yr rent;
  - Raser's private leases at ~$3.8/ac;
  - royalties of 2–5% paid only after production.

## 4. The missing middle: confirmed resource without a PPA

This is the rung a Terranox-shape company would sell at, and there is **almost no priced, arm's-length US comp**.
- **USG's 2017 review:** ~$10M liquidation value for ~100 MW of development projects, so **~$0.1M/MW**. A bidder walked away *because of* those assets.
- **Coyote Canyon (Terra-Gen 2021) and Enel's two greenfields (2023)** were bundled into operating deals at **zero separate price**.
- **The Crump Geyser JV (2011–12)** is the closest farm-in precedent. Nevada Geothermal Power and Ormat formed a 50:50 JV, with Ormat funding and managing, after slim holes plus a 5,000 ft well. The carry was undisclosed (NGP 6-K, via R04).
- **Exploration cost to get there:**
  - Ormat's FY2025 10-K: ~$10–50k per prospect for initial evaluation and **$1–5M per site before a full-size well** (via R04).
  - Literature: project-level exploration success is **<33%** (Wall & Dobson 2016, via L13). Full-size confirmation wells succeed ~50% of the time (GeoVision, via L13).
  - Zanskar claims Big Blind discovery cost **<$15k/MW**, against >$100k/MW historically (Z02, Z09).
- **Royalty alternative.** In the Centaurus financing of Fervo, Centaurus keeps a **$5.0/MWh royalty** after its priority returns (424B4, via R04). At ~7,900 MWh/MW-yr that is ~$39k/MW-yr, or **~$0.4M/MW PV** (8%, 25 yr, calc). This is a live precedent for a non-operator holding a per-MWh override.

### Why the middle is thin (and why that is the opportunity)
Buyers (Ormat, Invenergy) prefer to buy land themselves at auction and to buy operating cash flow. Developers' greenfield assets get no credit because:
- resource risk is binary;
- interconnection and PPA are not secured;
- the buyer's own exploration team discounts outside data.

A seller that delivers a **screened, drilled-to-temperature, interconnection-positioned** package, with data the buyer trusts, would be setting the first comps. R10 argues the biggest discrete jump comes at **"confirmed resource + interconnection position."** Blue Mountain's 51 MW of interconnection on 20 MW of plant, and Terra-Gen's 300–400 MW transmission line, are evidence that buyers pay for interconnection.

## 5. The lease-to-proven multiple

Translating $/acre into $/MW assumes ~3,000 leased acres per 20 MW hydrothermal project (unverified; footprints vary 5x).

| Step | Multiple | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Floor lease → recognised play | **25–100x** ($2–4 → $100–410/ac); ~300x at the $1,207 outlier | BLM sales; Fervo 424B4 |
| Floor lease → confirmed resource | ~$0.1–0.5M/MW × 20 MW / 3,000 ac ≈ **$670–3,300/ac**, i.e. **~150–800x the land cost** | USG review; R04 range (calc) |
| On all-in capital (land + $1–5M exploration per site, <33% success) | **~1–5x per drilled prospect**; higher if ML lowers cost per discovery toward Zanskar's claimed <$15k/MW. At $15k/MW cost and a $100–500k/MW sale: **7–33x** | Ormat 10-K; L13; Z09 (calc) |
| Confirmed → operating | $0.1–0.5M/MW → $4.3–5.6M/MW, but ~$4–7M/MW capex absorbs most of it | §2 |
| Land inside a contracted public developer | ~$12.9k per leased acre and ~$11.6M per PPA-MW (Fervo IPO, calc) | 424B4 |

**Bottom line.** On land alone the step-up runs 25–100x at disclosure and ~100x+ at confirmation. On fully loaded exploration capital the multiple is single-digit per site unless the discovery cost per MW collapses. That is exactly the claim an ML-first company has to make. The largest marked value is the **public-market rerating of contracted resource** (Fervo ~$7.7B on 658 MW of PPAs and ~596k acres bought at ~$4/ac). That rerating is roughly a ~3,000x paper mark-up on the land basis (calc).

## 6. Implications for a Terranox-shape geothermal company and the gap vs Zanskar

1. **The step-up is real but sits in two places, not one.**
   - (a) Land repricing at disclosure: $2–4 → $100–410/ac, documented in BLM auctions and Fervo's own prospectus.
   - (b) Contracted resource rerated by public markets: Fervo ~$11.6M per PPA-MW.
   - Operating plants trade near replacement cost ($4.3–5.6M/MW), so **building and owning plants is not where the excess return is.** This argues *for* the Terranox shape: own rights, de-risk cheaply, sell or royalty.
2. **"Confirmed resource without a PPA" is currently valued near zero** (USG ~$0.1M/MW; greenfields bundled free into Ormat deals). A Terranox-shape company should not plan to sell bare data-confirmed leases. It needs to carry each asset to a **buyer-trusted** state:
   - temperature at depth from TG/slim holes, which also meets BLM's $40/ac diligence requirement;
   - site control across federal, state and private land;
   - a filed interconnection position.
   - Then sell, farm out at a carry (the Crump Geyser pattern), or keep a **$/MWh override** (the Centaurus $5/MWh precedent, ~$0.4M/MW PV).
3. **The buyer universe is real and hungry.** Ormat has done four acquisitions since 2018, needs MW for its Google and NV Energy contracts, and invested $25M in Sage. Others: Invenergy, Fervo (40 GW pipeline), Sage, XGS, utilities, data-centre offtakers. Ormat already treats third-party resource holders as customers (Z08).
4. **The gap vs Zanskar:**
   - Zanskar is a vertically integrated developer. Its mission is to "develop, build, own, and operate." It has $180M of equity, owns Lightning Dock, a 100 MW NV Energy PPA and six planned 20 MW plants.
   - It is a modest lease buyer (~18 NV competitive parcels in 2022–25) and **cannot be a neutral seller** to Ormat, Fervo or Invenergy, because it competes with them.
   - Its capital goes into plants that trade at ~replacement cost.
   - The opening is a **neutral prospect generator and land/royalty bank**:
     - run ML over well logs, BHTs and TG holes across many basins;
     - quietly assemble state-trust and private rights, which avoid BLM's public nominations (R05, R06);
     - prove at slim-hole cost;
     - sell to *all* developers, including Zanskar, with retained overrides;
     - become the market that prices "confirmed, unbuilt" geothermal.
5. **Risks the comps expose:**
   - Raser (250k acres, equity wiped out) and USG (sold at roughly paid-in capital after ~15 years) show that land banks die without a monetisation path.
   - BLM All-Bids reports publish every bid, which leaks valuations.
   - Hot obvious ground is already bid to $100–400/ac by land banks.
   - The ML edge must be in *blind* targets bought at $2–20/ac.
   - The single most useful thing to establish next: **the first arm's-length priced sale of a confirmed-but-unbuilt US resource.** I found none.
