# Terranox for Geothermal: Final Memo

Date: 21 Oct 2026. Built from the 72 evidence files in `research/geothermal/evidence/`, the 30-play scoring run and 15 red-team votes. Numbers trace to primary sources (BLM sale PDFs, SEC filings, USGS/NREL/DOE reports, statutes). Anything not sourced is marked (unverified).

---

## 1. Verdict

**Yes, there is a Terranox-shaped geothermal company. But it is not "find heat, buy a lease, flip it."**

The best play is a **Neutral Rights Bank**: an asset-light owner of geothermal rights across 6+ western states. It is quietly filled from private ranch heat, state first-file leases and leftover BLM parcels. Then it carries the best prospects to a confirmation well and an interconnection position using other people's money. It sells or farms out to every developer and keeps a carry or a $/MWh override. Think Black Stone Minerals for heat, not a land flipper.

**Score: 20/25.** It scored 21 before red-teaming. All three verifiers wounded it and none killed it (revised to 20, 18 and 19). The merged shape below fixes the cheap-right leak and partly fixes the step-up gap, so 20 is the honest number.

Why it works:
- **Rights stay cheap where nobody looks.** Leftover BLM land costs $1/acre/yr. 53,686 acres were open over the counter on 10 Sep 2026. Ranch heat has leased for about $1/acre/yr.
- **Owning keeps the step-up.** Once a BLM lease is issued, its terms are fixed and never re-based to market.
- **Buyers are short of sites.** Ormat must source up to 150 MW of new Nevada geothermal for Google by 2028-30. Fervo has 658 MW of PPAs and a 3 GW Google framework.
- **Zanskar can't be the supplier.** It builds its own plants and competes with Ormat, Fervo and Invenergy.

The catch: no buyer has ever paid a big price for a confirmed resource that has no PPA. The only comp is about $0.1M/MW. Value jumps at confirmation plus PPA plus interconnection. So the bank has to carry assets to that point, not sell at the gradient-hole stage.

The oil-patch and Texas plays are refuted as flagships. All three verifiers refuted both. They survive only as later legs.

## 3. The GAP, ranked

1. **Be the neutral supplier.** Zanskar, Ormat and Fervo are all owner-operators. Nobody sells confirmed sites to every developer. Ormat's 150 MW Google gap, Fervo's 1 GW Google proposal, Invenergy, NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff and California's 1 GW firm mandate for 2031-37 are all buyers (B02, B03). Zanskar can't sell to its rivals.
2. **Acquire off-auction.** Zanskar buys at public auctions, where nominations leak and land reprices 116x after a discovery. Off-auction routes:
   - Private ranch heat (NV, OR, UT surface owners; Texas SB 785). No auction, no federal cap.
   - State first-file leases in UT, ID, NM and CA at about $1/acre/yr. California prospecting permits give 2-4 years of exclusivity and a lease entitlement (R05).
   - Day-after and two-year-window BLM filings. Day-after uptake fell from 58% (2023) to 3.7% (2025).
3. **Use labels nobody has read.**
   - The Chevron/Phillips TG archive: 786 released holes, 116 at 10°F/100ft or more across 28 prospects. Much of it was never released (L16).
   - Nevada geothermal well files rolling off 5-year confidentiality: 938 wells, 299 already public, the 2021-26 boom releasing through about 2031 (D03, D05).
   - Mining holes. They found about 250 MW of Nevada plants (McGinness, Blue Mountain, Don A. Campbell), and no published work extracts their temperatures (D06).
4. **Carry to confirmation with other people's money.** DOE cost-share (about 80% of Crump's Phase I), Crump-style farm-ins (developer pays the first $15M plus $2.5M cash, originator floored at 20%), and the April 2026 BLM categorical exclusion for exploration (B08, R03). Zanskar funds everything from its own balance sheet.
5. **Site for interconnection.** Score parcels on grid headroom as well as heat. Buyers pay for interconnection: Blue Mountain carried 51 MW for a 20 MW plant, and powered Texas land sold at about $0.47M/MW (R10, B06).
6. **EGS option on the same acreage.** Zanskar avoids EGS. Fervo-screen land (about 300-625°F at 13,000 ft or less) went from about $4 to $344-1,207/acre (L07). This is the size ladder.
7. **Sedimentary basins and Texas** (later leg). Real data edge (1.5M TIFF-only BEG logs, no RRC temperature field), but buyers have chosen the West so far.

## 4. The rights law test

The Terranox law: public bodies eventually reprice valuable rights to market, so the edge only lasts where the regime lets you lock the right at a fixed price before anyone knows. Geothermal mostly **confirms the law at the point of disclosure and breaks it at the edges.**

**Where the law holds (repricing is fast):**
- **BLM competitive auctions.** No preference right for the nominator. Parcel lists go public about 4 months before sale, and every bid is published after.
  - Nominated acreage rose from 25.8k (2020) to over 1M (2025); 27% were duplicates (GAO-26-108040).
  - Pumpernickel step-out: $2/acre (2023) to $232/acre (2025) a month after disclosure.
  - 2026 records: $701/acre NM, $1,207/acre UT.
- **State discretion.** Utah, Idaho and New Mexico officials can turn a first-file application into an auction. Arizona auctions every application.
- **Turkey.** First-come at a fixed fee (Law 5686), but the state's MTA auctions fields it proves, so the state captures the step-up on its own discoveries.
- **Arkansas lithium.** Regulators set the landowner royalty at about 3%. The repricing goes to the leaseholder, not the royalty holder.

**Where the law breaks (rights stay cheap once held):**
- **Issued BLM leases are never re-based.** Fixed 2005 terms: rent $1/acre noncompetitive, $2-3 competitive, $5 after year 10. Royalty 1.75% then 3.5%. Transfers cost $115 per lease and aren't repriced (R14).
- **The market is bimodal.** Nevada 2025 median winning bid was $3.50/acre, with 37% at the $2 floor. 97,120 acres went unbid with only 1 day-after filing. The Oct 2026 sale averaged about $24/acre, down from $32, and 23 of 83 parcels were unbid.
- **Private heat has no public price at all.**
  - Raser leased 4,444 NV acres for $5k on a 50-year term.
  - Texas SB 785 created a new heat estate in 2023 with no comps.
  - No statute reprices private rights.
- **Alberta.** First-come at C$625 plus C$3.50/ha/yr. Only 31 leases exist, and applications fell from 74 to 3 a year.
- **California prospecting permits.** 2-4 years exclusive, then a lease entitlement on discovery.

**Net:** the law is confirmed for any right you take in public view. It is broken for rights taken quietly (private, first-file, leftover) and then held. That is exactly why the best play acquires off-auction and never discloses before the whole fairway is locked. The binding federal limit is the 51,200 acres per state per corporate family cap, not price.

## 5. Four-part test for the best play

**1. Proving step that buyers trust: PASS (5/5).**
- About 50% of first wells in a new field succeed (IFC, 2,613 wells).
- DOE play fairway work got 2 of 2 blind hits in Nevada for $1.5M of shallow drilling: Gabbs Valley 124.9°C at 152 m (L01).
- Shallow holes cost $0.3-1.5M. Budget 500-900 m holes, since Argenta Rise missed at 244 m (L04).

**2. Illegible public data: PASS (4/5).**
- The TG archive, released NDOM files and mining holes are public or semi-public but unread.
- Public ML maps are commoditised: every model scores F1 below 0.10, and the limit is input data (L03).
- Docked one point because the released archive is copyable and the unreleased part sits with a Berkshire Hathaway Energy affiliate.

**3. Cheap right: PASS (4/5).**
- Noncompetitive BLM: about $520 fee plus $1/acre/yr. 100k acres of nominations cost about $17k.
- Ranch heat: about $1-10/acre.
- Adverse selection is real. Leftover parcels are what 18-22 bidders passed on. Only targets from new labels clear at the floor.

**4. Step-up and a buyer who pays: WEAK PASS (3/5).**
- Land: 25-100x at recognition, 116x after disclosure.
- Confirmed resource without a PPA: about $0.1M/MW. U.S. Geothermal's 100 MW of development projects had a $10M liquidation value, and a bidder walked away over them (B01).
- Operating plants: $4.3-5.6M/MW (Blue Mountain $88.7M for 20 MW).
- $5/MWh override: about $0.4M/MW in present value.
- Only one third-party farm-in on record (Crump Geyser, 2010).
- So the buyer pays at confirmation plus interconnection, through a carry, not at once.

**Size ladder: 4/5** (see section 6). Total: 5 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4 = **20**.

## 6. Size

- **Hydrothermal resource.** USGS: 30 GWe undiscovered (mean) against 9 GWe identified. Great Basin hidden: 23 GW P50, 79.5 GW P90, against under 0.6 GW installed (L05).
- **Federal cap math.** 51,200 acres × 6 states is about 307k federal acres. At San Emidio's density (17,846 acres for about 10 MW) and under-33% success, that is only about 0.5-3 GW of hydrothermal. Private and state land sit outside the cap and are 18-27% of developer acreage.
- **Value per GW.** 1 GW under a $5/MWh override is about $39M/yr, about $0.6B at 16x. $1B needs about 1.7-2.5 GW built on the bank's rights. $5-10B needs 10-20 GW.
- **EGS ladder.** DOE projects 90 GW by 2050 (300 GW high case), over 90% of it EGS. Fervo-density EGS is about 15 acres/MW, so 300k+ acres could underlie 10-20 GW.
- **Comps.** TPL $23.75B at 31.8x EBITDA. Viper $18B EV. Black Stone $3.1B. A pure flipper caps near EMX's $444M.

Honest read: hydrothermal alone gives a $0.3-1B company. The multi-billion case needs EGS-grade acreage under override, arriving after about 2032. That passes "not too small" on plausibility, as our rules allow.

## 7. Cheapest first test

**Phase 1: desk plus lock. 90 days, about $150-250k.**
1. Re-screen the 28 hot-hole TG prospects and the 299+ released NDOM wells against current BLM lease status (MLRS) and the 53,686-acre open list.
2. Add LLM extraction of temperatures from NBMG mining files and mining-company SEC hydrogeology sections.
3. Title-classify every parcel in the top cells: fee, Stock-Raising Homestead Act reserved (void for private leasing), state trust, BLM.
4. File noncompetitive applications on the top 10-20 open parcels (about $20-60k). Sign 5-10 ranch options covering about 30k acres for under $100k. Nominate the adjoining federal ground hidden among decoys.
5. Pitch one Crump-style package to Ormat's Google sourcing team under NDA.

**Pass bars:**
- At least 5 hot holes (10°F/100ft or more) on unleased or open ground.
- At least 15k acres locked for under $100k, with no repricing before lock.
- One signed LOI or term sheet within 6-9 months at a 20%+ carry or a $3-5/MWh override.

**Kill bars:**
- Fewer than 3 targets that aren't already held or failed.
- Buyers refuse any carry before a flow test, even with interconnection.
- Rivals file on our targets before we lock them.

**Phase 2 (if pass): 3-5 shallow 150-500 m holes, about $1-2M, under the April 2026 categorical exclusion,** aiming for a cost-shared confirmation well.

## 8. What kills it

1. **No step-up before the PPA.** Confirmed but uncontracted resource is about $0.1M/MW. If buyers only pay at COD, the bank becomes a capital-heavy developer, which is Zanskar's shape.
2. **Buyers self-source.** Ormat holds 434k acres and 34 US prospects and explores for about $10k per desk evaluation. Fervo holds 596k acres (256k idle) and $1.77B of IPO cash. Land is under 2% of capex, so they can just outbid.
3. **Leakage and copying.** The neutral model shows data rooms to many buyers. Land LLCs took about 52% of auction dollars and follow every signal.
4. **The cap.** 51,200 acres per state limits the federal book to about 0.5-3 GW of hydrothermal.
5. **Copyable data.** Enverus bought A2D (8M+ raster logs, basin temperature models, 350M courthouse records) for $100M plus $15M. Zanskar has the capital to copy the archive wedge, and its Freeport-McMoRan partnership may already reach the mining data.
6. **Sentiment.** Fervo closed at $9.02 on 21 Oct 2026 against a $27 IPO, about $1.5k EV per leased acre. The land-mark story is weaker than it looked in May.
7. **Raser precedent.** 250k+ acres, one plant built, equity wiped out in 2011. Land without confirmation goes to zero.

## 9. Literature review summary (24 deep-lit topics)

**L01 Play fairway.** DOE funded 11, then 6, then 5 projects (2014-21). Nevada spent $2.82M total and hit 2 of 2 blind systems (Gabbs Valley 124.9°C at 152 m; Granite Springs about 96°C at 250 m). Universities couldn't hold leases, and the 2019 Nevada sale cleared at about $4.17/acre. The value created went uncaptured. (osti.gov/servlets/purl/1724080)

**L02 Blind systems.** USGS 2008: 30.0 GWe undiscovered against 9.06 GWe identified. Coolbaugh 2006: about 157 undiscovered Nevada systems against 69 known. About 39% of known Great Basin systems are blind, and up to 75% of the resource. Most blind finds came by accident from mining and water holes. (pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2008/3082)

**L03 ML for geothermal.** Mordensky et al. 2025 put 85% of producing systems in the top 10% of map area. All western-US models score F1 below 0.10, and neural nets don't beat XGBoost. The ceiling is input data. No paper forward-tests an ML ranking against later drilling. (pdxscholar.library.pdx.edu/ece_fac/862)

**L04 INGENIOUS.** A $10M DOE/UNR project produced free regional layers (GDR 1391). Holes are capped at 1,000 ft and plugged. Argenta Rise, the top target, showed no anomaly at 244 m. The maps are now free; the edge goes to whoever acts first. (gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391)

**L05 USGS assessment.** 9,057 MWe identified, 30,033 MWe undiscovered, 517,800 MWe EGS. Great Basin hidden 23 GW P50 to 79.5 GW P90 at $58-62/MWh (Trainor-Guitton 2025). Half of operating plants sit on hidden systems. (osti.gov/pages/servlets/purl/3018341)

**L06 NREL GeoVision and Liftoff.** 90 GW by 2050 base, 300 GW high, over 90% EGS. GeoVision fixes discovery at 1-3% of undiscovered resource per year and gives no credit to better exploration, so ML lift is outside official forecasts. Pre-proof premium: 50% confirmation success, about 6 extra points of cost of capital. (energy.gov/eere/geothermal/geovision)

**L07 Utah FORGE and Fervo.** Cape Station's first 33 MW unit reached COD on 30 Sep 2026. Well cost fell from $9.4M to $4.8M (35% learning rate). Per-well output is disputed: about 1.4-2 MW net by a critic. Fervo land went from about $4 to $344-1,207/acre. (sec.gov Fervo S-1)

**L08 Superhot.** 25+ wells have passed 374°C at 2-4.7 km. IDDP-1 shut on corrosion after about 2 years. CATF finds MW per well, not drilling cost, drives LCOE. No commercial superhot power yet. Good upside tranche, too niche alone. (Reinsch et al. 2017)

**L09 Temperature at depth.** Raw BHTs read about 22 ± 10°C cold. Stanford's 400k-BHT model has 4.8°C mean error, rising to 7.9°C at 2-4 km and 11.2°C at 4-6 km. Kriging nearly matches neural nets. Schölderle 2022: bad metadata, not the correction method, is the main error. (arxiv.org/abs/2403.09961; mdpi.com/1996-1073/15/17/6367)

**L10 Sedimentary.** USGS mapped over 400,000 km² of the Gulf Coast at 90-150°C within 6 km. Permeability binds: Virginia Hills heat-in-place overstates deliverable power about 7x (0.085 MWe per well). Texas SB 785 and Alberta first-come tenure make rights cheap. Sites are small, 5-30 MW. (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271309)

**L11 Co-produced.** NREL puts realistic US co-produced potential at about 300 MWe. Wells produced for water alone give 10-200x the power of co-production (UND/Continental). Swan Hills went through CCAA. Only about 500 of 600k Alberta wells exceed 120°C. Too small on its own.

**L12 Lithium brines.** Salton brine lithium is worth 3-5x the power per MW-yr. USGS random forest gives 5.1-19 Mt Li in the Smackover but at 1.5 mi² resolution (Knierim 2024). DLE costs about $10k/t rising to $22k/t with brine uncertainty. Federal leases don't cleanly convey high-grade lithium. (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70259385)

**L13 Exploration risk.** IFC 2013: 50% first-well success, 78% overall. Wall & Dobson: exploration leads to full-size development wells under one-third of the time. Exploration-stage equity costs about 35%. Insurance has catalysed only "a few tens of MW." Portfolio scale is the structural fix. (osti.gov/servlets/purl/1394062)

**L14 Geophysics.** 70-75% of Great Basin resources are blind. Helicopter EM beats MT for prospecting. Earth MRI regional EM will be released publicly. Mining records could cut LCOE 23-29%. BRIDGE notes prospects stalled on land access. (osti.gov/biblio/2540220)

**L15 Geochemistry.** The Water Quality Portal holds 290M+ records from 2.7M+ sites. GeoThermalCloud's open ML flags 36% of 14,258 Great Basin points as high prospectivity, which separates weakly. Helium isotopes flag hidden permeability (Kennedy & van Soest 2007). Zanskar doesn't mention geochemistry.

**L16 TG holes.** Sass et al. 1999: Chevron and Phillips evaluated 75+ prospects. DOE bought only "several hundred" holes from CalEnergy. Released file: 786 holes, 116 at 10°F/100ft or more in 28 prospects. Mining holes found about 220-250 MW of Nevada plants. (pubs.usgs.gov/of/1999/of99-425)

**L17 EGS siting.** Only 0.27% of US land (about 5.4M acres) has a cost-optimal depth of 4 km or less, mostly western. Denser regional data raised 3-3.5 km potential 1.8-3.8x and added about 230 GWe. Siting needs temperature, rock and stress, not a flow test. (docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy19osti/71833.pdf)

**L18 Closed-loop.** Closed loop costs $275-560/MWh against $64-98 for EGS in the same rock (Frash 2024). Sage's own tiers: only 53 GW at 180°C and 2-3 km. Closed loop does not make "any hot rock" valuable; shallow heat stays scarce. (osti.gov/servlets/purl/3014949)

**L19 International.** 16,873 MW installed globally. Most countries tender rights through the state. Chile (open concessions, 48 MW built) and Türkiye (first-come licences) are the second markets. Only New Zealand has a free legacy archive like Saskatchewan's. Upside, not beachhead.

**L20 Canada/Alberta.** AR 251/2021: first-come, C$3.50/ha/yr, 31 leases with 6 holders. Year-5 renewal needs physical work on site. Western Alberta about 1,150 MWe (Banks & Harris 2018), mostly 80-150°C. Good second geography, too small alone. (kings-printer.alberta.ca/documents/Regs/2021_251.pdf)

**L21 Cost learning.** Cheaper drilling lowers the value of knowing where natural permeability is (Zanskar's specialty) and raises the value of knowing where heat is at drillable depth. Land at $1,207/acre is under 2% of a 50 MW block's capex. Land is the scarce input.

**L22 Structural ML.** Faulds & Hinz 2015: 32% of systems at step-overs, 25% at fault ends, 22% at intersections, 1% on plain range fronts. Regional stress maps miss rotations of up to 90° over tens of km (Lund Snee & Zoback 2020). The structural layers are public; new structural data and leases are the edge.

**L23 Data-centre demand.** 26 US geothermal PPAs, 1,642 MWe, 2021 to mid-2025, 60% next-gen. NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff lets customers of 5 MW+ fund named resources. Rhodium: 15-17 GW of hyperscale demand. No hyperscaler price is public. (osti.gov/biblio/3014949; rhg.com)

**L24 History of booms.** At the 2010 peak, 92% of federal leases didn't produce; leases fell from 785 to 568. Raser spent $123.5M for 6 MW net and was wiped out. NGP Blue Mountain breached its loan. The miner who sold leases plus data before proof did fine. The plant-building layer lost money, not the land layer. (blm.gov pls10.pdf)
