What the company does

  1. Reads records nobody has read. The Chevron/Phillips temperature-gradient hole archive has 786 released holes, 116 of them hot, across 28 prospects. Nevada geothermal well files roll off 5-year confidentiality (938 wells, 299 already public, the 2021-26 boom releasing through about 2031). Mining drill holes found about 250 MW of Nevada plants by accident, and no published work pulls their temperatures.
  2. Takes the right quietly, off-auction. Ranch heat (Raser leased 4,444 Nevada acres for $5k), state first-file leases at about $1/acre/yr, and leftover BLM parcels at $1/acre/yr (53,686 acres were open on 10 Sep 2026).
  3. Carries the best prospects to a confirmation well with other people's money. DOE cost-share and Crump-style farm-ins, where the developer pays the first $15M and the originator keeps at least 20%.
  4. Sells to everyone. Ormat must find up to 150 MW for Google by 2028-30. Fervo has a 3 GW Google framework. Invenergy, NV Energy's Clean Transition Tariff and California's 1 GW firm-power mandate are buyers too. The bank keeps a 20%+ carry or a $3-5/MWh royalty.
  5. Holds an EGS option on the same acres. Fervo-grade land went from about $4 to $344-1,207/acre at auction. That is the size ladder after about 2032.

Does geothermal break the repricing law?

Partly. The law holds at the auction: nominated acreage rose from 26k (2020) to over 1M (2025), bids are published, and land reprices 116x once someone discloses a find. It breaks once a right is held. Issued BLM leases are never re-based to market (rent $1-3/acre, royalty 1.75% then 3.5%, transfers cost $115). Private heat has no public price at all, and Texas created a new, unpriced heat estate for surface owners in 2023. The real limit is the 51,200-acre-per-state federal cap, not price.

So the rule is: take rights quietly, hold them, and never disclose before the whole fairway is locked.

Is it big enough

USGS puts undiscovered hydrothermal at 30 GW against 9 GW identified, and the hidden Great Basin resource at 23 GW (P50). 1 GW under a $5/MWh royalty is about $39M a year, roughly $0.6B in value. Hydrothermal alone supports a $0.3-1B company. The multi-billion case needs EGS acreage under royalty after about 2032, against comps like Texas Pacific Land ($23.75B) and Viper ($18B EV).

What kills it

  • Buyers won't pay before a PPA, so the bank turns into a capital-heavy developer like Zanskar.
  • Buyers source their own land: Ormat holds 434k acres, Fervo 596k, and land is under 2% of capex.
  • Data rooms leak to many buyers; Enverus already owns 8M+ raster logs.
  • Raser held 250k+ acres, built one plant, and was wiped out in 2011.

Plays that were tried and refuted

Texas Deep-Estate Bank and Oil-Patch EGS Land Bank both scored 21 but were refuted 3 of 3. Texas Pacific Land already owns the hot Delaware heat, lithium sits with the mineral estate and is leased up, and buyers chose Nevada over Texas. Alberta's first-come leases are cheap but too small. Superhot rock and closed loop are upside, not a beachhead. 30 plays were scored in all.
