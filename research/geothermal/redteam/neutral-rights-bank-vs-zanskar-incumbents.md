# Red team: "Neutral Rights Bank" (Black Stone/TPL of geothermal), seen through Zanskar and the incumbents

*2026-10-21. The workflow's shared WebSearch budget (200 calls) was already used up when this agent started, so no new web searches ran. This file rests on the primary-source evidence files in /home/user/nuketime/research/geothermal/evidence/ (BLM all-bids PDFs, SEC filings, company releases), cited by file ID and their URLs. A follow-up turn with fresh search budget should check whether Zanskar, Ormat or Enverus announced any royalty, farm-out or geothermal data product after Sep 2026 (unverified).*

## Verdict

**Not refuted, but weakened.** No incumbent occupies the neutral originate-prove-farm-out-and-keep-an-override role today:
- Zanskar is an integrated IPP.
- Ormat and Fervo are owner-operators.
- Enverus and TGS sell data and take no land.
- The land-bank LLCs speculate without data.

Three findings cut the score, though:
1. The data edge can be copied by the incumbents.
2. The cheap-right edge is public and open to everyone.
3. The value-capture point sits later than the gradient hole, and the buyers have shown they prefer to own land outright.

**The pure "lease plus TG hole, then farm out" form does not survive.** A capital-heavier form does: carry each prospect to a confirmation well plus an interconnection position, with private and state rights providing the stealth layer. **Revised total: 18/25** (down from 21).

## Rung-by-rung attack

### 1. Is Zanskar already doing this, or could it trivially pivot?

**Doing it: no.**
- Zanskar describes itself as building to "develop, build, own, and operate" plants (Series C release, https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-raises-115m-series-c-following).
- Its 100 MW NV Energy deal is build-operate-transfer (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-inks-100-mw-power-purchase).
- It discloses no licensing, prospect sales, farm-outs or royalties (Z05).
- It hires for drilling, project finance and O&M (Z06).

**Could it pivot: yes, technically.**
- It holds $180M of equity plus a $40M non-recourse development facility designed to grow to $100M (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-closes-40m-in-novel-development).
- Its CTO admits "dozens" more sites than it can build (Z03).
- The GEODE agreement with California Community Power is already a prospect-for-offtaker origination framework (https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-and-cc-power-sign-geode-agreement).
- Selling its surplus prospects to rivals would cost it little.

**Why it probably won't:** the structural argument holds.
- Zanskar competes with Ormat, Fervo and Invenergy for the same NV Energy and CTT PPAs, so feeding them prospects would arm its rivals.
- Its financing story ("at least 10 confirmed sites to attract project finance", Z01/Z05) needs it to keep its best sites.

**Residual threat: medium.** Zanskar can always outbid on step-outs next to its own discoveries; it lost Pumpernickel NV-2025-10-2143 by $1/ac (https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2025-10/NV_Geot_20251021_Bidding_Results.pdf). The ML layer is no moat either: Zanskar open-sourced GeoCore (LightGBM on public INGENIOUS layers; https://github.com/zanskar-geothermal/GeoCore).

### 2. Is Ormat already doing it in-house? (The strongest attack.)

Ormat already runs the playbook: data, cheap right, owned step-up. It just runs it for itself.
- **Don A. Campbell (42 MW):** Ormat reviewed mining-hole data obtained "at no cost", nominated the land in 2001 and won it at auction in 2007 (D06).
- **McGinness Hills (~160 MW):** the same path, built on Anaconda/FMC/Newcrest holes (D06).
- The "mining-hole temperatures" wedge in the pitch is therefore Ormat's historical edge, not an untouched gap.

Ormat's exploration is also cheap and well funded:
- About $10k per desk evaluation and $1-5M per site before a full-size well (FY2025 10-K, Z08).
- Unsuccessful exploration was written off at only $1.4M (2025) and $3.9M (2024).
- 34 US prospects (23 in Nevada) and 434,430 US acres, up 59k in one year (Z08).

**Implication:** a company whose dry-hole write-offs run about $1-4M a year has little reason to pay a large premium for a prospect de-risked only to the gradient-hole stage. Ormat pays up for *operating* MW: $4.4M/MW for Blue Mountain (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000129644525000008/ora-20250630.htm), about 8x EBITDA for Enel (R04).

**The only third-party farm-in on record is Crump Geyser (2010):** Ormat paid the first $15M plus $2.5M cash for 50% (B08; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1177440/000117625610001045/exhibit4-158.htm). It took place:
- after slim holes **and** a 5,000 ft full-size well;
- with a DOE grant attached.

The resource came in marginal at about 130 °C. It is a single deal, 16 years old, and done at the confirmation stage.

**Counter-point that favours the play:** Ormat must find up to 150 MW of *new* Nevada geothermal for Google, with deliveries 2028-30 (Feb 2026 8-K, B02). Its 10-K also says a party that outbids it "may still become a customer of Ormat's Product Segment" (Z08). Ormat is a plausible buyer, but of **confirmed** MW on a 2-4 year clock, not of TG-hole acreage.

### 3. Fervo: a buyer, but also a competing seller

- Fervo holds about 595,900 acres at about $4/acre, and about 256,000 of those are idle "Prospects" with no work started (424B4, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm).
- It has $1.77B of net IPO proceeds.
- It is the natural buyer of EGS-grade acreage. It is also a would-be farm-out source for land it won't develop, which makes it a direct competitor to a rights bank for the attention of Sage, XGS and Mazama.
- With $1.8B in hand it will prefer to buy land outright over accepting a permanent override (B07 concedes "expect a price concession").

**The "Centaurus $5/MWh precedent" is weaker than it is pitched.** That royalty came attached to **$75M of preferred project equity** (B02). It is a financing royalty, not a land-origination override. No geothermal developer has been found paying an override to a mere lease originator.

### 4. Enverus/TGS: can a data incumbent copy the data edge?

- On 8 Jul 2026 Enverus bought TGS's A2D library: more than 8M raster logs, more than 1.9M LAS files, more than 5M proprietary logs and more than 2M interpretations **including "basin temperature models"**. It already holds 350M+ courthouse ownership records (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/enverus-acquires-a2d-well-log-library-from-tgs-connecting-subsurface-data-to-energy-decision-workflows-302820900.html; https://www.tgs.com/press-releases/tgs-sells-north-american-well-data-products-business-to-enverus).
- That makes Enverus the one party that could ship a geothermal favourability-plus-ownership layer quickly. It would **sell that layer to everyone**, which erodes any first-mover information edge, especially on private and split-estate heat rights in sedimentary basins.
- Enverus takes no land (D08). It is a copier of the data edge, not of the business shape.

### 5. The specific data claims, attacked

**The 786 released Chevron/Phillips TG holes are public.**
- The released set includes 116 holes at 10 °F/100 ft or more across 28 prospects (L16).
- These holes were drilled because Chevron and Phillips had already flagged the prospects in the 1970s-80s. Many sit in known geothermal areas (San Emidio's 91.5 °F/100 ft hole lies inside an operating Ormat/USG field).
- Re-screening them against current lease status is a weekend task for Ormat or Zanskar.

**The unreleased remainder sits with CalEnergy** (Berkshire Hathaway Energy, which also owns NV Energy) (L16). An exclusive licence is possible but would have to be negotiated with an incumbent-affiliated holder. Whether it is still extant or accessible is unverified.

**NDOM wells rolling off confidentiality are public to all on release** (938 wells, 299 past the window; D03/D05). That gives a speed edge, not an exclusive one.

**Mining-company hole data** remains the most defensible wedge: it can be locked up with *exclusive* licences or data-for-royalty swaps (D06). Zanskar does list Freeport-McMoRan as a partner, though, with no details given (https://www.zanskar.com/who-we-work-with/). This channel may already be contested (unverified).

### 6. Cheap-right attack: is it copyable by land banks?

**Yes, at the access layer:**
- Anonymous LLCs (Buffalo River, Percheron, FLHN) took about 52% of 2022-25 NV+UT bonus dollars (Z08).
- Nominations and bids are public, and the land beside Pumpernickel repriced about 116x within a month of disclosure (Z04).

**But the leftovers are unscreened:**
- 26 of 27 unsold parcels from Oct 2025 drew no day-after filing.
- About 53,686 acres were still open at $1/ac in Sep 2026 (R02, https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2025-10/NV_Geot_20251027_Non_Comp_Sale_Results.pdf).

So the edge is *picking*, and the incumbents have shown they don't bother picking the unsold remainder. The caps still bind:
- 51,200 ac per state, including indirect holdings above 10% (R02);
- $40/ac of work by year 10.

These keep a pure federal bank to about 10 maximum-size leases per state.

## The killer, and the best AI-leveraged answer

**Killer.** No arm's-length sale of confirmed-but-unbuilt geothermal exists:
- U.S. Geothermal's development portfolio of about 100 MW carried a $10M liquidation value (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1172136/000106299318001265/defm14a.htm).
- Enel's greenfields were priced at about $0 inside a $271M deal (R04).
- The value jumps at PPA plus interconnection.

Meanwhile Ormat runs cheap in-house exploration and Fervo has cash and idle acreage. The incumbent buyers therefore will not pay a rights bank much at the TG-hole stage. Raser is the warning case: 250k acres, 8 projects, 1 built, equity wiped (L24).

**Best AI answer:**
1. **Use ML to cut the cost of carrying each prospect to a confirmation well plus an interconnection request, so the bank sells at the stage where value actually jumps.** Use DOE cost-share (Crump got about 80% in Phase I, B08) and farm-in money to fund the confirmation well.
2. **Use LLM title extraction (courthouse, split-estate, state trust records) to lock private and state heat rights quietly.** These rights carry no federal cap and no public nomination, which removes both the copy risk and the cap.
3. **Use exclusive mining-hole licences, paid for with royalty swaps, as the proprietary label source.**
4. **Package each prospect as lease + TG/slimhole data + queue position + water** (R09/R10), sell it into Ormat's 150 MW Google gap and the NV Energy CTT window, and keep a carry of at least 20% (the Crump floor) instead of a bare override.

**Does it rescue the play? Partly.** It rescues a "prospect generator to the confirmation stage". That company needs roughly $5-20M per confirmation well (Crump's $15M first tranche, B08), which is far heavier than the $17k nomination pitch. The pure asset-light Black Stone shape is not rescued until there are 10+ GW under contract, and that needs EGS-grade acreage sold to Fervo-like buyers.
