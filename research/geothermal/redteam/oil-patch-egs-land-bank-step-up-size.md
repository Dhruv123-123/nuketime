## Verdict

**Partly refuted. The stated step-up does not exist for oil-patch land, and the $1B case rests on a valuation mark that has since collapsed. A narrower version survives.**

- **Rung 4 (a buyer paying a big multiple at once) fails for the oil patch as pitched.** Every step-up comp in the pitch is federal Great Basin land next to a proven or disclosed system: BLM Utah/Nevada at $344–1,207/ac, and Cape/FORGE. No heat-right transaction at any price has been recorded in Texas, Oklahoma, the DJ, the Williston or Alberta. The named buyers are long land and have sited their projects elsewhere.
- **Rung 5 ($1B+) is weakened, not killed.** The pitch's size arithmetic uses Fervo's IPO mark of about $12.6–12.9k per leased acre. On 21 Oct 2026 Fervo closed at **$9.02**, against a $27 IPO price, for a **$2.66B market cap and an EV of about $891M**. That is about **$1.5k of EV per leased acre** (calc), even after Cape Station reached COD and Fervo signed a 396 MW Google PPA. Almost all of that EV belongs to PPAs and Cape, not to raw acreage. A land bank that sells into this market captures land's share of project value, which is under 2% of capex (L21). A $1B outcome therefore needs several GW of third-party EGS built on its acreage, which is a 2030s event.
- **Best AI-leveraged rescue:** turn the play into a "hot powered-land" developer for ERCOT large loads and keep a $/MWh override on any geothermal built. This rescues c4 partially, because powered land has a priced buyer today ($0.47M/MW, B06). But that step-up is paid for interconnection, not heat, so the geothermal data edge becomes a secondary feature. **Partial rescue.**

**Revised total: 17/25** (from 21).

## Rung 4: is there a buyer paying a big multiple at once?

### 4a. The comps are the wrong land

| Pitch comp | What it actually prices | Transfers to oil-patch private fee? |
|---|---|---|
| Fervo ~$4/ac → Cape/FORGE $1,207/ac (L07, L21) | BLM Utah land adjacent to the world's only commercial EGS field, after FORGE's state-funded data and Fervo's public results | **No.** It prices proximity to a proven field plus federal tenure, not "hot rock on a map" |
| 2025 BLM max bids $344 (UT) / $410 (NV) (Fervo 424B4, B01) | Great Basin hydrothermal/EGS fairways, bid mostly by land banks (Buffalo River, Percheron, FLHN, Invenergy, Cordillera) | **No.** Different estate, different buyers, all in the West |
| Fervo IPO ~$12.9k per leased acre (B01) | Equity value of 658 MW of PPAs plus Cape, divided by acreage | **No.** See 4c. It is not a land price, and the mark has since fallen about 67% |
| Standard Lithium/Equinor $2–4k/ac (A02) | The **mineral** brine estate in the Smackover | **Only if you hold the mineral estate.** SB 785 §141.004(c) excludes dissolved minerals from the surface heat estate, and that ground is already leased by Exxon, Chevron and Standard Lithium (sibling red team) |
| Texas powered land ~$0.47M/MW (B06) | Grid interconnection and a data-centre site | **Yes, but it is not a geothermal step-up** |

B01 states the core fact plainly: **"confirmed resource without a PPA is currently valued near zero."**
- In U.S. Geothermal's 2017 review, about 100 MW of development projects carried a ~$10M liquidation value, roughly $0.1M/MW, and a bidder (Party G) withdrew *because of* those assets.
- Greenfield sites went into the Terra-Gen 2021 and Enel 2023 Ormat deals at no separate price.
- No arm's-length sale of a confirmed but unbuilt US geothermal resource was found anywhere in the evidence set (B01, B02, Z09).

If confirmed hydrothermal resource in Nevada barely trades, *unconfirmed* EGS-grade heat on Texas fee land trades less.

### 4b. The named buyers are land-long and have chosen the West

- **Fervo** holds ~595,900 acres in CA, CO, ID, NV, NM, UT and WA, none in Texas. About **256,000 acres sit idle as "Prospects"** (424B4; Z08). Its new hyperscaler demand is being served by **densifying Cape Station**, not by new land. The 396 MW Google PPA (Sep 2026) is supplied from Cape's expansion in repeatable GeoBlocks, with delivery by June 2030 (ThinkGeoEnergy). Phase I is 3 × 33 MW, the first block reached COD on 1 Oct 2026, and Phase II (~400 MW) is targeted for 2028. One proven field can absorb a GW-scale pipeline, so Fervo has no pressing reason to buy Texas heat.
- **Sage**, Texas-native with a Meta deal for up to 150 MW, chose **Ormat's Blue Mountain, Nevada** for its next commercial EGS facility (Sep 2026; R07, B01).
- **SLB-Ormat** chose Desert Peak, NV (sibling red team).
- **Ormat** holds ~434k acres and 34 US prospects (23 in NV), with no Texas position (Z08).
- **XGS/Meta** went to New Mexico (B02).
- **Devon**, a Fervo investor and the pitch's "data partner", has no disclosed geothermal-rights activity (A01).

**Reading:** there is no evidence of demand for Texas, Midcontinent or Rockies EGS heat rights at any price. "Sell to Fervo or Sage" assumes demand that the record contradicts. Selling the same right to several developers at once (pitting them against each other) is not available when none of them wants it.

### 4c. Land is too small a slice of value to command a big multiple

- Land for 50 MW at $1,207/ac is **under 2% of capex** (L21). That is the pitch's argument that developers *can* pay more. It is also the reason they will not pay a venture-scale amount. The resource risk, the drilling ($7,000/kW FOAK falling to $3,000/kW) and the PPA hold the value, and developers price land as a cost line.
- The live non-operator precedent is Centaurus's **$5/MWh royalty** on Fervo, worth **about $0.4M/MW PV** (B01 calc). That beats a land sale, but it is paid only on MW actually built.
- Raser held 250k+ acres and its **equity was cancelled** in its 2011 Chapter 11. USG sold after ~15 years at roughly its paid-in capital (B01, R04). Geothermal land banks without a monetisation path go to zero.

### 4d. The oil-patch rock mostly fails the Fervo screen

- Aljubran & Horne (Stanford, 2024) find cost-optimal depth of 4 km or less on only **0.27% of US land, about 5.4M acres, "mostly in the West"** (L17, L07). That scarcity is mainly Great Basin, Snake River, Imperial and NM/CO, not the Midcontinent.
- **Sage's own count:** 53 GW at 180 °C and 2–3 km, about 1.3% of L48 potential (L18).
- **Kansas median BHT is 47 °C** (D02).
- **Alberta:** 100–210 °C at 3–5 km only in "hot" zones. The Edmonton block shows 122 °C at 3.5 km, and projects target about 120 °C at 4.5 km or less (L20), well below 175–230 °C.
- **Texas:** the hot ≤4 km EGS ground is the deep Delaware/Val Verde (BHTs 120–180 °C; ~195 °C modelled at 3.5 km in Pecos). Under SB 785 the heat there belongs to the surface owner, mainly **TPL (~880k surface acres, already doing Chevron data-centre power deals in Reeves County) and University Lands (~2.1M acres)** (sibling red team, B07). Fragmented rancher ground is mostly cooler East Texas and Gulf Coast, at ~31–33 °C/km.
- **DFW, Austin and San Antonio** are Rhodium's 4.1 GW Texas BTM demand (B06). Whether those metros have 200 °C at 4 km or less is (unverified). The pitch's "≥200 °C at 4 km near DFW or Austin load" is likely an empty set (unverified; to test first).

## Rung 5: is it a $1B+ company?

**Pitch arithmetic:** 200k acres × $12.6k/ac (Fervo IPO) = ~$2.5B.

**Corrected with live marks:**

| Mark | Per acre | 200k ac |
|---|---|---|
| Fervo IPO equity (May 2026) / 596k ac | ~$12.9k | ~$2.6B |
| Fervo market cap $2.66B (21 Oct 2026) / 596k ac | ~$4.5k | ~$0.9B |
| Fervo **EV ~$891M** / 596k ac | **~$1.5k** | **~$0.3B** |
| Best raw-land auction (UT 2026) | $1,207 | $0.24B |
| Typical 2025 BLM hot-play average (UT Apr-2025) | ~$112 | $22M |

All per-acre figures are calc. The FRVO marks are from StockAnalysis (third-party) and should be checked against filings.

**Why even the corrected row overstates:**
- Fervo's EV belongs to a **contracted, operating developer with ~1 GW of PPAs**, not to idle acreage. 256k of its own acres are idle.
- The market is now paying ~$1.35M of EV per PPA-MW (calc, $891M / 658 MW). Even contracted EGS is priced far below the IPO's ~$11.6M/PPA-MW.
- Fervo's ~55% drawdown since August came despite COD and a 396 MW Google PPA. That signals the market is discounting EGS execution risk and the step-up multiples it will pay (cause of the drop unverified).

**Paths to $1B for a land bank:**
1. **Sell land:** at $300–1,200/ac realised, you need **0.8–3.3M acres sold**. That is 15–60% of the entire national ≤4 km cost-optimal pool, most of which is western federal land, not oil-patch fee land. **Implausible.**
2. **Royalty:** at ~$0.4M/MW PV, you need **~2.5 GW of third-party EGS built on your acreage**. Rhodium's national BTM geothermal for data centres is 15–17 GW by the early 2030s, and Texas is 4.1 GW of it (B06). Getting 2.5 GW means capturing ~15% of national BTM geothermal, on land where no EGS developer has yet sited. **Plausible only post-2032 and only if ERCOT EGS happens at scale.** Under the "assume the tech works" rule this counts, but the timing is long and the cash flow arrives late.
3. **Multi-layer (heat + lithium + helium + pore space):** lithium needs the mineral estate, which is contested and already leased. Helium and pore-space values are (unverified) and lumpy. These add options, but each layer has its own incumbent (sibling red team).
4. **The TPL analogue:** a Texas surface/royalty company *can* be worth $23.75B (B07). But TPL's value is oil royalties and water on land it already owns, and it holds the hottest Delaware heat estate by default. The analogue shows the ceiling is large and that the incumbent already occupies it.

**Size verdict:** not "too small" in principle. The EGS resource is GW-scale (USGS 518 GWe; Sage's 562 GW at 3–4 km). But no current market mark supports $1B for an oil-patch heat land bank before ~2030. The size rung drops from 5 to 4.

## The best AI-leveraged answers to the killers

**A. "Hot powered land" for ERCOT large loads (best answer; partial rescue of c4).**
- **The AI step:** join LLM-read BHTs with the RRC well inventory (158,121 inactive wells as re-entry candidates), substation and transmission maps, the ERCOT large-load queue (438 GW), gas pipelines and fibre.
- **The target:** parcels that are hot (Val Verde, the Delaware edge outside TPL, Gulf geopressured) **and** interconnection- or gas-adjacent.
- **The deal:** option the surface (heat estate) plus a site lease, file or secure the interconnection position, and sell to data-centre developers as powered land at ~$0.47M/MW (Graham, TX comp; B06). Keep a $/MWh geothermal override for when EGS gets cheap.
- **What it fixes:** a buyer exists today and pays a big multiple at once (~630x raw land, B06).
- **What it does not fix:** the multiple pays for **interconnection, not heat**. The company becomes a powered-land developer competing with TPL, Stream and utilities, and the geothermal data edge becomes a secondary feature. It owns an asset (good under the standing rules), but the asset is grid access.

**B. Black Stone-style multi-basin royalty bank (structural answer; rescues c5 in time, not c4 now).**
- **The model:** build prospects in-house, offer them to developers under development agreements with minimum drilling commitments, and keep a 2–5% gross or $5/MWh override (B07, B01).
- **The footprint:** pair Texas/Alberta with the western ≤4 km pool where demand already exists (Utah/Nevada private and state-trust land, which avoids BLM's public nominations; R05).
- **What it fixes:** the ceiling becomes GW-scale royalties, and the land is sited where buyers actually are.
- **What it does not fix:** it abandons "oil-patch" as the flagship. The oil-patch data (BHT headers) shrinks to a supporting layer.

**C. Sell the BHT-corrected temperature map (no rescue).** This is a tool or service shape, scored low under the standing rules. Enverus, which owns A2D's 8M rasters plus 350M courthouse records (D08), can copy it.

## Cheapest kill test for the step-up

Spend 2–4 weeks and under $10k:
1. **Call Fervo, Sage, XGS, Mazama and Eavor land/BD teams** with one question: "Would you pay $X/ac or a $/MWh override for a 640-ac Texas fee block with a measured 200 °C at 4 km?" Without an LOI-grade "yes" from at least one of them, c4 stays at 2.
2. **Run the BHT model on the free Kansas set plus the public SMU/GDR Texas data** to check whether *any* ≥200 °C at ≤4 km cells lie within ~25 km of DFW, Austin or San Antonio (Rhodium's BTM radius). If none do, the hyperscaler half of the pitch dies and only rescue A survives.
