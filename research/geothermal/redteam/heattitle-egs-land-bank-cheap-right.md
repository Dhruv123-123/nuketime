## Verdict

**Not killed, but the multi-basin version is refuted. Only Texas private surface heat passes this test.**

The question is whether the right stays cheap once you move on it or once ML spreads. The answer depends on who the counterparty is:

| Leg of the play | Cheap today? | Stays cheap after you move or ML spreads? | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Texas private surface heat (SB 785), fragmented owners | Yes | **Mostly yes** | No nomination, no auction, and no price comps. Repricing comes from holdouts and neighbours talking, not from a public auction |
| Texas Permian trophy cells (TPL, University Lands) | **No** | n/a | Sophisticated holders that already own the heat by statute |
| Texas GLO state land | No longer quiet | **No** | Statutory sealed bid. In Aug 2026 the GLO opened an RFI that leads to an RFP and screens for *developers* |
| DJ / Uinta federal (BLM) ground | Only at the floor, and only if nobody looks | **No** | Nominations publish the legal description 4-16 months before the sale, nominators get no preference right, and all bids are published |
| Oklahoma and Colorado private | Unknown | Unknown | Who owns the heat is unverified. If it follows the mineral estate or water law, the counterparty is the most legible owner in America, or a state permit |

The real problem is that **the Texas right stays cheap mainly because nobody is paying for it yet**, not because it is mispriced. The cheap-right rung survives there. The step-up rung (c4) is where the play bleeds, and this lens confirms that.

## Rung 1: Federal and state legs reprice the moment you act

**BLM (most of the Uinta's non-tribal ground, and federal parts of the DJ).**
- Nominating costs $150 plus $0.14/acre. The nomination publishes the township, range and section; BLM sale notices name the land but not the nominator. The Nevada nomination deadline for the Oct 2028 sale is 30 Jun 2027, about 16 months ahead. The nominator gets **no preference right**, the auction is an ascending English auction, and BLM then publishes every bid by name (R03; 43 CFR 3203.5-3203.15).
- Crowding is already visible. Nominated acreage went from about 25.8k acres in 2020 to more than 1M acres in 2025, and about 27% of nominated acres were rejected as duplicates (R03, citing GAO-26-108040).
- The repricing has already happened. Zanskar bought Pumpernickel step-out ground at $2/acre in Nov 2023. After it announced Pumpernickel, the neighbouring parcel NV-2025-10-2143 cleared at **$232/acre** (Lithos48), against Zanskar's $231 (R03, Z04). That is about 116x on the same ground within months of disclosure.
- The play's own step-up comp, BLM hot-play land at $344-1,207/acre (B01, L07), is exactly this repricing. A land bank on federal ground *pays* it rather than *capturing* it.
- **The pitch's "noncompetitive federal lease for an oil and gas lessee" is overstated.** 30 U.S.C. §1003(b)(4) offers it only to an oil and gas lessee **with a producing well, for co-production** (R02). It is not a way to take a 5-20 km² EGS block in tight basement off-auction.
- **Utah adds a second layer.** The Ch. 477 (2026) amendment to Utah Code §73-22-3 defines the geothermal resource as heat above 100 °C, explicitly including enhanced systems, but makes geothermal fluid a **water right** (R06; https://le.utah.gov/xcode/Title73/Chapter22/C73-22-S3_2026050620260506.html). SITLA does allow first-come, time-stamped over-the-counter leases at at least $1/acre/yr, up to 2,560 acres a lease (R05). That is a real Uinta side door, but only on the school-section checkerboard.

**Texas GLO.** Texas Nat. Res. Code §141.073 and §141.076 already require sealed bids (R07). New fact: on 28 Aug 2026 the GLO issued a geothermal **Request for Information covering "all GLO-owned surface lands"**, with responses due **10 Dec 2026**. It "does not constitute a solicitation for bids" and feeds a possible later **Request for Proposals**. Respondents must state a development type (power, EGS, direct use), a timeline and a development plan, and the GLO invites those with past projects (fetched: https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/request-for-information-geothermal-exploration-and-development-in-texas/). State land is moving toward a developer-screened, price-discovering process. The $3-5/acre GLO comps in the pitch (L10) date from before this RFI and should not be assumed to hold for a pure land bank.

**Rung 1 kills the DJ/Uinta federal legs and the GLO leg as cheap-right sources.** They can still be targets, but only at auction prices.

## Rung 2: The hottest Texas cell has a sophisticated owner

The first test the pitch names is Delaware/Val Verde: BHTs of 120-180 °C, and a Pecos County model of about 195 °C at 3.5 km (R07).
- **TPL owns about 880k surface acres in the Permian.** Under SB 785 §141.004 it owns the heat beneath them by statute. In Q2-2026 it was already selling land and water to Chevron's multi-GW Project Kilby in Reeves County, and it spent $110.2M on data-centre land (sibling red team; https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1811074/000181107426000056/exhibit991q22026earningsre.htm).
- **University Lands** manages about 2.1M acres in 19 West Texas counties and markets them for energy leasing (R07; https://universitylands.utsystem.edu/).
- Neither will give an entrant a 10-year option at $1-5/acre. In the hottest part of Texas, the cheap counterparty does not exist.

## Rung 3: Private Texas surface heat stays cheap, with four leaks

**Why it stays cheap:** there is no public nomination, no auction and no all-bids report. Leases are recorded as county memoranda, often without price terms (R07, unverified). The estate has been legally clear only since 18 June 2023 (SB 785; https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.141.htm). Raser-era private comps show how cheap entry is: 4,444 Nevada acres for a $5k total bonus plus $1/acre/yr, and about 10,800 Utah acres for $41.2k (R06; Raser 10-K FY2009).

**The leaks:**
1. **Holdouts on aggregation.** An EGS block needs about 5-20 km², roughly 1,200-4,900 acres (R07), which means dozens of surface owners. Statutory pooling for the geothermal estate is unverified. Once the first memoranda are recorded and neighbours talk, the last 10-20% of tracts reprice, even with no auction.
2. **The mineral estate stays dominant.** Under §141.004(c), mineral operators can drill through the block, and title is clouded where pre-2023 deeds reserved "all minerals and other substances". Old deeds, not auctions, are what reprice the right: each tract needs title work.
3. **Renewal repricing.** Private geothermal leases run 5-30 year primary terms and royalties of 2-5% of gross that often step up (R06; Ormat 10-K FY2025, USG 10-K FY2017). An owner who learns of a nearby proof will demand more at renewal.
4. **Contagion from brine.** Brine minerals stay with the mineral estate (§141.004(c)). In East Texas, landmen were already pushing neighbours of T5 into 10-15 year mineral leases (fetched: https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/new-player-announces-geothermal-and-critical-minerals-project-in-east-texas/). Surface owners there are being educated about subsurface value by lithium, which lifts heat-lease asks too (inference).

**Oklahoma.** The OSCN index of Title 52 (oil and gas) has no geothermal section. The nearest entries are §803 (mineral owner's reasonable use of the surface, solar agreements) and §804 (fetched: https://www.oscn.net/applications/oscn/index.asp?ftdb=STOKST52&level=1). With no SB 785 equivalent found, ownership of heat on Oklahoma's split estates is unsettled (D02 flags this as "the critical diligence item"). A lessee may have to lease from both surface and mineral owners. Anadarko mineral owners are among the most legible owners in the US: Enverus holds 350M+ courthouse records (D08). That is not a quiet market.

**Colorado (DJ).** Geothermal fluid is tied to groundwater law (CRS 37-90.5, unverified; R06). Part of the asset may be a state-issued permit, which a land bank cannot buy cheaply ahead of others.

## Rung 4: Does ML spreading reprice the right?

**The case that it does:**
- Stanford merged 400,134 BHTs into InterPIGNN (MAE 4.8 °C). LANL's In-Context Earth reports 4.7 °C (L09).
- Project InnerSpace's GeoMap is free and includes depth-to-200 °C layers (D08).
- On 8 Jul 2026 Enverus bought A2D: more than 8M raster logs, more than 1.9M LAS files and "basin temperature models", which it can sit next to 350M+ courthouse records (D08; https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/enverus-acquires-a2d-well-log-library-from-tgs-connecting-subsurface-data-to-energy-decision-workflows-302820900.html).
- Reading 10M headers costs about $1.4-6k (D07). Any well-funded copier can match the header-reading edge within months.

**Why the repricing is mild in private markets:**
- Vendors sell to many buyers, so they raise everyone's prior a little rather than starting a bidding war on one parcel. With no auction, there is no venue where better information turns into a price within weeks.
- At the depths that matter, national models are still poor. Errors are 7.9 °C at 2-4 km and 11.2 °C at 4-6 km, and only about 3% of the data lie below 4 km (L09). A parcel-level edge from multi-BHT, shut-in-time-corrected headers survives until someone drills.
- **The binding repricer is a disclosed proof, not ML.** Pumpernickel shows this.

So ML spread lowers c2 (the data edge has a short half-life) more than it lowers c3.

## The deeper problem this lens exposes: cheap because unbid

- B01: "confirmed resource without a PPA is currently valued near zero."
- Fervo holds about 595,900 acres, about 256k of them idle as "Prospects", and none in Texas (Z08, B01).
- Sage took its next commercial EGS plant to Blue Mountain, Nevada (22 Sep 2026; https://www.sagegeosystems.com/).
- At $9.02/share on 21 Oct 2026, Fervo's EV of about $891M is about $1.5k per leased acre, and most of that value belongs to PPAs and Cape Station (sibling red team `oil-patch-egs-land-bank-step-up-size.md`).

A right that stays cheap because there is no demand is not the Terranox pattern. Terranox buys under-priced rights in a market that already pays for proven uranium. Here the cheap-right rung holds and the step-up rung carries the risk.

## Best AI-leveraged answer, and whether it rescues the play

**Answer: drop federal and GLO ground. Run an AI title-and-option machine on fragmented Texas surface heat, and sign each block before recording anything.**

1. **LLM abstraction of county deed images** to find clean SB 785 tracts, flagging pre-2023 reservations of "all minerals and other substances". Exclude TPL and University Lands ground. Rank tracts by corrected temperature at 3-4 km, using multi-BHT header reads trained on Kansas's 212,089 BHTs (D02).
2. **Option contracts written so repricing cannot happen.** Use long options (20-30 years), $1-5/acre, and a fixed royalty schedule set in advance, so a later proof cannot be renegotiated. Add most-favoured-nation and block-completion bonuses so early signers want neighbours to sign, which defuses holdouts. Sign the whole block in one campaign before any memorandum is recorded.
3. **Where Utah gives a quiet door, use it.** SITLA's time-stamped over-the-counter leases are a cheap Uinta leg; avoid BLM nominations entirely.
4. **Prove privately by re-entering an idle wellbore**, with an RRC P-4 operator transfer and a $25k blanket bond (A04), so the first disclosure is the farm-out.

**Does it rescue the play?** It rescues c3 for Texas private: the right truly stays cheap, and AI title work becomes a moat Enverus does not package. It does not rescue the multi-basin claim (DJ, Uinta-BLM, OK unverified), so c5 narrows. It does not fix c4. The answer is a credible **18**, best pitched as "Texas first, private only, with Kansas-trained BHT models", not as a four-basin land bank.
