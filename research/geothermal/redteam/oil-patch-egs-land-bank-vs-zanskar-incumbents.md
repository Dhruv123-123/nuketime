## Verdict

**Not refuted by Zanskar. Partly refuted by the incumbents and, more seriously, by the geology of its own target list. It is rescued only as a narrower play.**

- **Zanskar is not doing this and will not copy it.** Its thesis rejects tight sedimentary and basement rock.
- **The play's three load-bearing claims each fail in part:**
  1. **"Before anyone has a map."** Enverus has the ingredients for one: A2D's basin temperature models plus title records. Free GeoMap exists, and the Texas GLO is now publicly structuring geothermal leases.
  2. **"Separately held heat, lithium and helium rights."** These exist cleanly only in Texas, and only for heat. Alberta grants are non-exclusive and zone-specific.
  3. **"Fervo-screen rock at no more than 4 km across five basins."** In most of those basins the rock is not that hot at that depth.
- **Even on qualifying land, buyers are long on land.** No EGS developer has paid for Texas fee heat.

**Revised total: 15/25** (from 21).

## Rung 1: Zanskar, is it doing this or able to copy it?

**Doing it: no.**
- Its land is about 50k acres of BLM leases in Nevada plus the Lightning Dock plant in NM (Z04, Z06; https://www.zanskar.com/about).
- It targets naturally permeable convective systems at about 121-137 °C (Z03).
- It holds nothing in Texas, Oklahoma, the DJ Basin, the Williston or Alberta. None of Alberta's 31 geothermal leases is Zanskar's (R11; https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/energy-list-of-geothermal-leases.xlsx).
- Its news since 2025 never mentions lithium, despite the "Geothermal & Minerals" entity name (Z06; https://www.zanskar.com/news).

**Could it copy: technically yes, strategically no.**
- Its capital is committed to building plants: a 100 MW NV Energy PPA, a $115M Series C (Jan 2026) and $40M of development capital (Apr 2026) (https://www.zanskar.com/about).
- Under its permeability-led model, EGS-grade tight rock scores as a dry hole (L17).

**The gap is real, but for a reason.** Zanskar avoids this ground because its thesis rejects the geology, not because it hasn't noticed it. **This rung does not kill.**

## Rung 2: data incumbents (Enverus/TGS, S&P, free maps)

**Enverus already owns a near-complete copy of the edge.**
- On 8 Jul 2026 Enverus closed the A2D purchase from TGS for $100M plus a $15M earn-out (https://www.tgs.com/press-releases/tgs-sells-north-american-well-data-products-business-to-enverus).
- The library holds >8M depth-calibrated raster logs, >1.9M LAS files and >5M proprietary logs "not available from any public or regulatory source".
- It also holds >2M tops and interpretations **and "basin temperature models"** (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/enverus-acquires-a2d-well-log-library-from-tgs-connecting-subsurface-data-to-energy-decision-workflows-302820900.html).
- Enverus also runs 350M+ courthouse ownership records (D08; https://www.tgs.com/well-data).
- That puts both halves of the play's join, temperature × who owns the surface, inside one vendor. Enverus has not yet packaged them for geothermal (D08; checked against fetched releases only).

**The play's own cost numbers make the edge cheap.**
- LLM-reading 10M headers costs about $1.4-6k of compute (D07).
- The image cost is about $15M for the whole BEG archive, or about $1M for 100k deep wells (D01, D07; https://www.beg.utexas.edu/about/facilities/geophysical-log-facility).
- Any Permian operator (Devon, Chevron, Oxy), TPL, or Enverus itself could do this in weeks.
- Project InnerSpace's GeoMap gives free isotherm-depth layers (depth to 200 °C) across North America (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/project-innerspace-launches-geomap-geothermal-prospecting-tool-for-north-america/).

**The training set is shallow.**
- Kansas's 212,089 hand-keyed BHTs have a median log-bottom depth of **1.33 km** and a **median of 47 °C**. Only about 168 wells reach 8,000 ft or more (D02; https://www.kgs.ku.edu/PRS/wellStats.html).
- Model error grows to 7.9 °C at 2-4 km and 11.2 °C at 4-6 km, and only about 3% of data are below 4 km (L09).
- SMU states that BHT correction "below 3-4 km is still not established" (L09; https://gdr.openei.org/files/682/2_GPFA-AB_BHTCorrections.pdf).
- Under the standing rules, untested ML lift does not kill. But the label set says little about the 3-4 km band that is being priced.

**Reading:** the data edge is a **speed edge, not a moat**. c2 drops from 5 to 3.

## Rung 3: land incumbents (who already holds the hot ground)

**Texas Delaware/Val Verde, the hottest Texas fairway.**
- BHTs run 120-180 °C, with a modelled maximum of 195 °C at 3.5 km in Pecos (L10; https://repositories.lib.utexas.edu/items/01be6734-86ac-4892-a3f2-1c5e362b3409).
- Under SB 785 the heat belongs to the surface owner. The largest surface owner there is **TPL, with about 880k surface acres**. It is already supplying land for Chevron's data-centre power plant (Project Kilby) in Reeves County, and spent $110.2M on land in Q2-2026 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1811074/000181107426000056/exhibit991q22026earningsre.htm).
- **University Lands** holds about 2.1M acres and is "actively seeking to lease PUF lands for renewable energy" (https://universitylands.utsystem.edu/).
- The heat right on the best Texas ground is therefore held by institutions that can copy the play by adding geothermal to the uses they already sell.

**The Texas GLO is now pricing the heat estate in public (new).**
- On 28 Aug 2026 the GLO issued a Request for Information on geothermal exploration and development of **GLO-owned surface lands**. Responses are due **10 Dec 2026**.
- The GLO will use them to decide "how to structure a geothermal production lease" and may follow with an RFP (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/request-for-information-geothermal-exploration-and-development-in-texas/).
- This sets public comps and draws developers' attention to Texas heat. It works against "quietly, before anyone has a map".

**East Texas Smackover brine.**
- Lithium sits with the *mineral* owner (SB 785 §141.004(c); https://tcss.legis.texas.gov/resources/NR/htm/NR.141.htm).
- Standard Lithium/Equinor hold 110,064 acres (https://www.standardlithium.com/news/press-releases/standard-lithium-files-positive-preliminary-economic-assessment-for-the-high-grade-70000-tonnes-per-year-franklin-project-in-east-texas). Exxon (about 120k ac, AR) and Chevron (about 125k ac, TX/AR) are also there (A02, unverified).
- **T5 Smackover Partners** owns former Luminant coal-mine land in Franklin, Titus and Hopkins counties outright. It is developing geothermal plus lithium plus bromine itself, quoting about 800 ppm Li and 35-50k t/yr LCE (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/new-player-announces-geothermal-and-critical-minerals-project-in-east-texas/).
- **Result:** the brine layer of the bundle is not cheap and not unclaimed.

**Pore space.** Texas Class VI and CCS pore-space leasing by Oxy/1PointFive, Exxon and others is already a priced market (unverified; no search possible). It is not an unpriced option to add for free.

## Rung 4: physics. Does Fervo-screen rock at 4 km or less exist in the named basins?

This is the hardest rung, and it is internal to the play rather than about incumbents.

| Basin | Best evidence at ≤4 km | Meets 175-230 °C? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| DJ / Wattenberg | Corrected BHTs about 160 °C at 2.5 km, a 62 °C/km gradient, >200 °C at about 3 km. The Colorado Geological Survey finds it "not anomalous" | Yes, in a small hot spot, but disputed | L10, L17; https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/90541.pdf |
| Delaware / Val Verde (TX) | 120-180 °C BHTs; 195 °C at 3.5 km (maximum, Pecos) | Marginal, and on TPL/UL land | L10 |
| South Texas (Webb, Crockett) | 150 °C at 2.6-5.1 km (Webb) and 2.7-8.0 km (Crockett) | No at 4 km | https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1291 |
| Williston | About 120 °C at 3.5 km (Deadwood/Red River). A Williston well measured 121 °C where 150 °C was predicted | No | L10 |
| Alberta / WCSB | Of 480 fields, the maximum field average is 126 °C and only 20 exceed 95 °C. 0.1% of wells are deeper than 5 km | No (power-marginal) | L10; https://albertainnovates.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Geothermal-Power-from-Co-Produced-Fluids-and-Hydrocarbon-Reservoirs-Final-Report.pdf |
| Kansas / most Midcontinent | Median BHT 47 °C at 1.33 km; apparent gradient about 26-33 °C/km | No | D02 |
| DFW / Austin / San Antonio (first-test target) | No sourced ≥200 °C-at-4-km anomaly found in the evidence. Normal gradients imply about 120-150 °C at 4 km (unverified) | Probably no | — |

Wider context:
- Aljubran and Horne find that a depth of 4 km or less is cost-optimal on only **0.27% of US land, "mostly in the West"**. Seventy-eight percent of EGS potential sits at 6-7 km (L17).
- The scarce cheap-tier land the play prices is therefore mostly **BLM land in the Great Basin**, where Fervo (about 596k ac) and Ormat (about 434k ac) already sit. It is not oil-patch fee land.
- Sage's own cheapest tier, 180 °C at 2-3 km, is only 53 GW, about 1.3% of its resource (L18).
- "Assume the tech works" lets EGS at 5-7 km count. But at that depth the hot rock is everywhere: Texas is near the power minimum across much of the state at 6.5 km (L10). Scarcity, which is the thing the land bank prices, disappears.

**This rung nearly kills the "five basins" framing.** What survives is Wattenberg, the deep Delaware/Val Verde, and possibly the deep Anadarko (unverified).

## Rung 5: buyers. Will EGS developers pay for this land?

- **Fervo** holds about 595,900 acres, bought at about $4/ac. About 256k of them are idle "Prospects", **none in Texas** (Fervo 424B4, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026034849/fervoenergy-424b4.htm; L07, Z08). It is long on land and could itself sell or farm out land.
- **Sage** ran a 120-day EGS validation in Texas (Aug 2026). But it sited its next *commercial* EGS project at **Ormat's Blue Mountain, NV** (https://www.sagegeosystems.com/; ThinkGeoEnergy Texas tag).
- **SLB-Ormat** picked Desert Peak, NV (https://www.slb.com/newsroom/updates/2026/2026-0609-slb-ormat-update).
- **Ormat** holds about 434k acres, none in Texas (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1296445/000129644526000006/ora-20251231.htm).
- **Texas-native developers secure their own sites.** Renascent re-tested a depleted Eagle Ford shale well and targets 50-100 MWe for data centres and oilfield loads. Exceed GeoEnergy signed a 110 MW municipal PPA in Presidio, plus a 5 MW pilot with Austin Energy. T5 owns its land outright. None of the four fetched articles shows any of them buying third-party heat rights (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/renascent-energy-completes-successful-geothermal-test-on-depleted-shale-well-in-texas/ ; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/exceed-geoenergy-signs-110-mw-geothermal-ppa-with-presidio-texas/).
- **Hyperscaler demand is real.** Rhodium puts 2.9 GW of behind-the-meter (BTM) geothermal potential at DFW (https://rhg.com/research/geothermal-data-center-electricity-demand/), and ERCOT's large-load queue is about 438 GW (B06). But the observed Texas premium of about $0.47M/MW was paid for **grid interconnection**, not heat (https://thetexaslandagent.com/texas-data-center-land-young-county).
- **The play's own step-up comp is not a Texas comp.** Fervo's $4/ac to $1,207/ac was BLM land in Utah, sitting next to FORGE.

**Reading:** no heat buyer has transacted in Texas fee land at any price. c4 drops from 3 to 2.

## Rung 6: rights structure outside Texas

- **Alberta.** The lease is first-come at C$625 plus C$3.50/ha/yr. But it is **non-exclusive** and issued by stratigraphic zone. A 5-year physical-work test caps how long cheap paper can be held (R11; https://open.alberta.ca/dataset/cbf251cf-d1c5-420e-b104-f476c3dc6601/resource/c23dbf89-95d4-4fb8-9779-ddbb4f0cca5d/download/em-mineral-rights-information-bulletin-2023-02.pdf). One holder has already chained 16 leases along the Leduc trend (R11).
- **Colorado (DJ).** Geothermal fluid is tied to groundwater law (CRS 37-90.5; R06, unverified). That leaves no clean, separately held heat estate to option on Front Range fee land.
- **Oklahoma, North Dakota, Kansas.** Surface-versus-mineral ownership of heat is unresolved (A01, D02, R06). Kansas treats helium as part of the gas mineral estate (A06).
- **So the "heat + brine + pore space" bundle** exists cleanly only where the estates were never severed. That is rare in oil country (unverified share).

## Best AI-leveraged answers to the killers, and whether they rescue the play

**A. Wellbore-plus-heat bank in the deep Delaware, Val Verde and Anadarko (best answer; partial rescue).**
- **The asset:** combine the rights no incumbent bundles. That means P-4 transfers of idle deep wellbores at about $0 (Texas has 158,121 inactive wells; D01, A04) with an SB 785 surface heat lease on the same section.
- **The AI work:** LLM-read headers and completion files to find deep wells (>4 km TD, high BHT, casing still intact) on parcels outside the TPL and University Lands footprint.
- **The step-up:** re-entry plus a temperature log turns an unpriced right into a measured one. Renascent shows repurposed shale wells are a live development route.
- **Why it rescues:** Fervo, Sage and Enverus do not hold wellbores, and TPL does not operate wells.
- **Caveats:** plugging liability, the mineral lessee's dominant estate, and the buyers are mostly small Texas developers. **This partly rescues c1 and c3.**

**B. Hyperscaler powered-land framing instead of selling to EGS developers (partial rescue for c4).**
- **The pitch:** sell "firm-BTM-ready" campus sites, combining a heat right, an option on a wellbore and fibre within 25 km, to data-centre developers in ERCOT.
- **Why it might work:** Texas's speed-to-power premium is real (about 630x raw land), and on-site generation offsets required transmission capacity (B06).
- **The AI work:** join the heat model with fibre and substation maps and the large-load queue.
- **The risk:** the buyer's premium attaches to interconnection, so the heat right may be priced as a minor add-on. **This does not fully rescue c4.**

**C. Speed against Enverus: lock up title before a module ships (partial rescue).**
- **The plan:** license a field-of-use A2D subset rather than rebuild it (D08), and spend the time saved on title work.
- **Why:** the moat becomes signed options, not maps.
- **Timing:** the GLO RFI deadline of 10 Dec 2026 is the clock. Once the GLO publishes lease terms, private landowners will anchor to them. **This helps c3, but only for a window of months.**

**D. Fold into a multi-basin sedimentary bank led from Saskatchewan and the Williston (structural rescue, as in the sibling red team).**
- **The idea:** Terranox's native tenure, regulators and data muscle carry over, and Alberta adds zone staking at C$3.50/ha.
- **The limit:** this rescues the company shape, not the "Fervo-screen at ≤4 km" claim. Temperatures there are 95-126 °C, which means ORC or direct use, not EGS-grade rock.

**Net:** the play survives as a narrow, Texas-centred wellbore-plus-heat option bank in two or three hot pockets, sold mostly to Texas BTM developers. It does not survive as a five-basin EGS land bank. Zanskar is irrelevant here. The real competitors are TPL, University Lands and the GLO for land, Enverus for data, and the land-long developers for demand.

## Cheapest kill test (before buying scans)

Budget about $30-50k over 4 weeks:

1. Pull Enverus or county records of instruments recorded in 2023-26 that mention "geothermal" in Reeves, Pecos, Ward, Culberson, Val Verde and Weld counties. If TPL, University Lands or operators are already taking heat leases, kill the play.
