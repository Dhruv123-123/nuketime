# L11: Physics-based natural-state and regional hydrothermal simulation

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: numerical reservoir simulators (TOUGH2/3, Waiwera, FEFLOW, COMSOL), automated natural-state calibration (iTOUGH2, PEST, adjoint, MCMC, EKI, DSI), neural surrogates, and regional-scale convection models. Prior work: SENTINEL-COX uses only a 1 ms analytic "Tier 1" column + plume simulator and cut the TOUGH3/FNO surrogate from v1 (`../../model/MODEL.md`, section 1 and item 5). This review asks what real physics can add at the prospecting stage, where a field has 0-3 holes, not 30.

## What the field knows

**The simulators.** Field modelling is a mature, open-source craft.
- TOUGH2 and TOUGH3 (LBNL; Jung et al. 2017, Computers & Geosciences) solve multiphase water-steam-CO2 flow and heat in porous and fractured rock. iTOUGH2 (Finsterle) wraps them for inversion and sensitivity.
- Waiwera (Croucher et al., University of Auckland; https://waiwera.readthedocs.io) is the parallel PETSc-based successor, with supercritical water. PyTOUGH and the Python `pywaiwera` tools script mesh building and post-processing.
- FEFLOW (DHI) and COMSOL are used for single-phase, density-driven convection, mostly in Europe and in academic fault-zone studies.
- Model size is no longer the limit. O'Sullivan et al. (2019, Stanford SGW) ran the same field at 140,000, 550,000 and 2.3 million blocks: 109 s on 80 CPUs, 726 s on 160 CPUs and 5,056 s on 320 CPUs (2.4 vs 449 CPU-hours). Cost grows faster than block count, about 13-14x for 16x more blocks. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2019/Osullivan.pdf

**Natural state is the prior of the field.** Every developed field model is first calibrated to the pre-production "natural state": a steady state reached by running the model for about a million years from a deep heat and mass inflow. It is matched to downhole temperature profiles and surface outflows. Calibration has moved from hand tuning to automated inversion:
- PEST and iTOUGH2 Levenberg-Marquardt fits with tens to hundreds of zone permeabilities.
- Adjoint and randomised-SVD Gauss-Newton fits by Bjarkason, O'Sullivan and Maclaren (Auckland) that handle thousands of block-wise permeabilities (unverified exact counts; https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/37822).
- Adaptive delayed-acceptance MCMC (Cui, Fox and O'Sullivan 2011, Water Resources Research), which uses a cheap coarse model to screen proposals (https://www.stats.ox.ac.uk/~nicholls/linkfiles/papers/cui09.pdf).
- Posterior-informed approximation error with a hierarchical Bayes coarse model, which makes off-the-shelf MCMC practical (Maclaren et al., arXiv 1810.04350, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.04350).
- Ensemble Kalman inversion (EKI) for two-phase models with "hundreds or thousands" of parameters. It is derivative-free, parallel and tolerant of failed runs (de Beer et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.09017, https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09017).
- Data space inversion (DSI), which skips parameter calibration and maps prior simulations straight to posterior predictions (de Beer et al., arXiv 2407.15401, https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15401v3).
- A Bayesian natural-state calibration of Krafla, Iceland (TOUGH2 with supercritical extension), found rock-type permeabilities uncertain over more than two orders of magnitude even with many wells. It also predicted an untapped vapour zone (https://yvm2020.authorea.com/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10508100.1).

**Regional and fault-scale convection.** A thinner literature models whole basins, not fields:
- Taupo Volcanic Zone: Kissling and Weir (2005, JVGR) and earlier ANZIAM work reproduce plume spacing and the roughly 4 GW total heat output from basal heat input and permeability alone (unverified numbers; https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/1999/Kissling.pdf).
- Cascades: Ingebritsen and co-authors showed that regional groundwater flow sweeps heat sideways and hides it under a cold "rain curtain" (https://www.usgs.gov/publications/numerical-simulation-hydrothermal-circulation-cascade-range-north-central-oregon). This is the physics of our mask class.
- Basin and Range: McKenna and Blackwell (2004, Geothermics) modelled Dixie Valley as a transient range-front fault system (unverified details). Athens and Caers (2018, SGW) ran 800 Monte Carlo COMSOL models of a Dixie-style 2D basin with 14 parameters and kept 592 with realistic recharge. Basal heat flux, thermal conductivity and permeability dominated temperature. Fault width, water table and basin asymmetry hardly mattered (https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2018/Athens.pdf).
- Guillou-Frottier et al. (2020, Solid Earth 11:1571) mapped 3D fault-zone convection. Vertical faults give anomalies up to about 80-90 °C, dipping faults at most about 50-60 °C. For a 100 m wide vertical fault, anomalies above 30 °C reach more than 1 km sideways. Plumes can pulse with a period of about 240-250 years, and the initial pattern sets how many upwellings survive (https://se.copernicus.org/articles/11/1571/2020/).
- USGS Battle Mountain (Earney et al. 2024) built a joint gravity, magnetic and MT model of a possible blind system to site TG holes. It was geophysics only, with no flow model (https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271418).

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Setting | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| DSI (de Beer et al.) | Synthetic 3D field, 6 x 6 x 3 km, 8,788 cells, 119 data | 1,300 prior runs (2,000 launched, 1,413 converged). The 95% band held 92.0% of temperature, 93.2% of pressure and 92.3% of enthalpy profiles across 100 test cases. Stable from about 500 runs. |
| DSI vs MCMC | Synthetic 2D, 50 coefficients | 500 DSI runs matched an MCMC reference that used 4 x 500,000 iterations. |
| EKI | Synthetic and real two-phase fields | Far fewer runs than MCMC with credible UQ (numbers not in abstract). |
| FNO surrogate (Liang, Yu, Thibaut, Zheng, Hoiland, Pollack 2026 SGW) | Coupled flow-heat, 4 fault scenarios x 300 runs | Relative error < 0.025 in-scenario and about 0.07 on unseen fault scenarios. Up to 10,000x faster. Each full run took 30 min-2 h. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Liang.pdf |
| Monte Carlo + DGSA (Athens and Caers) | 2D Dixie-style basin | 800 runs show only 3 of 14 parameters control temperature at 3 km (100-200 °C). |

Note on Liang et al.: co-author Carl Hoiland is a Zanskar co-founder (unverified affiliation on the paper). Zanskar is moving from LightGBM maps toward physics surrogates for fault scenarios. That is a competitive signal.

## Datasets used

- Downhole temperature logs and well tests from developed fields (Wairakei, Ohaaki, Krafla, Olkaria). These are mostly proprietary.
- Dixie Valley public data: wells, MT, gravity, heat flow (GDR; Blackwell SMU logs).
- Surface heat-output inventories (TVZ, Cascades spring discharge, USGS).
- Synthetic ensembles, the standard test bed in every DSI, EKI and FNO paper.
- For US regional work: SMU/NGDS heat flow, INGENIOUS layers, GeoDAWN magnetics and gravity, USGS Quaternary faults, NWIS water levels, and TG holes. Already covered in M02, M05 and M23.

## What this means for our model design

- **Add a Tier 2 physics prior, not a field model.** Run 20,000-100,000 cheap 2.5D/3D Waiwera or TOUGH3 natural-state models of Basin and Range fault settings: step-over, termination, intersection and accommodation zone, crossed with dip, fault permeability, basal heat flux and aquifer sweep. Train an FNO surrogate on them. Cost at about 5 CPU-minutes per run is about 10,000 CPU-hours, roughly $500-1,000 of cloud time (unverified price).
- **Use DSI, not calibration, at prospect stage.** With 0-3 holes per prospect, parameter calibration is ill-posed. DSI's direct data-to-prediction map, at about 500 runs per setting family, gives a posterior for "temperature at 1.5 km" from TG and 2 m data. Its 92-93% coverage on 95% bands is good enough to feed RNPV.
- **Replace Tier 1 column kernels with physics-learned shapes.** Guillou-Frottier's numbers (anomaly above 30 °C reaching more than 1 km from the fault; vertical faults 80-90 °C, dipping 50-60 °C) give the plume-width and amplitude priors in the Cox detection kernel. The simulator ensemble lets us learn them per structural setting.
- **Make mask class physical.** Run the Ingebritsen-style lateral sweep in the ensemble. The surrogate then predicts how far an outflow plume moves the shallow anomaly downstream of the upflow, given NWIS hydraulic gradients. That gives the shared latent mask class a mechanistic prior and an "upflow offset" vector for siting holes.
- **Spend parameters where sensitivity lives.** Athens and Caers show heat flux, conductivity and permeability dominate. Do not spend latent dimensions on water table or basin shape. Run DGSA or Sobol on our own ensemble to prune.
- **Physics as a sanity filter on ML hotspots.** Reject or down-weight LightGBM/Cox hotspots whose implied temperature and outflow cannot be produced by any surrogate run consistent with local heat flow. That is a cheap guard against leakage and artefacts.
- **Sell proven acreage with a calibrated natural-state model.** After the slim hole, a Waiwera natural-state model with EKI UQ becomes the data-room exhibit. Buyers such as Ormat and utilities expect one, and it raises sale price per acre.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No regional-scale Bayesian natural-state model of the Great Basin.** Every calibrated model is one field. An amortised surrogate plus DSI run over every 2 km mesh cell would give the first physics-consistent temperature-at-depth posterior for the whole basin.
2. **No surrogate trained across structural-setting types.** Liang et al. used 4 fault scenarios. A foundation-style neural operator trained on Faulds' full setting taxonomy, conditioned on mapped fault geometry, is open ground.
3. **Unused legacy field models.** Decades of TOUGH2 decks and natural-state reports for Nevada and California fields sit in OSTI, GRC and Stanford papers as figures. AI can digitise their calibrated permeability and heat-flux values into a library of real posteriors to set our priors (unverified volume).
4. **No one couples point-process discovery with physics.** Discovery models (USGS, Zanskar) are statistical. Simulators assume a known system. Joining them in SENTINEL-COX is new.
5. **Agentic model building.** Mesh building, rock-type zoning and natural-state convergence still take weeks of expert time per field. An LLM agent that drives PyTOUGH or pywaiwera, reads geology reports and iterates EKI could build a defensible natural-state model per prospect in a day.
6. **Pulsing and transient plumes.** Periods of about 250 years suggest some "dead" TG holes sample a cold phase. No exploration model accounts for this.
