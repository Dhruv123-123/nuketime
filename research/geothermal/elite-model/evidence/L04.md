# L04: ML for geothermal favourability, 2018-2026, and its real skill

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: supervised and unsupervised ML that maps where geothermal systems are (PU learning, random forest, XGBoost/LightGBM, Bayesian NN, CNN, GNN). It builds on `../../evidence/L03-ml-geothermal.md` and `../../model/evidence/M14-ml-favorability.md`. It does not repeat their full write-ups. New here: the exact skill table, the 2025 USGS numbers from the abstract, a 2025 U-Net fault-mapping score, a 2026 calibrated PU random forest from mineral work, a 2026 failed blind-target test, and what all this means for the elite model. Web budget: 10 searches, 11 fetches (2 blocked: ScienceDirect, ESS Open Archive).

## What the field knows

1. **The algorithm barely matters.** The USGS team built nine western-US maps with logistic regression, SVM, XGBoost and a neural net. Every F1 score was below 0.10. Complexity did not help. XGBoost matched or beat the neural net (Mordensky et al. 2023, *Geothermics* 110, 102662, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70240475).
2. **Adding smooth layers can make it worse.** Adding strain-rate layers did not beat the 5-feature model (Mordensky et al. 2023, GRC 47, "Cursed?", https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10536404).
3. **Negatives chosen by experts bias the map.** Random negatives at natural rarity (about 1:5,100) beat 62 hand-picked negatives on the metric that matters (Caraccioli et al. 2023, GRC 47, https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10536402).
4. **Better labels and layers do help.** The 2025 USGS Great Basin map used about ten times more training sites, ordinal labels by upflow strength, and added "low-signal" sites. It is the best public result (Mordensky et al. 2025, *Geothermics* 133, 103450, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70270953).
5. **Continuous labels beat yes/no labels.** Regression on 3,869 well "convective signal" residuals put half of the high-signal wells in the top 3% of predictions (Mordensky et al. 2023, GRC 47, https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10536406).
6. **Bayesian NNs give stable uncertainty, not better accuracy.** The BNN made stable per-pixel distributions where ordinary NNs varied run to run. It reported no hard test score, and its random split after copying labels to neighbouring pixels leaks (Brown et al. 2022, arXiv 2209.15543; Smith et al. 2023, *Geothermics* 111, 102693).
7. **Unsupervised methods find structure but are never scored.** NMFk on 14,341 groundwater-chemistry sites (18 attributes), and EnviTrace 2026 on 126 attributes, report no accuracy at all (https://essopenarchive.org/doi/full/10.1002/essoar.10510850.1 ; https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Vesselinov.pdf). The same is true of a 2026 k-means/DBSCAN/GMM pass on about 75 legacy spring analyses (Sekar and Okoroafor, SGW 2026, https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Sekar.pdf).
8. **CNNs work best on the feature layers, not on the final map.** No peer-reviewed CNN favourability map with a clean blind test exists. CNNs do work for the inputs, such as fault detection (Hermant et al. 2025, below).
9. **Expert structural maps still win at fine scale.** Faulds et al. (SGW 2026) map over 1,430 favourable structural settings (7.7% of the INGENIOUS area). Of 120 training sites at or above 120 °C, 34% sit at step-overs and 27% at fault intersections. They say automating this "needs expert oversight" (https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Faulds.pdf).
10. **Favourable is not the same as hot.** At NE Reese River Valley, four favourable settings plus gravity, MT and 139 two-metre probes led to 10 TG wells (about 244 m). All were cold: mean gradient 46 °C/km, below the regional 56 °C/km (Jacinto et al., SGW 2026, https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Jacinto.pdf). This is a rare, published, dated negative.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Study | Method | Labels | Validation | Reported skill |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mordensky 2025 (USGS Great Basin) | Simple non-linear ML (XGBoost family), 16 features, ordinal labels | ~10x more sites; 28 power systems as test | Monte Carlo CV, 1,200 runs | **15/28 power systems in top 1%; 26/28 (93%) above 80th pct vs ~50% for older maps; 85% in top 10%; 5/9 hidden and 10/19 exposed in top 1%** |
| Mordensky 2023 convective regression | XGBoost regression | 3,869 well residuals | Random split | Half of high-signal wells in top 3% (truncated labels) vs top 13% (all data) |
| Mordensky 2023 "When less is more" | LR, SVM, XGBoost, ANN, PU undersampling | 278 positives, 0 negatives | Random 80/20 | **F1 < 0.10** for all |
| Caraccioli 2023 | XGBoost, LR | 83 pos, 62 expert neg | 60 random splits | Natural-imbalance XGBoost beats NVML ANN on CDF-at-positives; F1 misleading |
| Brown 2022 / Smith 2023 (NVML) | BNN, ANN, PCA+k-means | ~83 pos, ~62 neg, 250 m grid | Random 67/33, leaky | No usable score |
| Zanskar GeoCore 2025 | LightGBM, H3 grid, ~4M cells | Known systems + cold wells | 8-fold spatial block CV | No public score |
| Aljubran & Horne 2024 | Physics-informed GNN, temperature regression | BHTs | 10 km spatial blocks | **MAE 4.8 °C** at 0-7 km |
| Shahdi 2021 (NE US) | XGBoost temperature regression | O&G BHTs | Random | MAE 3.19 °C, RMSE 4.94 °C |
| Hermant 2025 (TLS Geothermics) | U-Net (FaultSEG) fault segmentation from 3DEP DEM + Sentinel-2 | 1,100 hand-mapped faults, 264 km | Held-out tiles | **Test PR-AUC 0.595, IoU ≥ 0.58**; found new faults later confirmed on lidar |
| Saduov 2026 (Nevada Au-Ag, mineral analogue) | PU random forest + isotonic calibration + area-of-applicability mask | 171 Au / 132 Ag positives | 5-fold spatial GroupKFold, 14-20 km blocks, 10 km buffer | **ROC-AUC 0.91, AP 0.75-0.78; top 10% of area holds 84-86% of positives (8.5x lift)** |
| Akita/Iwate, Japan (ISPRS 2026) | RF, GAN | 152 wells; inputs include hot springs | Not stated | 94-97% accuracy, which is leakage-grade |

How to read this table. Scores above 0.9 come either from mineral work with dense labels, or from geothermal work that feeds surface manifestations in as inputs. The honest geothermal yardstick is area efficiency: what share of known systems falls in the top X% of the map. On that yardstick the 2025 USGS map is the bar to beat: **about 54% of power systems in the top 1% of the area**.

## Datasets used

- USGS 2008 assessment layers (heat flow, Quaternary faults and magmatism, seismic density, SHmax) and the data release doi:10.5066/P9V1Q9XM.
- USGS 2025 Great Basin favourability features and predictions, doi:10.5066/P14EET2C (https://www.usgs.gov/data/geothermal-resource-favorability-select-features-and-predictions-united-states-great-basin).
- DOE INGENIOUS Great Basin compilation (GDR 1391), plus the FSS polygon inventory (over 1,430 polygons).
- Nevada Play Fairway / NVML feature stack and model GeoTIFFs (GDR 1351).
- 3,869 well heat-flow residuals (INGENIOUS); about 14,341 groundwater-chemistry sites (NMFk); GeoDAWN airborne geophysics (about 149,030 line-km).
- SMU/NGDS BHTs; Stanford Thermal Earth Model (GDR 1592).
- USGS 3DEP lidar/10 m DEM and Sentinel-2 (fault CNN). Colorado School of Mines GeoAI hyperspectral/LST/PSInSAR toolkit (DOE EE0008760, OpenEI).
- USGS legacy hot-spring chemistry (Mariner 1977; Fournier 1980), digitised.

## What this means for our model design

- **Use LightGBM/XGBoost as the base learner.** It is the common baseline for USGS, Zanskar and the mineral work. Spend effort on labels, not architecture.
- **Reproduce the USGS 2025 map on day one and report against it.** The bar is 15/28 power systems in the top 1%. Score both random and spatial-block CV and publish the gap. Nobody in geothermal has.
- **Make the label continuous and censored.** Use heat-flow residuals, geothermometer temperatures and TG gradients, not 0/1 flags. That is how the best two geothermal results won.
- **Copy the mineral-side recipe in full:** PU learning plus isotonic calibration plus an area-of-applicability mask plus spatial GroupKFold with a buffer zone. It gave AUC 0.91 in Nevada Au-Ag and is ready to lift over.
- **Never feed surface manifestations as inputs.** Use them only in the detection (observation) model. Otherwise the map cannot find blind systems.
- **Use CNNs upstream as feature makers.** Run a U-Net on 3DEP lidar to map faults, step-overs and tips everywhere. Then compute FSS geometry automatically, scored against Faulds' 1,430 hand polygons.
- **Feed dated negatives such as Reese River (10 cold TG wells) as censored observations.** They teach the model where "favourable" fails, which is rare and valuable.
- **Report uncertainty per cell from an ensemble or BNN** and drive sensor and lease choice from it, as SENTINEL-COX already plans.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No geothermal paper reports the same model under random and spatial CV.** We can publish the first honest gap and set the benchmark.
2. **No time-split back-test exists:** train on data before year T, score on systems found after T. AI extraction of dated discovery records (M13/M18) makes this possible.
3. **No automated structural-setting classifier.** A vision model on lidar plus a fault graph could label step-overs and intersections at scale. Faulds says this still needs experts. An LLM-plus-vision reviewer can stand in for that expert at volume.
4. **No model learns the labelling propensity (SAR PU).** "Found because it leaked" is never modelled. Our Cox effort layer is that missing piece.
5. **Unsupervised chemistry is never scored.** We can turn NMFk-style signatures into features and test whether they add skill under spatial CV.
6. **Published negatives are scattered in workshop PDFs.** An extraction agent could build the first open negative-well registry from SGW/GRC papers and OSTI reports.
7. **No CNN favourability map trained on full raster stacks with a blind test.** With fault, InSAR, LST and gravity rasters stacked, a segmentation model could predict upflow footprints at 250 m (untested; lift unverified).
