# L22: Multi-agent LLM reasoning for science and geology

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Ten web searches. The fetch budget ran out after one page fetch (GeoAgent abstract), so most numbers below come from search snippets and from what I already knew of these papers. Numbers I could not check this session are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**1. Agents that use tools beat bare LLMs. Agents that only talk do not reliably beat simple ensembles.**
- Coscientist (Boiko et al., Nature 624:570, 2023, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10733136) used a GPT-4 planner with four tools: web search, documentation search, Python, and a robot lab. It planned and ran Suzuki and Sonogashira couplings and solved a reaction-optimisation task. The value came from the tools and the lab loop, not from the model reasoning alone.
- Google's AI co-scientist (Gottweis et al., arXiv 2502.18864, 2025; https://deepmind.google/blog/co-scientist-a-multi-agent-ai-partner-to-accelerate-research/) is a set of agents (Generation, Reflection, Ranking, Evolution, Proximity, Meta-review). They run an Elo tournament of hypotheses that compete in pairwise debates. Its claims: higher Elo tracked higher accuracy on hard GPQA questions. Drug-repurposing candidates for AML were confirmed in vitro. It rediscovered an unpublished mechanism of bacterial gene transfer (cf-PICI) in days (unverified detail).
- Sakana's AI Scientist (Lu et al., arXiv 2408.06292, 2024) wrote full ML papers for about $15 each. Its automated reviewer scored about 0.65 balanced accuracy on ICLR papers, close to humans at about 0.66 (unverified). AI Scientist v2 (Yamada et al., arXiv 2504.08066) used agentic tree search. One of three submitted papers passed an ICLR 2025 workshop review (scores about 6/7/6). A later version was reported as published in Nature (https://aiweekly.co/alerts/sakana-ai-16335, unverified).
- Debate. Du et al. (arXiv 2305.14325) found that three agents debating for two rounds raised arithmetic and GSM8K accuracy by about 8 to 15 points over one agent (unverified exact numbers). Later work is colder. Smit et al. ("Should we be going MAD?", ICML 2024, https://icml.cc/virtual/2024/poster/34657) found that multi-agent debate does not reliably beat self-consistency (majority voting over samples), and that it is sensitive to settings. Huang et al. (arXiv 2310.01798) showed that LLMs cannot yet correct their own reasoning without outside feedback. Often they turn right answers wrong. "Talk Isn't Always Cheap" (arXiv 2509.05396) shows that debate can lower accuracy when agents give in to confident wrong peers. "Minority Sentinel" (arXiv 2606.29270) studies when to overrule the majority vote.

**2. Geospatial agents are real but still weak on multistep GIS work.**
- GeoAgent (Chen and Wang et al., arXiv 2410.18792) puts a code interpreter, static analysis and RAG over library docs inside Monte Carlo Tree Search. It beats bare LLMs on function calls and task completion in a new geospatial coding benchmark (single-turn and multi-turn tasks for data access, analysis and maps).
- GeoBenchX (JPMorgan, arXiv 2503.18129) has about 200 multistep GIS tasks and about 23 tools, scored by an LLM judge. Top frontier models solve most of the easy tasks. They do much worse on hard multistep tasks and at saying "this cannot be done with the data given" (unverified exact rates).
- LLM-Geo / autonomous GIS (Li and Ning, 2023) and GIS Copilot for QGIS (Akinboyewa et al., 2024-25) show end-to-end spatial workflows written by the model. Errors cluster in CRS handling, joins and choosing the right dataset (unverified).
- GeoLLM (Manvi et al., ICLR 2024) asked LLMs for location facts (population, wealth) using nearby OpenStreetMap context. Its predictions correlated with ground truth up to about r = 0.7-0.8 and beat satellite-embedding baselines (unverified). LLMs hold a usable but biased geographic prior.

**3. Geology and exploration agents exist, but nobody has shown one find a deposit.**
- MineAgent and MineBench (arXiv 2412.17339) use a multimodal LLM with a hierarchical judging and decision design for remote-sensing mineral exploration. They report gains over generic MLLMs on their own benchmark (unverified size of gain).
- K2 (Deng et al., arXiv 2306.05064) and GeoGalactica (30B) are geoscience-tuned LLMs tested on exam-style question sets (GeoBench from Chinese graduate entrance exams and AP exams). These test knowledge, not discovery.
- DARPA CriticalMAAS with USGS (https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/usgs-ai-mineral-assessment; USGS lessons learned, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271124; arXiv 2506.16006) is the closest real programme. AI georeferences scanned geologic maps, pulls out legend features and mineral sites, and feeds prospectivity models for national assessments. DARPA reports big speed-ups in assessment time (unverified size).
- Document extraction for exploration: Dimeski et al. (WNUT 2022) pulled structured drillhole intercepts out of ASX company reports. The Geological Survey of Queensland and FrontierSI ran an industry-report value extraction project (https://frontiersi.com.au/project/gsq-industry-report-value-extraction-project/).

**4. Agent benchmarks for data-driven science show low success rates.**
- ScienceAgentBench (Chen et al., ICLR 2025, arXiv 2410.05080) has 102 tasks from 44 papers, including geographic information science. The best agent solved about 32-34% of tasks alone, and o1 about 42% (unverified).
- DiscoveryBench (Majumder et al., 2024) has about 264 real hypothesis tasks. The best systems solved about 25% (unverified).

**5. LLM ensembles can be well calibrated forecasters.** Halawi et al. (2024) used a retrieval plus forecasting pipeline and reached a Brier score of about 0.179, against 0.149 for the human crowd. Schoenegger et al. (Science Advances 2024) found that an ensemble of 12 LLMs matched a crowd of 925 humans (unverified numbers). Taken together, ensembles plus retrieval plus aggregation give calibration, while open-ended debate does not.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | Best reported result | Lesson for us |
|---|---|---|
| Tool-using planner with a real loop (Coscientist) | Planned and ran real reactions on its own | Give agents real tools: PostGIS, raster stats, our likelihood |
| Tournament plus Elo plus evolution (co-scientist) | Elo tracked accuracy; wet-lab confirmations | Rank hypotheses, but score them with data, not with LLM taste |
| Tree search over code (AI Scientist v2, GeoAgent) | Workshop paper accepted; better GIS task completion | Search over analysis plans, and check each plan by running it |
| Debate (Du et al.) | About +8-15 points on arithmetic (unverified) | Wiped out by self-consistency in later tests |
| Self-consistency or ensembles | Often equal to or better than debate (Smit 2024) | Default to N independent samples plus voting |
| LLM forecaster ensembles | Near human-crowd Brier (Halawi; Schoenegger) | Use for priors on non-geologic events (BLM sale timing, buyer interest) |
| Science agent benchmarks | 25-42% task success | Expect 1 in 2-4 open analyses to be wrong; verification is mandatory |

## Datasets used

- Agent benchmarks: ScienceAgentBench (102 tasks), DiscoveryBench (264), GeoBenchX (about 200 GIS tasks), GeoAgent's geospatial code benchmark, MineBench, K2's GeoBench, GPQA (co-scientist).
- Geology sources used by agents: USGS NGMDB scanned maps, USGS MRDS, CriticalMAAS map-competition data (georeferencing and legend-feature labels), ASX company reports, GSQ open-file reports, OpenStreetMap (GeoLLM).
- Our sources these patterns apply to: NREL GDR, INGENIOUS layers, SMU heat-flow database, NGDS, state oil and gas well files, BLM MLRS lease and sale records, USGS NGMDB.

## What this means for our model design

- **The agents never set the score.** SENTINEL-COX (the LGCP count and RNPV per acre) stays the single scorer. Agents propose, extract and check. They change the map only through likelihood terms that can be audited. Debate cannot fix calibration; data can.
- **Use a hypothesis tournament, ranked by held-out likelihood, not by Elo.** Copy the co-scientist loop (generate, reflect, evolve, meta-review). Each "hidden system concept" for a basin (for example, a blind outflow under a playa fed by a range-front fault step-over) must state predictions we can test: TG well gradients, spring chemistry, a geophysical anomaly. The concept's rank is the gain in log-likelihood on data held back from the agent. LLM Elo only breaks ties.
- **Use self-consistency, not debate, for extraction.** For scanned TG logs and driller reports, run N = 5 independent extractions with different models and prompts. Take the median. Treat the spread as extraction noise and pass it into the per-source censored likelihood. Send items with high spread to a human. CriticalMAAS and the GSQ project show this is a real, valuable layer.
- **Use adversarial roles that are tied to tools.** A "prior-claimant" agent queries BLM MLRS, state lease rolls, county records and the GDR to show that a cell is already known or held. That feeds the search-effort thinning layer directly. A "physics skeptic" agent runs the Tier 1 column simulator to test whether the claimed temperature fits the heat flow. Debate only helps when each side has different evidence and tools.
- **Run the agent stack as a dated hindcast.** Freeze all data at a past date t (for example 2015). Run the full agent pipeline. Score it against later finds (Blue Mountain-era finds, Zanskar's Big Blind and Pumpernickel). Block leakage by stripping post-t documents and checking that the LLM does not "remember" the answer (ask it to name the find before showing any data). This answers the benchmark gap below.
- **Make cheap per-cell dossiers at scale.** At about $15 per AI Scientist paper, a 1-3 page dossier for each of the top 5,000 cells (data seen, concept, holes, next measurement) costs well under $50k (unverified arithmetic). The VOI engine picks cells, and the dossiers make deal review fast.
- **Treat an LLM geographic prior as one weak, fenced feature.** Following GeoLLM, ask for "known geothermal indications near X" as one feature. Keep it only if the leakage-safe hindcast shows lift. Otherwise it just echoes the literature, which is the very search bias we are trying to model.
- **Use LLM forecaster ensembles for the business side.** Ensembles forecast BLM sale timing, nomination odds, competitor bidding and offtake interest. These feed P(build) and capture(stage) in RNPV.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No geothermal agent benchmark exists.** We could build "HeatHindcast": dated data snapshots, blind-system labels and lease outcomes. We could publish part of it as a moat and a hiring signal.
2. **No agent has been scored on prospective discovery in earth science.** All results are rediscovery, exam questions or benchmark tasks. We could pre-register agent predictions for cells before cheap 2 m probes or TG holes, then report hit rates.
3. **Nobody joins land status with geology inside the agent loop.** CriticalMAAS stops at assessment. Our agent links mapped heat to the exact parcel, lease status and buy path.
4. **No one has measured the calibration of agent probability statements in geology.** We can calibrate agent outputs against the LGCP posterior and the hindcast, and fold the error into the variance split.
5. **No one has measured memorisation leakage in geoscience agents.** Frontier LLMs may know Zanskar's press releases. A leakage audit is cheap and makes our hindcast claims believable to buyers.
6. **No one runs agents as active-learning controllers in exploration.** Co-scientist picks experiments by human taste. We can let agents propose measurements and let EVSI per dollar choose among them.
