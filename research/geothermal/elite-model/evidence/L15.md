# L15: Value of information, POMDPs and active search for exploration drilling

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Builds on `../../model/evidence/M17-value-of-information.md` (Trainor-Guitton recipe, Darajat MT numbers, our VOI-per-dollar table). This file does not repeat M17. It adds what the planning and ML literature says about *sequential* choice: POMDP drill planners, dependent-prospect dynamic programming, bandits and Pandora's box, amortized experimental design, and active search. Eight web searches and three fetches ran before the shared fetch budget ran out. Numbers I could not re-check are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Exploration is a sequential decision problem, not a map.** Each hole or survey changes beliefs about its neighbours, and the next best action depends on what came back. The textbook is Eidsvik, Mukerji and Bhattacharjya, *Value of Information in the Earth Sciences* (Cambridge, 2015). It covers spatial VOI, Bayesian networks and sequential sampling. The key point: VOI is non-additive across correlated sites, so greedy one-step VOI under-values data that teaches you about many sites.

2. **Dependent prospects: dynamic programming with look-ahead pays.** Martinelli, Eidsvik and Hauge ("Dynamic decision making for graphical models applied to oil exploration", NTNU preprint S12-2011; later EJOR 2013) put 25 North Sea prospects into a Bayesian network of shared source rock, charge and seal. They compared drilling orders. Naive (independent) value 20,213 MUSD; myopic 21,321; 1-step look-ahead 21,841; 3-step 23,197. So 3-step look-ahead gave **about +14.8% over naive** on the same assets. On a small 6-prospect net, depth-3 look-ahead reached 4.93 vs an exact DP of 4.96. Shallow look-ahead gets nearly all the value.

3. **Bandit theory frames "which wildcat next".** Bickel and Smith (Decision Analysis 3(1):16-32, 2006) gave the binary learning model for sequential exploration of dependent wells. Brown and Smith ("Optimal Sequential Exploration: Bandits, Clairvoyants, and Wildcats", Operations Research 61(3):644-665, 2013) built heuristic policies plus **information-relaxation upper bounds** (a "clairvoyant" who knows outcomes, penalised). The bounds certify how close a heuristic is to optimal. They reported near-optimal gaps of a few percent on oil-field examples (unverified). Gittins indices are optimal only for independent arms. Weitzman's Pandora's box (1979) gives a reservation-price rule for "inspect a box at a cost, then stop and take the best". That is almost exactly our parcel problem: pay to measure a parcel, then decide to hold or drop.

4. **POMDP drill planners work and are in commercial use.** Mern and Caers, "The Intelligent Prospector v1.0" (GMD 16:289-313, 2023), cast mineral exploration as a POMDP. State = subsurface realisation; actions = drill at x, MINE or ABANDON; reward = ore minus 150-unit cost, 0.1 per hole. Solver = modified POMCPOW (Monte Carlo tree search with progressive widening) on a particle belief, 10,000 rollouts per step. It mined 1,097 of 1,154 profitable ore units (**about 95%**) with a cap of 25 holes, and beat grid (4/9/16) and random patterns at reducing volume error. Mern et al., "Intelligent Prospector v2.0" (arXiv 2410.10610, 2024, with Kurt House of KoBold) adds **several competing geological hypotheses** as the prior and detects early when all of them are wrong. It was used on the Zambian ultra-high-grade copper find in 2023. M17 cites a KoBold example of 9 vs 36 holes for the same go/no-go accuracy (unverified).

5. **The same Stanford group extended POMDPs to other subsurface problems.** Wang, Zechner, Corso, Kochenderfer and Caers, "A POMDP model for safe geological carbon sequestration" (NeurIPS 2022 CCAI; arXiv 2212.00669). Moss, Corso, Caers and Kochenderfer, **BetaZero** (arXiv 2306.00249, 2023/24): AlphaZero-style belief planning with a learned value and policy network. It beats POMCPOW on a mineral exploration POMDP with far fewer online rollouts (unverified magnitude). Lesson: offline learning of the value function makes deep look-ahead cheap at decision time.

6. **Amortized experimental design removes the compute wall.** Foster et al., "Deep Adaptive Design" (ICML 2021, arXiv 2103.02438), train a policy network on simulated histories to maximise total expected information gain. At run time each design costs one forward pass (milliseconds), against minutes-to-hours for step-by-step Bayesian design, with equal or better information (their reported results). Later work (iDAD, RL-BOED) handles non-differentiable simulators.

7. **Active search is the right ML framing for "find hidden systems".** Garnett et al., "Bayesian optimal active search" (ICML 2012) and Jiang et al., "Efficient nonmyopic active search" (ICML 2017) maximise the *number of positives found* under a budget, not model accuracy. Non-myopic active search found materially more positives than greedy one-step search on drug and citation benchmarks (unverified size). Multi-fidelity Bayesian optimisation (Kandasamy et al., MF-GP-UCB, NeurIPS 2016) and the knowledge gradient (Frazier and Powell, 2008) add costed sensors of differing quality. Geoscience well-placement work uses GP Bayesian optimisation for fields and CO2 storage (Durham repository "GP models for well placement optimisation"; IBM, "Bayesian optimization for field scale geological carbon storage").

8. **Drilling leaks information to neighbours.** Hendricks and Porter (AER 1988) and Hendricks and Kovenock (RAND 1989) showed, on US offshore oil leases, that neighbour tract holders bid better after a wildcat. They also delay drilling to free-ride on each other. A public temperature gradient hole or a BLM nomination is a signal to Zanskar and Ormat.

## Best methods and their reported results

| Method | Domain | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| BN + depth-3 look-ahead DP (Martinelli 2011/13) | 25 North Sea prospects | +14.8% value over naive order; depth-3 within 0.6% of exact DP on small case |
| POMCPOW POMDP (Mern and Caers 2023) | synthetic ore bodies | ~95% of profitable ore mined, <=25 holes; beats grids |
| IP v2.0 multi-hypothesis POMDP (Mern et al. 2024) | Cu, Zambia | Field use; early falsification of wrong priors (no public numbers) |
| BetaZero (Moss et al. 2024) | mineral POMDP | Beats POMCPOW with fewer online sims (unverified) |
| Information relaxation bounds (Brown and Smith 2013) | dependent wells | Certifies heuristic gap to optimal, a few % (unverified) |
| DAD (Foster et al. 2021) | design benchmarks | Millisecond decisions, EIG equal or better than greedy |
| Darajat MT VOI (Trainor-Guitton) | geothermal | VOI $11-50k/well; 2.5-4.4x higher under a pessimistic prior (M17) |

## What this means for our model design

- **Make the decision layer a POMDP over a portfolio, not per-cell VOI.** State = SENTINEL-COX latent field plus our rights holdings. Actions = buy/drop right, deploy sensor s at x, drill slim hole, sell. Reward = RNPV/ac from M34 minus costs. Per-cell EVSI under-values sensors that recalibrate the shared mask class.
- **Use depth-2 or 3 look-ahead, not exact DP.** Martinelli's numbers say this captures nearly all value. Add a Brown-Smith information-relaxation bound so the backtest reports "within X% of a clairvoyant".
- **Train an amortized policy (DAD/BetaZero style) on GHOST Tier 1 simulations.** Compute is plentiful. Offline we can run millions of synthetic campaigns. Online, every cell in the West gets a ranked next action in milliseconds and is replanned each night as new data lands.
- **Score policies by systems found per dollar (active search), with a Pandora's-box reservation value per parcel.** This matches the business. We want the count of rights that later prove up, not map accuracy. The reservation value also tells us when to stop measuring and simply lock the right.
- **Carry several hypothesis classes in the belief, as IP v2.0 does.** Use fault-hosted Basin and Range, magmatic, sedimentary-hosted and "masked by cold aquifer". Include a fast falsification check so we abandon a whole play type early.
- **Put competitor reaction and information leakage into the reward.** A BLM nomination, a public TG hole or a state lease filing reveals the parcel. The policy should lock neighbouring rights before any public signal, and prefer private-land or non-disclosing sensors first.
- **Model sensors as multi-fidelity, cost-aware arms.** Water-well logs, 2 m probes, E-field MT, gradient hole and slim hole each get a cost, lag and reliability matrix. A knowledge-gradient acquisition chooses among them. Batch the actions to match drilling seasons and permit lags.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No POMDP or active-search study for geothermal exploration exists.** All published geothermal VOI is static, one decision at a time (Trainor-Guitton). We would be first to do this, and it is a data-room story.
2. **No reliability matrices for cheap sensors against known blind systems** (repeated from M17). A POMDP is only as good as its observation model. A calibration campaign fills this.
3. **No historical replay benchmark.** Nobody has asked whether a sequential policy, given the dated 1970-2020 record (NGDS, TG holes, AI-extracted scanned logs), would have found Blue Mountain, McGinness Hills or Big Blind sooner and cheaper than what happened. Our document AI can build this time-stamped dataset.
4. **No planner includes land and rights actions.** Mineral POMDPs assume you already own the ground. Our action set adds buy, hold, drop and sell, with option value on cheap leases (Dixit-Pindyck real options).
5. **No game-theoretic exploration planner for a contested public lease market.** An LLM-agent simulation of rivals (Zanskar, Ormat, flippers) reacting to our public signals could train a policy robust to them.
6. **LLM agents as hypothesis generators.** IP v2.0 relies on human-stated hypotheses. An agent that reads the literature and well files could propose and retire geological scenarios inside the belief, with the POMDP doing the ranking.
