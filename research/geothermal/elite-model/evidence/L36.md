## What the field knows

**1. Mineral prospectivity invented area-capture.** Chung and Fabbri (2003, *Natural Hazards* 30:451-472) split landslides by date: fit on old events, score on new ones, and plot cumulative share of new events against share of area flagged. That is the "prediction-rate curve". Agterberg and Bonham-Carter (2005, *Natural Resources Research* 14:1-17) made the same curve standard for mineral potential maps, with a "success-rate" (training) versus "prediction-rate" (held-out) split. Fabbri and Chung (2008, *NRR* 17:107-118, "On blind tests and spatial prediction models") argued only time- or space-blind tests count. Yousefi and Carranza (2015, *Computers & Geosciences* 79:69-81) added the prediction-area (P-A) plot: where the "% deposits captured" curve crosses the "% area remaining" curve. Nykänen et al. (2015, *Ore Geology Reviews* 71:853-860) brought ROC to Ni-Cu prospectivity.

**2. Earthquake forecasting built the gold standard for prospective tests.** CSEP (Collaboratory for the Study of Earthquake Predictability) freezes model code and forecasts before the test window, runs them in a neutral testing centre, and scores with likelihood tests (Schorlemmer et al. 2007, *SRL* 78:17-29). The RELM five-year California test (Zechar et al. 2013, *BSSA* 103:787-798) is the best example of many rival spatial models scored prospectively on the same events (unverified page numbers). Molchan (1991) diagrams plot miss rate against fraction of space alarmed. That is area-capture, with a proper random-guess diagonal and significance bands.

**3. Ecology proved random CV lies on spatial data.** Roberts et al. (2017, *Ecography* 40:913-929) set block CV rules: blocks bigger than the autocorrelation range. Ploton et al. (2020, *Nature Communications* 11:4540) showed a biomass map with good random-CV skill had near-zero skill under spatial CV. Valavi et al. (2019, *Methods Ecol Evol* 10:225-232) ship `blockCV`. Milà et al. (2022, *Methods Ecol Evol* 13:1304-1316) propose NNDM/kNNDM, which matches CV distances to the real prediction distances. Wadoux et al. (2021, *Ecological Modelling* 457:109692) go further: only a probability (design-based) sample gives an unbiased map-accuracy estimate.

**4. Presence-only labels break AUC.** Lobo et al. (2008, *Global Ecology and Biogeography* 17:145-151) list why AUC misleads with pseudo-absences. The continuous Boyce index (Hirzel et al. 2006, *Ecological Modelling* 199:142-152) needs presences only: it is the predicted-to-expected ratio per score bin. That is a lift curve. Geothermal has the same problem: about 278 positive cells against about 710,000 unlabelled (M14).

**5. Petroleum learned calibration by keeping score.** Capen (1976, *JPT* 28:843-850) showed experts' 90% ranges held the truth far less often than 90%. Otis and Schneidermann (1997, *AAPG Bulletin* 81:1087-1109) describe Chevron's process: every prospect gets a pre-drill chance of success, then a post-drill look-back. Over hundreds of wells, predicted and realised success rates converged (exact counts unverified). Rose (2001, AAPG Methods 12) made "performance tracking" the industry norm. This is pre-registration under another name.

**6. Forecast verification gives the scoring rules.** Gneiting and Raftery (2007, *JASA* 102:359-378) on proper scores. Brier (1950) and the Murphy (1973) split into reliability, resolution and uncertainty. Decision curve analysis (Vickers and Elkin 2006, *Medical Decision Making* 26:565-574) scores a model by net benefit at each action threshold, not by accuracy.

**7. Geothermal has barely started.** No published geothermal model is scored on a time split, and none prospectively (M14, L01, L13, L14). Mordensky et al. 2023 ("Cursed?") used random splits and F1. Zanskar GeoCore uses 8-fold spatial block CV (M13). Nevada play fairway printed no AUC or area-capture (L01).

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method / study | Reported skill | Note |
|---|---|---|
| USGS 2025 Great Basin favorability (Mordensky et al., doi 10.5066/P14EET2C) | 54% of producing systems (15 of 28) in top 1% of area; 26 of 28 above 80th percentile (M01, M29) | In-sample on known, mostly surface-expressed systems. Not a blind test |
| Earlier USGS maps | About half of systems in top 20% of area (M03) | Same caveat |
| Mordensky et al. 2023, "Cursed?" | Median F1 below 0.04; XGBoost flagged about 2 of 55 test positives | Random split. F1 ceiling under PU labels is about 0.40 (M14 F9) |
| Zanskar base rate | 49 blind systems in about 27,000 drill samples: 1 in 550 holes (0.18%) | The null model any blind-hit claim must beat |
| Nevada PFA field test | 2 of 2 top areas gave >95 °C at ≤250 m; 2 of 12 TG holes >110 °C (L01) | Right basin, not right km² |
| DOE Industry-Coupled Program | 8 of 14 sites producing (57%) | Known systems only (M29) |
| IFC 2013 well success | 50% first well; 74% development | Per well, inside working fields |
| Typical mineral MPM papers (e.g. Lawley et al. 2022, *Ore Geol Rev* 141:104635) | Most known deposits in roughly 5-10% of area (unverified exact numbers) | Mostly random or in-sample splits |

The lesson is plain. Published "lift" is almost always in-sample, on surface-expressed systems, at coarse resolution. True blind, time-split skill is unknown for every public model.

## Datasets used

- USGS western US features and labels, Mordensky and DeAngelo 2023, doi 10.5066/P9V1Q9XM (278 positive cells).
- USGS 2025 Great Basin favorability, 1,200 realisations, doi 10.5066/P14EET2C (benchmark only in hindcasts; see MODEL.md Rule A).
- USGS 2026 electric-grade list, 205 systems with ordinal labels (28 producing, 78 known, 99 probable): https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273870
- INGENIOUS KGS (403 at ≥37 °C; 120 training sites at ≥120 °C), GDR 1391; Faulds et al. 2026 SGW.
- Zanskar 370-site inventory (summary stats only, private).
- NBMG geothermal well permits and logs (dated: the clock for time splits).
- BLM lease sale results by date (for decision replays of "what could we have bought, at what price").

## What this means for our model design

- **Build a dated "discovery ledger" first.** Every label and every input row needs a first-public-signal date and a data-available date. Without it no time split is honest. Freeze worlds at 1980, 1990, 2000, 2008, 2015 and 2020, and only allow data published before each freeze.
- **Score with a Molchan / area-capture curve on blind systems only, with a random-guess diagonal and bootstrap bands.** Report capture at 1%, 5% and 10% of area, and the continuous Boyce index. Drop F1 and plain AUC as headline numbers.
- **Beat three named baselines on the same split:** (a) the 1-in-550 base rate, (b) a "distance to nearest known system" smoother (the CSEP lesson: smoothed-history models are hard to beat), (c) the frozen-label GBM. The USGS 2025 raster is shown, but never as a fair rival in hindcasts, because it saw the answers.
- **Use NNDM-style CV distances.** Match held-out distances to the real distance from known systems to the cells we will buy. Report leave-one-province-out too.
- **Make the headline metric decision-based.** Replay each freeze as a land game: with budget B at that date's lease prices, which acres would the VOI policy lock, and how many later-proven MWe were inside? Report systems per $M and MWe per $M against "lock the USGS top 10%". This is decision curve analysis for land.
- **Pre-register CSEP-style.** Hash the model, the forecast raster and the cell list, and timestamp it (a public git commit or a notarised hash) before each field season, NBMG release or BLM sale. Score later with log-likelihood and Brier, split into reliability and resolution.
- **Add one design-based survey.** Following Wadoux 2021, spend part of the Sentinel budget on a stratified-random sample of cells. It is the only unbiased estimate of map accuracy, and it fixes the presence-only bias for good.
- **Track calibration like Chevron did.** Every visit and every drill hole gets a pre-drill P(hit). Publish the reliability diagram yearly. Buyers of heat rights will pay more for a seller with a calibrated record.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No dated label set exists in geothermal.** An LLM agent can read OSTI reports, GRC Transactions, NBMG permits and SGW papers and assign each of the about 400 KGS a first-signal date, discovery mode (spring, water well, mine, TG hole, model) and source citation. This is the core asset for every hindcast.
2. **No feature-leakage audit.** Many "regional" layers were surveyed because a system was found (gravity, MT, detailed fault maps). An agent can date each survey footprint and flag post-discovery data inside a frozen world, automatically.
3. **No prospective scoreboard.** Nobody runs a CSEP-like testing centre for geothermal. We could run one: publish frozen forecasts and score them, ours and others' (USGS, INGENIOUS). The neutral scorer becomes the trusted bank.
4. **No decision-level backtest.** Nobody has replayed BLM lease sales with a model. Agents can rebuild lease prices and parcel availability by year from BLM records and run the land game.
5. **Too few blind tests.** About 10 dated blind finds is thin. AI-extracted warm wells, TG holes and logs from scans turn many more "events" into scored points. Synthetic Nevadas from the Tier 1 simulator give thousands of planted tests, with the real effort geometry.
6. **No red team.** A separate adversarial agent can try to find leaks, random-split habits or cherry-picked freezes before results go out.
