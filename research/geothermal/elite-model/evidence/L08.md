# L08: Play fairway analysis outside Nevada (Hawaii, Cascades, Snake River Plain, Utah, Rio Grande rift, East Africa, Indonesia)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: methods and lessons from play fairway analysis (PFA) outside Nevada. The US drilling scorecard is already in `../../model/evidence/M03-play-fairway-results.md` and is not repeated. Here: how each region built its map, and what it got wrong. Items from memory and not re-checked are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**1. PFA is a petroleum idea moved into geothermal.** Each team mapped three or four "play elements" (heat, permeability, fluid, seal). Each became a Common Risk Segment (CRS) map, then combined (Pauling et al. 2023, NREL/TP-5700-86139).

**2. Snake River Plain (Idaho).** Shervais et al. built three CRS maps: heat (heat flow, vents, groundwater temperature, geothermometry, helium isotopes), permeability (faults, magnetic and gravity lineaments, each weighted by dilation and slip tendency from 3DStress at an assumed 70 degree dip) and seal (lake sediments, aquifer extent). Confidence came from kriging standard error and map scale; geothermometer cells with error over 30 C were zeroed. Grid: 2 km. Weights were set by hand. Result: eight prospect areas, each claimed at Raft River scale (10 MW) or more. The big lesson is masking. The Snake River aquifer is 200-550 m thick in the eastern plain and about 980 m in the central plain. It carries heat away, so shallow heat flow and BHT gradients read falsely low. There is a heat-flow data hole from Idaho Falls to Hagerman exactly over the aquifer. Source: Shervais Phase 1 report, GDR 1231.

**3. Hawaii.** Ito et al. 2017 used the most explicit probability model of any PFA team. Each element (heat, permeability, fluid) had its own logistic model. The resource probability was the product PrH x PrP x PrF ("voter-veto"). Weights were elicited from experts, with quality factors. No blind validation was run. Phase 3 deepened Lana'i Well 10 to 1,057 m and found 42 C/km, about twice background but sub-commercial. Lautze 2025 says the Phase 1 probabilities were "too conservative". The team now drops groundwater temperature from the heat model because rainfall swamps it. Sources: Ito et al. 2017 *Geothermics* 70; Lautze 2018 and 2025 SGW; GDR 544, GDR 1388.

**4. Washington Cascades.** Steely, Cladouhos, Forson and others (DOE EE0006728, final report 2021, OSTI 1823116) used CRS weighted sums with expert plus AHP weights. The weights came out near equal (Mt Baker 0.32/0.34/0.33). Phase 3 holes: Mt Baker MB76-31 gave 64 C/km (about 2x regional) and the St Helens control hole gave about 15 C/km, as the map predicted. The lesson is the "rain curtain". Heavy rain on arc volcanoes flushes the top few hundred metres. Surface springs and shallow wells say "cold" over real heat. Source: GDR 1333.

**5. Eastern Great Basin (Utah).** Wannamaker and others combined expert AHP weights with probability kriging for confidence. They leaned hard on regional 3D MT to find deep conductors under the Black Rock-Sevier and Cove Fort areas (unverified detail). The North Cove Fort hole was cancelled over expected heat and H2S. Phase 2 data barely moved drilling grades. Sources: Wannamaker et al. 2020, OSTI 1695591; OEDI submission 3749.

**6. Rio Grande rift and Tularosa Basin (New Mexico).** LANL's "hydrogeologic windows" scored each layer 0-1 by how well it matched known sites, then summed to an index of 0-6. Known sites averaged 3.6. The strongest single signal was boron: median about 0.5 mg/L at thermal sites against about 0.05 mg/L in about 4,000 wells. Tularosa ran deterministic logic and stochastic weights of evidence side by side. They found 8 and 10 plays, with 6 shared. LANL later ran unsupervised NMFk (matrix factorisation plus k-means) on 43-120 sample sites per area, with no published skill. Sources: LANL HGW report, GDR 611; Ahmmed et al. 2023 SGW (GeoThermalCloud); Vesselinov et al. 2021 *Geothermics* (unverified citation).

**7. East Africa rift.** Published regional maps are mostly multi-criteria GIS. A 2024-25 Eastern Africa study used fuzzy AHP on five layers: hot springs and geysers, young volcanics, major faults, regional heat flow and land surface temperature. It named 13 "extremely high" zones (6 Ethiopia, 2 Kenya, 2 Tanzania, 2 Uganda, 1 Rwanda-Burundi). Validation was "checked against known wells", with no hit-rate number. Heat sits under central volcanoes (Olkaria, Menengai, Aluto); the real risk is permeability and fluid, not heat. Sources: MJRD (MUST) article 226; UNIDO 2023 *Geothermal State of Play for East African Countries*; IRENA East Africa report.

**8. Indonesia.** Indonesia has about 23-29 GW of stated potential at about 300-350 named prospects (unverified range). Data-driven work is thin: evidential belief functions in West Java (Wibowo and Carranza 2006), and a 2025 random forest on GGMPlus gravity, Landsat-8 ratios and LST that mapped lithology at 72.8% accuracy. The key physical lesson (Hochstein and Browne school, unverified as a single source) is "upflow under the volcano, outflow 5-15 km down the flank". Hot springs on the flank are often far from the drill target. Many prospects are outflow-only, so the spring is not over the resource.

**9. Other volcanic ML (Japan).** Kiavarz et al. 2026 (ISPRS) ran random forest and a GAN on 152 wells in Akita and Iwate. 94.1% (RF) and 96.7% (GAN) of held-out wells fell in the "high" class. No negatives were defined, so this mostly shows the class is large.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Region / method | Reported skill | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| Hawaii logistic voter-veto (Ito 2017) | No blind test. Lana'i: 42 C/km at 1,057 m | Best structure; expert weights |
| Washington CRS + AHP | Mt Baker 64 C/km hit. Control 15 C/km as predicted | Only PFA with a designed negative control |
| SRP CRS + kriging confidence | Camas-1: 67-80 C, seal and permeability confirmed | Best confidence handling |
| Tularosa deterministic vs stochastic WofE | 8 vs 10 plays, 6 shared (about 50-75% agreement) | Only head-to-head method test |
| LANL Rio Grande index | Known sites mean 3.6 of 6. Boron 10x contrast | Chemistry beat geophysics |
| NMFk (LANL, EnviCloud 2026) | No skill numbers. 126 attributes, 8 latent signatures in Great Basin | Useful for imputation, not proven |
| East Africa fuzzy AHP | No numbers | Heat is not the question there |
| Indonesia RF lithology | 72.8% overall accuracy | Mapping aid, not resource model |

The honest summary: outside Nevada, **no PFA team published a blind, quantitative hit rate**. The best evidence of skill is the few drilled holes (M03): anomalous heat at every high-ranked site drilled, background at the one control, and no commercial confirmation.

## Datasets used

- GDR PFA portal (https://gdr.openei.org/pfa): Hawaii GDR 544 and 1388; Washington GDR 1333; SRP GDR 1231 and 1232; Rio Grande GDR 611; Oregon GDR 646; Eastern Great Basin OEDI 3749.
- Layers used: groundwater temperature and chemistry (boron, chloride), geothermometry, helium isotopes, heat flow, MT, gravity, magnetics, seismicity, InSAR/GPS, 3DStress slip tendency, lake-sediment seal maps, aquifer thickness, mud logs and core.
- New Mexico water-well chemistry (about 4,000 wells); GeoDAWN airborne survey (about 149,000 line-km over 51,857 km2); 14,342 Great Basin water samples.
- Abroad: GGMPlus gravity, Landsat-8 LST, East African spring inventories, ARGeo and GDC/KenGen data (mostly closed), Badan Geologi prospect list (access unverified).

## What this means for our model design

- **Make masking a layer, not noise.** Every non-Nevada PFA was fooled by cold water: the Snake River aquifer, Hawaii's rain, the Cascades rain curtain, the Indonesian flank outflow. Our shared latent mask class should have explicit drivers: recharge (PRISM rainfall), aquifer thickness, and hydraulic head gradient. A "cold" reading in a high-mask cell should carry almost no weight.
- **Use the Hawaii product structure, with learned weights.** P(heat) x P(permeability) x P(fluid) is the right shape. One element can veto the others. Replace the expert weights with a hierarchical model that shares weights across regions, so Nevada's labels help Idaho.
- **Carry confidence per cell the SRP way, but make it calibrated.** SRP set confidence from kriging error and map scale. We should do the same inside the likelihood (per-source noise and support size), not as a separate overlay.
- **Price depth by setting.** Basin and Range hits showed at 150-250 m. Arc and island hits needed 450-1,057 m and were still under 70 C. The RNPV/acre target must depend on play type (Moeck catalogue), or the model will over-rank arcs.
- **Weight chemistry heavily.** Boron (10x contrast in New Mexico), geothermometry, and helium isotopes were the strongest direct signals. AI extraction should pull old water chemistry first.
- **Always score against negatives.** The Japan "96.7% of wells in the high class" result is the trap. Our backtest must use designed negatives (cold oil wells, holes like MSH17-24) and report capture per share of area.
- **Separate upflow from outflow.** In Indonesia, East Africa, and at Granite Springs, the heat anomaly is not over the upflow. The model needs a lateral transport kernel from spring to source, not a point source.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No cross-region model.** Each PFA was fitted alone with its own expert weights. A single hierarchical model across all seven teams' layers has never been run. The GDR holds the inputs.
2. **No blind backtest of any PFA map.** We can replay each map against wells drilled after it was published (state permits, GDR logs, the 2026 Granite Springs deepening). That gives the first real hit-rate table.
3. **Masking is described, not modelled.** No one has fitted a rainfall- and aquifer-dependent mask. A physics-guided ML term (1D advection-conduction column fitted to the TG logs we extract) can learn it.
4. **Outflow back-tracing.** No public tool takes a warm spring's chemistry and hydraulic setting and outputs a probability map of the upflow.
5. **Grey literature in other languages.** Indonesian (Badan Geologi, Pertamina), Japanese (NEDO) and East African (GDC, ARGeo, UNU-GTP theses) reports hold thousands of temperature and chemistry points. Multilingual document AI can pull them. These are volcanic-play labels the US lacks.
6. **Lease-aware ranking.** No PFA ranked cells by "can we buy it cheaply now". Joining maps to BLM, state and fee status is our edge.
