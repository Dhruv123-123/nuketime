# L33: Weather AI analogues and a learned 3D subsurface world model of the West

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: what GraphCast, GenCast, Aurora, NeuralGCM and "world model" research teach us, and whether we can build a learned 3D subsurface model of the western US. The shared web-search budget was used up before this review ran, so no new searches were made. Weather-model numbers are from memory of the primary papers and are marked (unverified) where I am not sure of the exact figure. Subsurface numbers are taken from sibling reviews L10, L12, L20, L28 and L29, which cite primary sources. It does not repeat them.

## What the field knows

1. **Weather AI won because of a reanalysis, not because of the network.** GraphCast (Lam et al., Science 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.12794), Pangu-Weather (Bi et al., Nature 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.02556), FourCastNet (Pathak et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.11214), GenCast (Price et al., Nature 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.15796) and Aurora (Bodnar et al., Nature 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13063) all train mainly on ERA5 (Hersbach et al. 2020, QJRMS, doi:10.1002/qj.3803). ERA5 is about 80 years of hourly 0.25 degree, 37-level global fields. It is made by assimilating billions of raw observations into a physics model. The raw observations are sparse and patchy. The reanalysis is dense, gridded and physically consistent. That step turned messy data into a learnable target.
2. **Three model families matter.** Deterministic graph or transformer steppers (GraphCast, Pangu). Generative ensemble models (GenCast, a conditional diffusion model that samples 50-member ensembles). Pretrain-then-fine-tune foundation models (Aurora, about 1.3B parameters, pretrained on over a million hours of mixed reanalysis, forecast and climate data, then fine-tuned for air quality, ocean waves and cyclones (unverified figures)).
3. **Hybrid physics plus learning is competitive and more stable.** NeuralGCM (Kochkov et al., Nature 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07222) keeps a differentiable dynamical core and learns only the unresolved physics. It runs stable multi-decade climate rollouts, which pure ML models struggle with.
4. **Generative downscaling works.** NVIDIA CorrDiff (Mardani et al., Communications Earth and Environment 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.15214) turns 25 km fields into 2 km fields with a regression-plus-diffusion model. It adds realistic fine detail with calibrated spread.
5. **Benchmarks drove progress.** WeatherBench 2 (Rasp et al., JAMES 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15560) fixed the targets, splits and scores (RMSE, CRPS, spread-skill). Everyone then competed on the same scorecard.
6. **"World models" in AI** (Ha and Schmidhuber 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.10122; LeCun's JEPA line; DeepMind Genie) learn a latent state plus a transition and an observation decoder. The useful idea for us is not the time-stepping. It is a single latent state that every sensor is a noisy view of, and that you can query, sample and plan against.
7. **Subsurface has the pieces but no reanalysis.** There are dense 3D products: USGS National Crustal Model (Boyd and Shah, 2018-2020, unverified citation), Sui, Shen and Boyd 2025 Monte Carlo crustal thermal model (https://www.usgs.gov/publications/a-crustal-thermal-model-conterminous-us-constrained-multiple-data-sets-a-monte-carlo), CONUS-MT-2023 resistivity (Murphy et al., via EarthScope, unverified), USArray shear-velocity models (Shen and Ritzwoller 2016, JGR, unverified), CRUST1.0 and LITHO1.0. Each was inverted alone, at 10-70 km scale, from one data type. None is a joint, ensemble, parcel-scale state. Learned 3D or national models exist only for temperature: Stanford InterPIGNN (4.8 C MAE, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09961), LANL In-Context Earth (4.7 C MAE, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16665) and LANL Transparent Earth (https://arxiv.org/html/2509.02783v1) (from L10, L29).
8. **Synthetic worlds can stand in for the missing history.** Weather has about 10^5 training snapshots of one planet. We have one subsurface. The geology fix is simulated worlds. The Noddyverse (Jessell et al., ESSD 2022, unverified) is about one million synthetic 3D kinematic geology models with their gravity and magnetic responses. OpenFWI (Deng et al., NeurIPS 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02926) pairs velocity models with seismic data. Latent diffusion geomodels (Di Federico and Durlofsky 2024) and Diffusion Posterior Sampling (Chung et al., ICLR 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687) let a learned prior be conditioned on any forward operator (from L28).

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Task | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| GraphCast (36.7M-param GNN, 6 h step) | 10-day global weather | Beat ECMWF HRES on about 90% of 1,380 targets; about 1 minute per 10-day forecast on one TPU (unverified exact) |
| GenCast (diffusion, 12 h step, 0.25 degree) | 15-day ensembles | Beat ECMWF ENS on 97.2% of 1,320 targets; better extreme and cyclone-track skill (unverified exact) |
| Aurora (1.3B, pretrain + fine-tune) | Weather, air quality, waves, cyclones | Beat operational systems on most targets at a fraction of compute; fine-tuning on small task data worked (unverified exact) |
| NeuralGCM (hybrid) | Weather to climate | Matched ML on 1-10 day skill; stable multi-decade runs |
| CorrDiff | 25 km to 2 km downscaling | Better CRPS than regression-only; realistic extremes |
| In-Context Earth (set transformer) | US temperature at depth | 4.7 C MAE; 11.2 C at 4-6 km; PIT KS 2.5% (L10) |
| Nested FNO / geothermal FNO | 3D flow and heat surrogates | ~700,000x (CO2) and up to 10,000x (geothermal) faster than simulators (L12) |
| Data space inversion / EKI | Geothermal natural state | 92-95% coverage with 600-2,000 forward runs (L10, L12) |

Training cost is small at our compute budget. GraphCast took about four weeks on 32 TPU v4 devices (unverified). A West-wide subsurface state is a similar size to one ERA5 snapshot. The West is about 3 million km2. At 1 km by 100 m to 10 km depth, that is about 3 x 10^8 voxels, times about 10 variables. One ERA5 step is about 10^6 columns times 37 levels times 6 variables. So the grid is not the hard part. The hard part is having many consistent training states.

## Datasets used

- Weather: ERA5, ECMWF HRES and ENS archives, CMIP6 (Aurora), HRRR (CorrDiff), WeatherBench 2.
- Subsurface 3D (West): USGS National Crustal Model; Sui-Shen-Boyd 2025 thermal model; CONUS-MT-2023 and EarthScope SPUD EMTF transfer functions; USArray/EarthScope seismic tomography; CRUST1.0 and LITHO1.0; Bouligand Curie depth; SMU and NGDS heat flow and BHTs (about 400k readings); GDR 1592 Stanford TEM; INGENIOUS (GDR 1391); local 3D geologic models (Brady, Tuscarora, Utah FORGE, Patua; Siler et al., unverified list); state gravity and magnetic grids; ANSS seismicity; Quaternary fault database.
- Synthetic: Noddyverse; OpenFWI; TOUGH3 or Waiwera hydrothermal runs we would generate; GemPy and LoopStructural for structural worlds.

## What this means for our model design

- **Build a "subsurface reanalysis" first, call it SRA-West.** It is a 50-100 member ensemble of joint 3D states (T, resistivity, Vs, density, magnetisation, lithology class, fault-damage permeability proxy, fluid presence). Make it by ensemble assimilation (EKI or ensemble smoother) of every raw observation through its own learned forward operator. This is the ERA5 step that weather had and geothermal lacks.
- **Use a GenCast-style 3D latent diffusion prior, not a deterministic map.** Pretrain it on about 10^5-10^6 synthetic "Westlets" (50 x 50 x 10 km patches): Noddyverse-style kinematic geology plus TOUGH3 fault-hosted upflows plus masking aquifers. Then condition with Diffusion Posterior Sampling on the real data. Samples feed SENTINEL-COX's existence and T(z) terms directly.
- **Treat every sensor as an observation operator on one latent state.** This is the world-model idea. BHTs, TG logs, MT, gravity, magnetics, springs geochemistry, InSAR and AEF embeddings each get a decoder. Missing data then needs no imputation. It also replaces SENTINEL-COX's per-source 1D likelihoods with 3D ones once v1 is calibrated.
- **Copy Aurora's pretrain-then-fine-tune split.** One big backbone learns the West. Small heads fine-tune for the targets we sell: P(exists), T at 0.5-3 km, flow class, and RNPV/acre. New data types (a new MT survey, our own sensor) become a fine-tune, not a rebuild.
- **Copy CorrDiff for parcel scale.** Run the backbone at 1-2 km, then a conditional diffusion downscaler to 250 m near faults and parcels. Spend detail only where rights are for sale.
- **Copy NeuralGCM's hybrid core.** Keep conduction and advection physics as a differentiable core (or the L12 FNO surrogate). Learn only the residual (unresolved fault permeability, recharge). This keeps samples physically legal: no impossible temperature inversions, a known Trainor-Guitton failure (L10).
- **Build a WeatherBench for the subsurface ("GeoBench-West").** Freeze data by date. "Forecast" what wells drilled after year X found. Score CRPS of T(z), Brier score of discovery, and spread-skill. This is the dated hindcast in MODEL.md, made public as a moat and a recruiting tool.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No subsurface reanalysis of any region exists.** Every 3D crustal product is a single-method inversion at 10-70 km. A joint ensemble state at 1 km would be a first. AI makes it feasible: learned forward operators turn months of joint inversion into GPU hours.
2. **No generative prior is trained on hydrothermal physics plus structural geology together.** Noddyverse has no heat or flow. TOUGH3 studies have no realistic structure. Joining them makes a "Westlet" generator nobody has.
3. **No one scores subsurface models as forecasts.** Weather improved because of skill scorecards. Geothermal has no dated benchmark. We can set the standard.
4. **Document AI can supply the missing "observations" density.** Weather has billions of observations; we have about 400k BHTs. Extracted scanned TG logs, driller reports and mining holes (L21, MODEL.md Stage 0) are our ERA5 inputs. An agent swarm can also turn published 3D site models and cross-sections into labelled training states.
5. **No active-sensing loop sits on a world model.** Weather assimilation is passive. Ours can choose the next sensor or right to buy by expected information gain on the shared latent state, using the EVSI/$ engine.
6. **Time-lapse is unused.** InSAR, GNSS and seismicity are true time series. A dynamic head (the literal GraphCast step) could learn subsidence and microseismic responses to fluid flow, a permeability signal no static map uses.
