# L02: INGENIOUS and the Great Basin ML baseline

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Builds on `../../model/evidence/M02-ingenious.md` and `M14-ml-favorability.md`. It adds new numbers, not a repeat.

## What the field knows

- **INGENIOUS** (DOE GTO, UNR/NBMG with USGS, NREL, LBNL, Utah GS; 2021 to about 2026) mapped the Great Basin for hidden hydrothermal systems. Study area is 494,269 km² (Hart-Wagoner et al. 2024). The base data are on GDR 1391 (DOI 10.15121/1881483).
- **Structure is the backbone.** Faulds et al. (SGW 2026) combined 16 regional datasets into more than 1,430 favorable structural settings (FSS). They cover about 7.7% of the area. Median polygon is about 13.4 km². The 28 power plants sit in under 2% of the FSS. So about 98% of FSS are untested by production.
- **Type matters, and area shows it more strongly than counts.** Displacement transfer zones (DTZ) are 6.3% of FSS area but 33.3% of the area of the 120 training sites at 120 °C or hotter. That is about 5.3x enrichment. Pull-aparts are about 1.5x by area and 2.2x by count. Step-overs are 33.3% of FSS area but 24.5% of hot-site area (0.74x). (Ratios are my arithmetic.)
- **Labels are thin and partly unexplained.** There are 403 known systems at 37 °C or hotter. For 17.1% of them, mostly isolated warm wells in basins, no FSS could be assigned. Detailed geologic maps cover under 25% of the region. Detailed gravity covers only a small part, though basins are over half the area. Pleistocene lake sediments and glacial cover hide faults.
- **Hidden share.** About 75% of Great Basin resources are thought to be hidden (Coolbaugh 2006). Nearly 40% of known Nevada systems are already hidden ones (Faulds 2021).
- **Heat flow was split into conductive and convective parts.** DeAngelo et al. (2023) used a weighted 2D LOESS fit on SMU and USGS wells. They down-weighted outliers. Each well got a "residual" (measured minus background). The gradient-quality weighting worked best. These residuals are now the main ML labels.
- **Deep control.** Peacock and Bedrosian (2023) built a 3D MT resistivity model from more than 800 public transfer functions. Conductance maps cover five depth slices from about 2 to 200 km (DOI 10.5066/P9TWT2LU). High conductance at 15 to 20 km marks fluids at the brittle-ductile transition.
- **Blind finds happen at reservoir depth.** Zanskar's Big Blind (west Nevada) is about 121 °C (250 °F) at about 820 m (2,700 ft). Zanskar's regional models flagged about 260 km² (100 sq mi). Shallow TG holes followed, then a federal lease, then a deep well in Jul to Aug 2025 (MIT Tech Review, Dec 2025).

| Mordensky and DeAngelo 2026 data release (DOI 10.5066/P14QM39R) | Zone classification then favorability; 53 public features; 120 realizations; P50 map | Paper metrics not found (unverified). Data cover 2019 to Jun 2025. The grid CSV needs about 64 GB RAM to load in ArcGIS |

| Trainor-Guitton et al. 2025 | XGBoost quantile regression on MW, 37 plants, 248 zero-MW sites | P50 and P90 MW feed NREL reV |
| Jacinto et al. 2026 (Argenta Rise) | Full play-fairway workup of top target | 139 two-metre points: only two subtle anomalies, 2 to 3 °C. Ten TG wells to 244 m: mean 24.35 °C, gradient 33 to 71 °C/km (mean 46.2). That is below the regional 25.7 °C and 56 °C/km. 43 MT stations show conductors at about 1.5 km. 1,207 gravity stations. Study area about 1,150 km² |

Plain reading. The regional map is good at ranking. It puts producing systems, hidden ones included, in a small slice of area. At the prospect scale its one clean test failed. Argenta was **colder than regional background**, even though structure and MT looked right. The authors list six causes: under-sampling a large area, a cool aquifer masking heat, a resource outside the array, diffuse flow, recharge-dominated downflow, and a deeper reservoir.

## Datasets used

- GDR 1391 INGENIOUS Great Basin Regional Compilation (16 layer groups: 2-m temps, Quaternary faults, volcanics, strain, heat flow, MT conductance, gravity, magnetics, sinter/tufa, seismicity, spring and well chemistry, thermal conductivity, slip and dilation tendency).
- GDR 355 Great Basin structural inventory and FSS definitions (Faulds, earlier work).
- USGS conductive heat-flow maps and residuals (DeAngelo 2023).
- USGS 3D temperature model of the Great Basin v1.1, Nov 2024 (Burns et al.; ScienceBase 671f9d3ed34ed0f827eaa16d).
- USGS electrical conductance maps (10.5066/P9TWT2LU).
- USGS Great Basin favorability P05 to P95 (10.5066/P14EET2C).
- USGS 53-feature zone and favorability release (10.5066/P14QM39R).
- SMU / NGDS heat-flow wells. NVML (Nevada Machine Learning) labels.
- Argenta Rise 2-m, TG, MT, gravity and reprocessed 1980s seismic (in papers; GDR release unverified).

## What this means for our model design

- **Ingest the USGS P05 to P95 favorability and the 53-feature table as a prior layer, not as a target list.** Everyone has them. They set the baseline we must beat in area-capture curves.
- **Use FSS type and area as a permeability prior.** Weight DTZ about 5x and pull-aparts about 1.5 to 2x. Down-weight step-overs slightly. Update these weights as our own holes come in.
- **Treat the cold Argenta TG wells as depth-censored, hydrology-tagged negatives.** "Below background at 244 m" should reduce P(shallow anomaly). It should not zero P(system at 800 m) when a cool recharge aquifer is mapped. This plugs straight into SENTINEL-COX's censored column likelihood.
- **Add a "basin-cover" term to the search-effort thinning.** Under 25% detailed mapping, thin gravity and lake cover mean many FSS are not yet seen. The Cox intensity should be higher where effort is low and cover is thick, not lower.
- **Detrend elevation and other smooth features before learning.** Split each into a regional trend and a local residual. USGS showed this removes the west-east bias.
- **Fit separate sub-models per domain.** PCA shows the feature space is high-rank, and six clusters differ in plant share. Use the USGS zone classification or our own k-means as the shared latent class in SENTINEL-COX.
- **Bring in deep MT conductance as a "bottom-up" heat-source term.** It is a 3D feature (15 to 20 km fluids, Moho melt) that the 2D favorability stacks mostly ignore.
- **Target the 500 to 1,000 m window.** Big Blind sits at about 820 m. INGENIOUS TG holes stop at 244 to 460 m. Our value-of-information spine should price a deeper hole explicitly.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **Automated FSS mapping under basin cover.** Faulds lists semi-automated FSS as a goal that still needs experts. A model trained on the 1,430 expert polygons could learn FSS from lidar, InSAR, gravity, magnetics and drainage patterns. Then run it under lake beds where no one has mapped. This adds new targets that are not on the public map.
2. **Assigning the 17.1% orphan systems.** Warm basin wells with no FSS are labels the structure model cannot explain. An AI agent can pull logs, chemistry and old reports for each and assign a setting or flag a hidden fault.
3. **Prospect-scale models.** No public model works at the 1 to 5 km upflow scale. The Argenta authors call for a "local-scale play fairway model". We can build one that fuses 2-m, TG, MT and gravity with groundwater flow.
4. **A masking model.** No one has turned "cool aquifer" into a numeric layer. Combine depth to water, recharge, head gradients and TG profile curvature (Bredehoeft-Papadopulos fit) to predict where shallow probes will lie.
5. **Time-split back-tests.** No one has trained on pre-2010 data and tested on later blind finds such as Big Blind. We can do this with dated records.
6. **The 53-feature and P05 to P95 maps as features for a second-stage learner.** USGS gives cell-level uncertainty. A VOI engine can use the spread itself to pick where one cheap measurement moves the most value.
7. **Ranking FSS by stress and recency.** Slip tendency, dilation tendency, slip rate and recency are named as ranking tools but not yet fitted per FSS. An agent can compute them from public stress and fault data.
