# L13: Joint and probabilistic geophysical inversion (MT, gravity, magnetics, heat)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: joint and Bayesian inversion of magnetotellurics (MT), gravity, magnetics and thermal data for geothermal work. Covers trans-dimensional (trans-D) Bayesian MT and ML inversion. It builds on `../../model/evidence/M08-magnetotellurics.md` and `M09-gravity-magnetics.md` and does not repeat their data inventories. Web budget: 10 searches and 9 fetches. The shared fetch budget ran out partway, so some numbers come from memory and are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Single-image deterministic inversion hides the uncertainty that matters.** A smooth 3D MT model gives one "best" picture. It does not say how sure we are that the clay cap's base, the upflow, or the basement is where the picture puts it. Ardid's Auckland thesis (2021) says this directly about clay caps: deterministic inversions understate the uncertainty in conductor geometry ([ResearchSpace 2292/57878](https://researchspace.auckland.ac.nz/handle/2292/57878)).

2. **Well data turned into priors is the main lever.** Ardid built a 1D Bayesian MT inversion. Its structural priors come from methylene blue (MeB, a smectite clay test) in wells. He then combined MT, MeB, lithology and temperature logs at Wairakei-Tauhara. From these he estimated temperature gradients, clay formation temperatures and heat output, and fed the inferred clay cap into a TOUGH2 flow model. The paper version is Ardid et al. 2021, *Geophysics* 86(3) (unverified citation details).

3. **Trans-D Bayesian MT is mature in 1D. In 2D it is now practical.** Reversible-jump MCMC lets the data choose how many layers are needed, so the model's complexity is part of the uncertainty. Current work adds hierarchical noise and empirical data covariance, plus parallel tempering, and inverts MT jointly with seismic data (Shahsavari, Dettmer, Unsworth and Schaeffer, [arXiv 2510.15779](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2510.15779)). There are 2D trans-D Gaussian-process MT inversions ([OUCI record](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4ronbOLl)). Earlier work includes Mandolesi et al. 2018 on 1D RJ-MCMC MT (unverified) and Ray and Key's trans-D CSEM (unverified).

4. **Probabilistic 3D MT is possible, but only with a reduced parameterisation.** Rosas-Carbajal et al. ran 3D MCMC on time-lapse MT at the Paralana EGS in South Australia (GJI 203:1946-1960, 2015, [USGS 70170986](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70170986)). They described the plume with a few Legendre moments. The deterministic inversion put the resistivity change too shallow. The probabilistic one put it at the injection depth, oriented N-S. Manassero, Afonso et al. (GJI 2020) used reduced-order forward models to make full 3D MT MCMC affordable. They report "staggering" efficiency gains over standard MCMC ([record](https://cosechador.siu.edu.ar/bdu/Record/I19-R120-10915-131838)). Part II added joint MT plus surface-wave inversion (unverified).

5. **Randomize-then-optimize (RTO) gives near-Bayesian ensembles from ordinary inversion code.** Blatter, Morzfeld, Key and Constable (GJI 2022, Parts I and II) perturb the data and the prior and rerun a regularised inversion many times. The spread of the results approximates the posterior ([RTO Part 2 PDF](https://igppweb.ucsd.edu/~mmorzfeld/docs/RTO_Part_2.pdf)). The cost is roughly N times one deterministic inversion, and it is embarrassingly parallel (unverified detail).

6. **Joint inversion of MT and gravity works through structure, not exact rock physics.** Cross-gradient coupling, from Gallardo and Meju 2003 (unverified), makes the resistivity and density models change in the same places without fixing a formula between them. CGG ran 3D joint MT-gravity at Sorik Marapi, Indonesia, and cut "fault tears" into the regularisation ([Soyer et al., EAGE 2020](https://www.viridiengroup.com/sites/default/files/2021-08/Delineating_Geothermal_Structure_from_3D_Joint_Inversion_FINALPUBLISHED.pdf)). MT was the more robust method. Gravity improved a lot once shallow MT was included. An MeB log that came in after the inversion matched the modelled fault break. Darajat used the same multiphysics workflow ([CGG](https://www.cgg.com/resources/technical-content/industry-article/geologically-consistent-multiphysics-imaging-darajat)). In the Great Basin, USGS work at Argenta Rise found that gravity basement depth matched the base of the MT conductors (M09).

7. **Probabilistic MT can constrain other methods one after another.** Giraud et al. (Solid Earth 14:43, 2023, [PDF](https://se.copernicus.org/articles/14/43/2023/se-14-43-2023.pdf)) turned 1D trans-D MT posteriors (457 sites, 540 drill-hole depths) into probabilities of where the cover-basement contact sits. Those probabilities became bounds for a magnetic inversion.

8. **Temperature from resistivity is learned, not derived.** Spichak's "electromagnetic geothermometry" calibrates a neural network on resistivity profiles from MT against borehole temperatures ([Stanford SGW 2010](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2010/spichak.pdf); Elsevier book 2015).

9. **Physics-informed networks now assimilate MT into natural-state reservoir models.** Ishitsuka et al. 2025 (AGU, DOI 10.1029/2025JH000683, [record](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/reliable-and-practical-inverse-modeling-of-naturalstate-geothermal-systems-using-physicsinformed-neural-networks-threedimensional-model-construction-and-assimilation-with-magnetotelluric-data/)) used PINNs at Kakkonda, Japan. Adding MT to well data improved the permeability field. PINNs beat purely data-driven ML on temperature and pressure when wells were few.

10. **ML inversion is still mostly synthetic and mostly 1D.** Bayesian CNNs give fast 1D MT inversions with uncertainty bands ([Poddar, Sharma, Vashisth, arXiv 2508.03472](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/2508.03472)). Field validation was left as future work. 2D and 3D deep-learning MT papers (for example Puzyrev 2019 GJI, unverified, and a 2024 *Remote Sensing* paper, [MDPI 16:3400](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/16/18/3400)) report good fits on synthetic tests. Few of them test on drilled field data.

## Best methods and their reported skill (numbers)

| Method | Case | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| ANN resistivity-to-temperature (Spichak) | Soultz GPK2, 13 km MT profile | Extrapolating 2000-3878 m from the upper 2 km: **1.8%** relative error. With intermediate data, 3878-5046 m: **0.4%**. From 0-2 km straight to 3.9-5 km: **18.5%**, up to about 50 °C too low. 6 km forecast 220.6 °C against 217.3 °C in the literature |
| 3D MCMC time-lapse MT | Paralana EGS | Plume at the correct depth. The deterministic inversion put it too shallow. Required resistivity changes were too large, a sign of base-model error |
| Trans-D MT then constrained magnetics | Cloncurry, 457 MT sites | Model misfit 2.6e-2 to **1.2e-2 SI**. Model entropy **0.29 to 0.09** (synthetic). The field profile needed manual relaxing in 5 areas |
| BCNN 1D MT | 30,000 synthetic 3-layer models | Truth inside the ±3σ band, including at 2.5% noise. Large RMSE on resistive layers, about 1,000 Ω·m |
| 3D joint MT and gravity, cross-gradient with fault tears | Sorik Marapi | Qualitative. Sharper graben boundaries. One MeB well matched |
| PINN with MT and wells | Kakkonda | Qualitative. Better permeability and temperature than data-only ML |

The lesson: **interpolating temperature near wells works well, at a few percent. Extrapolating far below the deepest well fails, at 18.5% or about 50 °C.** No geothermal paper we found reports a calibrated hit rate for blind targets, such as P(T > 150 °C at 1.5 km) checked against drilling.

## What this means for our model design

- **Feed ensembles, not single pictures.** Every MT and gravity inversion we use should be an ensemble of 50-200 plausible models, made by RTO or trans-D MCMC. SENTINEL-COX's 1D column likelihood then takes, at each cell, the probability of a conductor and the spread in its depth, not one smoothed value.
- **Use the 1D trans-D route at each station first.** It is cheap, needs no 3D mesh, and matches our "1D columns only in v1" rule. Run it over every public SPUD and GDR transfer function in the West. Hierarchical noise handles messy legacy data.
- **Learn the resistivity-to-temperature link as a likelihood with a "false conductor" class.** Fit it on paired MT and temperature columns (public wells plus our gradient holes). Add mixture terms for basin clay, brine and fossil caps. This is the shared latent mask class from MODEL.md applied to MT.
- **Never trust MT-derived temperature below the deepest nearby well.** Spichak's 18.5% failure says the posterior variance must grow quickly with distance below the deepest tie. Our depth bins at 2-3 km should get a wide prior unless a deep conductor or a nearby deep well supports them.
- **Use joint gravity, magnetics and MT for the frame, through cross-gradient or probabilistic sequential coupling.** Output an ensemble of basement and fault surfaces (M09 item 1). Pass "distance to a fault intersection at depth" to the count layer with its spread.
- **Amortise inversion with simulation-based inference.** Train a neural posterior estimator on millions of synthetic 1D and 2.5D columns drawn from Great Basin priors. Then every legacy station inverts in milliseconds, with calibrated posteriors. This links to GHOST-SBI Tier 1.
- **Rank new MT sites by expected information gain on RNPV, not on resistivity.** RTO ensembles let us compute how much one new E-node station would shrink P(T > 150 °C at 1.5 km).

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No West-wide probabilistic re-inversion of legacy MT.** Thousands of SPUD, GDR and USGS transfer functions have only ever been inverted one survey at a time, deterministically. A neural posterior estimator run over all of them gives a uniform probabilistic conductor atlas, and nobody has one, including Zanskar (based on M13; unverified).
2. **No public, calibrated resistivity-to-temperature training set.** Pairs of MT stations and wells exist in pieces across theses, OFRs and GDR. AI document extraction (GeoFM Stage 0) can build the pairs. Every gradient hole we drill next to an E-node adds a private label.
3. **No skill score against drilling outcomes.** No paper checks Bayesian MT temperature claims against later blind wells. We can hindcast Pumpernickel, Big Blind and other named blind finds using only data dated before the discovery.
4. **Scanned paper MT pseudo-sections and old 1980s audio-MT and Schlumberger soundings.** AI can digitise them into apparent-resistivity curves. A 1D Bayesian inversion then turns them into new evidence that no one else uses.
5. **Joint inversion with heat-flow physics is rare.** Afonso's group couples MT to thermal and petrological models for the lithosphere (unverified). Nobody does this at basin scale with conductive and advective plume physics. An amortised surrogate for column heat transport plus Archie and clay conduction is within reach.
6. **Structure-aware ML priors.** A generative model (diffusion or VAE) trained on Great Basin fault-block geometry could act as the prior in MT, gravity and magnetic inversion. This is better than smoothness priors and fits the "fault tear" result from Sorik Marapi.
