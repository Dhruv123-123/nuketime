## What the field knows

1. **Modern ML scores are not probabilities by default.** Guo et al. (2017, ICML, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks", https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599) showed deep nets are over-confident, and that one fitted "temperature" fixes most of it on in-distribution data. Boosted trees are also mis-calibrated; Niculescu-Mizil and Caruana (2005, ICML) fixed them with Platt or isotonic scaling (unverified details).
2. **Calibration breaks under shift.** Ovadia et al. (2019, NeurIPS, "Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty?", https://arxiv.org/abs/1906.02530) found every method gets worse as data drifts. Deep ensembles degraded least. Temperature scaling fitted in-distribution did not survive shift. Our blind-system cells are a shifted population by construction.
3. **Deep ensembles are the strong practical baseline.** Lakshminarayanan et al. (2017, NeurIPS, https://arxiv.org/abs/1612.01474): 5 independently trained nets plus a proper scoring rule. Full-batch HMC (Izmailov et al. 2021, ICML, "What Are Bayesian Neural Network Posteriors Really Like?", https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.14421) beat ensembles only slightly, at huge compute. MC dropout (Gal and Ghahramani 2016) and SWAG (Maddox et al. 2019) are cheaper and weaker (unverified ranking per task).
4. **Distance-aware models fix a specific failure.** Standard nets can be confident far from training data. SNGP (Liu et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10108) and DUQ (van Amersfoort et al. 2020) make uncertainty grow with distance from training data. The geospatial version is the "area of applicability" (Meyer and Pebesma 2021, Methods in Ecology and Evolution, https://doi.org/10.1111/2041-210X.13650): a dissimilarity index in weighted feature space, with cells above a threshold masked as "no prediction".
5. **Conformal prediction gives coverage guarantees with no model assumptions,** but only under exchangeability. Angelopoulos and Bates (2021, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction", https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511) is the standard primer. Conformalized quantile regression (CQR; Romano, Patterson and Candès 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03222) gives adaptive interval widths. Spatial data breaks exchangeability. Fixes: weighted conformal for covariate shift (Tibshirani et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.06019); conformal beyond exchangeability with a bounded coverage loss (Barber et al. 2023, Annals of Statistics, https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.13415); local spatial conformal for kriging-type prediction (Mao, Martin and Reich 2024, JASA, "Valid Model-Free Spatial Prediction", unverified venue and year); and online adaptive conformal that re-tunes as new labels arrive (Gibbs and Candès 2021, ACI, https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.00170).
6. **Conformal selection is the decision tool, not the interval.** Jin and Candès (2023, JMLR, "Selection by Prediction with Conformal p-values", https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01408) turn any score into conformal p-values and run Benjamini-Hochberg. The output is a short list with a guaranteed false discovery rate (FDR). That is exactly "which cells to lease", with a promise like "at most 30% of this list is dry".
7. **Rare events need their own checks.** Reliability diagrams and ECE are unstable when positives are under 1%. Weather and seismology fixed this long ago. Gneiting, Balabdaoui and Raftery (2007, JRSS-B) set the rule "maximise sharpness subject to calibration" and use PIT histograms and CRPS. Earthquake forecasting (CSEP; Schorlemmer et al. 2007, Zechar et al. 2010) tests gridded rate forecasts prospectively with N-tests (total count), L-tests (likelihood) and S-tests (spatial shape) against Poisson. Rare binary events use extremal scores such as SEDI (Ferro and Stephenson 2011) (unverified details).
8. **Presence-only maps have a known calibration trap.** In presence-only data, the true base rate is not identifiable from the data alone. Elkan and Noto (2008, KDD) show a PU classifier equals the true probability times a constant c, the label frequency. Phillips and Elith (2010, Ecology 91:2476, "POC plots") calibrate presence-only species models using a few presence-absence surveys. The continuous Boyce index (Hirzel et al. 2006) is the standard presence-only rank check.
9. **Spatial validation matters more than method.** Ploton et al. (2020, Nature Communications 11:4540) and Roberts et al. (2017, Ecography) show random CV overstates skill. Wadoux et al. (2021, Ecological Modelling) argue that design-based probability samples, not block CV, give unbiased map accuracy. Coverage claims inherit the same bias: intervals calibrated on random splits are too narrow far from wells.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | Reported result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature scaling | ECE on CIFAR-100 ResNet from roughly 16% to roughly 1% (unverified exact values) | Guo 2017 |
| Deep ensembles (M=5) | Best or near-best NLL and Brier under shift across image, text and tabular benchmarks | Ovadia 2019 |
| HMC BNN | Small gains over ensembles at hundreds of TPU-days (unverified) | Izmailov 2021 |
| Bayesian NN, Nevada geothermal | Stable per-pixel distributions over 10 runs; plain nets unstable. No AUC reported; label-copy leakage | Brown et al. 2022, arXiv 2209.15543 (from M14) |
| USGS Great Basin Monte Carlo | 1,200 realisations, P05-P95 per cell (DOI 10.5066/P14EET2C) | Mordensky et al. 2025 (from M14) |
| CQR | Marginal coverage at nominal 90% with intervals 10-40% shorter than split conformal on regression benchmarks (unverified range) | Romano 2019 |
| Conformal selection | FDR held at target level in drug-discovery screening tests | Jin and Candès 2023 |
| Temperature at depth, Molasse benchmark | Only 87% coverage at nominal 90% after care | cited in MODEL.md section 7 |
| Physics-informed graph net, US temperature | MAE 4.8 °C (no coverage reported) | Aljubran and Horne 2024 (from M14) |
| Digital soil mapping, quantile regression forests | PICP near nominal 90% on random CV, lower on spatial CV (unverified) | Vaysse and Lagacherie 2017; Poggio et al. 2021 (SoilGrids 2.0) |

Headline: no published geothermal favorability model reports a reliability diagram, a Brier score, a PIT histogram, or a coverage rate on blind systems. The field reports ranks (AUC, F1) and spreads (P05-P95), but never checks whether the spreads are right.

## Datasets used

- CIFAR-10/100, ImageNet, and shifted versions (CIFAR-10-C) for calibration benchmarks.
- USGS western US favorability features and labels, DOI 10.5066/P9V1Q9XM (~710k cells, 278 positives).
- USGS Great Basin 1,200-realisation favorability, DOI 10.5066/P14EET2C.
- Nevada Play Fairway 250 m feature maps (Brown 2022; Faulds et al.).
- SMU Geothermal Laboratory and NGDS well temperatures (temperature-at-depth UQ).
- CSEP earthquake catalogues (ANSS ComCat) for the prospective-testing template.
- SoilGrids 2.0 and WoSIS soil profiles (spatial interval coverage benchmarks).
- USGS 2026 electric-grade systems list, 205 systems with ordinal labels (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70273870).

## What this means for our model design

- **Calibrate the quantity we pay for.** SENTINEL-COX outputs a Poisson rate per cell. Test it the CSEP way: N-test (do we predict the right number of discoveries?), S-test (in the right places?), L-test, on a time split (data frozen at 2010, score 2010-2025 discoveries, blind ones only). This is the honest rare-event check that AUC is not.
- **Make the PU constant explicit.** Our effort-thinning term is the Elkan-Noto label frequency c(x). Fit c from dated search effort, then check calibration with POC plots against the cells where we have true negatives (cold wells, our own sensor controls). Report calibration per mask class, since c differs most by mask.
- **Ensemble, but diversify by data, not seed.** Build a deep ensemble of 10-20 members using spatial-block bootstraps and different feature subsets (the "Cursed?" result says feature choice drives variance). Add a distance-aware term (SNGP head or AOA dissimilarity index) so cells far from any well get wide, not confident, posteriors.
- **Turn the buy list into conformal selection.** Compute conformal p-values from a spatially weighted calibration set (weights from feature-space similarity to the target province) and run BH. The deliverable to buyers and the investment committee is "list of N parcels, FDR at most q", with a Mondrian split per province and mask class.
- **Wrap temperature columns in CQR plus local spatial conformal.** Keep the 85-95% coverage gate from MODEL.md section 7, but calibrate on spatial blocks larger than the autocorrelation range, and report coverage separately inside and outside the area of applicability.
- **Run adaptive conformal online.** Every sensor or dart reading is a new label. ACI re-tunes the interval width as the field campaign runs, so coverage holds even while the label distribution shifts towards blind cells.
- **Score with proper rules on rare events.** Use log score, Brier skill score with a decomposition, PR-AUC, Boyce index and SEDI. Treat ECE only as a picture, with adaptive bins.
- **Pre-register forecasts.** Publish frozen, hashed cell-rate maps before each lease sale and drilling season. A public calibration record is a sales asset for a heat-rights bank.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No calibrated geothermal map exists.** Nobody publishes reliability or coverage on blind systems. Being first gives us a defensible credibility edge over Zanskar's AUC-style claims.
2. **Conformal under survey bias.** Weighted conformal assumes known shift weights. Our effort model gives estimated weights from dated search history. Combining the two (effort-weighted conformal) is unpublished. An agent can tune and stress-test it on synthetic worlds.
3. **Synthetic calibration worlds.** Use GHOST-SBI to generate thousands of synthetic Great Basins with planted blind systems and realistic masking. Check coverage and FDR where the truth is known. This replaces the missing ground truth with compute.
4. **Noisy, AI-extracted labels.** Temperatures read by AI from scanned logs carry extraction error. Conformal methods robust to label noise exist (Einbinder et al. 2022; Sesia et al. 2023, unverified) but have never been applied to OCR-derived geoscience labels. Feed the extractor's own confidence into a noise-aware calibration step.
5. **Decision calibration.** Calibrate the RNPV/acre ranking itself, not just the probability: check that realised value per tranche matches predicted value per tranche (Zhao et al. 2021, "Calibrating Predictions to Decisions", unverified).
6. **Agentic forecast audit.** A standing agent that re-runs CSEP-style tests each time a new well, lease or discovery appears, and flags drift by province.
