# L07: DOE geothermal data infrastructure and what it funds (2024-2026)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: DOE's public data plumbing for finding hidden hydrothermal systems. It covers GDR, GeoVision, the hidden-systems and exploration-drilling programmes, the GeoDAWN and GeoFlight airborne surveys, Utah FORGE data, and the money flowing in 2024-2026. It builds on `../../evidence/D04-ngds-gdr.md` (GDR size and NGDS retirement), `../../evidence/L06-nrel-geovision.md` (GeoVision numbers), `../../model/evidence/M31-new-data-2025-2026.md` (GDR records 1700-1892) and `../../evidence/B05-policy.md` (budget). It does not repeat them. It asks one question: **how does DOE's data pipeline shape what our model can see, and when?** Web budget used: 11 searches and 12 fetches.

## What the field knows

**1. GDR is big, but it is an R&D deposit box, not an exploration inventory.** The GDR holds about 764 TB in 1,426 datasets from 146 providers (https://gdr.openei.org/). Of the 178 public records numbered 1700 to 1892, 108 are Utah FORGE (M31). The National Geothermal Data System (NGDS) is now "retired". Its SMU node (44,000+ wells, GDR 1704) and AASG node (GDR 1805, Zenodo) were moved over in 2025-2026 (D04). There is no shared schema across packages. Accuracy is often "not assessed" (GDR 252).

**2. GeoVision gives hidden hydrothermal almost no credit for better exploration.** GeoVision 2019 holds 23,038 MWe of undiscovered hydrothermal in ReEDS. It assumes that resource becomes available at only 1% per year (BAU) or 3% per year (Technology Improvement case). The TI rate was "conservatively held constant" because a gain from better exploration could not be quantified (L06; https://www.energy.gov/eere/geothermal/downloads/geovision-harnessing-heat-beneath-our-feet). The 60 GWe by 2050 headline is mostly deep EGS. The base layer is still USGS 2008: 30,033 MWe mean undiscovered hydrothermal (https://pubs.usgs.gov/fs/2008/3082/). DOE planning therefore treats discovery rate as fixed. A model that raises discovery rate is pricing something the official plan ignores.

**3. Hidden-systems programmes run in waves.** GRED I (2000, 7 awards: Rye Patch, Blue Mountain, Lightning Dock and others), GRED II (2002, 7 awards: Raft River, Truckhaven, Glass Mountain and others) and GRED III (2004) paid industry to drill at named prospects (https://openei.org/wiki/Geothermal_Resource_Exploration_And_Definition_Projects). Blue Mountain and Lightning Dock later became power plants. Lightning Dock is now Zanskar's field and GEOGYM benchmark site (GDR 1856/1868). Play Fairway ran from 2014 to 2021. The FY2020 multi-topic FOA gave UNR Topic 1, "Exploration RD&D: Hidden Geothermal Systems in the Basin and Range", which became INGENIOUS (part of $17.5M across two awards; https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/u-s-doe-selects-5-projects-to-receive-up-to-28m-for-geothermal-research/). The INGENIOUS structural paper (Faulds et al., Stanford 2026, SGP-TR-230) maps **more than 1,430 favourable structural settings over about 7.7% of the Great Basin**. It tags **403 known systems at 37 °C or above** and **120 training sites at 120 °C or above**. Step-overs are 34.2% of the hot training sites, intersections 26.7% and terminations 14.2%. About 17% of known systems have no clear structural setting; most are isolated warm wells in basins (https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Faulds.pdf).

**4. Airborne surveys are the biggest new public layer, and they are under-used.**
- **GeoDAWN** (DOE + USGS Earth MRI). It flew from Nov 2021 to Nov 2022 and was released 4 Mar 2024 (DOI 10.5066/P93LGLVQ). It covers 149,030 line-km over 51,857 km² of NW Nevada and eastern California. Area 1 has 200 m lines and Area 2 has 400 m lines, flown at 100-200 m above ground. It has magnetics and radiometrics, plus 3DEP lidar (https://www.usgs.gov/data/geodawn-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-northwestern-great-basin-nevada-and).
- **GeoFlight** (Salton Trough). It flew from Jan 2022 to Mar 2023 and was released **25 Aug 2025** (DOI 10.5066/P14C7WNM). It covers 94,671 line-km over 16,772 km² on 200 m lines (https://www.usgs.gov/data/geoflight-airborne-magnetic-and-radiometric-surveys-salton-trough-southern-california).
- The USGS planning paper says airborne gravity, EM and hyperspectral surveys may follow (https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70261199). M09 adds the eastern Great Basin magnetics (about 126,000 line-km, Feb 2026) and 11,079 line-km of HELITEM AEM (Sep 2025).
- The gap between flying and public release is **16 to 29 months**. That gap is the window in which anyone with early access, or a fast reader on release day, has an edge.

**5. DOE is now paying to turn GeoDAWN into faults, as an ML contest.** The **GEMS Prize** ($300k, DrivenData, closes 3 Dec 2026) asks for an algorithm that makes "enhanced fault datasets" to "identify hidden geologic faults to discover new geothermal resources". It trains on the USGS Quaternary fault database. It tests on newly mapped faults in the GeoDAWN region. The final round is re-scored on labels expanded by expert review of all submissions (https://www.drivendata.org/competitions/306/competition-doe-gems/). Winning code may become a public baseline, so a fault layer from GeoDAWN will become a commodity during 2027 (unverified whether open source is required).

**6. The 2026 funding wave is about drilling, and its data must go to GDR.** NOFO DE-FOA-0003472 ($171.5M, announced 25 Feb 2026, applications due 30 Apr 2026) is rolling for up to 72 months, with cycles about every 12 months (https://www.energy.gov/hgeo/geothermal/funding-notice-next-generation-geothermal-field-tests-and-geothermal-resource). In Sep 2026 DOE selected **21 projects, up to $99M**: 5 EGS field tests and **16 exploration-drilling projects**. DOE says project data "will be public through GDR" (https://www.energy.gov/node/4860304). The 16 drilling sites are: Brawley (DAVINCI), Cove Fort (Univ. Utah), a Basin and Range site (Zanskar), Humboldt County NV (Fervo), San Ildefonso Pueblo NM, Mt Augustine AK (GeoAlaska), Wind River WA (Hexagon), Sierra Army Depot CA (GreenFire), Nevada crystalline rock with an MT-to-temperature 3D model (TLS Geothermics), South Brawley (LiPower), Pisgah Crater CA (INTEK), eastern Oregon (Oriah), Socorro County NM (XGS), Escalante Desert UT (Raser), Roosevelt Hot Springs extension, Beaver County UT (400C), and Grand View ID (Invenergy) (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/us-doe-announces-99m-funding-for-enhanced-geothermal-field-tests-and-exploration-drilling/).

**7. FORGE dominates GDR bytes, and it was extended.** Utah FORGE got **$80M more for 2024-2028** (https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/utah-forge-secures-4-year-extension-more-funding-for-enhanced-geothermal-research/). Its packages are DAS microseismic records (e.g. GDR 1747), well logs, core and stimulation data. These are EGS data from one granite site. They help calibrate conductive gradients and stress, not hidden hydrothermal detection.

**8. Budget and organisation.** The FY2026 request raised geothermal from $118M to $150M while solar and wind went to $0. GTO is now the "Office of Geothermal" inside the Hydrocarbons and Geothermal Energy Office (HGEO). NREL is renamed the National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) (B05, M31). The final FY2026 appropriation is (unverified).

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

- **Play Fairway / INGENIOUS weighted overlays.** Phase 1 PFA scored 34 benchmarks above 130 °C at an average of 51.4, against a high cut-off of about 45 (L01). The two PFA-drilled sites were hot at TG depth. The top INGENIOUS site, Argenta Rise, came back cool (D04/M29). No held-out AUC is reported in the 2026 structural paper.
- **ML favorability (USGS/NREL XGBoost, BNN, LightGBM).** This is covered in M14. These models feed on the same INGENIOUS layers, so they share the same blind spots.
- **Historic GRED.** At least 2 of 14 GRED I/II sites (Blue Mountain, Lightning Dock) became power plants, about 14% (unverified count; other sites may have, too).
- **Airborne magnetics.** There is no published geothermal skill number from GeoDAWN yet. The GEMS Prize is the first benchmark, and its scores will be public after Dec 2026.

## Datasets used

GDR (gdr.openei.org; key IDs 616, 1390/1391 INGENIOUS, 1592 Stanford Thermal Earth Model, 1682 BRIDGE, 1704 SMU, 1732 supply curves, 1805 AASG, 1852 EGS suitability, 1856/1868 Lightning Dock, 1877 ordinances, 1878 VOI app). GeoDAWN mag/rad (10.5066/P93LGLVQ) and GeoDAWN lidar (3DEP). GeoFlight mag/rad (10.5066/P14C7WNM). Eastern Great Basin Earth MRI magnetics, HELITEM AEM. USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database (GEMS training labels). INGENIOUS FSS polygons (1,430+). GeoVision ReEDS inputs. FORGE DAS and well data. OpenEI GRED project pages.

## What this means for our model design

- **Treat DOE drilling awards as a live label feed and a competitor signal.** Each of the 16 exploration-drilling sites will publish temperature logs to GDR over 2027-2029. Add a "pending label" class for these cells. Down-weight cells near them for rights buying (the land is taken). Use them as validation folds when results land.
- **Model release dates explicitly.** Each layer should carry `flown_date` and `public_date` (GeoDAWN: Nov 2022 to Mar 2024; GeoFlight: Mar 2023 to Aug 2025). The VOI-Bank should score "edge decay": how soon a cell's signal becomes common knowledge. Buy before the next big public release.
- **Derive physics features from raw 200 m flight lines, not the gridded product.** Pull the line CSVs. Compute magnetic-low "alteration holes", depth-to-source and edge detections. Use radiometric K/Th ratios as a proxy for alteration. Feed these into the shared latent mask class in SENTINEL-COX.
- **Enter GEMS, or at least copy its benchmark.** Build our fault-from-GeoDAWN model anyway. Score it on the GEMS test set as an external skill check. Then go past faults: rank fault *geometry* (step-over, termination) as FSS, which GEMS does not ask for.
- **Price against GeoVision's fixed 1-3% per year discovery rate.** Our pitch number is "discovery rate with our model minus 1-3% per year". The RNPV per acre should include an option value for systems that public plans assume stay hidden.
- **Target the 17% of known systems with no structural setting.** These are basin-hosted, warm-well systems (Faulds 2026; Steptoe Valley template, GDR 1568). FSS-driven favorability (INGENIOUS, Zanskar) under-ranks them. Give the model a separate basin-hosted play branch.
- **Mine GRED and old DOE programme reports as labelled negatives and partial positives.** Fourteen GRED I/II drill sites have known outcomes. They give "data package before vs outcome" rows, which D04 found nobody has tabulated.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No common schema across 1,426 GDR packages.** An LLM extraction pass can turn every package into well-level rows: location, depth, temperature, flow, date, and data package before drilling. That would be a national exploration-outcome table. "Ask GDR" exists but is search, not structured extraction.
2. **Raw airborne line data is barely modelled.** GeoDAWN's 149,030 line-km and GeoFlight's 94,671 line-km have no published geothermal ML. Self-supervised encoders trained on the line data, plus lidar fault scarps, could make the first learned "upflow signature" layer.
3. **Embargoes hide funded results for 3-5 years** (GLADE to 2031; CSM code to 2029; M31). AI can mine conference papers, slide decks and permit filings to infer outcomes years before the GDR release.
4. **Nobody watches DOE pipeline signals.** An automated watcher over NOFO selections, GDR new IDs, ScienceBase releases and BLM nominations near DOE sites would give a weekly "where the market will look next" map.
5. **Cross-survey fusion.** GeoDAWN, the eastern Great Basin survey and the AEM blocks were flown with different specs. A learned levelling and stitching model gives one seamless Great Basin magnetic cube that no one has published.
