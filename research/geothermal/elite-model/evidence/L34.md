## What the field knows

**1. Outside the US, "ML for geothermal" mostly means GIS overlays, not learned models.** Most country maps are multi-criteria overlays (AHP, fuzzy AHP, weights of evidence). Examples are the Eastern Africa fuzzy-AHP map with 13 "extremely high" zones (L08) and Western Anatolia. An example of the Turkish work is Yalcin and Kilic Gul (2017, *Geothermics* 67), a GIS multi-criteria map of the Akarçay basin, Afyon (unverified details). Learned models are rarer. Where they exist, they almost never use designed negatives.

**2. The most useful non-US ML work sits below the map, in the physics links.** Three strands carry over directly to our observation layer:
- **Resistivity to temperature ("electromagnetic geothermometry").** Spichak and Zakharova trained neural nets to predict well temperatures from MT resistivity at Hengill (Iceland) and Soultz (France). Their book is *Electromagnetic Geothermometry* (Elsevier, 2015). They report errors of a few percent when they extrapolate a few hundred metres below the deepest well (unverified number). Iceland's ÍSOR clay-cap model (Árnason et al., WGC 2000) is the physics behind it: a smectite cap at about 70 to 220 °C and a chlorite-epidote core above about 240 °C (M30).
- **Chemistry to reservoir temperature.** In Mexico, Santoyo, Díaz-González and colleagues used computational and ANN tools to refit Na/K and gas geothermometers on world well-fluid databases. Example: Díaz-González, Santoyo and Reyes-Reyes (2008, *Revista Mexicana de Ciencias Geológicas*) (unverified details). In Turkey, Tut Haklıdır and Haklıdır (2020, *Natural Resources Research* 29) trained deep nets on Western Anatolia hydrochemistry to predict reservoir temperature. They report it beats classic geothermometers on their set (unverified numbers and sample size).
- **Correcting bottom-hole temperatures.** Bassam, Santoyo et al. (2010, *Computers & Geosciences*) used an ANN to estimate static formation temperature from shut-in BHT series in Mexican and other geothermal wells. It matched or beat Horner-type methods (unverified error figures).

**3. Natural-state inversion of convective systems with ML is most advanced in NZ, Iceland and Japan.**
- **Japan.** Ishitsuka et al. (2025, *JGR Machine Learning and Computation*, doi:10.1029/2025JH000683) built a 3D physics-informed neural net (PINN) of the Kakkonda field. It beat purely data-driven ML on temperature and pressure when wells were few. Adding MT improved permeability (repo slices). An earlier Ishitsuka et al. paper (*Natural Resources Research*, about 2021) compared Bayesian and neural-net estimates of deep temperature for supercritical targets (unverified details).
- **New Zealand.** The Auckland group ran ensemble Kalman inversion on Waiwera. It needed about 600 to 800 forward runs, against about 10^4 for MCMC. In a synthetic test, 94.7% of true permeabilities fell inside the posterior 95% band (de Beer et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.09017).
- **Iceland.** A Bayesian calibration of Krafla found rock permeabilities still uncertain over more than two orders of magnitude, even with many wells (Scott et al. 2022).
- **Japan, data-driven.** Kiavarz et al. (2026, ISPRS) report 94 to 97% "accuracy" for random forest and a GAN on 152 wells in Akita and Iwate. They used no negatives, so this is leakage-grade (M14).

**4. Europe's best-known systems are deterministic and probabilistic models, not ML.**
- **ThermoGIS (TNO, Netherlands, https://www.thermogis.nl).** It maps every deep aquifer (Rotliegend, Triassic, Lower Cretaceous and others) from NLOG well and seismic data. It runs Monte Carlo on thickness, porosity, permeability and temperature to give P90, P50 and P10 doublet power and a levelised cost per cell (Vrijlandt et al., EGC 2019; unverified version details). Temperature comes from the Netherlands thermal model of Bonté, Van Wees and Verweij (2012, *Netherlands Journal of Geosciences* 91(4)), calibrated to borehole temperatures (unverified calibration count). This is the closest public analogue to our RNPV-per-acre layer. It makes probabilistic economics per cell, nationwide, and keeps it public and versioned.
- **SCAN (Netherlands).** The state shoots new seismic and drills data wells, then makes the data public (unverified scope). It is a public version of our "prove" step.
- **Germany: GeotIS (LIAG).** Agemar, Schellschmidt and Schulz (2012, *Geothermics* 44) kriged roughly 10,000 corrected temperature values into a national 3D temperature model (unverified count). Germany's deep-geothermal ML is still mostly research-grade.
- **GeoPLASMA-CE (Interreg Central Europe, about 2016 to 2019).** Shallow geothermal only, in pilot cities such as Vienna, Ljubljana and Kraków. It made "traffic-light" maps that put potential, conflicts and permits on one layer (unverified pilot list). We borrow the method, not the resource.
- **Italy.** Coro and Trumpy (2020, *Journal of Cleaner Production* 267:121874, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jclepro.2020.121874) trained presence-only niche models (MaxEnt and others) on global geothermal plant locations to predict where plants could go. The Italian national geothermal database (Trumpy and Manzella, *Geothermics* 2017; unverified) gave well temperatures.

**5. Volcanic arcs share one playbook.** Indonesia, the Philippines, Kenya, Ethiopia and Chile all use MT and TEM for the clay cap, fluid and gas geothermometry, and methylene-blue clay tests while drilling (M26, L08). Their main public data are PDFs: UNU-GTP (now GRÓ GTP) theses, ARGeo reports and WGC papers, holding thousands of well temperature, resistivity and chemistry points.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Where | Reported skill | Our read |
|---|---|---|---|
| ANN resistivity to temperature | Hengill, Soultz | A few % error, extrapolating hundreds of m below wells (unverified) | Best template for an MT/AEM likelihood term |
| Deep net chemistry to reservoir T | Western Anatolia | Better than classic geothermometers (unverified size) | Spring-chemistry likelihood; Turkish grabens are our best analogue |
| ANN static formation T from BHT | Mexico | At least as good as Horner (unverified) | Plug into the AI log-extraction stage |
| 3D PINN natural state + MT | Kakkonda | Beats pure ML when wells are sparse | Later-stage, per-prospect proving |
| EnKI natural state | Waiwera NZ | 94.7% coverage at 95%; 600 to 800 runs | Cheap enough for per-prospect UQ |
| ThermoGIS Monte Carlo | Netherlands | Nationwide P90/P50/P10 MW and cost | Template for the RNPV-per-cell output |
| RF and GAN on wells | Akita/Iwate | 94 to 97% "accuracy", no negatives | Trap; do not copy |
| MaxEnt on plant sites | Global, Italy | Presence-only niche fit | Shows the effort-bias problem our Cox thinning solves |

## What this means for our model design

- **Pretrain the cap-to-temperature likelihood abroad.** Fit P(T at depth | resistivity profile, cap base depth, setting) on Iceland, NZ, Japan and Kenya well-plus-MT pairs (the Spichak template). Then fine-tune on the few Great Basin pairs with a "setting" offset for fault-hosted systems.
- **Use Turkey as the out-of-domain test set.** The Western Anatolian grabens are amagmatic, fault-hosted, extensional systems like the Great Basin. A model trained in Nevada that ranks Turkish known fields well (with designed negatives) earns a real "transfers" claim. Zanskar cannot easily make that claim.
- **Replace hand-written geothermometers with a learned, calibrated one.** Train a chemistry-to-reservoir-temperature model with uncertainty on the pooled Mexican, Turkish and world fluid sets. It enters THERMOS-B as a per-sample likelihood with a mixing and re-equilibration error term.
- **Add an ANN static-formation-temperature correction to Stage 0 extraction.** When AI pulls BHT series out of scanned logs, convert them to formation temperature with a Bassam-style model trained on wells with full shut-in histories. The residual becomes the censoring noise.
- **Copy ThermoGIS's output contract.** Every cell gets public-style P90, P50 and P10 MW and a levelised cost, with versioning. That is our RNPV-per-acre layer, with a risk-of-existence factor and land rights on top. Buyers in Europe already trust this format.
- **Treat presence-only niche models (MaxEnt, Japan RF) as a baseline to beat, not a method.** Our backtest table should show their capture-per-area next to SENTINEL-COX. The gap shows the value of effort thinning and designed negatives.
- **Use EnKI or PINN only after a lease is locked.** These methods are now cheap (about 10^3 runs). They belong in the "prove" step, to turn a cheap lease plus a slim hole into a sellable resource estimate.

## Gaps we could exploit with AI

1. **No cross-country benchmark exists.** Nobody has scored one model across Nevada, Western Anatolia, the Rhine Graben and the East African rift with negatives. We could publish one, and it would become the reference.
2. **Multilingual archives are untouched.** UNU-GTP theses, Japanese NEDO reports, Indonesian Badan Geologi and Pertamina reports, Turkish MTA reports, and Spanish-language CFE and Chilean reports hold labelled temperature, chemistry and resistivity profiles. A document-AI pass could yield tens of thousands of points (unverified count). That makes the largest geothermal training set anywhere.
3. **No public, learned resistivity-to-temperature model exists.** The Spichak work is old and site-specific. A pooled, uncertainty-aware model would be a reusable asset, and it could also be licensed.
4. **Shallow-geothermal portals (GeoPLASMA, ThermoGIS) ignore ownership.** None of them joins resource probability to who owns the heat. Our rights layer is the missing join, and the same idea works for direct-use heat in Europe later.
5. **Volcanic-arc failure data are not used as negatives.** Dry and non-commercial wells in Kenya, Indonesia and the Philippines appear in theses and WGC papers. Extracted, they give the designed negatives every published model lacks.
