## What the field knows

Mineral prospectivity mapping (MPM) is forty years older than geothermal favourability ML. It has already hit, and partly solved, the problems we face: few positives, no true negatives, biased labels, and maps that must justify money.

1. **Knowledge-driven vs data-driven.** Weights of evidence (WofE; Bonham-Carter, Agterberg and Wright 1989) turned binary evidence maps into Bayesian log-odds weights. It assumes evidence layers are independent given the deposit, and that assumption is almost always broken, so posterior probabilities come out too high. Fuzzy logic and index overlay (Porwal, Carranza, Knox-Robinson, early 2000s) encode expert rules where labels are too few. Nevada Play Fairway (L01) is a WofE-plus-expert-weights system in all but name.
2. **The mineral systems approach** (Wyborn 1994; McCuaig, Beresford and Hronsky 2010) splits a deposit into source, pathway, trap and preservation. Each becomes a separate set of proxy layers. This is the same split as heat source, permeability and cap in geothermal play fairway.
3. **Tree ensembles became the workhorse.** Random forest (Rodriguez-Galiano et al. 2015; Carranza and Laborte 2015) and gradient boosting beat WofE and logistic regression in most head-to-heads, mainly because they handle interactions and missing layers. The largest public example is the USGS-GSC-Geoscience Australia continental model for sediment-hosted Zn-Pb (Lawley et al. 2022, https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70226879), with grids released on ScienceBase (https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/619552fbd34eb622f6906b26).
4. **Deep learning helps less than hoped on small labels.** CNNs on image patches (reviewed by Zuo, Carranza and others; "Deep Learning in MPM: from algorithmic advances to exploration", https://civilica.com/doc/2648543/) need heavy augmentation. Gains over RF are often within noise when validation is spatially blocked (unverified as a field-wide claim, but repeated across reviews).
5. **Graph neural networks** treat cells or geological units as nodes, and faults or adjacency as edges. Oxford reports "improved MPM using GNNs" (https://www.earth.ox.ac.uk/node/4208591; https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cd710a18-9b40-4231-b193-4e6366dcc156). The gain comes from passing information along structures, not from the network itself (unverified detail).
6. **Positive-unlabeled (PU) learning is now standard.** Xiong and Zuo (2021, Computers and Geosciences; https://scholarhub.vn/publication/:slug/ea16a28c-4a2f-42e6-9be0-bd5bc503e9d1) introduced bagged PU for MPM. EarthByte (Sydney) used PU with plate-kinematic features through time to map copper prospectivity in the American Cordillera (https://www.earthbyte.org/spatio-temporal-copper-prospectivity-in-the-american-cordillera-predicted-by-positive-unlabeled-machine-learning/). The field learned that random "negatives" inflate AUC badly.
7. **Foundation models arrived in 2024.** SRI's GFM4MPM (Daruna et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12756) pretrains a vision transformer with masked-image modelling on unlabelled geoscience layers across the US and Canada, then fine-tunes on a handful of MVT deposits. A 2025 SRI follow-up adds explainability (https://www.sri.com/publication/information-computer-science-pubs/enabling-scalable-mineral-exploration-self-supervision-and-explainability/).
8. **AI on legacy maps at national scale.** DARPA CriticalMAAS with USGS built georeferencing, legend and polygon extraction from scanned geological maps, then ran AI-assisted mineral assessments (https://www.darpa.mil/news/2025/usgs-ai-mineral-assessment; lessons learned: https://www.usgs.gov/publications/extracting-data-maps-lessons-learned-artificial-intelligence-critical-mineral).
9. **The USGS three-part assessment** (descriptive model, grade-tonnage model, number of undiscovered deposits) is still how the government counts what is left. It is the mining twin of our Cox count with size marks.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Reported result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| WofE | Captures most known deposits in 10-20 % of area in many regional studies (unverified typical) | Overconfident when layers are dependent |
| Gradient boosting, Lawley 2022 (Zn-Pb, three countries) | Roughly 9 in 10 known CD and MVT deposits fall in under a tenth of the land area (unverified exact) | Continental H3 grid; uses spatial blocking |
| Bagged PU (Xiong and Zuo 2021) | Higher AUC and tighter high-prospectivity area than RF with random negatives (unverified numbers) | Fix for missing negatives |
| PU through time (EarthByte Cordillera Cu) | Recovered most porphyry deposits in held-out time windows (unverified numbers) | Uses age-dated labels |
| GNN (Oxford) | Beats RF and CNN on the same data (unverified margin) | Edges along faults matter |
| GFM4MPM (SRI 2024) | Pretraining lifts AUPRC over a supervised baseline when labels are few (unverified margin) | Label scarcity is the regime where pretraining pays |
| Mineral-X / KoBold Bayesian drill siting | Mingomba (Zambia) tier-1 copper find; no hit-rate number published (see L14) | Value came from owning the licence |

**Companies.**
- **KoBold Metals** (Bill Gates, Andreessen backing; valued about $3B, see M32). Ingests every legacy record into one store (TerraShed, unverified name), uses Bayesian models to place each hole, and builds its own sensors. Its crown jewel, Mingomba, was a known but unresolved copper field. KoBold bought the right first, then used ML to drill efficiently (https://skillings.net/the-frontier-blueprint-how-kobold-metals-uses-ai-to-locate-deep-copper-in-zambia/). Lesson: the model's money is made on rights it already controls.
- **Earth AI** (Australia). Owns cheap rigs, drills its own AI targets, and sells or joint-ventures them. It reports a molybdenum discovery in New South Wales (https://miningmagazine.com/exploration/news/1461300/new-molybdenum-deposit-discovered-using-ai) and a new gold system at Willow Glen (Jan 2025, https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/02012025/earth-ai-discovers-new-gold-system-at-willow-glen/), and raised $20M (https://www.mining.com/?p=1170737). It claims about a 75 % hit rate (M32). This is the closest shape to our business.
- **VerAI Discoveries** (Boston/Israel). Asset-generator model: AI targets under cover, stakes claims, then brings in majors by earn-in while keeping equity or royalty (https://yespress.io/verai-discoveries; interview https://www.gbreports.com/interview/yair-frastai) (deal terms unverified). This is exactly "find, lock cheaply, farm out".

## Datasets used

- USGS MRDS and USMIN deposit points; Geoscience Australia OZMIN; GSC CMMI (labels).
- National magnetics and gravity grids, EMAG2, Earth MRI airborne surveys; continental geology (SGMC for the US); MT from EarthScope/USArray; LAB and Moho depth models; GNSS strain.
- Lawley 2022 prospectivity GeoTIFFs (ScienceBase 619552fbd34eb622f6906b26; data.gov.au 436359bf).
- DARPA/USGS AI critical mineral assessment competition data v2.0, July 2025 (https://www.usgs.gov/data/training-and-validation-data-ai-critical-mineral-assessment-competition-ver-20-july-2025) and the map georeferencing challenge set (https://sciencebase.gov/catalog/item/64c14e4fd34e70357a32990f).
- Plate reconstructions (GPlates/EarthByte) for time-aware features.

Almost all of these are shared inputs for geothermal (gravity, magnetics, MT, strain, geology). The MPM community has already cleaned and gridded them at continental scale.

## What this means for our model design

- **Adopt the mineral systems split as model structure, not as features.** Model heat, permeability and seal as three latent fields with their own evidence, then combine. This is WofE logic with dependence handled, and it matches play fairway priors (L09).
- **Use PU with a labelling model, not random negatives.** MPM shows random negatives inflate AUC. SENTINEL-COX's effort-thinned Cox count is a stronger form of the same fix; keep it, and report bagged-PU and Lawley-style gradient boosting as baselines.
- **Score by area capture, not AUC.** Report "share of known systems in the top x % of land" and, better, risked dollars per acre leased. This matches how Lawley and the USGS judge maps and how we buy rights.
- **Add a GNN pass along faults.** Make Quaternary faults and step-overs edges between cells. It is cheap and is where MPM saw real gain. It also suits Faulds-style structural settings.
- **Pretrain on unlabelled layers (GFM4MPM recipe).** Masked-image pretraining on the whole West's geophysics stack, then fine-tune on ~280 positives. Label scarcity is the exact regime where SRI saw lift.
- **Reuse CriticalMAAS tools for legacy maps.** Their georeferencing and legend extractors can read old USGS hot spring maps, state geothermal maps and GRC figures into GeoFM Stage 0.
- **Copy the three-part assessment format for outputs.** Number of undiscovered systems, size distribution, and permissive tract, so USGS and buyers read our numbers in a familiar frame.
- **Business: copy KoBold and VerAI on rights, Earth AI on labels.** Lock ground before the model is proven, and own cheap measurement hardware so each hole is a new label.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No MPM model uses dated search effort.** Deposits are labelled as if found by chance. Our discovery-thinned Cox process, with dated drilling and survey history, is new in both fields. Publish it; it is a credible paper.
2. **No decision-coupled maps with rights cost.** Mineral-X couples drilling, not lease purchase. Joining prospectivity to lease price and EVSI per dollar is open ground.
3. **No geothermal foundation model.** GFM4MPM exists for minerals only. A West-wide pretrained encoder over heat flow, MT, gravity, InSAR and Landsat thermal would be first.
4. **Time-aware labels.** EarthByte used geological time. We can use calendar time: train on systems known by 1990, test on those found after. AI can date each discovery from scanned reports.
5. **Agent-based geologist ensembles.** Fuzzy-logic MPM encodes one expert. LLM agents, each holding a different conceptual model (L09), could produce many expert maps whose spread is honest epistemic uncertainty.
6. **Cross-commodity transfer.** Epithermal gold, lithium brines and geothermal share fluid pathways. A model pretrained on mineral deposit labels (thousands of points) may transfer to hydrothermal upflow (unverified hypothesis, cheap to test).
