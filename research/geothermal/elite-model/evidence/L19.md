# L19: Label scarcity, leakage and honest validation in geospatial ML

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: how the geospatial ML field handles few labels, biased labels, leaky splits, extrapolation and survivorship, and what that means for SENTINEL-COX (`../../model/MODEL.md`). This builds on `../../model/evidence/M14-ml-favorability.md` (F1 ceiling, Ploton, Roberts, SCAR break) and `M19-labels-and-negatives.md` (hard negatives from CalGEM and NDOM). It does not repeat them. New here: the post-2021 validation methods (NNDM, kNNDM, DI plus LPD, design-based checks), PU learning when labels are "selected not at random", a leakage taxonomy, and a hindcast protocol that fixes feature-vintage leakage, which no prior file named. ## What the field knows

**1. Random splits flatter spatial models, often by a lot.** Ploton et al. (2020, Nature Communications 11:4540, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7486894) mapped African forest biomass. Random CV looked good. Spatial CV showed little skill away from plots (about R² 0.5 vs near 0; unverified). Roberts et al. (2017, Ecography 40:913) set the rule: blocks larger than the autocorrelation range. Hijmans (2012, Ecology) showed a distance-only null model can score high AUC (unverified size).

**2. But blocks can be too harsh, and the fix is distance matching.** Wadoux et al. (2021, Ecological Modelling 457:109692) showed spatial block CV is pessimistic when the prediction area is not far from data. Only a probability sample is design-unbiased. Milà et al. (2022, MEE 13:1304) built NNDM: leave-one-out that removes neighbours until the test-to-train nearest-neighbour distance (NND) distribution matches the prediction-to-train NND distribution. Linnenbrink et al. (2023/24, kNNDM, GMD 17:5897, https://gmd.copernicus.org/articles/17/5897/2024/gmd-17-5897-2024.pdf) made it k-fold by picking the fold grouping that minimises the Wasserstein distance between the two NND curves. The key idea: **the CV split should mimic the actual prediction task.** For us the task is "predict where nobody has looked", so the NND target is the distance from unexplored cells to the nearest label.

**3. Area of applicability (AOA).** Meyer and Pebesma (2021, MEE 12:1620, https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.07939) define the dissimilarity index DI: distance in importance-weighted, scaled feature space from a cell to the nearest training point, divided by the mean training distance. Cells with DI above the largest DI seen inside CV folds are "outside the AOA". CAST now adds local point density (LPD) and "errorProfiles", a shape-constrained fit of CV error vs DI that maps expected error per pixel (CAST paper, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06978).

**4. Feature selection must use the spatial split.** CAST's forward feature selection with spatial CV drops variables that let the model memorise place. In the CAST demo the same model scored R² 0.71 under random CV and 0.47 under kNNDM. A 5-variable model beat the 11-variable one under kNNDM (0.52 vs 0.47) while looking equal under random CV (0.71 vs 0.71). This is the same "more layers made it worse" story as the USGS "Cursed?" paper (M14 F1).

**5. PU learning assumes what geothermal violates.** Elkan and Noto (2008, KDD) and Bekker and Davis (2020, Machine Learning 109:719, survey https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1811.04820) correct scores by a constant label frequency c under SCAR (labels picked completely at random). Bekker, Robberechts and Davis (2019, ECML) relax it to SAR: c depends on observed propensity attributes e(x). Kumar and Lambert (2023, PULSNAR, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.08269) estimate the class prior when labels are "selected not at random", by clustering positives into subtypes and estimating c per subtype. Geothermal is SNAR: a system is labelled because its water leaked to the surface, which is a property of the system itself. PULSNAR's "subtypes with different c" maps directly onto our leaky vs blind mask classes.

**6. PU is now used in prospectivity with spatial CV.** Alfonso, Müller, Mather and Anthony (2024, GSA Bulletin, doi 10.1130/B37614.1, https://www.earthbyte.org/?p=10321) ran spatio-temporal PU for porphyry copper in the Cordillera. They trained on North America, scored well there, and did poorly in South America: a transfer failure that an AOA check would have flagged. Bilal et al. (2026, Remote Sensing, doi 10.3390/rs18132091, via L18) split geology from effort proxies in a PU model and gained 23% PR-AUC under block CV.

**7. Leakage is the norm, not the exception.** Kapoor and Narayanan (2023, Patterns, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10499856) found leakage in 294 papers across 17 fields. Their taxonomy has eight types. Three hit us: non-independence between train and test (spatial autocorrelation), features that are proxies of the label (hot springs, sinter, "known geothermal resource area" boundaries), and temporal leakage (using data from after the prediction date).

**8. Survivorship bias has a finance-grade fix.** Finance learned that back-tests on today's list of companies overstate returns, because failures dropped out. The fix is a point-in-time database (CRSP style) and walk-forward tests with purge and embargo gaps (López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, ch. 7). Geoscience has nothing like it. Our labels have three survivorship filters: (a) only leaky systems got noticed; (b) only developed systems got measured well; (c) only some company reports survived into public archives. Failed TG holes are under-reported compared with successes (M19).

## What this means for our model design

- **Use kNNDM, not fixed blocks, as the default CV.** Set the target NND curve from unexplored Great Basin cells to the nearest label. This is the honest test of "find what nobody has looked at". Report fixed 25 km blocks and random CV beside it, so the inflation is visible.
- **Build a point-in-time feature store.** Every feature row carries a vintage (survey date). A hindcast frozen at year Y may use only data acquired before Y. This kills feature-vintage leakage: gravity, MT, heat-flow wells and 2 m surveys were often collected *because* a system was found, so a 2026 feature stack "knows" the answers. MODEL.md freezes labels by first-signal date but does not yet freeze features. Add that.
- **Purge and embargo in space and time.** In each hindcast, drop training labels within a buffer (about the variogram range, 10-20 km) of every test discovery, and drop labels found within 2 years after the freeze from both sides.
- **Treat labels as SNAR with subtypes.** Estimate label frequency c per mask class (leaky, partly masked, blind), PULSNAR-style, and tie c to the P_det term already in the Cox model. Report the implied class prior and compare with USGS 2008 (about 3x undiscovered vs identified).
- **Ship DI, LPD and an error profile per cell.** Use DI in the VOI bank as a direct bonus. A cell outside the AOA with high prior heat is exactly where a cheap measurement has the highest value.
- **Run adversarial validation each release.** Train a classifier to tell training cells from target cells. If it wins easily, weight training rows by the density ratio (weighted conformal; Tibshirani et al. 2019) before calibrating intervals.
- **Ban proxy features in blind-system runs.** No springs, sinter, KGRA lines, lease-sale history or drilling-permit density as predictors. Effort layers enter only the thinning term, never the intensity.
- **Score on decisions, not AUC.** Primary metric: blind systems captured per locked acre and per dollar in the time-split replay, with bootstrap CIs. AUC and F1 are secondary and always shown with their ceiling.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **A discovery chronology.** No public table gives each of the 403 KGS a first-signal date, first-well date and discovery method. An LLM agent reading GRC papers, BLM lease files, OSTI reports and NBMG bulletins can build it in days. This is the backbone of every honest hindcast and nobody has it.
2. **Feature vintages at scale.** An agent can tag each survey and well with its acquisition date from metadata and report text. That makes point-in-time freezes possible for 1980, 1990, 2005 and 2015.
3. **Calibrated validation bias from synthetic worlds.** Simulate many Great Basins with known hidden systems, apply the real discovery process (leakage plus roads plus wells), and measure how much each CV scheme over- or under-states true skill. Then correct real CV scores by that bias.
4. **Recovering the missing failures.** Survivorship in archives is fixable: LLM search of BLM case files, state permit logs and bankruptcy filings for dry holes and abandoned prospects adds negatives that were never published.
5. **Geothermal leakage audit.** Re-run the USGS benchmark under random, block, kNNDM and time-split CV and publish the gap.
