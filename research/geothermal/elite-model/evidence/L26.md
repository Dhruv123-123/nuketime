## What the field knows

1. **Classical geothermometers are equilibrium physics with known failure modes.** Silica (Fournier 1977, Geothermics 5:41) and cation ratios (Na-K-Ca, Fournier and Truesdell 1973, GCA 37:1255; K-Mg and the Na-K-Mg ternary, Giggenbach 1988, GCA 52:2749) give reservoir temperature if the water re-equilibrated at depth and did not mix or cool on the way up. The USGS assessment prefers K-Mg and Giggenbach's smoothed chalcedony-quartz curve, and warns that Basin and Range waters are Na-HCO3, not the Na-Cl waters the curves were built on (Williams, Reed and Mariner 2008, USGS OFR 2008-1296). Equilibration takes about 100 years for Na-K-Ca at 150 C and tens of years for quartz at 100 C (Barton 1984, cited in OFR 2008-1296).
2. **Mixing is the norm, not the exception.** Fournier's silica-enthalpy and chloride-enthalpy mixing models back out a hot end-member from a diluted, cooled water. This is the key move for blind systems: a 30 C irrigation well can carry a 150 C parent. In Gabbs Valley, NV, 32 C wells gave chalcedony temperatures above 110 C (M03, via M07).
3. **Multicomponent geothermometry is the physics upgrade.** GeoT (Spycher et al. 2014, Geothermics 51:113) and RTEst (Palmer, Ohly, Smith and Neupane 2014, INL) compute saturation indices for many minerals and solve for the temperature where they converge. They also fit dilution fraction and CO2 loss as free parameters. INL applied RTEst across Idaho and Great Basin springs (unverified case list).
4. **Helium isotopes see deep permeability through a cold cap.** Air is 1 Ra. Crust is about 0.01 to 0.05 Ra. MORB mantle is about 8 Ra. Kennedy and van Soest (2007, Science 318:1433) found a regional east-to-west rise in minimum R/Ra across the northern Basin and Range, with local spikes of 0.8 to 2.1 Ra over background. Blind McGinness Hills fluids read 0.35 to 0.57 Ra; Dixie Valley 0.70 to 0.76 Ra. Banerjee et al. (2011, Geology) model the spikes as deep meteoric circulation on faults of 1e-12 to 1e-15 m2. So R/Ra labels permeability, not heat.
5. **Soil CO2 flux works over leaky systems and weakly over capped ones.** The standard method is accumulation chamber plus a graphical statistical split of background and anomaly (Chiodini et al. 1998, Applied Geochemistry; sequential Gaussian simulation for totals, Cardellini et al. 2003). Lewicki and Oldenburg (2004, Geothermics, unverified volume) modelled how a hidden system leaks CO2 and concluded detection needs dense grids, delta13C and seasonal repeats. At Los Humeros, 2,823 points gave a background cut of 20.7 g/m2/day and 90% of points in background (Jentsch et al. 2020, arXiv 2002.11515).
6. **Regional water chemistry already predicts.** Coolbaugh et al. (2002, Nevada GIS) interpolated NWIS boron. Weights-of-evidence contrast was 2.03 for all systems, 3.54 for high-temperature and 4.09 for power-producing ones. B and Li correlate at r = 0.78.

The learned-geothermometer literature reports good fits, but almost all of it trains on high-enthalpy, mostly Na-Cl fields in Turkey, Mexico and Kenya, with tens to low hundreds of samples and random splits. None tests on diluted Na-HCO3 groundwater or on blind systems. Treat its error numbers as optimistic (see L19 on leakage).

## Datasets used

- **NBMG Great Basin groundwater geochemistry** (Zehner, Coolbaugh and Shevenell 2006): 24,769 samples, 14 analytes, NV, CA, UT, ID, AZ, OR, WY.
- **GeoThermalCloud Great Basin set**: 14,258 points, 18 attributes, very sparse (TDS 1,740; delta18O 1,471).
- **USGS Water Quality Portal / NWIS**: national, post-2005 data not in NBMG.
- **NURE HSSR water** (1975 to 1980): single-lab Si, Na, K, Mg, Li, B, Cl at thousands of western wells and springs. Unused for geothermal.
- **Gregoire, Peek and Hurwitz 2024** (doi 10.5066/P96Q13U3): 1,867 water and 313 gas samples with air-corrected 3He/4He.
- **USGS National Produced Waters Geochemical Database** (about 165k samples, unverified) for sedimentary plays.
- **GDR**: Utah FORGE soil He R/Ra (1024), soil CO2 (1025), fluids (1629); Play Fairway geochem (671, 673, 1090); critical materials in fluids (1126, 1266); Hawaii noble gases (1373, 1410).
- **California GAMA** groundwater tritium-helium data: likely thousands of well He analyses (unverified count).
- **1970s to 1980s USGS soil Hg and He open-file reports** (e.g., OFR 80-612 Long Valley): scanned, not digitised.

## What this means for our model design

- **Do not ingest geothermometer temperatures as features. Ingest raw chemistry as a likelihood.** For each sample, a forward model maps (reservoir T, hot fraction f, cold end-member, CO2 loss, re-equilibration flag) to predicted solutes. GeoT-style saturation physics is the forward model. The output is a posterior on T and f, not a point value. This plugs straight into SENTINEL-COX's per-source censored likelihood.
- **Treat a cold, dilute sample as a censored observation, not a negative.** Low silica in a well with B, Li and Cl excess means "upflow exists but is diluted below detection of the cation clocks". Only conservative tracers (B, Li, Cl, 3He) constrain f there.
- **Model helium as a separate permeability channel.** R/Ra, He concentration, Ne and tritium together give a mantle fraction and a fault-openness score. Fuse it with temperature only in the latent "hot AND open" state. Never let high R/Ra alone raise heat.
- **Back-project every chemistry anomaly along the groundwater flow path.** Outflow plumes move kilometres to tens of km. Use head gradients from NWIS water levels and the USGS regional flow models to give each anomaly a source-location kernel upstream, not a blob around the well.
- **Harmonise labs as a learned bias term.** Give each source and era (NURE LLL, NURE SRL, 1970s USGS, modern ICP-MS) its own offset and noise on silica and trace elements. NURE silica is likely biased low from unacidified storage. Fit the offsets on paired nearby samples.
- **Use chemistry to choose which wells to sample next.** VOI over candidate ranch wells picks where a $40 ICP bottle or a $300 to $700 He-isotope run moves acreage value most. A field He/Ne screen first, isotope run second.
- **Learn confounders explicitly.** Playa and evaporite brines (Br/Cl, Cl/B), irrigation return flow (NO3, delta18O evaporation line), mining waters (SO4, As, Hg) and CO2-rich waters each get a mixture component so they stop masquerading as geothermal B/Li.
- **Keep soil gas as a late, local layer.** CO2 flux, Rn/Tn ratio, Hg and H2 rank fault segments at 10 to 200 m after helium and temperature pick the area. They do not belong in the regional prior.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No Bayesian, mixing-aware geothermometer runs at regional scale.** GeoT and RTEst solve one sample at a time with hand tuning. An amortised neural posterior estimator (simulation-based inference) trained on millions of PHREEQC/GeoT forward runs could give T, f and their uncertainty for 50,000 samples in minutes. Nobody has published this (to my knowledge).
2. **No learned geothermometer trained on dilute Na-HCO3 groundwater with blind-system labels.** We can build the training set ourselves from DeAngelo et al.'s 205 Great Basin systems plus well temperatures, and validate by leaving whole systems out.
3. **NURE HSSR has never been used for geothermal.** It is a pre-pumping, single-lab baseline over remote basins. An AI cleaner can parse the 47 old file formats and the microfiche-only quads.
4. **Masked imputation across sparse chemistry.** Most samples have 5 of 18 analytes. A tabular foundation model (masked autoencoder or TabPFN-style) trained across WQP, NBMG, NURE and produced waters can impute missing Mg, Li or B with uncertainty, so a sample with only Na, K and Cl still yields a partial likelihood.
5. **Digitising the gas and isotope archives.** 1970s soil Hg and He surveys, state spring reports, and Unocal or WHOI helium tables sit in scans. LLM extraction (L21) turns them into labelled points, including the negatives that show where people looked and found nothing (search effort for the Cox model).
6. **GAMA and other dating datasets as free helium coverage.** Groundwater-age programs measured 3He, 4He, Ne and tritium for water age, not geothermal. Re-reading them for excess mantle He is pure reuse.
7. **New isotope clocks on solids.** Clumped isotopes (Delta47) in vein calcite and travertine, and Li and Sr isotopes, give past fluid temperature and source from rock chips or museum and core-library samples. No regional screen exists. A cheap lab campaign on existing core and outcrop samples could add a new thermal channel where no water exists (unverified feasibility at scale).
