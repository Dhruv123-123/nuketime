## What the field knows

**Moeck catalogue.** Moeck (2014, *Renewable and Sustainable Energy Reviews* 37:867-882, "Catalog of geothermal play types based on geologic controls", doi 10.1016/j.rser.2014.05.032, citation from memory, unverified) and Moeck & Beardsmore (SGW 2014) split plays by how heat moves.
- **Convection-dominated (CV).** CV-1a magmatic extrusive, CV-1b magmatic intrusive, CV-2a plutonic with recent volcanism, CV-2b plutonic without (radiogenic granite), CV-3 extensional domain. CV-3 is "high thermal gradients provided by an elevated mantle" from thinning, with fault-controlled circulation of meteoric water. OpenEI lists 41 CV-3 fields from 85 °C to 305 °C, including Dixie Valley, Steamboat and Coso. https://openei.org/wiki/CV-3:_Extensional_Domain
- **Conduction-dominated (CD).** CD-1 intracratonic basin, CD-2 orogenic belt / foreland basin, CD-3 crystalline basement (EGS). Heat is stored, not delivered; a reservoir rock and a seal matter more than faults.
- **Play elements** borrowed from petroleum: reservoir, heat charge, seal, timing and fairway. https://en.openei.org/wiki/Definition:Moeck-Beardsmore_Play_Types
- Brophy et al. (2011, GRC) gave a parallel set of "occurrence models" by tectonic setting. Moeck et al. later applied play typing to the German Molasse Basin (CD-2), adding permeability classes.

**Great Basin is almost all CV-3.** Faulds et al. (SGW 2026) state that Great Basin systems are "amagmatic", with exceptions on the margins (Coso, Long Valley). Magmatic settings are 0.3% of the >1,430 favorable structural settings (FSS) and 0.5% of the 403 known systems. So Moeck's top-level label carries almost no information inside Nevada. The useful sub-typing is structural. Faulds' eight schematic models are terminations, intersections, step-overs/relay ramps, accommodation zones, displacement transfer zones (DTZ), pull-aparts, major normal faults and fault bends, plus hot sedimentary aquifers, volcanic centres and dike tips. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Faulds.pdf

**Blind share.** About 40% of known Nevada systems are hidden (Faulds et al. 2021a), and about 75% of Great Basin resources are hidden (Coolbaugh et al. 2006). Faulds et al. (SGW 2017) say the main hiding mechanism is upflow on a fault feeding lateral outflow into basin fill, often with no spring at all. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2017/Faulds.pdf

**Conceptual models in industry practice.** Cumming (SGW 2016) argues every reliable size method rests on analogy, and fewer than about 100 developed fields exist, against the 1,000 to 10,000 a frequentist method would need. He favours Bayesian reasoning with P10/P50/P90 conceptual models and a separate probability of exploration success. He warns that MT low-resistivity area often overstates the reservoir by several times. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2016/Cumming.pdf

**Size by setting.** Wilmarth & Stimac (SGW 2014): 53 fields above 200 °C have power density mean 16.2, median 12.0 MW/km², range 4.1-48.3. A "Main Sequence" of 31 extensional fields rises with temperature. Rifts above 270 °C exceed 20 MW/km² (median 160 MW). Arc volcanoes stay below 15 (median 70 MW). Dixie Valley sits on the main sequence. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2014/Wilmarth.pdf . Young & Terry (GRC 2015, NREL/CP-6A20-66437) built per-play-type PDFs of reservoir properties for Monte Carlo use, but published no parameters in the abstract. https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/using-geothermal-play-types-as-an-analogue-for-estimating-potenti/

## Best methods and their reported skill

- **Setting-type enrichment (calc from Faulds 2026 count shares, training sites ≥120 °C vs all FSS):** DTZ 6.7/1.4 = **4.8x**; major normal fault 1.7/0.6 = 2.8x (small n); pull-apart 5.0/2.3 = **2.2x**; intersection 26.7/26.1 = 1.0x; step-over 34.2/39.6 = 0.86x; termination 14.2/16.7 = 0.85x; accommodation zone 5.8/6.8 = 0.85x. Transtensional types are enriched. Common extensional types are neutral. FSS cover 7.7% of the area; median polygon 13.4 km².
- **Weights of evidence by temperature class** (Coolbaugh 2002, via M11): young NE faults give contrast 2.52 for >160 °C systems, but only about 0.9-1.3 for cooler ones. The prior must depend on the target temperature.
- **Nevada play fairway** (Faulds 2017): 9 layers in 4 groups (regional, intermediate and local permeability, plus heat). 34 benchmark systems ≥130 °C scored 51.4 on a 28-65 scale. Of 5 detailed sites, Gabbs Valley was confirmed blind (124 °C at 152 m). Ranking of FSS remains "generally subjective".
- **Big Blind** (Zanskar 2025): 121 °C at about 823 m, no surface features, no prior wells. It is a new archetype: deep, unvented, found from a heat-flow model. https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/zanskar-announces-discovery-of-big-blind-geothermal-site-in-western-nevada-usa/
- **Granite Mountain** (Adams et al., SGW 2026): a hybrid step-over plus intersection. MT conductor pinches out near a 3.3 °C 2 m anomaly, read as a narrow upflow. A worked example of the conceptual model as a hypothesis to test. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Adams.pdf

No published model treats play type as a latent variable with a likelihood. Play typing today is a label a geologist assigns by hand.

|---|---|---|---|

## Datasets used

Faulds FSS polygons, typed (INGENIOUS, GDR); USGS 403 known / 120 training sites; Qfaults; OpenEI play-type pages (41 CV-3 fields); Wilmarth & Stimac 53-field power-density table; NBMG geothermal grey literature; GRC and SGW paper archives (conceptual-model figures); Brophy 2011 occurrence models; Young & Terry play-type PDFs.

## What this means for our model design

- **Make archetype a latent mixture, not a feature.** Each cell gets a categorical latent A in {B1-B7, none}. Each archetype carries its own column model (depth to 100 °C, reversal yes/no), its own detection model by search channel, and its own size prior. This replaces the single "mask class" in SENTINEL-COX with a physically named one.
- **Set the archetype prior from setting.** P(A | x) = softmax of tectonic province, FSS type, basin-fill thickness, recharge and aquifer type. Start the FSS coefficients at the enrichment numbers above (DTZ 4.8x, pull-apart 2.2x, others ~1x) and let the data move them.
- **Make the prior temperature-dependent.** Enrichment for >160 °C is about twice that for cooler systems. Fit separate intensity offsets for 120-160 °C and >160 °C.
- **Size marks by play.** Use power density from Wilmarth & Stimac for >200 °C extensional fields, and the Great Basin size fit in M33 for 120-200 °C. Arc settings get a lower ceiling. This stops arcs and Cascades cells from being over-ranked (L08).
- **Correct label bias by archetype.** Historic labels are mostly B1 and B2 (shallow, found by accidental holes, M18). B3 has one public example. Give B3 a weak but non-zero prior and let effort thinning explain why it is rare in labels.
- **Encode conceptual models as small simulations.** Each archetype becomes a parametric 2D section (fault, upflow, outflow layer, cap) in the GHOST Tier 1 simulator. Sample many per cell; keep those that match the data. This is Cumming's P10/P50/P90 conceptual model, done by machine.
- **Use archetype to pick sensors.** B1 says sample water wells down-gradient. B3 says MT or deep TG holes. B4 says log full wells past the lid. EVSI per sensor should be computed per archetype and then weighted.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No machine-readable archetype library.** Hundreds of conceptual-model figures sit in GRC, SGW, WGC and NBMG PDFs. An LLM plus vision model could turn each into a structured card: setting, upflow location, outflow length, cap depth, reservoir T, size, first signal. About 300-600 cards for the West looks reachable (unverified).
2. **Play typing is manual.** Faulds calls FSS ranking subjective. A classifier trained on the 1,430 typed FSS could type faults outside the Great Basin.
3. **No likelihood for a conceptual model.** Nobody scores competing conceptual models against data. Simulation-based inference over archetype templates would do so.
4. **Blind archetypes are defined by how they were found, not by physics.** Our B1-B7 table ties physics to the signal each one leaves. That makes it testable.
5. **Conduction plays are ignored in Great Basin ML.** CD-type sedimentary aquifers are a separate rights market (state trust land, oil leases) that favourability maps score low.
