# L29: Fusing irregular, patchy geodata (GNNs, set models, neural fields, missing modalities)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: how to fuse many data types that sit on different supports (points, lines, grids, wells, scanned logs) when most cells are missing most of them. Builds on `L10` (temperature at depth), `L14`/`L16` (foundation models, AEF, TerraMind) and `../../model/MODEL.md` (SENTINEL-COX). Note: the shared web-search budget was used up before this slice ran. Numbers come from our own evidence files (checked by earlier slices) or from papers I know well. Anything I could not re-check today is marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Our data are sets, not images.** A cell has 0-40 BHTs, maybe one MT station, a fault 3 km away, a spring 8 km away, and a gridded gravity value. Grid CNNs force all of this onto one raster and invent values. Three model families take irregular inputs natively:
   - **Set models** (Deep Sets, Zaheer et al. 2017, https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06114; Set Transformer, Lee et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1810.00825; Perceiver IO, Jaegle et al. 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.14795). They read any number of (location, type, value) tokens and do not care about order.
   - **Neural processes** (CNP/NP, Garnelo et al. 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1807.01622; ConvCNP, Gordon et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.13556; Transformer NP, Nguyen and Grover 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2207.04179). These are "learned kriging". They take a context set and output a predictive distribution at any query point. They are trained by hiding part of the data, so they handle missing inputs by design.
   - **Graph neural networks.** Nodes are wells, cells or fault segments. Edges are distance, adjacency or shared structure. Kriging Convolutional Networks (Appleby et al. 2020, AAAI, https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.09784, unverified) and Positional-Encoder GNNs (Klemmer et al. 2023, AISTATS, https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.10144, unverified) are the spatial-interpolation versions.
2. **Geoscience has already used all three.** Stanford's Thermal Earth Model (InterPIGNN) is a physics-informed GNN over ~400k Harrison-corrected BHTs (Aljubran and Horne 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09961; GDR 1592). LANL's In-Context Earth (arXiv 2605.16665, 2026) is an 830k-parameter transformer that reads the 20 nearest BHTs and returns a 128-bin temperature distribution: it is a set model / neural process in all but name. Oxford used GNNs for mineral prospectivity (Cu, Fe, Sn, southern England) and reported clear gains over tabular ML (https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:cd710a18-9b40-4231-b193-4e6366dcc156).
3. **Implicit neural representations (neural fields) now build 3D geology.** A small MLP maps (x, y, z) to a scalar field (stratigraphic level, temperature, resistivity). Key papers: SIREN (Sitzmann et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09661) and Fourier features (Tancik et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.10739) fix the smoothness bias. Hillier et al. 2021 built 3D structural models with GNNs on tetrahedral meshes (Math. Geosci. 53, unverified). Hillier et al. 2023, GeoINR 1.0 (Geosci. Model Dev. 16, unverified), fits interfaces, orientations and unconformities from sparse contacts. Neural-field full-waveform and resistivity inversion papers (2022-2025, unverified) show the same trick works for geophysics: the field is the parameterisation, a forward operator is the loss.
4. **Missing modality is the default in Earth data, and there are known fixes.** HeMIS (Havaei et al. 2016, MICCAI, https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.05194) embeds each modality alone and fuses by mean and variance across whatever is present. ModDrop (Neverova et al. 2015) randomly drops whole modalities in training. Product-of-experts VAEs (Wu and Goodman 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.05335) combine only the experts you have. Ma et al. 2022 (CVPR, https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05454) showed plain multimodal transformers degrade badly when a modality goes missing at test time unless trained for it. MultiMAE (Bachmann et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.01678) and TerraMind (IBM/ESA 2025, https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11171) train by masking across modalities, so they can generate a missing one.
5. **Weather science has already solved "fuse sparse stations, satellite swaths and grids end to end".** Aardvark Weather (Allen et al., Nature 2025, unverified details) uses SetConv encoders to put raw irregular observations straight into a learned grid. Andersson et al. 2023 (Environmental Data Science, "Environmental sensor placement with convolutional Gaussian neural processes", unverified) used a ConvGNP to choose Antarctic sensor sites by expected information gain. That is our VOI loop.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Task | Reported skill | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| LANL In-Context Earth (set transformer, 20 nearest BHTs) | US temperature at depth | **MAE 4.7 C**; universal kriging 5.1; Transparent Earth 5.8; AEF embeddings 6.0; Stanford 6.4 (same split) | Calibrated, KS 2.5% (from L10) |
| Stanford InterPIGNN (physics-informed GNN) | CONUS 0-7 km T | **MAE 4.8 C** on 10 km spatial blocks; 6.4 C on ICE split | MC-dropout UQ only; conductive by design |
| Oxford GNN prospectivity | Cu/Fe/Sn MPM | "Significantly improves" three metrics over RF/CNN (margin unverified) | Gain from neighbour message passing |
| ConvCNP vs GP | Climate downscaling, sensor fields | Matches or beats GP log-likelihood at a fraction of test cost (unverified numbers) | Amortised, translation-equivariant |
| HeMIS / modality dropout | Brain MRI segmentation with 1-4 of 4 modalities | Degrades gracefully; plain fusion collapses when modalities drop (unverified figures) | Mean+variance pooling is the key |
| GeoINR | 3D structural models from sparse contacts | Honours contacts and orientations, handles unconformities that RBF interpolants smear (qualitative) | Interface fields, not physics |
| Geothermal favorability (any algorithm) | 278 positives vs ~710k cells | Changing algorithm (LR, XGBoost, BNN, NMFk, GNN) moves AUC little | Data-limited ceiling (L03/M14) |

The honest message: on temperature, set and graph models now beat kriging by roughly 0.4-1.5 C MAE. On favorability, no fusion architecture has beaten the label ceiling yet. Architecture wins come from (a) using raw irregular data instead of rasterised layers and (b) handling missing data without imputation, not from depth.

## Datasets used

- SMU/NGDS BHT compilations, ~400k Harrison-corrected BHTs (Stanford GDR 1592).
- LANL In-Context Earth training set (US BHT and log data, per arXiv 2605.16665).
- INGENIOUS and Nevada Play Fairway layers (M02, M03): gravity, magnetics, MT, Quaternary faults, geochemistry, heat flow.
- EarthScope / USArray MT transportable array (sparse ~70 km grid).
- AlphaEarth Foundations 64-d embeddings, HLS for Prithvi, TerraMesh for TerraMind (L14/L16).
- Faulds structural-setting catalogue (~1,430 favourable settings) for fault graphs (M11).
- Outside geothermal: BraTS (missing-modality MRI), ERA5 + station data (Aardvark), Antarctic temperature (ConvGNP sensor placement).

## What this means for our model design

- **Make every raw reading a token, not a raster pixel.** Each token is (x, y, z, data type, value, error class, source, year). This plugs straight into SENTINEL-COX's per-source likelihoods and keeps the dated search-effort tag on every token.
- **Use a transformer neural process as the field layer for T(z), gradient and resistivity.** Train it on the West by hiding random subsets of tokens (and whole modalities), with In-Context Earth as the starting point and the Stanford map as a conductive prior mean. Output P10/P50/P90 T at our five depth bins, which is exactly the RNPV/acre input.
- **Build a fault graph and pass messages along it.** Nodes are fault tips, step-overs and intersections; edges are segments with slip rate and dip. Cells inherit an embedding from nearby graph nodes. This is where the MPM gain came from and it encodes Faulds-style structural settings directly.
- **Fuse modalities HeMIS-style, and train with modality dropout.** Each data type has its own small encoder. Fuse by attention pooling over whichever are present, plus a learned per-modality "absent" token. Never mean-impute an MT value where there is no MT.
- **Keep "missing" as a separate signal routed to the effort term.** Where data are absent is mostly about who searched where, not about heat. Feed the presence mask into the Cox thinning term and keep it out of the existence term, to stop label leakage.
- **Use the generative head as the VOI engine.** A model that can sample "what would an MT station / gradient hole here read?" gives expected information gain per dollar directly, the ConvGNP sensor-placement recipe. This replaces hand-built EIG for the GHOST-SBI Tier 1 sensor ranking.
- **Add a neural field T(x, y, z) in v2, constrained by physics.** Fit a SIREN-style field per prospect to all local tokens, with a heat-equation residual loss and a convective upflow term. It gives a continuous 3D model at prospect scale without the 3D FNO/TOUGH3 surrogate that MODEL.md cut from v1.
- **Benchmark against the boring baselines on the same spatial blocks.** Universal kriging, In-Context Earth and the LightGBM-on-layers stack (Zanskar-style). Only keep the fusion model where it wins on blocked log-likelihood, not on random-split MAE.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No geothermal model ingests raw multimodal tokens.** Every favorability map (Play Fairway, INGENIOUS, Zanskar as far as public) uses rasterised, interpolated layers. A token-level neural process over BHTs, MT stations, springs chemistry, faults and AI-extracted scanned logs would be the first.
2. **No missing-modality-aware geothermal model.** Nobody trains with modality dropout or reports skill as a function of which data exist. We can publish a "skill by data availability" curve per cell, which also tells us what to buy.
3. **No convective neural field.** Stanford, SMU, USGS and LANL models are conductive by construction. A physics-regularised neural field that is allowed an upflow plume, fitted to local residuals, would see exactly the blind systems they smooth away.
4. **Scanned logs as a modality.** Thousands of TG and driller logs are images. A document model can emit tokens with an extraction-confidence error class, so OCR uncertainty flows through fusion instead of being thresholded.
5. **Amortised VOI over rights.** Nobody links a generative fusion model to a lease-acquisition decision. Our edge is sampling the unseen modality and pricing the acre in one pass.
6. **Cross-commodity pretraining.** Terranox's uranium pipeline already handles irregular drillholes and geophysics. A shared token encoder pretrained on mineral and oil-and-gas well data, then fine-tuned on heat, could lift skill where geothermal labels are scarce (unverified lift).
