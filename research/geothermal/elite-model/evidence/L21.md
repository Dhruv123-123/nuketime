# L21: AI document extraction for geoscience archives

Scope: turning scanned well logs, temperature logs, driller reports and well files into numbers the model can use. Covers raster-log digitising, vision-language models (VLMs) on charts, geoscience LLMs (K2, GeoGalactica, GeoGPT), OCR of forms and handwriting, and how accurate each one is. Builds on Stage 0 of SENTINEL-COX (MODEL.md) and on M04 and M06.

Note: the web-fetch budget ran out early in this run. Numbers marked (verified) were read from a primary page or abstract this run. The rest come from memory of the papers and are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Raster well-log digitising is a solved-in-principle, narrow problem.** The oil industry has had commercial tools for decades (Neuralog, Golden Software Didger, TGS log digitising services). Patents describe semi-automatic curve tracing (US9911210B1, "Raster log digitization system and method"; https://patents.google.com/patent/US9911210). These tools still need a person to set depth and value scales and to pick each curve.
2. **Deep learning now automates the curve step.** Deepkapha AI's "Digitization of Raster Logs: A Deep Learning Approach" (Patwardhan, Maiti et al., arXiv 2022; https://deepai.org/publication/digitization-of-raster-logs-a-deep-learning-approach) and its follow-up **VeerNet** (Journal of Imaging 9(7):136, 2023; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10381386/) segment curves from scanned log images with an encoder-decoder network trained on synthetic logs. A template-matching method from Abu Dhabi University does the same with classical vision (https://repository.adu.ac.ae/items/37f927f2-330e-4770-9668-fd9e45877717/full).
3. **Foundation-model pipelines arrived in 2025.** Halliburton (Rathore, Ailneni, Nair, Freestone, EAGE Digitalization 2025; https://www.earthdoc.org/content/papers/10.3997/2214-4609.202539054) split each log into header and plot. A VLM answers questions about the header (curve names, units, scales). Text-prompted Segment Anything (SAM) masks each curve. Gridlines are removed, depth is normalised, and curves go out as LAS. This is the closest published template for our Stage 0.
4. **General VLMs read charts, but drift on dense ones.** ChartQA-style benchmarks are simple. The 2026 WB-ChartExtract benchmark (Berkane, Wang, Majumder, arXiv 2605.27298) has about 7x more data points per chart than ChartQA (verified). Single-pass VLM tables are noisy. Sampling many outputs and taking the per-cell median gives up to 23% relative error reduction, and the spread gives a usable uncertainty (verified).
5. **Document OCR is near-saturated on clean print, not on old forms.** OmniDocBench (Ouyang et al., CVPR 2025; https://arxiv.org/html/2412.07626v2) and AI2's olmOCR-Bench are the standard tests. LlamaIndex argues OmniDocBench is now saturated by frontier VLMs (https://www.llamaindex.ai/blog/omnidocbench-is-saturated-what-s-next-for-ocr-benchmarks). Handwriting, stamps, faint carbon copies and tables with ruled cells remain the error sources.
6. **Geoscience LLMs help with language, not with pixels.** K2 (Deng et al. 2023; https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05064) further pre-trained LLaMA-7B on about 5.5B tokens of geoscience text and built GeoBench from geoscience exams (unverified token count). GeoGalactica (30B, Galactica base, arXiv 2401.00434) followed (unverified). **GeoGPT** from Zhejiang Lab and the IUGS Deep-time Digital Earth programme opened to the world in 2025, with open weights such as Qwen2.5-72B-GeoGPT and GeoGPT-R1-Preview on Hugging Face (https://www.ddeworld.org/news/detail/297; https://huggingface.co/GeoGPT-Research-Project/GeoGPT-R1-Preview). None of these is benchmarked on reading scanned logs. Frontier general VLMs beat them on extraction (unverified, but no paper claims otherwise).
7. **Map extraction has the best public benchmark in geoscience.** The USGS-DARPA AI for Critical Mineral Assessment Competition (2022-2023) scored map georeferencing and legend-to-polygon/point/line extraction. USGS published lessons learned (https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70271124) and the training data (https://www.sciencebase.gov/catalog/folder/64c14e71d34e70357a329912). Polygon and line extraction scored well below point extraction, and legend reading was a key failure mode (unverified detail).

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Task | Method | Reported skill |
|---|---|---|
| Curve digitising, raster logs | VLM header reading + text-prompted SAM + gridline removal (Halliburton 2025) | Mean NRMSE 0.167 on their test logs (verified); multi-scale multi-curve tracks left as future work |
| Curve digitising, raster logs | VeerNet encoder-decoder, synthetic training | High curve-classification F1 on synthetic data, weaker on real scans (unverified exact numbers) |
| Dense chart to table | VLM self-ensemble, per-cell median | Up to 23% relative error cut vs single pass on WB-ChartExtract (verified) |
| Page OCR, print | Frontier VLMs, olmOCR, MinerU class tools | Near ceiling on OmniDocBench text; tables and formulas still lag (verified that benchmark is called saturated) |
| Handwritten forms | VLM OCR or Transkribus-style HTR | Character error of a few percent on clean hand, much worse on faint copies (unverified) |
| Geologic maps | CriticalMAAS competition entries | Points much better than polygons and lines (unverified numbers) |
| Geoscience QA | K2, GeoGalactica, GeoGPT | Beat same-size general models on GeoBench; no extraction benchmark (unverified) |

Key point: no paper reports accuracy on **temperature-depth logs** or **driller report temperatures**. Every number above is a proxy.

## Datasets used

- Synthetic raster logs generated from LAS files (VeerNet; Patwardhan et al.). Easy to make at any scale.
- Halliburton internal raster log test set (closed).
- ChartQA and WB-ChartExtract (World Bank series) for chart extraction.
- OmniDocBench (about 1,000 annotated pages, CVPR 2025) and olmOCR-Bench (about 1,400 PDFs) (unverified sizes).
- CriticalMAAS map training and validation data on ScienceBase (open).
- GeoBench and GeoSignal (K2); GeoGPT corpora (partly open).
- Our target archives (from M04, M06, MODEL.md): NBMG WellLog scans (2,002 PDFs, 500+ T-logs on 442 TG holes); NDWR driller reports (146,235 online, only 9.8% with keyed temperature); CA DWR OSWCR (1,111,251 reports, no temperature field); CalGEM geothermal wells (4,346); NDOM well files (938 geothermal, about 775 O&G); Utah DOGM files; KGS elog scans with 212k BHT readings (a free ground-truth set); Texas RRC and BEG log images; GDR PFA well and temperature surveys (e.g. Granite Springs Valley, https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/969).

## What this means for our model design

- **Build Stage 0 as a split pipeline.** VLM reads headers, units, scales, well IDs and locations. A curve tracer (SAM masks plus a classical tracer) reads the plotted T(z) line. This copies the only published end-to-end design (Halliburton 2025) and avoids asking a VLM to read pixel positions.
- **Run every extraction as a self-ensemble.** Sample 5-20 reads per page and per curve from two or more models. Take the median. Use the spread as the extraction sigma in the censored column likelihood. This turns "extraction confidence" from a guess into a measured number.
- **Train and score on synthetic temperature logs.** Render thousands of fake T-log scans from known T(z) curves (SMU and GDR digital logs) with old fonts, grids, noise, skew and fax blur. This gives unlimited labelled data and a clean skill number before touching real scans.
- **Use KGS BHT and OFR 99-425 as real-scan scorecards.** KGS has both scanned headers and keyed BHTs. OFR 99-425 has gradients for NBMG holes. Score median gradient error and recall per archive. Keep the MODEL.md pass bar (median gradient error 10% or less, 80% usable).
- **Keep "never keyed" as effort data.** A driller report that mentions no heat is a weak negative at that depth. The extractor must output "temperature absent" as a real value, so the Cox model's search-effort layer gets the negatives too.
- **Location error is the bigger risk than value error.** Township-range-section and quarter-quarter calls give 400-800 m errors. Extract the full legal description and any lat/long, then carry the error box into the likelihood.
- **Do not rely on geoscience LLMs for reading.** Use GeoGPT or K2 class models at most for term normalisation (lithology, "hot water", "steam") and report QA. Use the best frontier VLM for pixels.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No public benchmark for temperature-log digitising.** We can build one (a few hundred hand-checked NBMG and CalGEM logs plus synthetic renders) and own the best-calibrated extractor.
2. **No one has mined driller report free text for heat at state scale.** Nevada has about 132,000 reports with no keyed temperature, California 1.1 million. A VLM pass at about $0.002-0.01 per page (unverified) costs $300-$13,000 for both states.
3. **No extraction-uncertainty-aware geothermal likelihood.** Published pipelines output a curve. None output a calibrated error bar that feeds a Bayesian model. Self-ensembles give us that.
4. **No cross-document linking.** The same hole appears in NBMG, NDOM, SMU and old GRC papers under different names. An agent that links them by location, depth and operator gives duplicates for free validation and fills missing fields.
5. **No extraction of "where people looked".** Old company reports (Phillips, Chevron, AMAX grids) state survey areas and dry results. An agent can turn these into dated effort polygons, which is exactly the thinning layer SENTINEL-COX needs.
6. **Multi-curve multi-scale tracks are open.** Halliburton names it as future work. Many T-logs plot temperature and gradient on one track. A per-curve VLM legend read plus colour/dash separation is a clear target.
