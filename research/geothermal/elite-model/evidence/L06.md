# L06: Next-gen geothermal startups and their subsurface data moats

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Literature slice L06, elite-model track. About 10 web searches plus fetches. It builds on `../../model/evidence/M32-competitors-tech.md` and does not repeat its tables. Anything not checked against a primary source is marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**Short version.** The next-gen startups (Fervo, Quaise, Sage, Eavor, XGS, Mazama, GreenFire) are **access and production** companies. Their edge is drilling, completion and loop hardware. None of them publishes a regional discovery model. They pick sites from **existing deep wells that already prove heat**, plus transmission and permitting. Their data moat is *in-reservoir* data from a handful of sites, not *regional* data. Zanskar is the only funded startup whose core product is discovery.

**Fervo (S-1, 2026).** This is the best primary source on how a well-funded startup picks land ([S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026025821/fervoenergy-sx1.htm)):
- It leases where "existing deep wells ... confirm significantly elevated temperatures at shallow depths". That is true for "the vast majority" of its sites.
- Hard screens: reservoir 300-625 F, vertical depth at most about 13,000 ft. Higher PPA prices allow cooler or deeper targets.
- Well depths are refined from "subsurface thermal gradient, stress conditions, and structural or tectonic setting".
- Resource numbers are heat-in-place (HIIP) from DeGolyer and MacNaughton, with a 30.5% thermal recovery factor, for ten "GeoClusters". Cape Station is put at about 4.3 GW. A wider set of reviewed leases is put at over 40 GW.
- Land: about 595,900 acres at about $4/acre (2019-21). BLM 2025 high bids reached $344/acre (Utah) and $410/acre (Nevada).
- Data claim: "more than 500 terabytes of high-fidelity operational data", "proprietary, data-assimilative models", and "permanent AI-enhanced fiber optics" in wells.
- Drilling: over 25 wells. Drill time down about 75% and cost per foot down about 70% from 2022 to 2025. GeoBlock capex is about $7,000/kW.

So Fervo's ML is aimed at **reservoir operation and drilling**, not discovery. Its Google partnership (2021) also targeted subsurface interpretation and drilling (https://blog.google/outreach-initiatives/sustainability/google-fervo-geothermal-energy-partnership/).

**Fervo well results and their critics.** Cape Station's first production well was reported at 8-10 MW from about 93 kg/s in a 24-hour test (Sep 2024 preprint). GeoExpro (Yearsley and Kombrink, Oct 2025) argued that flow was still falling, and that net power after injection pumping was about 6.2 MW, maybe about 4.8 MW after 7 days ([GeoExpro](https://geoexpro.com/critical-look-at-fervo-cape-station-data-raises-questions-over-sustainability/)). Project Red gave about 3 MW gross (S-1). The lesson for us: *temperature at depth is the screen; deliverability is still the main risk*, even for the leader.

**Quaise.** Project Obsidian sits south of Newberry Volcano, Oregon. It targets 300-500 C at about 3 miles and 50 MW. The site was picked for "volcanic history" and high gradient. No analytics or ML is described ([Quaise](https://www.quaise.com/news/a-first-look-at-project-obsidian)). Quaise raised a $134M Series B first close in Jul 2026 (M32).

**Mazama.** Also at Newberry. Well 1 hit 331 C bottom-hole. Well 2 ("Athena") reached 10,350 ft in 15 days, 80% faster than well 1, toward 15,500 ft and over 400 C. Cost is put at $4,500-4,950/kW. It aims to certify more than 10 GWe recoverable at Newberry ([ThinkGeoEnergy](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/mazama-energy-reports-80-drilling-time-improvement-on-second-geothermal-well/)). Newberry was chosen because decades of public DOE/AltaRock data already proved the heat (unverified detail).

**Eavor.** Geretsried (Germany) feeds about 0.5 MW from one loop, against a Phase 1 plan of about 8.2 MWe from four loops. Costs exceeded the EUR 200-350M range ([CleanTechnica](https://cleantechnica.com/2026/01/15/when-next-generation-geothermal-meets-first-of-a-kind-reality/)). Closed loops are conduction-limited, so output scales with rock temperature and conductivity. NREL found Eavor-Loop economics depend strongly on gradient ([NREL](https://research-hub.nrel.gov/en/publications/techno-economic-performance-of-eavor-loop-20/)).

**XGS.** Closed-loop conductive design in New Mexico, with a Meta PPA for 150 MW (M32). In Sep 2026 DOE selected XGS for a deep vertical appraisal well with Baker Hughes, New Mexico Tech and Sandia. The well will collect downhole temperature, logs, core and continuous drilling data "to refine ... subsurface and thermal models" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260928062817/en/XGS-Energy-Selected-by-U.S.-Department-of-Energy-to-Advance-Next-Generation-Geothermal-Development-in-New-Mexico)). XGS is still in *appraisal*, so it needs exactly the temperature-at-depth data we would sell.

**Sage Geosystems.** Pressure geothermal and storage. It has a site at Ormat's Blue Mountain ([ThinkGeoEnergy](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/sage-to-deploy-egs-approach-at-ormats-blue-mountain-geothermal-site-in-nevada/)) and a Meta deal (unverified). It works hot dry rock, often in Texas oil-patch settings (unverified). No discovery model is published.

**Bedrock Energy.** Shallow geoexchange for buildings. $12M Series A (Jan 2025). It sells "subsurface thermal simulation" and a construction platform ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250122142479/en/Bedrock-Energy-Raises-$12M-Series-A-to-Scale-Geothermal-Heating-and-Cooling)). Its projects are in Utah and Colorado. Each borefield yields shallow conductivity and temperature data, but at 150-300 m and in towns (unverified).

**Zanskar.** $115M Series C (Jan 2026), $180M equity in total. It calls itself a "geothermal development operating system" and claims more anomalies found than anyone in decades. It gives no hit rate or MW numbers in public ([ESG News](https://esgnews.com/zanskar-raises-115-million-to-scale-ai-driven-geothermal-discovery-and-power-plant-development/)). Its methods are covered in M13.

**Others to watch** (all unverified detail): GreenFire (coaxial retrofit, 1.2 MWe at Coso); Fleet Space (ExoSphere ambient-noise seismic, sold to mining and geothermal); Teverra (physics-informed ML services); Gradient Geothermal (oilfield co-production); Seequent (Leapfrog, the default 3D tool). None of them sells a calibrated, rights-linked discovery posterior.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Player | Method | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| Fervo | Horizontal EGS, DAS/DTS fiber, data-assimilative reservoir models | 8-10 MW gross per Cape well over 24 h (about 6.2 MW net per critics); drilling time down 75% |
| Fervo | Land screen: deep-well heat + 300-625 F + under 13,000 ft | 596k acres at $4/acre; HIIP reports on 10 clusters |
| Mazama | Superhot EGS, fast PDC drilling | 331 C well 1; 80% faster well 2 |
| Eavor | Closed loop | 0.5 MW from one loop vs 2 MW per loop plan |
| XGS | Conductive loop; appraisal well | Data well funded; no results yet |
| Zanskar | LightGBM favorability + Bayesian VOI + field sampling | "Several" blind finds; no public skill score |

No startup publishes an out-of-sample discovery skill score (AUC, hit rate on blind holes). Zanskar's GeoCore and the Nevada play-fairway ML papers (M14) are the only public skill numbers.

## Datasets used

- Legacy deep wells (oil, gas, DOE). Fervo's main site screen.
- Utah FORGE open data (wells 58-32, 16A(78)-32, logs, microseismic), next to Cape Station. On the GDR (https://gdr.openei.org).
- Newberry data from DOE/AltaRock EGS work (NWG 55-29), used by Quaise and Mazama (unverified).
- INGENIOUS layers and GeoCore (Zanskar).
- New Mexico Bureau of Geology heat-flow and well data (XGS partner).
- BLM lease sale results (price per acre; Fervo cites $344-410).
- Startup-private: Fervo's 500 TB of fiber and operating data; Zanskar's about 27,000 temperature samples.

## What this means for our model design

- **Add an "EGS/closed-loop buyer" value head.** Score each parcel on Fervo's public screen: temperature 300-625 F above 13,000 ft, plus stress regime and transmission. This prices the land for buyers that do not need natural permeability.
- **Put deep-well confirmation on its own axis.** Fervo leases only where deep wells prove heat. Our model should flag parcels where *no* deep well exists but our posterior says 150+ C at under 4 km. That gap is where buyers have not looked.
- **Price closed-loop value as conduction only.** For XGS and Eavor-type buyers, value depends on T(z) and rock conductivity, not permeability. Our 1D column likelihood already gives T(z). Add a conductivity posterior and an Eavor/XGS output model (NREL Eavor-Loop 2.0 curves).
- **Treat deliverability as the residual risk, not heat.** The Cape and Geretsried shortfalls show heat is well predicted but flow and drawdown are not. Keep flow risk as a separate, wide term in RNPV. Do not let a good thermal posterior inflate value.
- **Use BLM bid prices as a label of what the market already knows.** A parcel at $400/acre is already priced in. Our edge is the gap between our RNPV/acre and the last bid. Add sale prices from 2019-2026 as an "already discovered" effort layer in the Cox thinning.
- **Harvest startup appraisal data the day it goes public.** DOE-funded wells (XGS NM, FORGE, Newberry) must report to the GDR. Watch the GDR and refit priors when a new deep log lands.
- **Turn startup press releases into weak labels.** LLM-parse S-1s, NEPA filings and DOE awards for coordinates, depths and temperatures. Fervo's ten named GeoClusters and their NEPA areas are de facto positive labels.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No public, calibrated discovery skill.** No startup reports out-of-sample hit rates. A replay backtest with honest numbers is a selling point to every buyer.
2. **No one maps "EGS-ready but unproven" land.** Fervo requires an existing deep well. Areas with high predicted gradient but no deep well are unpriced. AI-extracted scanned logs (our Stage 0) can fill them.
3. **No buyer-neutral product.** Each startup's data serves its own drilling. A posterior per parcel, scored for each technology (hydrothermal, EGS, closed-loop, superhot), is open ground.
4. **Superhot mapping is volcano-only.** Quaise and Mazama both crowd onto Newberry. A national superhot map (over 374 C by 6 km) from Curie depth, MT and heat flow would show other targets. We can lock those first.
5. **Shallow geoexchange data is wasted.** Bedrock-style borefield thermal tests are never pooled. Partnering to collect them gives cheap conductivity and gradient labels in the Mountain West.
