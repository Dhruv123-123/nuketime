# L01: Nevada Play Fairway Analysis (Faulds et al. 2015-2021)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: the UNR/NBMG/USGS Nevada play fairway project only (DOE DE-EE0006731). It covers method, weights, structural settings and drilling validation. Programme-wide history, the 2019 lease sale and the BLM reprice are in `../../evidence/L01-play-fairway.md`. The cross-team scorecard is in `../../model/evidence/M03-play-fairway-results.md`. This file adds the parts that matter for building the model: the exact scoring mechanics, the new 2026 structural-setting statistics, and what the holes really measured.

## What the field knows

**The idea.** Most Great Basin heat sits in "favourable structural settings" (FSS) on Quaternary faults. These are places where fault geometry keeps fractures open: step-overs, fault intersections, fault tips (terminations), accommodation zones, pull-aparts and displacement transfer zones. The team estimates that about 39% of known Nevada systems are blind and that about 75% of the total resource is blind (Coolbaugh et al. 2006, quoted in the 2015 peer review deck). Play fairway analysis (PFA) borrows the oil-industry "common risk segment" idea: map heat and permeability evidence, map how confident you are in each, and multiply them.

**Phase 1 (2014-15), regional map.** Nine layers were combined over a 240 x 400 km transect (about 96,000 km²) of Nevada (Faulds et al. 2021 final report, OSTI 1724080):
1. structural setting type (polygons digitised by hand);
2. age of the youngest Quaternary faulting;
3. Quaternary slip rate;
4. GPS second-invariant strain rate;
5. slip and dilation tendency in the modern stress field;
6. earthquake density;
7. horizontal gravity gradient;
8. modelled temperature at 3 km;
9. spring and well temperatures and geothermometry.

Layers 1-3 and 5 form "local permeability". Strain, earthquakes and gravity form "intermediate" and "regional permeability". Layers 8-9 form "heat". The fairway equals permeability combined with heat. Each layer also has an error layer, so the final map has a matching uncertainty map. The GDR 616 release ships all 44 layers, error layers, a logistic-regression regional-permeability layer, and a "Degree of Exploration" layer.

**The weights.** The exact numbers are printed only in Figure 2 of Faulds et al. 2016 and 2017, and we did not extract them (unverified). The text does give the multipliers that sit inside the local-permeability term (2015 peer review deck):
- Fault age: <15 ka x10; <130 ka x5; <750 ka x2; <2.6 Ma x1; >2.6 Ma x0.1.
- Slip rate: 2-5 mm/yr x2; 1-2 x1.75; 0.5-1 x1.5; 0.1-0.5 x1.25; 0.01-0.1 x1.
- Geochemistry quality: charge balance 20%, Na-K-Ca vs quartz agreement 30%, maturity index 20%, measured temperature 20%.

Fault age therefore spans a factor of 100 and slip rate only a factor of 2. That is a strong expert belief that recency matters far more than rate. Weights of evidence and logistic regression were run against **34 benchmark systems hotter than 130 °C**. The expert weights were then set "taking the statistics into account". There was no held-out test.

**Phase 1 output.** The map found about 375 FSS covering about 12% of the area (>11,000 km²). Fairway scores ran from about 28 to 65. The 34 benchmarks averaged 51.4. Scores above about 45 counted as high. The team called the region "target rich".

**Phase 2 (2015-17), five detailed areas.** From 24 candidates the team picked five: SE Gabbs Valley, Granite Springs Valley, Sou Hills, Crescent Valley and Steptoe Valley. Selection mixed geology with **land status, transmission within 20 km, sage-grouse habitat and industry interest**. New data per area were 237-415 gravity stations, 0-124 two-metre temperature stations (292 in total), 0-15 water analyses, 540 km of purchased legacy oil and gas seismic, and lidar at two sites. The FSS layer gained a 0-1 quality factor, with +0.1 where MT showed low resistivity. Every area turned out to hold several smaller FSS: 5 at Gabbs, 6 at Granite Springs, 5 at Sou Hills, 9 at Crescent, 12 at Steptoe.

**Phase 3 (2018-19), drilling.**
- **SE Gabbs Valley** (Craig 2021 thesis; Craig et al. 2021 *Geothermics*). The setting is a displacement transfer zone where the dextral Petrified Springs fault ends in normal faults. Concealed NW faults cross NNE-ENE faults. Gravity gradients, a magnetic low, a low-resistivity zone and a 2 m anomaly about 5 °C above background all line up. Irrigation wells at 32 °C, about 7 km away, gave chalcedony and mixing-model temperatures of 110-140 °C. Six temperature-gradient (TG) holes were drilled to about 150 m. Two hit **124.9 °C and 114.5 °C**. The other four ranged from 79 °C down to 29 °C. The upflow sits less than 0.5-1 km WSW of the hottest hole, over the centre of the magnetic low. The resource estimate is **P90/P50/P10 = 6/16/38 MWe**. No GeoProbe holes were possible because the water table sat at 60-80 m in coarse gravel.
- **N Granite Springs Valley.** Ten GeoProbe holes to about 50 m reached a maximum of 46 °C. Six TG holes reached about **96 °C at about 240-250 m**, with isothermal segments (convection) at 110-250 m near old sinter. The upflow is not located.
- **Score.** Two of two drilled areas were hot and blind. Neither has a deep test. In May 2026 NBMG got permits 1739, 1743 and 1744 for Granite Springs wells, including 68-02 to about 503 m (Nevada Division of Minerals). Results were not public as of this writing (unverified).

**The 2026 structural-setting statistics.** Faulds et al. (2026, SGW) report the INGENIOUS FSS inventory: more than 1,430 FSS covering 7.7% of the Great Basin study area, with a median polygon of 13.4 km². By type, all FSS / known systems ≥37 °C (n=403) / training sites ≥120 °C (n=120):

| FSS type | All FSS | Known ≥37 °C | Training ≥120 °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Step-over | 39.6% | 27.5% | 34.2% |
| Fault intersection | 26.1% | 22.1% | 26.7% |
| Termination | 16.7% | 16.6% | 14.2% |
| Accommodation zone | 6.8% | 5.7% | 5.8% |
| Pull-apart | 2.3% | 2.7% | 5.0% |
| Displacement transfer zone | 1.4% | 3.2% | 6.7% |

The ratio of hot-site share to FSS share is a crude enrichment factor. It gives **DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x, intersection about 1.0x, step-over about 0.86x, termination about 0.85x, accommodation zone about 0.85x** (our arithmetic). The two transtensional Walker Lane types are rare but hot. Gabbs is a DTZ. The roughly 28 producing plants sit in under 2% of known FSS.

## Best methods and their reported skill

- **Nevada expert PFA (2015-17).** No AUC or area-capture figure was published. Benchmarks averaged 51.4 against a range of 28-65. In the field, 2 of 2 high-score areas gave >95 °C at ≤250 m, but only 2 of 12 TG holes reached >110 °C. The map finds the right basin, not the right square kilometre.
- **Smith et al. 2023 (*Geothermics* 111:102693; arXiv 2209.15543).** A Bayesian neural network on the same layers, with about 10 features on a 250 m grid and 83 positives (≥39 °C) against 62 negatives (deep cool oil and gas wells). The positives were augmented with 4 neighbours each, giving 620 examples. No AUC was reported. The test split may leak through the neighbour augmentation. The map "resembles" the expert map. The value is in the uncertainty maps: the paper recommends a 5% quantile map.
- **Mordensky et al. 2025 (USGS, *Geothermics* 133:103450).** About 10x more training sites, positives weighted by upflow strength, and Monte Carlo cross-validation. **15 of 28 producing systems (54%) fall in the top 1% of area. 26 of 28 (93%) are above the 80th percentile.** This is the current public benchmark to beat.
- **Mordensky et al. 2023 ("When less is more").** Simple models matched complex ones on these labels.

## Datasets used

GDR 616 / OEDI 6866 (Nevada PFA regional layers and error layers); GDR 756 (reports and appendices); GDR 969 (Granite Springs wells and temperature survey); GDR 1085 and 1088 (Crescent Valley and Sou Hills geodatabases); USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database; NBMG geothermal site descriptions; about 44,400 gravity stations; 247 GPS stations; 880 cold and 987 thermal water analyses; Ormat and US Geothermal proprietary gravity, magnetics and water chemistry (shared, not public); purchased legacy oil and gas seismic (540 km, licensed); 2 m temperature surveys; MT 3D inversions; INGENIOUS FSS inventory (Faulds et al. 2026); Nevada Division of Minerals geothermal drilling permits.

## What this means for our model design

- **Make FSS type a learned categorical feature, not a flag.** The enrichment ratios (DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x, the rest about 0.85-1x) should enter as a hierarchical prior by type and by province (Walker Lane vs central Basin and Range). Then let the data update it.
- **Learn the recency and slip-rate curves.** The expert multipliers (x100 range for age, x2 for slip rate) are untested beliefs. Fit monotone splines or GAM terms and start them from the expert values as priors.
- **Put the model at sub-FSS scale.** Every Phase 2 area split into 5-12 FSS, and the Gabbs upflow sat within 0.5-1 km of one hole out of six. Our latent field must run at 100-250 m and be fed by 2 m probes, magnetics and well chemistry. A 13 km² polygon score is not enough.
- **Use TG holes as censored depth-temperature labels.** Twelve Nevada PFA holes (29-125 °C at 50-250 m) are precise local labels, including the 4 cool Gabbs holes that sit 1-2 km from a hit. That is exactly the near-miss negative signal that the SENTINEL-COX column likelihood is built to use.
- **Thin by search effort.** GDR 616 already ships a "Degree of Exploration" layer. Use it as a starting covariate for the Cox thinning, so that the many unexplored high-score FSS read as "unknown", not "barren".
- **Encode land and transmission screens separately from geology.** The Phase 2 shortlist mixed geology with land status and transmission. Our VOI-Bank should keep geology (P of heat) apart from deal value (RNPV per acre). That way a high-geology cell on private ranch land is not filtered out the way the public team filtered it.
- **Watch permits as a live label feed.** The Granite Springs 2026 wells (permits 1739, 1743, 1744) will give the first deep label on a PFA blind find. Ingest the logs the day they appear on the GDR, and pre-position options on nearby fee land before then.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No published back-test of the expert weights.** Figure 2 shows WofE and expert weights side by side, but nobody has scored the expert map out of sample. An agent can rebuild the Phase 1 map from GDR 616, compute AUC and area-capture on the 2025 USGS label set, and calibrate score against measured gradient.
2. **The FSS inventory is hand-digitised and called "minimum".** Basins lack mapping and gravity. A vision model trained on the 1,430 polygons, using lidar, 3DEP DEMs, gravity and magnetics, could propose concealed FSS in basin fill. These are the places Faulds says cannot be seen without detailed geophysics.
3. **Upflow location inside an anomaly is unsolved.** Granite Springs had >220 °C/km and still no upflow. A physics-informed inversion (a conductive plus advective 2D model fit to TG profiles, 2 m data and chemistry mixing lines) could rank the next hole. An agent can run thousands of these cheaply.
4. **Legacy seismic is locked in licensed scans.** 540 km were bought for five areas. AI interpretation of scanned SEG-Y or paper sections across Nevada could map buried faults basin-wide.
5. **Water-chemistry masking.** At Gabbs, 32 °C wells 7 km away gave a 110-140 °C geothermometer. A learned mixing model over the 987 thermal and 880 cold analyses, plus scanned county well records, could flag hidden upflows from ordinary ranch wells.
