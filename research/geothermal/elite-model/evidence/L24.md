# L24: Remote sensing ML for geothermal (TIR, hyperspectral, InSAR, vegetation, snow)

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Builds on `model/evidence/M10-remote-sensing.md` and `evidence/D10-satellite.md`. Those set the physics limits; this file asks what the ML literature has shown, and what an elite model should do that nobody has done. Web budget note: the shared search and fetch budget ran out after 3 searches and 1 fetch. Paper numbers below come from those searches, from M10/D10 reads, or from memory. Recalled items are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**Thermal IR (ASTER, Landsat, ECOSTRESS).**
- The classic result is Coolbaugh et al. (2007, RSE) at Bradys, Nevada. Night ASTER TIR, corrected for albedo, topography and thermal inertia, picks out ground a few °C warm over a known field. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2006.09.001
- Even at Yellowstone, ASTER radiant flux (about 2 GW) is only 30-45% of the geochemical heat budget (Vaughan et al. 2012, JVGR). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvolgeores.2012.04.022
- The signal is small next to the noise. Wei et al. (2026, Remote Sensing 18:1362) report surface geothermal signals of 1-3 °C against a seasonal swing of more than 20 °C. They fit a sinusoid to about 50 Landsat 8 scenes per pixel and recover only +1-2 °C offset over fields with >100 °C at 100 m. https://doi.org/10.3390/rs18091362
- Romaguera et al. (2018) at Olkaria-Menengai, Kenya, needed a mean LST residual of at least 2 K to call a pixel geothermal. https://ris.utwente.nl/ws/files/72985263/Romaguera2018detecting.pdf
- Yuan et al. (SGW 2026) split Landsat LST stacks into solar and stable geothermal parts. Only 2 of 4 persistent anomaly types were geothermal. The others were landslides, drainage and human heat. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Yuan.pdf
- ECOSTRESS (about 70 m, varying time of day, 2018 onward) adds pre-dawn and diurnal scenes (Silvestri et al. 2020, https://doi.org/10.3390/rs12010184; Soszynska et al. 2024, RSE 114103, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2024.114103).
- Deep learning on thermal anomalies is mature in *volcanology*, not in geothermal. Examples: a deep CNN for volcanic thermal anomalies (Remote Sensing 15:3718, 2023, https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/a-deep-convolutional-neural-network-for-detecting-volcanic-thermal-anomalies-from-satellite-images), and NASA work on "Detection of Subtle Thermal Anomalies: Deep Learning Applied to the ASTER Global Volcano Dataset" (https://espo.nasa.gov/node/93787). Their skill numbers were not retrieved (unverified).

**Hyperspectral alteration (AVIRIS, HyMap, ProSpecTIR, EMIT, PRISMA).**
- This is the only remote method with documented *blind* discoveries.
- Columbus Salt Marsh, NV: 4 m ProSpecTIR over 380 km², then about 100 two-metre probes. It found a 4.1 × 1.7 km warm area and geothermometers of 115-137 °C. https://www.dri.edu/publication/3786
- Fish Lake Valley, NV (Littlefield and Calvin 2014, RSE): AVIRIS, HyMap and ProSpecTIR gave 4 new targets, including two new fault-controlled sinter/travertine areas. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2013.09.007
- Pyramid Lake tufa and sinter mapping (Kratt, Calvin and Coolbaugh 2010, RSE). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2010.05.006
- Kraal and Schwering (2024, Sandia, GRC Trans 48): AVIRIS-Classic at 14 m found altered outcrops that the USGS ASTER map missed. The best blind clues are 1-500 m² patches in Quaternary fault footwalls. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3009542
- EMIT (60 m, 380-2500 nm, 2022 onward) and PRISMA (30 m) now map alteration regionally (Almasi et al. 2026, EMIT + PRISMA in Iran). https://pure.kfupm.edu.sa/en/publications/integrated-emit-and-prisma-hyperspectral-analysis-for-mapping-hig/

**InSAR.**
- InSAR sees production, not natural blind reservoirs.
- Brady: a 4 × 1.5 km subsidence bowl from 2004-2014, at 600 m depth or shallower, along the fault strike (Ali et al. 2016, Geothermics 61). https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geothermics.2016.01.008
- Coso: up to −27.6 mm/yr (Envisat).
- LANL's deep-learning denoiser recovers ±2.5 mm (Reinisch et al. 2024). https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2426558
- NISAR L-band data has been public since 20 July 2026. https://asf.alaska.edu/notices/nisar-l-band-data-now-publicly-available/
- Exploration use is indirect: geodetic strain is an importance feature in INGENIOUS (Trainor-Guitton et al. 2025). https://www.osti.gov/biblio/3018341

**Vegetation and snow.**
- Red-edge vegetation stress over a CO2 leak showed up within 5 days at 0.33 m pixels (Bellante et al. 2013). https://remotesensing.montana.edu/documents/bellante_et_al_2013.pdf
- Snow melt-out as a heat integrator is physically attractive. 3 W/m² melts about 78 mm SWE over a 100-day season (M10 calc). We found no systematic study of it (unverified absence).

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Result | Caveat |
|---|---|---|
| "Geothermal AI" (Moraga, Duzgun, Cavur, Soydan 2022, Renewable Energy): mineral markers + LST + faults + InSAR, Brady and Desert Peak | 92-95% accuracy within a site; 72-76% across sites | Pixel accuracy, not discovery skill. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.renene.2022.04.113 |
| Demir, Cavur, Yu, Duzgun 2024 (Energies 17:3255), three sites | Desert Peak and Coso transfer to each other; Brady transfers to neither | Satellite-only models learn site style. https://doi.org/10.3390/en17133255 |
| Cavur et al. 2024 (Remote Sensing 16:1223), ASTER at Coso with a 97-sample local spectral library, fused target detectors | Overall accuracy: kaolinite 96%, opal 95%, alunite 87%, chalcedony 83%, hematite 60% | Needs local field spectra; 30-90 m pixels. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2331359 |
| Landsat sinusoid stack (Wei 2026) | +1-2 °C offset, about 2 K amplitude deficit over hot fields | Hot fields with surface expression only |
| Yuan 2026 solar/geothermal split | Qualitative; 2 of 4 anomaly classes real | No validation numbers |
| Romaguera 2018 LST residual | Threshold ≥2 K; 1.7 K inside fields vs 1.2 K outside | High-enthalpy field |
| Airborne hyperspectral → 2 m probes (Columbus Salt Marsh, Fish Lake Valley) | 1 confirmed blind system; 4 new targets | Human expert workflow, not ML |
| INGENIOUS GBM (48 features, 37 plants) | TIR and hyperspectral are not top features | Coarse regional rasters |
| LANL GeoThermalCloud (unsupervised NMF/clustering) | Finds "hidden signatures" | No hit-rate numbers. https://www.osti.gov/biblio/2480434 |

The honest summary: nobody has published a blind-system *discovery rate* for any remote-sensing ML model. The reported numbers are pixel accuracies at known fields.

## Datasets used

- **Thermal:** Landsat 5-9 Collection 2 LST (1984 onward), ASTER L1T/AST_08 night TIR (2000 onward), ECOSTRESS L2 LST/ET (2018 onward), MODIS LST (1 km, context only).
- **Hyperspectral:** EMIT L2A reflectance and L2B mineral IDs (LP DAAC), PRISMA, AVIRIS-Classic/NG/3, HyMap and ProSpecTIR legacy flights, USGS ASTER alteration maps, USGS Earth MRI hyperspectral (extent unverified).
- **Labels and training sets:** GDR 1313 "Geothermal Mineral Alterations in Brady and Desert Peak" (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1313) and GDR 1303 "Appendices for Geothermal Exploration Artificial Intelligence Report" (https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1303), with code and training data on data.gov/AmeriGEOSS.
- **InSAR:** Sentinel-1 (OPERA DISP-S1), ERS/Envisat/ALOS archives, NISAR GUNW/GSLC, TerraSAR-X at Brady.
- **Ancillary:** OpenET, Sentinel-2 (snow, red edge), SMAP and NISAR soil moisture, ERA5 or HRRR weather, 3DEP lidar.

## What this means for our model design

- **Treat every remote layer as a detection process with a known miss rate, not as a feature.** Pixel classifiers reach 92-95% at home and drop to 72-76% away, and Brady fails to transfer at all. In SENTINEL-COX, each layer should add a likelihood term P(observation | latent flux, cap depth, cover). A non-detection should lower, but not zero, the intensity.
- **Measure completeness by injection and recovery.** Borrow the method astronomers use to count faint exoplanets. Inject synthetic heat-flux anomalies (0.3-10 W/m², with realistic shapes along faults) into real Landsat/ECOSTRESS stacks through a surface energy-balance forward model. Then run our detector. The recovery curve gives P(detect | flux, size, cover) for every valley, with no need for labelled negatives.
- **Build the LST residual with a learned "expected temperature" model.** Train a large model (or fine-tune a geospatial foundation model, such as NASA-IBM Prithvi on HLS, unverified fit) to predict each pixel's LST from weather, soil moisture, albedo, terrain, emissivity and neighbours, *excluding* the depth signal. The geothermal layer is the persistent residual over 200+ scenes. This is Yuan 2026 and Wei 2026 done with modern ML across the whole Great Basin.
- **Make hyperspectral the main remote positive, at sub-pixel level.** Use EMIT as a regional screen with sub-pixel unmixing for opal, sinter, alunite, kaolinite and borate. Train a super-resolution or unmixing model on EMIT-AVIRIS pairs where both exist. Route hits to airborne or drone SWIR and 2 m probes (the Columbus Salt Marsh funnel).
- **Add a "fossil vs live" latent per alteration patch.** Alteration can be dead. Tie it to thermal residuals, spring chemistry and the shared latent mask class, so sinter without heat raises "old system or deep cap" rather than "drill here".
- **Use InSAR for structure and for market intelligence.** NISAR plus Sentinel-1 strain and fault-creep maps are permeability covariates. Subsidence bowls at neighbour fields show who is producing and how hard. That feeds the RNPV and buyer side.
- **Date-stamp every remote layer for hindcasts.** Freeze at each system's first-signal date. ASTER (2000), Landsat TIR (1984) and AVIRIS flights have clear dates, so they fit the existing time-split hindcast and the search-effort thinning.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **A published discovery-rate or completeness number for any remote method.** Injection-recovery on real stacks gives one, valley by valley.
2. **A Great Basin snow melt-out and frost residual.** Sentinel-2 and Landsat snow time series from 2015 onward, detrended for aspect, wind and shrub height with lidar. It is free and unbuilt (unverified absence).
3. **Cross-scale hyperspectral labels.** Pair 60 m EMIT pixels with 4-14 m airborne maps and field spectra to train an unmixing model that finds 100 m² opal patches. Kraal and Schwering show these patches are the real blind clues.
4. **Multi-sensor time-series foundation model.** Pre-train self-supervised on Landsat, ECOSTRESS, Sentinel-2, EMIT and NISAR over the West. Then fine-tune on the 2 m probe and TG-hole labels that only we collect. The labels, not the pixels, are the moat.
5. **Agentic anomaly triage.** Yuan 2026 shows that half of persistent warm anomalies are landslides, drainage or human heat. A vision-language agent can check each anomaly against NAIP imagery, lidar, land use and well records, and kill false positives at scale before anyone drives out.
6. **Release-day ingestion.** NISAR backfill (late 2026), new EMIT scenes, Earth MRI hyperspectral and TRISHNA (about 2027) each create a short window before others read them.
