## What the field knows

1. **Open fractures are the critically stressed ones.** Barton, Zoback and Moos (1995, Geology 23:683) showed that flowing fractures in three deep wells sit close to Coulomb failure in the measured stress field. Dixie Valley repeats this along one fault: productive segments are critically stressed, tight segments are not (Hickman et al., via M30). This is the physical basis for slip tendency (Morris et al. 1996) and dilation tendency (Ferrill et al. 1999).
2. **The stress input is the weak link.** Lund Snee and Zoback (2020, Nat. Commun. 11:1951) used more than 50,000 focal mechanisms and about 2,300 SHmax indicators. SHmax rotates up to about 90 degrees over tens of km at the edge of the Great Basin and Rio Grande rift (via L22). Faulds et al. (2013) built Ts/Td with one regional ratio (Shmin/SHmax 0.527) and dips set to maximise Ts. Their layers are upper bounds, not probabilities (L22).
3. **Probabilistic fault-slip tools exist, but in the induced-seismicity world.** Walsh and Zoback (2016, Geology) built Fault Slip Potential (FSP). It samples stress, friction and pore pressure by Monte Carlo and returns P(slip) per fault (unverified detail). Geothermal favourability work has not adopted it at regional scale.
4. **ML has multiplied earthquake catalogs about ten-fold.** Ross et al. (2019, Science) used template matching in Southern California and found about 1.8 million events, roughly 10x the network catalog (unverified count). Deep-learning pickers do the same at lower cost: PhaseNet (Zhu and Beroza 2019, GJI) and EQTransformer (Mousavi et al. 2020, Nat. Commun.). SeisBench (Woollam et al. 2022, SRL) packages them. QuakeFlow and GaMMA (Zhu et al. 2022-2023) chain picking, association and location.
5. **Dense catalogs light up fluid paths, not just faults.** Ross et al. (2020, Science) imaged the Cahuilla swarm. About 22,000 events migrated along a permeable, fault-valve structure driven by natural fluid (unverified count). At Coso, more than 60,000 relocated events outline both through-going faults and diffuse permeable fracturing (M12). Pumping at Brady and San Emidio lowers local seismicity (Cardiff et al. 2018, EPSL; WHOLESCALE via L22). So seismicity responds to pore pressure. That is a permeability signal.
6. **Dynamic triggering marks fluid-rich, near-critical crust.** Hill et al. (1993, Science) saw the 1992 Landers quake trigger swarms at The Geysers, Coso, Long Valley and other geothermal areas. Brodsky and Prejean (2005, JGR) and Aiken and Peng (2014, JGR) found triggering is far more common in geothermal and volcanic fields (unverified ratios). Passing surface waves act as a free, regional "stress test" of every valley.
7. **Ambient noise and large-N arrays map structure, not heat.** Shapiro et al. (2005, Science) and Bensen et al. (2007, GJI) set up noise interferometry. Large-N nodal arrays (Long Beach, about 5,200 geophones; PoroTomo at Brady, 238 nodes; Hengill, 498 nodes) give basin depth, buried faults and Vp/Vs and Q anomalies (M12). DAS on dark fibre gave a 3 km Vs section at Brawley in days (Rodríguez Tribaldos and Ajo-Franklin 2021; M27).
8. **Zanskar's seismology is prospect-scale.** OSTI 3015294 (Jan 2026) describes large-N low-cost seismometers, micro-earthquake tomography and "ambient fracture-seismic emissions" that "guided well siting". Repo files disagree on the partner: Z03 says LBNL, L14 says NREL (Sonsteng, Hoiland, Edwards 2026). Treat the partner as (unverified). Zanskar's job ads ask for ambient-noise and microseismic pipelines in ObsPy (M12).

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Task | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| PhaseNet (Zhu and Beroza 2019) | P/S picking | P precision about 0.94, recall about 0.86; S about 0.85/0.76 on NCEDC test data (unverified) |
| EQTransformer (Mousavi 2020) | Detection and picking | Detection F1 about 0.99 on STEAD; found about 2x more events in Tottori aftershocks (unverified) |
| Template matching (Ross 2019) | Catalog completeness | About 10x more events; Mc lowered by about 1 unit (unverified) |
| CNN first-motion polarity (Ross, Meier, Hauksson 2018, JGR) + HASH/SKHASH (Skoumal et al. 2024) | Focal mechanisms from small events | Polarity accuracy above 95% (unverified); turns M0-M1 events into mechanisms |
| SATSI / MSATSI (Hardebeck and Michael 2006; Martínez-Garzón 2014), STRESSINVERSE (Vavryčuk 2014), Bayesian (Arnold and Townend 2007) | Stress inversion | SHmax to about +/-10-20 degrees from 20-50 good mechanisms (unverified rule of thumb) |
| FaultSeg3D U-Net (Wu et al. 2019, Geophysics) | Faults in 3D reflection seismic | Trained on 200 synthetic volumes; transfers to field data (unverified) |
| U-Net fault mapping from DEM and Sentinel-2 (Hermant 2025, TLS Geothermics) | Surface faults | PR-AUC 0.595, IoU at least 0.58 (L04) |
| Faulds 2013 Ts/Td | Permeability proxy | Young NE-striking faults: weights-of-evidence contrast 2.52 (M30); no blind test |
| PSET emission tomography, San Emidio (Warren et al. 2019) | Permeable volume | Matched known reservoir and a newly drilled southern zone, retrospective (M12) |

No method above has a published hit rate on undrilled geothermal targets. All successes are post hoc (M12).

## What this means for our model design

- **Re-pick the whole western US archive once.** Run PhaseNet/EQTransformer plus template matching over all public continuous waveforms in the Great Basin, 2000 to now. Expect about 5-10x more events and Mc lower by about 1 (unverified). This is a one-off compute job, perhaps tens of thousands of dollars of cloud time (unverified). It gives us a private-quality catalog from public data.
- **Make stress a field with uncertainty, not a constant.** Invert the new small-event focal mechanisms on a 10-20 km grid with a Bayesian stress inversion. Feed SHmax, stress ratio and regime samples into an FSP-style Monte Carlo. Output P(critically stressed) per fault segment and per depth. This replaces Faulds-style upper bounds in SENTINEL-COX's permeability-class prior.
- **Add a detectability layer to the Cox process.** The VOI-Bank already thins counts by search effort. Do the same for seismicity: an Mc map from station geometry and noise. "No events" where Mc is high is missing data, not a negative.
- **Add four seismic permeability features per segment.** (a) event rate above local Mc; (b) swarm and migration flags (diffusivity from the r-sqrt(t) front, which gives a hydraulic diffusivity in m2/s); (c) b-value; (d) dynamic-triggering sensitivity from big distant quakes. Mask operating fields, mines and blasts.
- **Feed ambient-noise basin depth into the 1D column likelihood.** Depth to bedrock sets sediment insulation and where the range-front fault sits. Use TA plus nodal plus DAS Vs as a covariate with its own error.
- **Let the seismic sign depend on system type.** Vp/Vs, Q and velocity anomalies flip sign between steam, liquid, sealed-cap and fault-hosted systems (M12). Tie them to the shared latent mask class already in SENTINEL-COX.
- **Use seismic VOI to buy.** A month of 10-20 nodes over a candidate gives local mechanisms and a tight SHmax. That flips Ts on many segments. Rank this against an MT carpet or a TG hole in the VOI-Bank.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No regional "permeability-from-seismicity" layer.** INGENIOUS uses a smoothed earthquake count. No one has joined an ML-enhanced catalog to fault segments with stress-aware P(slip). We can build it from public data.
2. **No blind test of any seismic feature.** We can run a time-split test: compute features from data before each discovery date (Gabbs Valley, Big Blind, McGinness Hills) and score lift.
3. **Dynamic triggering as a regional permeability probe has never been used for exploration.** An AI detector run on every large teleseism since 2005 across all stations could map "valleys that respond to stress waves".
4. **Hydraulic diffusivity from natural swarms.** Swarm migration fronts give diffusivity (Shapiro-style r = sqrt(4 pi D t)). No catalog of natural Great Basin swarm diffusivity exists. That is a direct permeability number before drilling.
5. **Stress from scanned logs.** Breakouts and drilling-induced fractures sit in scanned caliper and image logs in O&G and geothermal wells (D07). Vision models can extract them and add local SHmax points where earthquakes are rare.
6. **Seismic foundation models.** Pretrain one model on all western US continuous noise to embed each station-day. Cluster embeddings near known systems. This could surface weak tremor or emission signatures that hand pickers miss (speculative).
7. **Buried-fault inference from aligned hypocentres.** Fit 3D planes to relocated clusters and add them as faults that Qfaults lacks, then score their tendency. This fills the "buried faults have no Ts score" gap in M30.
