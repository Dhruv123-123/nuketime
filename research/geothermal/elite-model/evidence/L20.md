# L20: Earth observation and geoscience foundation models for subsurface prediction

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: Prithvi, Clay, SatMAE, TerraMind, AlphaEarth Foundations (AEF) embeddings, seismic and seismology foundation models (FMs), and whether any of them predict what is under the ground. Web budget ran out partway, so some numbers come from memory of the papers and are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Surface FMs are now a commodity.** Three families matter.
   - Masked-autoencoder (MAE) image models. SatMAE (Cong et al., NeurIPS 2022) added time and band-group encodings to MAE. Prithvi-EO-1.0 (100M params, US HLS data) and Prithvi-EO-2.0 (300M and 600M params, about 4.2M global HLS time series, with time and location embeddings) are open from NASA and IBM ([arXiv 2412.02732](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.02732)). Clay v1.x is an Apache-licensed multi-sensor MAE ViT (Sentinel-2, Landsat, NAIP, Sentinel-1, others) that ships embeddings ([Clay docs](https://docsearch.algolia.com/mcp/docs/repo/clay-foundation/model)).
   - Any-to-any generative models. TerraMind (IBM and ESA, 2025) learns nine modalities, including S1, S2, DEM, land cover and text, and can "think" by generating missing modalities ([arXiv 2504.11171](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.11171); [HF](https://huggingface.co/ibm-esa-geospatial/TerraMind-1.0-base)).
   - Embedding fields. AlphaEarth Foundations (Google DeepMind, July 2025) fuses Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, GEDI lidar, GRACE gravity, ERA5 climate, elevation and geotagged text. It outputs one 64-dimensional unit vector per 10 m pixel per year, 2017 onward. It is free on Earth Engine as `GOOGLE/SATELLITE_EMBEDDING/V1/ANNUAL` (CC-BY 4.0) ([arXiv 2507.22291](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22291); [DeepMind blog](https://deepmind.google/blog/alphaearth-foundations-helps-map-our-planet-in-unprecedented-detail/)).
2. **FMs do not always beat plain supervised models.** PANGAEA tested many geospatial FMs on diverse tasks. It found that a supervised UNet is often as good or better, especially with enough labels or with high-resolution inputs ([arXiv 2412.04204](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.04204v2); see also [Geospatial Foundational Disappointments](https://christopherren.substack.com/p/geospatial-foundational-disappointments)). FMs help most when labels are sparse. That is our regime.
3. **The first direct subsurface test of AEF exists, and it is LBNL's.** Nakata, Liu, Chen, Nakata and Varadharajan (2026), "Subsurface Property Mapping using Google AlphaEarth Foundations" ([arXiv 2604.14756](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14756)). They use frozen 64-d AEF vectors, sampled at about 1 km, to predict (a) near-surface Vs30 and (b) temperature from 1 to 6 km over the conterminous US. The temperature labels are more than 400,000 borehole readings (SMU, USGS, state surveys). Heat flow itself was not a target.
4. **Physically readable embeddings.** Work in 2026 maps AEF dimensions to physical land-surface variables, so an LLM can explain them ([arXiv 2602.10354](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10354v1)). Chinese reviews argue AEF is changing quantitative geoscience ([Earth Science Frontiers 2025](https://cjournal.hep.com.cn/1005-2321/CN/10.13745/j.esf.sf.2025.9.68-en)).
5. **Seismic reflection FMs work for interpretation.** The Seismic Foundation Model (SFM; Sheng et al., [arXiv 2309.02791](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2309.02791)) is an MAE ViT pretrained on about 2.3M 224x224 2D patches cut from 192 3D surveys (unverified counts). It improves facies, geobody, denoising, interpolation and inversion tasks over training from scratch. TGS reports scaling 3D seismic FMs on its proprietary library ([EAGE 2025](https://www.tgs.com/technical-library/advances-in-scaling-and-architecture-of-3d-foundation-models-for-seismic-data)). A workflow review covers the full geophysics-FM pipeline ([arXiv 2504.17384](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17384)).
6. **Seismology waveform FMs exist.** SeisLM (NeurIPS 2024 workshop) pretrains wav2vec2-style on unlabeled continuous waveforms. It matches or beats supervised pickers on phase picking and event classification with fewer labels ([arXiv 2410.15765](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.15765)). SeisCLIP and others follow the same idea (unverified details).
7. **Nobody has a geothermal FM.** Geothermal ML still uses gradient-boosted trees or small nets on hand-built layers: INGENIOUS, USGS favorability (Mordensky et al.) and the [USGS western US favorability features](https://christopher-catalog-dev.app.cloud.gov/dataset/geothermal-resource-favorability-select-features-and-predictions-for-the-western-united-st). There is also a Mines/NREL seed project on remote sensing for EGS favorability ([Mines Nexus](https://nexus.mines.edu/nexus-seed-presentation-machine-learning-informed-analysis-of-remote-sensing-data-to-identify-favorable-areas-for-enhanced-geothermal-systems/)). I found no published use of AEF, Prithvi or Clay for hidden hydrothermal targets.

## Best methods and reported skill

| Method | Task | Reported skill |
|---|---|---|
| AEF + MLP (Nakata 2026) | US temperature at 1-6 km, 20 km grid, 20,971 cells | Test **R2 = 0.919, RMSE = 6.0 C**. Random 80/20 split. No no-embedding baseline number given. |
| AEF + slope, XGBoost | Vs30, 2,886 US stations | R2 0.533 vs 0.185 baseline; RMSE 126 vs 167 m/s (-24%); MAE -34% |
| AEF, California only | Vs30 | R2 0.586 vs USGS map 0.398 |
| AEF vs other featurisers | 15 mapping tasks | About 24% lower error on average than the next best (unverified exact figure) |
| Prithvi-EO-2.0 600M | GEO-Bench | Beats Prithvi-EO-1.0 and most open GFMs by several points (unverified exact numbers) |
| SFM | Seismic facies, geobody | Better than from-scratch ViT and UNet on standard sets (unverified numbers) |
| SeisLM | Phase picking, event classes | Matches supervised SOTA with less labelled data |

Two warnings sit on the headline AEF number. First, random splits leak space. Boreholes cluster, so neighbours of test wells are in training. The authors say this themselves. Second, depth is a feature, and depth explains most of the temperature variance. A spatial-block, depth-held-in test is likely to drop R2 a lot (unverified). Still, AEF beat a slope-plus-tectonics baseline for Vs30 by a wide margin. So the embedding does carry geology: lithology, soil, vegetation stress, landform, and through GRACE a little gravity.

## Datasets used

- **AEF annual embeddings**, 2017 to present, 10 m, 64-d, Earth Engine.
- **HLS** (Harmonized Landsat Sentinel-2) for Prithvi. **Sentinel-1/2, Landsat, NAIP** for Clay. **TerraMesh** (about 9M multimodal samples, unverified) for TerraMind.
- **SMU Geothermal Laboratory database, USGS assessment wells, state surveys**: about 400k temperature readings (Nakata 2026).
- **USGS Vs30 compilation** (2,886 CONUS stations after filtering).
- **SFM pretraining**: 192 public and partner 3D seismic surveys (unverified). **TGS** multi-client library (closed).
- **SeisLM**: continuous broadband waveforms, plus STEAD-type labelled sets.
- **Geothermal benchmarks to plug in**: INGENIOUS layers, USGS 2008 and 2023-25 favorability features, NGDS/GDR.

## What this means for our model design

- **Add AEF as a free surface-covariate block, not as the model.** Pull all 64 dims at 10 m for 2017-2025 over the Great Basin. Aggregate to our 500 m cells (mean, std and a few quantiles per dim). Feed a low-rank projection (8-16 PCs) into the CREAM-COX log-intensity with a shrinkage prior. Cost is about one day of Earth Engine time.
- **Mask label leakage first.** Embeddings at known systems see power plants, pads, pipelines and roads. A model will learn "infrastructure = geothermal". Zero out or block cells within about 2 km of developed sites. Better, learn a "built footprint" direction from non-geothermal industry sites and project it out of every vector.
- **Use AEF twice: as geology and as search effort.** The same human-footprint signal that leaks labels is a good proxy for access and past exploration. Put it in the thinning (effort) term on purpose, and keep it out of the existence term. That is a clean way to say "this cell is unexplored, not empty".
- **Re-run Nakata 2026 as our desk test, done right.** Same labels plus our AI-extracted scanned TG logs. Spatial block CV (50-100 km blocks). Predict gradient, not raw temperature, so depth cannot inflate skill. Compare against INGENIOUS-only and INGENIOUS+AEF. If AEF adds even a few percent of explained gradient variance under block CV, it earns its place. If not, it stays only in the effort term.
- **Use embedding similarity for analog search.** Take the AEF and Clay vectors around blind systems (Blue Mountain, McGinness Hills, Big Blind, Pumpernickel and others from M18). Rank all BLM and state parcels by nearest-neighbour distance. This is a cheap prior proposal. The Cox model and VOI engine then decide.
- **Fine-tune Prithvi-EO-2.0 for faint surface signs, with PU learning.** Targets are sinter and tufa, alteration, warm-ground snowmelt, and phreatophyte stress in multi-season HLS. Labels come from known spring and fumarole points (positive) and unlabeled cells. The output is one more censored detector in the shared mask-class layer, not a direct prospectivity score.
- **Use waveform FMs to deepen microseismic catalogs.** Run SeisLM or a similar fine-tuned picker over archived continuous data (USArray TA, Nevada Seismological Lab, EarthScope). The result is a catalog with a lower magnitude floor and Vp/Vs and swarm features per cell. Seismicity is a strong permeability proxy.
- **Use seismic FMs only on legacy reflection data.** Scanned 2D oil-industry lines exist in several Basin and Range valleys. Vectorise them to SEG-Y, then let an SFM-style model pick basin-bounding and step-over faults. This feeds the structure layer. Skip 3D seismic FMs, since there is almost no 3D in our basins.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No subsurface multimodal FM.** No one has pretrained a masked model on stacked public geophysics: gravity, aeromagnetics, MT resistivity (EarthScope), heat flow, Quaternary faults, geochem, depth-to-basement and AEF. Train a TerraMind-style any-to-any model with missing-modality masking on the whole western US. It can then impute "what the MT would show" where there is no MT. That is exactly the VOI question.
2. **No FM for borehole temperature logs.** Millions of profile curves (TG holes, oil BHTs, water wells) have never been used to pretrain a 1D sequence model. Pretrain it on extracted logs. It learns convection signatures (isothermal sections, reversals) and can denoise and complete short logs.
3. **No honest spatial-CV benchmark** for FM embeddings on geothermal targets. Publishing one would add credibility and draw partners. Zanskar's stack (M13) is LightGBM on handcrafted layers.
4. **Blind systems are, by definition, weak at the surface.** Pure surface FMs will struggle. The edge is indirect signals, such as lithology, groundwater and vegetation shifts, and subtle multi-year change. The 2017-2025 AEF time series lets us test change vectors (year minus year) for thermal vegetation and snow anomalies that one-shot imagery misses.
5. **Document FM plus Earth FM fusion.** Link LLM-extracted well records to AEF and Clay pixels via contrastive training (text-to-pixel, as AEF did with Wikipedia). The model then learns which ground looks like "hot water at 150 m" from driller notes.
6. **LLM-readable embeddings.** Using physically interpreted AEF dims ([arXiv 2602.10354](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.10354v1)), a reasoning agent can explain why a cell scores high. That helps landmen and buyers trust the bank's picks.
