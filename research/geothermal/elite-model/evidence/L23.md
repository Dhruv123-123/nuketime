## What the field knows

**1. Geology already has formal data standards.** GeoSciML (OGC standard, v4.1, 2017) defines geologic units, faults, contacts, boreholes and earth materials. It has controlled vocabularies from the IUGS Commission for the Management and Application of Geoscience Information (CGI), such as simple lithology, fault type and contact type. https://www.ogc.org/standard/geosciml/ (unverified URL). In the US, the USGS GeMS schema (Techniques and Methods 11-B10, 2020) is the delivery standard for new geologic maps (unverified). For geothermal, the National Geothermal Data System (NGDS, about 2011-2014) published USGIN "content models": borehole temperature, well header, heat flow, thermal spring, geothermal fluid chemistry and others (unverified list). These are the closest thing to a geothermal ontology. They are flat tables. They are not a reasoning graph. Nobody has linked them into one graph of "systems, wells, measurements, faults, parcels".

**2. Geoscience knowledge graphs are growing fast, mostly in China and in minerals.** Wang et al. (2018, Computers & Geosciences 112:112-120) built a KG from geology papers by text mining (unverified details). Zhou Chenghu et al. (2021, Science China Earth Sciences) set out the "geoscience knowledge graph" agenda for the Deep-time Digital Earth (DDE) programme (unverified). Ma (2022, Computers & Geosciences 161:105082) reviews KG construction in geoscience (unverified). DDE then built the GeoGPT and K2 language models on top (K2: a 7B LLaMA tuned on geoscience text, WSDM 2024, unverified). The US analogue is DARPA CriticalMAAS (2022-2025) with USGS. Its "MinMod" knowledge graph (USC ISI, Knoblock and others) links mineral sites, commodities, grade and tonnage across MRDS and reports, so that models can query it (unverified). xDD / GeoDeepDive (Peters, Ross, Livny) is a machine-reading corpus of millions of geology papers linked to Macrostrat (size unverified).

**3. The mineral systems approach is the strongest reasoning template we have.** Wyborn, Heinrich and Jaques (1994) said ore deposits form only where a whole system works: a source, a fluid pathway, a trap and preservation. McCuaig, Beresford and Hronsky (2010, Ore Geology Reviews 38:128-138) turned this into a targeting method. You list each "critical process". You translate each into "mappable proxies". Then you score each proxy for how well the data shows it (unverified pages). Joly, Porwal and McCuaig applied this in a knowledge-driven model of the west Kimberley, Australia (JCU repository, https://researchonline.jcu.edu.au/40032/). Geothermal play fairway analysis (PFA) is the same idea under another name: heat x permeability x fluid x seal. The Hawaii team (Ito et al. 2017, Geothermics 70) made it a probability product with explicit priors. In M03 these were PrH about 0.06 and PrP about 0.14. Only two of six DOE PFA teams learned weights from data (M03).

**4. Causal methods exist, but geology has barely used them.** Causal discovery tools are mature: PC and FCI (Spirtes, Glymour, Scheines), LiNGAM, NOTEARS (Zheng et al. 2018, NeurIPS), and invariant causal prediction (Peters, Buhlmann, Meinshausen 2016, JRSS-B). Runge et al. (2019, Nature Communications 10:2553) laid out causal inference for Earth system science, but for time series, not maps (unverified). Spatial confounding is well known in statistics. Reich et al. (2021, International Statistical Review 89:605-634) review spatial causal inference (unverified). In prospectivity, Zhang, Lawley and others (2024, Natural Resources Research 33:995-1023) state plainly that data-driven prospectivity models are "inferential rather than causal". They show that many feature and algorithm mixes score high but give very different maps (checked). The only causal-learning prospectivity result I found is EGU24-8771 (checked, abstract only).

**5. Neuro-symbolic methods let rules and nets train together.** Theory-guided data science (Karpatne et al. 2017, IEEE TKDE) argues for physics rules inside ML. Semantic loss (Xu et al. 2018, ICML), DeepProbLog (Manhaeve et al. 2018, NeurIPS) and Logic Tensor Networks (Badreddine et al. 2022, Artificial Intelligence) add logical rules to neural training. In geoscience the practical form is still simple: monotonic constraints in gradient boosting, physics losses (L12), and rule-based vetoes in PFA. I found no geothermal paper that uses formal logic rules inside a learned model.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Automated MPM with Bayesian optimisation, conformal prediction and causal learning (EGU24-8771) | AUC up 17.84% over baseline; false positive rate down 84.31%; six datasets; claims better out-of-distribution transfer | Checked, abstract only. Relative or absolute is not stated. Baseline not named. Open-source code claimed. |
| Weights of evidence, Great Basin (Coolbaugh et al. 2002) | Young NE-striking faults: contrast 2.52 for systems over 160 C | From M11. A "Bayesian" evidential model, the oldest data-driven baseline. |
| Nevada PFA, expert fuzzy logic blended with WofE and logistic regression (Faulds et al. 2017) | 34 benchmark systems averaged 51.4 on a scale of about 28-65 | From M03. Knowledge-driven score, partly fitted. |
| Tularosa: expert logic vs stochastic WofE | 8 vs 10 plays found; 6 shared | From M03. Expert and data agree only 50-75% of the time. |
| USGS 2025 favourability (Mordensky et al.) | 54% of producing systems in top 1% of the map | From M03. Purely data-driven benchmark to beat. |
| Lawley et al. GSC Zn-Pb prospectivity, with features extracted from map text by NLP | Very high AUC, often above 0.95 (unverified) | Shows text-derived "knowledge features" can carry signal. |
| Workflow uncertainty (Zhang et al. 2024) | Different feature and model mixes all score well but disagree spatially | Checked, qualitative. A warning that AUC does not pick the map. |

The pattern is clear. Knowledge-driven models (mineral systems, PFA) transfer to new areas but are hand-weighted. Data-driven models score well in-sample but overfit and disagree (M14 "Cursed?"). The best published results blend the two. Causal and neuro-symbolic methods promise the transfer gain, but the evidence is one abstract.

## What this means for our model design

- **Build one typed geothermal knowledge graph as the evidence store under SENTINEL-COX.** Node types: system, well, measurement, fault segment, structural setting, spring, geologic unit, parcel, lease, document, search campaign. Use GeoSciML/GeMS and CGI terms for geology and the NGDS content models for measurements. Every fact carries its source page, date, extraction confidence and search channel. That last field feeds the Cox thinning directly.
- **Write the existence term as a mineral-systems product.** P(system) = P(heat) x P(upflow pathway) x P(fluid) x P(cap and preservation). Each factor is its own latent field with its own mappable proxies. This is the Hawaii PFA structure, but with weights learned from the hindcast. It also gives an honest "which link is weakest" readout per parcel. That readout picks the next sensor in the VOI loop.
- **Draw an explicit causal DAG for labels and masking before fitting.** Search effort is caused by outcrops, roads and old mining. Labels are caused by effort. Cold groundwater masks heat. The DAG tells us which layers to adjust for and which ones are colliders. Shallow alteration, for example, is both an effect of the system and a cause of exploration effort. Conditioning on it naively biases the count.
- **Use invariance across basins to pick features.** Run invariant causal prediction or anchor regression across basin hold-outs (Walker Lane, central Nevada, Black Rock, Utah). Keep only features whose effect is stable. This is a direct, testable answer to the "Cursed?" result that more layers made the USGS model worse.
- **Add symbolic rules as soft constraints, not vetoes.** Examples: monotone in heat flow; no blind upflow without a permeable structure within about 2 km; a cold, high-flux aquifer lowers the observed gradient but not the latent heat. Implement them as LightGBM monotone constraints and a semantic-loss term. LLM agents can propose new rules from the literature. The hindcast keeps a rule only if it raises held-out skill.
- **Use graph similarity to find analogues.** Embed each candidate's subgraph (fault geometry, host rock, chemistry, heat) and retrieve the nearest proven systems. Their sizes feed the size marks (M33) as an empirical prior.
- **Flag contradictions in the graph as VOI targets.** A warm well beside a "cold" TG hole, or a geothermometer that disagrees with a BHT, is cheap information value. The graph makes these clashes queryable.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No geothermal knowledge graph exists.** Minerals has MinMod and DDE. Geothermal has flat NGDS tables and PDFs. An LLM extraction pipeline (L21) that fills a typed graph with provenance would be a data moat that Zanskar's raster stack does not have.
2. **No formal mineral-systems model for blind hydrothermal systems.** PFA used the idea but hand-weighted it. A fitted, factor-by-factor model with separate heat, pathway, fluid and cap fields is new.
3. **No causal-discovery study in geothermal.** There is one causal prospectivity abstract in minerals. An honest DAG plus invariance test on the USGS labels is a publishable first and a real edge in transfer to new states.
4. **No ontology for negative evidence.** Dry holes, cold wells and failed TG holes are scattered and untyped. A graph that records "searched here, by this method, found nothing" is exactly what the Cox thinning needs.
5. **No link from geology to rights.** No public graph joins systems and wells to BLM lease status, state leases and private surface owners. Joining them turns a favourability map into a buy list.
6. **Agents can draft the conceptual model.** Multi-agent LLMs (L22) could write a mineral-systems style critical-process sheet for each candidate from the graph. A fitted model then scores it. That makes expert-quality reasoning cheap at the scale of thousands of parcels.
