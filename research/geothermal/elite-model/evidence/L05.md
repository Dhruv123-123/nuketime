# L05: Zanskar's technical approach, what it is and is not

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Builds on `model/evidence/M13-zanskar-methods.md` and `M32-competitors-tech.md`. It does not repeat them. New here: the real label counts inside GeoCore (6,009 positives against 148 negatives), its only skill number (94.5% of hot springs in the top half), the Big Blind and Pumpernickel well facts, the May 2026 Utah slide numbers, "trained on simulations", and what two 2026 job posts say about the next model. ## What the field knows

Zanskar's public record is thin on metrics and rich on hints. Five primary items carry it.

1. **GeoCore paper (Stanford SGW 2025).** Grujic, Hossler, Lipscomb, Morrison and Smith, all Zanskar. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2025/Grujic.pdf . It is a framework paper: YAML configs, Snowflake feature store, MLflow tracking. The case study is LightGBM on the INGENIOUS Great Basin stack, on an H3 resolution-8 grid (679,826 cells, ~0.53 km edge).
   - Labels: 6,009 "Hot or Warm" wells at 10 m or deeper are positives. Only 148 "Cold" wells at 400 m or deeper are negatives. That is a 40:1 imbalance, and the negatives are rare deep holes.
   - Validation: spatial block CV. 15 km clusters merged into 5 folds. Test set: 309 hot springs at 40 °C or hotter.
   - Features: 12 sets in 8 groups. Geodetic strain, earthquake density, gravity and magnetic gradients, layered MT conductance, distance to Quaternary volcanics, fault recency, fault slip rate.
   - SHAP top drivers: lower-crust and mantle conductance, fault slip rate, strain, quake density.
   - The authors say defining "negative favorability" is hard. They suggest other labelling or more negatives.
2. **Discoveries base-rate paper (SGW 2025).** Edwards et al. 370 sites, 112 blind or hidden, ~27,000 samples, 1 blind system per ~550 holes. See M13 F1.
3. **Surrogate paper (SGW 2026).** Liang, Yu, Thibaut, Zheng, Hoiland, Pollack. FNO and U-FNO trained on 1,200 fault-scenario simulations, ~10,000x speedup. No UQ was actually run. See M13 F3.
4. **Discovery releases.**
   - Lightning Dock, NM: bought from Cyrq in 2024. A physics simulator found a deeper zone below the shallow one the old owners used. ~15 MW plant on one well.
   - Pumpernickel, NV (Sept 2025): ~137 °C at ~762 m. Two wells, with total-loss and artesian zones. Damage zones over 18 m thick. Phase 1 is 20 MWe from heat in place under 2 km. It was a previously abandoned prospect, not greenfield blind. https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/zanskar-validates-ai-based-discovery-method-with-drilling-at-pumpernickel-geothermal-site-nevada/
   - Big Blind, NV (Dec 2025): ~121 °C at ~823 m. No surface features, no prior wells. Two intermediate wells drilled in July and August 2025 on a federal lease. Called "the first blind system confirmed as commercial in 30+ years". Capacity is not confirmed; "similar systems" could exceed 100 MWe. https://zanskargeothermal.substack.com/p/zanskar-reveals-big-blind-the-discovery
5. **Talks, slides and interviews.**
   - MIT Technology Review (Dec 2025): models are "trained on known hot spots and on simulations Zanskar creates". A crew then drills shallow holes over a ~100 sq mi candidate area. Edwards: "dozens of sites that look just like this". https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/12/04/1128763/ai-geothermal-zanskar
   - ASME (May 2026): Hoiland says "the trick lies in how you manage the spatial aspects and biases of the datasets". BEL is named again. https://magazine.asme.org/issues/may-2026/news-hidden-geothermal-goldmines
   - Utah Legislature slide (20 May 2026): over 250,000 acres under control, over 300,000 ft drilled, over 100 projects, six states, Utah second by acreage. Claims "10x faster, 10x cheaper, 2x more discoveries" than the industry over 30 years. Which multiplier applies to which metric is unclear. https://le.utah.gov/interim/2026/pdf/00002457.pdf
   - Volts podcast: "missed way more than we've hit". See M13.
6. **Earlier lineage.** Connor Smith's UNR thesis work with Faulds on ML for the Nevada Play Fairway, published as a USGS report (pubs.usgs.gov/publication/70241433). It used ~83 positive and ~62 negative training sites and compared BNNs, XGBoost and others (counts from memory, unverified). GeoCore is its industrial successor.

**Patents.** Two searches found no granted patent or published application with Zanskar as assignee. But a 2026 job post asks for "patentable workflows and tools that our team will deploy in the field". Filings may sit in the 18-month pre-publication window (unverified).

## What their model is, and is not

**It is:**
- (a) A regional LightGBM favorability classifier on commodity public layers.
- (b) Base-rate economics from a private 27,000-sample compilation.
- (c) Site-scale stochastic fault-scenario simulation, ranked by temperature times permeability, moving to neural emulators and BEL.
- (d) A fast field loop: shallow TG holes, then two intermediate wells per prospect.
- (e) Simulations used as training data, so physics fills in where labels are thin.

**It is not, in public:**
- A calibrated regional probability. LightGBM scores are ranks, not P(system).
- An observation or effort model. Unsampled cells and cold cells blur together.
- A positive-unlabelled framing. 148 negatives is a label famine.
- A blind-only test.
- A size distribution.
- A geochemistry or hydrologic-masking layer.
- A private or state land focus. All three named wins sit on federal land or on a bought plant.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | Reported skill | Note |
|---|---|---|
| GeoCore LightGBM, INGENIOUS, block CV | 94.5% of 309 hot springs fall in the top 50% of favorability | Weak bar. A random map scores 50%. No AUC or top-1% capture is given. Folds 1 and 5 are worse. |
| Base-rate model | 0.181% blind hits per historic hole | A floor, because it comes from untargeted holes. |
| FNO surrogate | Relative error under 0.025 in-scenario, ~0.07 on unseen fault scenarios, ~10,000x speedup | One anonymised site. |
| Stochastic scenario ranking, Pumpernickel | 2 of 2 wells hit ~137 °C with losses | Sparse-data site. |
| Prospect toolkit, Big Blind | 2 of 2 wells hit ~121 °C permeable reservoir at 823 m | No hit rate across all anomalies tested. |
| Company-level | 3 named discoveries in ~3 years, over 300,000 ft drilled | Implies many dry or cool TG holes. The denominator is not disclosed. |

Back-of-envelope: if 300,000 ft is mostly TG holes of 150-300 m, that is roughly 300-600 holes for 2 blind or greenfield wins. That is about 1 in 150-300, or 2-4x the historic base rate (my estimate, unverified).

## Datasets used

- INGENIOUS Great Basin (Ayling et al. 2023, GDR): features and well-temperature labels.
- USGS Quaternary Fault and Fold Database; slip rate and recency.
- Geodetic strain (GNSS), earthquake catalogues, gravity and magnetic grids.
- MT conductance: INGENIOUS layered conductance, sourced from USMTArray.
- Quaternary volcanic centres.
- Private: ~27,000 temperature samples, legacy O&G, federal, water and mineral wells, own TG holes, LBNL large-N seismic (OSTI 3015294), Lightning Dock production data.
- PAD-US for land filtering.
- H3 grid as the cell unit.

## What this means for our model design

- **Beat the 94.5%-in-top-half bar on a blind-only test.** Report the share of blind systems captured in the top 1% and top 5% of area, plus a reliability diagram. Their published bar is low, so a clear, honest metric is a sales asset in itself.
- **Fix the label famine with censored likelihoods.** Use each of their 6,009-type shallow warm wells and every cold shallow well as a censored observation of the 1D column. Do not threshold wells into classes. This turns tens of thousands of shallow wells into negatives with honest weight, which GeoCore cannot do with 148.
- **Train on simulations too, but make them speak to the regional layer.** Zanskar uses simulations as training data at site scale. We should generate thousands of synthetic Basin-and-Range systems, with surface signatures, masking and BHT bias, to pre-train the regional model and test whether it can find blind cases.
- **Copy their two-well proving recipe as our "prove" stage.** Both wins were two intermediate wells (762-823 m) after TG screening. Cost and price our heat rights at exactly that decision gate.
- **Compete where their footprint is not.** Their acreage is BLM-heavy across six states. Put our VOI spend on private and state cells in their own "two-thirds of faults unsampled" set.
- **Add a structure-permeability head.** Their hiring shows permeability is the open problem. Use slip tendency from stress maps, fault intersections, InSAR and LiDAR scarp freshness as a separate permeability likelihood. Never just multiply it into temperature.
- **Read their discoveries as labels.** Big Blind, Pumpernickel and the BLM lease bids are public footprints. Add Zanskar lease nominations as a "competitor belief" layer and an informative prior (M-series lease data).

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **A calibrated, effort-aware regional probability.** Nobody publishes P(blind system | data, search effort). The log-Gaussian Cox process with thinning in SENTINEL-COX fills it.
2. **Blind-only benchmark.** There is no public blind test set. An LLM pass over OSTI, GRC and NBMG reports could rebuild the ~112 blind or hidden sites with their discovery dates. That gives a time-split test: train on data before 1990, predict discoveries after 1990.
3. **Negative mining from archives.** AI extraction of cold logs, mining holes and water-well driller logs could lift negatives from 148 to over 10,000.
4. **Geochemistry and masking.** Absent from every Zanskar paper and posting except one "geochemical inputs" line. Geothermometers plus outflow modelling are cheap lift.
5. **The denominator.** Zanskar never states hits per hole drilled. If we publish ours, we set the standard buyers ask for.
6. **Regional emulators.** Their FNO work is site-scale. A regional neural operator mapping heat flow, faults and recharge to the shallow temperature field would let us invert the whole Great Basin at once.
