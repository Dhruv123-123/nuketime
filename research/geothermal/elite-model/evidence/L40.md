## What the field knows

**1. Most failures are "hot but tight" or "hot in the wrong place", not "no heat".** In US Great Basin and Cascades exploration, heat is common. Open, connected fractures are rare. Ormat's back-solve of 40 fields gives recovery factors of 4-53%, and well productivity spans 0.5 to over 100 kg/s/bar (Libbey and Murphy, SGW 2026; M30). Permeability was the weakest leg in 9 of 11 DOE Play Fairway projects (M03). Classic hot-but-tight examples: Desert Peak 27-15 and Coso 46A-19RD, both later used as EGS stimulation wells; Utah FORGE 58-32 (about 197 C at 2.3 km in granite with almost no flow); the Medicine Lake Fourmile Hill and Telephone Flat wells (hot, poor flow) (all unverified in detail).

**2. Faults that carry fluid are the ones critically stressed today.** Barton, Zoback and Moos (1995, Geology 23:683) showed that flowing fractures in deep wells are those near failure in the current stress field. At Dixie Valley, productive and non-productive wells on the same range-front fault differ by local stress orientation and magnitude, not by fault presence (Hickman, Barton and Zoback 1998; Barton et al. 1998) (unverified well IDs). Siler, Hinz and Faulds (2018, GSA Bulletin) used boundary-element models to show that stress concentrates at fault tips, step-overs and intersections. Faulds and Hinz count over 1,430 favourable settings; only about 1 in 4-5 is known to be hot (M11).

**3. Faults seal themselves fast.** Facca and Tonani (1967, Bull. Volcanol.) named the "self-sealing" field: silica and calcite precipitate where fluids cool or boil and build their own cap. Lowell, Van Cappellen and Germanovich (1993, Science 260:192) modelled silica sealing of upflow fractures in years to decades for mm-scale apertures (unverified exact range). So a fault that has not slipped for a long time is likely sealed, even if it is well oriented. Permeability is a race between slip (opening) and mineral deposition (closing). The same seal is also why many systems are blind (M30).

**4. Cold lids hide systems and fool shallow tests.** On the eastern Snake River Plain, Kimama is near-isothermal (4.5 C/km) to about 950 m, then 72-75 C/km below a smectite seal (Lachmar et al. 2017). The Cascades rain curtain sweeps the top 0.5-1 km; the whole US arc vents only about 240 MW through hot springs (Ingebritsen and Mariner 2010). A 2 m probe or a 100 m well over a lid reads "nothing". That is a non-detection, not a negative.

**5. Outflow plumes pull drill targets the wrong way.** Hot water rises on a fault, then runs laterally along a permeable layer for kilometres. Wells drilled on the warm shallow anomaly hit the plume, then see a temperature reversal and cool rock below. Long Valley had reversals in almost every pre-1988 thermal well (Sorey et al. 1991). At Granite Springs, gradients over 220 C/km did not locate the upflow; at McGinness Hills Campbell, the 120-130 C basin-fill aquifer is outflow over a conductive deep section (Kraal 2025) (M03, M18). Cumming (2009, SGW; 2016, in *Geothermal Power Generation*) shows that MT clay caps dome over the upflow and thin along the outflow; drilling on the top of a flat cap or on hot springs is the textbook mistake.

**6. Fossil systems and wrong conceptual models.** Sinter, alteration and hyperspectral anomalies can mark extinct systems. Sanyal and Morrow (2012, SGW) found success rates move in steps when the conceptual model is revised, not with drilling practice. Argenta (INGENIOUS's top-scored site, 10 TG holes) came back cool (M02).

**7. Headline success rates hide the failure mode.** IFC (2013, "Success of Geothermal Wells: A Global Study", about 2,500 wells in 52 fields) gives about 50% for first exploration wells and higher (roughly 70-80%) for development wells (unverified exact values). Wall and Dobson (2016) say under one-third of western US projects reached full development (M29). None of these datasets tags *why* a hole failed.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | What it handles | Reported result |
|---|---|---|
| Critically stressed fracture analysis (Barton 1995; Hickman 1998) | Open vs sealed faults | Flowing fractures cluster at Coulomb ratio near 0.6 in image logs; productive vs dry wells separated at Dixie Valley (unverified quantitative split) |
| Slip/dilation tendency plus structural setting (Faulds; Siler 2018) | Permeability prior | Step-overs and terminations host most Great Basin systems; displacement transfer zones about 4.8x, pull-aparts about 2.2x enrichment (M11) |
| Weights of evidence with water-table depth as "degree of exploration" (Coolbaugh et al. 2006/2007) | Masking as detection bias | First regional map to treat deep water tables as reducing detection (L02) |
| MT clay-cap geometry (Cumming 2009; Ussher et al. 2000 WGC) | Upflow vs outflow; seal | Cap base tracks about 200-240 C isotherm in volcanic systems; dome shape points to upflow (unverified generality outside volcanic hosts) |
| Temperature-profile shape (Bredehoeft and Papadopulos 1965) | Lid, reversal, upflow | Detects vertical flux down to about 1.5 cm/yr (M23, calc) |
| He R/Ra in fluids | Open deep-rooted faults | Elevated R/Ra marks mantle-connected permeable faults in the Great Basin (M22) |
| Libbey and Murphy permeability classes (SGW 2026) | Flow per well | Four classes; top fields >60 kg/s/bar, bottom ≤1 |

No published model jointly predicts heat, permeability and mask, and none is scored on failure mode.

## What this means for our model design

- **Predict failure modes, not "success".** Replace the single label with a competing-risks ladder per cell: no heat, heat but tight, outflow only (reversal), masked/unseen, fossil, success. Each mode gets its own likelihood. This turns every dry hole into a useful label.
- **Two-hurdle core.** P(economic) = P(heat at depth) x P(open permeability | heat, structure, stress, age of slip). Fit them as separate latent fields that share structure covariates.
- **Add a sealing clock.** Model P(open) as a hazard: it decays with time since last slip and with fluid silica/calcite saturation, and resets with recent seismicity, Quaternary offset or high slip tendency under a *sampled* stress tensor. Recent slip proxies: Qfault age class, UNR microseismicity, InSAR strain.
- **Explicit outflow geometry.** For every warm observation, the upflow location is a latent variable placed up hydraulic gradient along mapped structure, with plume length L from M23. Reversal profiles and flat MT caps raise "outflow here, upflow elsewhere" and move value to the upgradient cell, which is the parcel to buy.
- **Mask-aware detection.** Keep SENTINEL-COX's shared mask class, but give it physical priors: water-table depth, recharge, aquifer type and smectite-cap depth. A blank shallow test under a lid should barely move the posterior.
- **Fossil flag.** Penalise alteration-only and sinter-only evidence unless there is a live signal (warm water, He, seismicity). Dated sinter or travertine, where it exists, feeds the age term.
- **VOI per hurdle.** Report which hurdle dominates each cell's variance. Heat-limited cells get gradient holes. Permeability-limited cells get pressure/earth-tide loggers, He sampling or a 3C geophone. Lid cells get MT or deep logs.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No failure-mode label set.** Thousands of dry or tight holes sit in BLM, NBMG, DOGAMI, Utah DOGM and OSTI reports as scans. An LLM-plus-vision pipeline can read completion reports and tag each hole: max T, reversal, lost circulation, flow test, reason abandoned. This would be the first public "why it failed" dataset.
2. **Lost circulation as free permeability data.** Mud logs and driller notes from oil, mining and water wells record loss zones and depths. Nobody has mapped them regionally as fracture-permeability labels.
3. **Sealing-rate calibration.** No regional model ties fault slip age to measured permeability. We can fit one by joining extracted flow results with Qfault ages and seismicity.
4. **MT cap-shape classification.** Train a classifier on synthetic dome versus tongue clay-cap models, then run it on public MT (EarthScope/USArray and project surveys) to flag outflow-only targets before anyone drills.
5. **Joint mask-and-permeability posterior.** Lid and seal are often the same smectite or silica layer. Modelling them as one latent shared by MT, profile shape and chemistry is new.
6. **Specific capacity at scale.** NDWR yield and drawdown records can be turned into a transmissivity map with AI cleanup. It is a cheap regional permeability prior nobody uses for geothermal.
