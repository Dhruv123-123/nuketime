# L12: Physics-informed ML and neural operators for subsurface heat and flow

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: Fourier neural operators (FNO), DeepONet, PINNs, reduced-basis and statistical surrogates for geothermal and nearby subsurface problems. Focus is speed and accuracy, and what that buys a prospecting model. Anything not checked against the primary source in this session is marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

1. **Neural operators are now a standard surrogate for subsurface flow.** The FNO (Li et al., ICLR 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.08895) learns a map from input fields (permeability, sources) to output fields (pressure, temperature). It is grid-independent, so it can be trained on coarse grids and used on finer ones. DeepONet (Lu et al., Nature Machine Intelligence 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.03193) splits the map into a "branch" net for the inputs and a "trunk" net for query points. Most mature work is in CO2 storage and oil reservoirs, not geothermal.
2. **CO2 storage set the speed records.** U-FNO (Wen et al., Adv. Water Resources 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03697) and nested FNO (Wen et al., Energy Env. Sci. 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.17051) model 3D/4D plume and pressure. Nested FNO is reported "nearly 700,000 times" faster than the reference simulator. Multi-fidelity FNO (Tang, Kong and Morris, https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09113) matches a high-fidelity-only model with 81% less data-generation cost, on 110k and 1M cell models.
3. **Geothermal work arrived in 2024-2026 and is mostly 3D temperature.** Liang et al. (SGW 2026, https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Liang.pdf; authors include Ahinoam Pollack) compare U-Net, FNO, U-FNO and Fourier-MIONet on fault-dominated coupled heat-flow models of an anonymised real site. FNO wins and generalises best. Speedups up to 10,000x.
4. **PINNs work for natural-state inversion when data are sparse.** Ishitsuka et al. (JGR Machine Learning and Computation 2025, doi:10.1029/2025JH000683) build a 3D PINN of the Kakkonda field, Japan. It beats pure data-driven ML on temperature and pressure when wells are few. Adding magnetotelluric (MT) resistivity improves permeability. Transfer learning from pretrained nets helps. An earlier 2D Kakkonda PINN appeared in Applied Energy 2023 (https://repository.kulib.kyoto-u.ac.jp/dspace/bitstream/2433/284028/1/j.apenergy.2023.120855.pdf) (unverified details).
5. **Spectral bias is the known failure.** Ortiz Romero, Hidalgo and De Simone (InterPore 2026, https://events.interpore.org/event/58/contributions/8788/) show FNO cannot resolve sharp thermal fronts and near-well features in advective heat transport. They propose FNO for the global field plus a local DeepONet correction. No numbers were given in the abstract.
6. **Statistical surrogates already do calibrated geothermal UQ.** The Auckland group (O'Sullivan, Maclaren, Nicholson) does Bayesian natural-state calibration with Waiwera/TOUGH2. Krafla (Scott et al., WRR 2022, doi:10.1029/2021WR031254) shows permeability posteriors spanning more than two orders of magnitude even with many wells. Data space inversion (de Beer et al., https://arxiv.org/html/2407.15401v3) skips the parameter posterior and maps prior simulations straight to predictions. In 3D it hit 92-93% coverage of 95% intervals using about 1,400 converged runs. Below 100 samples it failed.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Problem | Speed | Accuracy | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FNO (3D, 64^3, 8 steps) | Fault-controlled heat + flow, geothermal | up to 10,000x vs commercial simulator (30 min-2 h per run) | Relative L2 error < 0.025 in-scenario; ~0.07 on unseen fault scenarios | Liang et al. SGW 2026 |
| FNO in PESTPP-IES loop | CO2 reinjection reactive transport, 1D | 1.009 s to 0.044 s per run (23x); whole inversion only 28-35% faster | R2 0.997-0.9998; final misfit 1-2.7% worse than PFLOTRAN | Wang et al. SGW 2026, https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/db/GeoConf/papers/SGW/2026/Wang1.pdf |
| Nested FNO | Basin-scale 4D CO2 | ~700,000x | High-res; numbers in paper | Wen et al. EES 2023 |
| Multi-fidelity FNO | CO2, 1M cells | 81% less training-data cost | "Comparable" to HF model | Tang et al. 2023 |
| FNO with binary well tensor | Reservoir proxy, variable wells | "orders of magnitude" | 95% of predictions < 5% max-mean relative error | Badawi and Gildin, https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09728 |
| 3D PINN + MT | Kakkonda natural state | Not reported | Better than data-driven ML at few wells (qualitative) | Ishitsuka et al. 2025 |
| Data space inversion | Geothermal natural state + production | ~500-2,000 prior runs, fully parallel | 92-93% coverage of 95% band (3D synthetic) | de Beer et al. 2024-25 |

Key lessons from the numbers. First, the raw per-run speedup (10^4-10^5) is large, but the end-to-end gain can be small if the loop is not rebuilt around the surrogate (Wang: 23x per run, 1.3x per inversion). Second, out-of-distribution error roughly triples (0.025 to 0.07) when the fault geometry changes. Third, FNO gets the smooth thermal field but blurs sharp upflow zones, which are exactly what we hunt.

## Datasets used

- Synthetic ensembles from TOUGH2/TOUGH3, Waiwera, PFLOTRAN, and commercial simulators (Petrasim/CMG-type). Typically 300-2,000 runs per study.
- Liang et al.: 1,200 realisations, four fault scenarios, 64x64x64 grid.
- Wang et al.: 1,100 PFLOTRAN runs, Latin hypercube; four GNS lab column tests (150 C, 7.5 MPa).
- Kakkonda (NEDO/AIST well temperature and MT data, Japan).
- Krafla (Landsvirkjun well data, Iceland).
- CO2 work: Sleipner-like and synthetic basin models; no public geothermal benchmark exists.
- Relevant to us but unused by this literature: INGENIOUS Great Basin layers (GDR), SMU heat-flow database, USGS well temperature logs, Utah FORGE and Brady/Desert Peak natural-state models on GDR, and Liang et al. explicitly anonymised their site.

## What this means for our model design

- **Train a "Basin-and-Range column-plus-fault" neural operator once, as a shared asset.** Inputs: fault geometry and dip, cap thickness, basal heat flow, permeability field, recharge. Output: T(x, z) to 3 km. One 3D FNO on ~5,000 TOUGH3 runs (cheap at our compute budget) gives 10^4x speed and lets every cell in SENTINEL-COX run a full physics forward model, not only the 1D column.
- **Use the surrogate as the simulator inside SBI, not as a map.** Amortised neural posterior estimation over the FNO turns a well's T-log, spring geochemistry and MT section into a posterior on "hidden upflow within 2 km" in milliseconds. This upgrades GHOST-SBI Tier 1 from analytic to full physics without the runtime cost.
- **Build the whole loop around the surrogate.** Wang et al. show the inversion only gained 30% because the rest of the pipeline was not changed. Our EVSI/$ engine should call the surrogate in batch on GPU across all candidate sensors and parcels at once.
- **Make the surrogate honest about its domain.** Add an OOD detector (ensemble disagreement or a learned density on inputs) and fall back to the 1D column likelihood when the cell's structure is unlike the training set. Liang's 3x error jump on unseen faults is the warning.
- **Fix spectral bias at the upflow.** Use the FNO for the regional field and a local DeepONet or U-Net correction in the top 1 km near faults, as Ortiz Romero et al. propose. Score on peak temperature and upflow location, not average L2.
- **Use multi-fidelity training.** Many cheap conductive/analytic runs plus a few hundred full two-phase TOUGH3 runs (Tang et al. saved 81% of data cost). Our Tier 1 analytic simulator becomes the low-fidelity source.
- **Take the PINN idea where data are sparse but not for the West-wide map.** A PINN-style physics loss (steady heat advection-diffusion) as a regulariser on the cell-level GP/Cox intensity is cheap and keeps the temperature field physical between wells. Full per-site PINNs (Kakkonda) are a prospect-stage tool, after we hold the rights.
- **Treat MT as a first-class physics input.** Ishitsuka shows MT improves permeability. Our surrogate should emit predicted resistivity (via a clay-cap petrophysics link) so MT surveys can be scored by EIG.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No public geothermal neural-operator benchmark.** Every paper uses its own private ensemble. We could generate and own a Great Basin hidden-system ensemble (10^4-10^5 TOUGH3 runs on fault/cap/basal heat priors drawn from INGENIOUS and the dated discovery record). That is a data moat Zanskar does not appear to have (their published method is LightGBM on map layers; see M13).
2. **No surrogate is trained on "blind" systems.** Studies model known reservoirs. Nobody models the masking physics: cold-water cap, lateral outflow, deep upflow with no surface sign. An AI agent could design the prior ensemble specifically for blind-system geometries (Big Blind, Pumpernickel style).
3. **No link from surrogate to land value.** No paper pipes a neural-operator posterior into dollars per acre or a lease decision. Our VOI-Bank spine does exactly this.
4. **No amortised inference across thousands of sites.** Calibration is always one field at a time (Krafla, Kakkonda). Amortised NPE over a neural operator could run "natural-state inversion" for every scanned TG log in the West in hours.
5. **No surrogate ingests messy real observations.** Inputs are clean synthetic fields. We can pair it with LLM extraction of scanned logs (Stage 0) and feed censored, noisy readings through the same likelihood.
6. **No geothermal foundation operator.** Pretraining one operator across many basins and fine-tuning per region (transfer learning helped at Kakkonda) is open. Compute is plentiful; this is a plausible 6-12 month build.
7. **Coverage is rarely checked.** Only de Beer et al. report interval coverage. We should report coverage on held-out real wells as a standard.
