# L10: Temperature at depth - Stanford TEM, kriging/GP, GNN and transformer inference, BHT correction, uncertainty

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Builds on `../../evidence/L09-temperature-at-depth.md`, `../../model/evidence/M04-bht-corrections.md` and `M16-temperature-at-depth.md`. Those cover the correction formulas, FORGE and the USGS conductive models. This file adds what is new: the detail inside the LANL transformer, the kriging-with-VOI work in Nevada, simulation-based inversion (EKI, PINNs, reduced-basis surrogates) and how honest each method's uncertainty is. 9 web searches used.

## What the field knows

1. **National temperature maps are now a commodity and are about 5 C good where data are dense.** Stanford's Thermal Earth Model (InterPIGNN) is a physics-informed graph neural network. It trains on about 400k Harrison-corrected BHTs. It reports 4.8 C MAE on 10 km spatial blocks, over 0-7 km and 18 km2 cells (Aljubran and Horne 2024, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.09961 ; data GDR 1592). Its physics is steady conduction with no flow term. Uncertainty is MC dropout only (median 10.3% of T). No data noise is modelled.
2. **A small transformer that reads nearby wells beats it.** LANL's In-Context Earth (ICE; O'Malley et al. 2026, https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.16665) is a 4-layer encoder of about 830k parameters. It takes the 20 nearest observations as tokens and masks the query temperature. It outputs a distribution over 128 ordinal temperature bins. US MAE is 4.7 C. On the same split, universal kriging gets 5.1, LANL's Transparent Earth 5.8, AlphaEarth embeddings 6.0 and the Stanford model 6.4.
3. **Kriging is a strong baseline, but it does not transfer.** With frozen weights and 20 local points, ICE scores 2.2 / 6.2 / 5.4 C in Alberta, Australia and the UK. Kriging scores 9.2 / 28.5 / 8.9 C there. Within the US, kriging is only 0.4 C behind. So most of the "ML lift" at home is about borrowing structure across regions, not beating geostatistics.
4. **Every model gets worse with depth and in the West.** ICE errors are 3.1 / 7.9 / 11.2 C at 0-2 / 2-4 / 4-6 km. Only 3% of readings are deeper than 4 km and over 95% of wells have one reading. ICE "captures only the lower bound" of profiles in the central Basin and Range. The western US has its widest bands. Its MAE rises from 3.9 (random split) to 4.9 C (1 degree spatial blocks).
5. **Uncertainty is rarely calibrated, and data noise is usually ignored.** ICE reports a PIT KS statistic of 2.5% (against 5.9% for a Gaussian head). It gives no coverage figures. The Bavarian Molasse 3D Empirical Bayesian Kriging (EGU26-13093) fused 196 Monte-Carlo-corrected BHTs and 19 logs. It reached 5.6 K leave-one-out error, but only about 87% of points fell inside its 90% band. A Bayesian neural net for Antarctic heat flow (Liu et al. 2026, The Cryosphere 20:1543) found that data (aleatoric) variance is about 9x the model (epistemic) variance: medians of about 650 vs 70 mW2/m4. Stanford models only the epistemic part. So its bands are likely much too narrow.
6. **The BHT error is set by missing metadata, not by the formula.** A single Harrison or Kehle correction is about +/-16 C at 96%. Horner with 3+ runs reaches about +/-2-5 C. Equilibrium logs are under 1 C. Sobol analysis (Schölderle et al. 2022) shows that unknown inputs matter more than the choice of method (M04). Kriging along real well trajectories, with 5.1 million log points from 113 Shunbei wells, gets static temperature within about 5 C (Wang et al. 2025, Processes 13:2303). Dense data beats clever correction.
7. **Geostatistics already links temperature to drilling decisions in Nevada.** Trainor-Guitton et al. (SGW 2023, Granite Springs Valley) ran 50 SGeMS realisations each of cosimulation and of "local varying mean" kriging. Fault distance, fault intersections, Coulomb stress and dilation were the secondary proxies (R2 0.56-0.68). They ranked nine candidate TG holes by prior value (Vprior) and by entropy. Cosimulation made physically impossible temperature inversions. LVM made realistic gradients but noisy fields. The authors call for a method that has both. https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2023/Trainorguitton.pdf
8. **Physics-based inversion of convective systems is now cheap enough to run at scale.** Ensemble Kalman inversion on Waiwera reservoir models needs about 600-800 forward runs. MCMC needs about 10^4. In a synthetic 3D test, 94.7% of true permeabilities fell inside the posterior 95% band (de Beer et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/html/2410.09017v4). PINNs have inverted the natural state at Kakkonda (Japan) from wells plus MT. They beat data-only ML when wells are few (Ishitsuka et al. 2025, AGU, doi:10.1029/2025JH000683; no error numbers seen). Non-intrusive reduced-basis surrogates evaluate in about 2-3 ms, against 8 h for the full simulation, a speed-up of about 10^7 (Degen et al. 2025, GMD 18:9219).

## Best methods and reported skill

| Method | Skill | Uncertainty honesty |
|---|---|---|
| ICE in-context transformer (LANL 2026) | 4.7 C US; 2.2-6.2 C transfer; 11.2 C at 4-6 km | Ordinal-bin distribution; PIT KS 2.5% |
| Universal kriging (same test) | 5.1 C US; 9-28 C transfer | Kriging variance; noise model is Gaussian |
| Stanford InterPIGNN | 4.8 C (own spatial blocks); 6.4 C on ICE split | MC dropout only, 10.3% median |
| 3D EBK + Monte Carlo BHT (Molasse) | 5.6 K LOO | 87% in 90% band |
| Trajectory kriging, dense logs (Shunbei) | within about 5 C, under 5% relative | not reported |
| USGS 3D conductive model, Great Basin (Burns 2024) | fits 379 clean conductive wells | removes convective wells by design |
| Sui, Shen and Boyd 2025 MCMC crustal geotherms | posterior per column | full Bayesian, conductive only |
| EKI on Waiwera models | 94.7% coverage (synthetic 3D) | ensemble posterior, Gaussian noise |

## Datasets used

- SMU/NGDS BHT and equilibrium compilations (GDR 1704; 30,000+ BHT sites, 14,000+ equilibrium sites); Stanford's 400,134 BHTs and outputs (GDR 1592).
- Alberta AER/AGS well temperatures, OZTemp (Geoscience Australia), UKOGL (all used by ICE); ETOPO1 elevation.
- IHFC 2024 heat flow (doi 10.5880/fidgeo.2024.014); NGHF 2019.
- USGS Great Basin 3D temperature (doi 10.5066/P149FR54); geothermometer depth predictions (doi 10.5066/P14B8XOH).
- Utah FORGE logs (GDR 1012) as a shallow-to-deep test bed. Granite Springs Valley 2 m and TG data (Nevada play fairway).
- Kansas multi-BHT wells (6,950), North Dakota static re-logs: paired data for learning BHT bias (M04).

## What this means for our model design

- **Use an ICE-style in-context transformer as the column prior, not a fixed map.** Retrain it with typed tokens: reading type, raw BHT plus shut-in time, mud and era, logging company, extraction confidence and location error. Then the bias is learned, not pre-corrected with Harrison. Pre-train on Kansas, Texas, Alberta, Australia and the UK. Adapt to the Great Basin with 20-40 local tokens. The ICE transfer results say this works with no retraining.
- **Keep the ordinal-bin output.** A binned distribution handles one-sided BHTs (censored above), boiling-curve caps and multimodal "conductive or upflow" answers. It also gives P(T(z) > economic line) directly for the VOI-Bank. A Gaussian head cannot.
- **Split the target into a conductive part plus a convective residual.** Take the conductive mean from Burns 2024 or Sui 2025, with ICE as a check. Model the residual with a latent flow-regime class: conductive, upflow, outflow tongue or cold lid. The national models remove convective wells, so their residuals are clean labels for the class.
- **Train an amortised column inverter on simulations.** Run 10^5-10^6 cheap 2D Waiwera/TOUGH/reduced-basis runs of faults, upflows and outflow plumes. Train neural posterior estimation to map a shallow profile plus context to P(T at 0.5-3 km). Use EKI (about 700 runs per site) only on optioned parcels. This is the "conductive or not" step that no public model has.
- **Make the kernel follow the faults.** The Granite Springs result shows that fault-proxy trends help, but that a free cosimulation breaks physics. Use a physics-mean (LVM-style) GP or transformer, with anisotropy along the mapped fault dip. Add hard monotonic or boiling-curve limits.
- **Model data noise, and gate on calibration.** Use per-source heteroscedastic, skewed and outlier-mixture likelihoods (M04 quality ladder). Validate on 10 km spatial blocks and on a Basin and Range-only hold-out. Require 85-95% coverage of 90% bands and PIT KS under 3%. Publish error by depth band and region, because the 4-6 km and western errors are 2-3x the headline.
- **Rank measurements by entropy times value.** Combine Trainor-Guitton's entropy and Vprior with our RNPV. Pick re-logs, conductivity samples and darts where the bin distribution straddles the economic line.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No continental model is convection-aware.** All of them are conductive or treat flow as noise. A temperature transformer with a flow-regime head, trained on simulated plus labelled systems, would be first.
2. **No published Great Basin skill test.** Stanford, ICE and USGS give no regional error for blind-system terrain. We can build the benchmark (FORGE, Blue Mountain, McGinness, Patua, the play fairway sites) and own it.
3. **Raw BHT metadata is thrown away.** Every map ingests already-corrected numbers. LLM extraction of header fields (circulation time, mud, run number) from scanned logs feeds a learned bias model.
4. **Profile shape is unused.** ICE uses points. Nobody tokenises full T(z) curves from scanned NBMG, CalGEM and SMU logs. Curvature (Péclet number) and inversions are the strongest upflow and outflow signals (M16).
5. **No joint model of geothermometers and temperature.** USGS used geothermometers only to back out depth. Adding chemistry tokens to the in-context model gives deep-temperature anchors where no wells exist.
6. **No calibrated aleatoric bands on national maps.** The Antarctic BNN shows that data noise dominates. A properly calibrated map is itself a sellable product, and it is an honest P90 for buyers.
