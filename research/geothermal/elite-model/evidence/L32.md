## What the field knows

1. **Well success, not project success, is what gets published.** IFC (2013): 2,613 wells, 57 fields, 14 countries. A "successful" well ends at 3 MWe or more. 78% overall. 50% for the first well in a field, 59% for the first five, 74% in development, 83% in operations. Sanyal and Morrow (2012): 2,528 wells, 52 fields, mean 68%, roughly normal across fields. The rate is wild for the first wells and settles only after about 40 wells. Both confirmed this run in Lowry et al. 2017, pp. 33-34 (https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/1394062).
2. **Project success is much lower.** Wall and Dobson (2016, SGW 41) say there is "no common definition" of exploration success. They find western US exploration led to full-size development wells under one-third of the time (as quoted in GeoVision 2019, p. 37). The IFC set is survivorship-biased: only fields that got built are in it.
3. **Steps in success track the conceptual model, not drilling skill.** Sanyal and Morrow found no smooth learning curve in MW per well. Capacity jumps when the conceptual model is revised. Knowing where to drill is the lever.
4. **Drilling cost curves are public and parametric.** Lowry et al. 2017 (Sandia, the curves inside GETEM): baseline large-diameter wells of about $4.5M (31.6 days) and $16.4M (73.5 days) at the two reference depths (about 2.5 and 5 km; the table captions conflict). Doubling rate of penetration saves only 10-14%. Large diameter costs 27-50% more than small. The tenth well should cost no more than 75% of the first (Lukawski et al. 2014). GETEM rig day rates: $28,500-38,400. A 2022 GETEM drilling-curve update exists (OSTI 1983898).
5. **The national cost models hard-code success rates as constants.** ATB 2024 (fetched; https://atb.nrel.gov/electricity/2024/geothermal) runs GETEM with 5 / 3 / 2 successful full-size exploration wells (conservative / moderate / advanced). Exploration drilling success is 76 / 80 / 95% for near-field EGS and 52 / 60 / 95% for deep EGS. Development success is 76 / 80 / 95%. Hydrothermal binary capex is $9,483/kW for identified sites and $7,425/kW for undiscovered sites (the identified range runs to an odd $38,108). Flash is $4,350 and $3,738/kW. Undiscovered sites get costs averaged from nearby identified ones. Nothing in the ATB varies success with data quality.
6. **GETEM's exploration block is a short fixed recipe** (unverified, from memory of the SAM inputs). It has a non-drilling exploration cost of about $0.75M, about 2 exploration wells at about 0.5x production-well cost, about 2 confirmation wells at about 1.2x, and a drilling success rate of about 75%. GETEM now lives inside SAM (OSTI 1922402; https://sam.nlr.gov/geothermal.html).
7. **Costs of a funnel stage.** ESMAP 2012: a 3-5 well test campaign costs $11-30M, up to 15% of capex, at peak risk. TG holes cost $16.50/ft median drilling only (OpenEI), and about $125k all-in in the Nevada Play Fairway (Faulds 2021, OSTI 1724080). Fervo cut Cape well time about 75% and cost per foot about 70% over 2022-2025 (repo L21).
8. **Better exploration buys cost cuts.** The GeoVision Exploration Task Force (Doughty, Dobson et al. 2018, LBNL) modelled how better exploration lowers the cost of finding hidden systems (unverified detail). GeoVision assumes the undiscovered 30 GWe is reached through "improved exploration", without a number per method.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | Result | Note |
|---|---|---|
| IFC learning curve by well order | 50 → 59 → 74 → 83% | Per well, survivors only |
| Sanyal-Morrow field distribution | Mean 68%, stable after about 40 wells | Gives a between-field variance prior |
| Wall-Dobson decision-point outcomes | Under 33% project success | The only US project-level number |
| ATB/GETEM fixed rates | 60-95% exploration success | Scenario knobs, not estimates |
| Lowry well-cost model | ±50% cost variance; ROP x2 = -10 to -14% | Materials-dominated |
| DOE Industry-Coupled Program (1977+) | 8 of 14 sites producing by 1995 (57%) | Surface-expressed sites (OSTI 860716) |
| Petroleum risking (Otis and Schneidermann 1997; Milkov 2015) | Portfolio success counts match predictions within about 5 years | Shows calibration is reachable once outcomes are logged (L17) |
| Oil exploration benchmark | About 33% (Tordo et al. 2010) | Matches Wall-Dobson |

No published geothermal study reports success by data package (MT, TG holes, 2 m, geochemistry). No public model has been scored on calibrated chance of success before drilling.

## What this means for our model design

- **Make the target a ladder, priced by stage.** Predict P(reach rung k) for desk anomaly, shallow anomaly, 85 °C+ convective, slimhole hot, productive well (3 MWe+), bankable. IFC gives the upper rungs and Wall-Dobson the project rung. Our model fills the lower rungs.
- **Replace GETEM's constant success rate with our posterior.** Fork the open GETEM/SAM code. Feed it P(success | cell, data so far) and a flow class in place of the fixed 75-80%. RNPV/acre in SENTINEL-COX then moves with evidence, which ATB cannot do.
- **Use the IFC and Sanyal shape as a hierarchical prior.** Model field-level success as a Beta with a mean of about 0.68 and a between-field spread fitted to Sanyal. Add a well-order learning term from IFC. Then shrink toward a "blind, pre-discovery" offset of 0.2-0.35 from Wall-Dobson.
- **Treat the conceptual model as a state that jumps.** Sanyal shows success moves in steps when the conceptual model changes. Model this as a regime switch updated by each new hole, not as a smooth learning curve.
- **Carry cost uncertainty, not a point cost.** Use Lowry curves with ±50% lognormal noise, a depth-by-diameter term and a Lukawski well-order discount. Add a Fervo-style learning trend. VOI and the stopping rung both depend on this spread.
- **Score the model on cost per confirmed MW.** The headline metric should be $ spent per bankable MW over a simulated portfolio, not AUC. At a 20% desk prior a 3-hole TG screen cut cost per discovery from about $30M to $12-16M (M29 arithmetic). Report that number with confidence bands.
- **Log every hole as an actuarial row.** Record the data before drilling, the predicted probability, the outcome and the cost. This is what ESMAP says the market lacks.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **A US project-outcome table.** Build it with LLM agents from BLM lease case files (MLRS), Nevada and Utah well permits, GRC and Stanford papers, SEC filings and trade news. Every prospect gets dated rungs and an end state (built, relinquished, sold). That gives true denominators and fixes survivorship bias.
2. **Success by data package.** With that table, fit how much MT, TG holes, geochemistry and mining-hole temperatures each raise P(success). Nobody has published this.
3. **Dry-hole and "sub-commercial" mining.** Relinquished leases with a drilled well are failures hidden in public records. AI extraction of completion reports and lease relinquishments turns them into negative labels.
4. **Calibrated chance of success.** No geothermal player publishes predicted vs actual. A public calibration record (in the Otis-Schneidermann style) is a sales asset for a rights bank.
5. **Dynamic GETEM.** No one couples a learned subsurface posterior to GETEM in a loop. An agent can run thousands of GETEM/SAM cases per cell across temperature, depth, flow and cost draws, and return a full RNPV distribution.
6. **Cost-curve refresh from filings.** Agents can harvest well days, depths and costs from Fervo, Ormat, Cyrq and state permit files each quarter. That would keep the Lowry curves current without waiting for a lab update.
