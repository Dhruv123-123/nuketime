# L31: What signal found each blind geothermal discovery

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Method note: the shared web-search budget was used up before this agent started, so no new searches ran. US facts come from primary sources already checked in `model/evidence/M18`, `evidence/L02`, `A05` and `Z02`. Kenya and Indonesia items are from memory and marked (unverified). This file adds to M18 a **signal ladder** (first, decisive and confirming signal), per-signal counts, the volcanic-arc pattern, and design consequences.

## What the field knows

**1. The first signal was almost never a geothermal survey.** Faulds et al. (2019, DOE DE-EE0006731) state that most known Great Basin blind systems were found by regional gradient drilling (Desert Peak) "or by accident" in farm wells (Stillwater) or mineral holes (McGinness Hills, Tungsten Mountain, Blue Mountain). Benoit (1982) calls Desert Peak, found by Phillips' 53 shallow gradient holes in 1973-76, "the first blind geothermal discovery in the Basin and Range".

**2. The signal ladder per case** (first signal / decisive signal that justified a lease or well / confirming tool):

**3. Counts from the ladder (calc, 11 US blind cases).**
- First signal was a hole drilled for something else (mineral, farm, oil-company gradient, O&G): **8 of 11**. A map or model came first in 3 (Gabbs, Granite Springs, Big Blind), all after 2015.
- A temperature in a borehole was the decisive signal in **10 of 11**. Geophysics alone was decisive in **0**. Geophysics (magnetic low, HTEM clay, gravity, MT) helped place wells in about 4.
- A 2 m or 1 m probe survey, where run, lit up an anomaly in **5 of 5** (Tungsten, Don Campbell, Lightning Dock, Gabbs, Granite Springs). Survivorship caveat: failed 2 m surveys are rarely written up.
- Water geothermometry gave a decisive reservoir estimate in at least 2 (McGinness, Tungsten).
- Lag from first hot hole to plant: about 10 to 65 years (M18).

**4. Outflow first, upflow later.** In many cases the first hole hit a warm outflow plume, not the upflow. At Don A. Campbell the 120-130 °C aquifer is outflow, and deep volcanics show only a conductive gradient (Kraal 2025). At Granite Springs a hot gradient has not yet found the upflow. So "hot shallow hole" says a system exists, but it points to the wrong place by up to several km.

**5. Volcanic arcs and rifts: the hidden part is the upflow, not the system (unverified).**
- *Indonesia.* Most fields have some surface sign. Many are "outflow-dominated" in steep terrain. Neutral chloride springs can sit 5-15 km downhill from the upflow. Fields such as Muara Laboh and Rantau Dedap were sited on the base of a low-resistivity smectite clay cap mapped by MT/TEM, plus fumarole gas geothermometry. This is the Cumming (2009, Stanford Geothermal Workshop) "resource conceptual model" method: the clay-cap shape, gas chemistry and spring chemistry vote on where the upflow is (unverified).
- *Kenya.* Olkaria Domes and the deeper Olkaria NE/East sectors were extended from MT/TEM resistivity lows and soil CO2/radon, next to expressed vents. Menengai caldera had only weak fumaroles. GDC sited its first wells there in 2011 from MT, gravity and gas surveys and found high-enthalpy steam (unverified). These are "concealed" more than truly blind.
- *Takeaway.* In arcs and rifts the winning signal is **MT clay-cap shape plus gas chemistry**. In the Basin and Range it is **a temperature in a borehole**. One model needs both channels.

**6. Discovery rates.** IFC (2013, via L13): the first well in a field succeeds about 50% of the time. Wall & Dobson (2016): project-level exploration success is under one third. Coolbaugh et al. (2006): Nevada holds about 157 undiscovered systems at ≥100 °C, 3.2 times the 69 known. Faulds and Hinz (2015): 39% of known Great Basin systems are blind; Coolbaugh (2007): about 75% of all are blind.

## Best methods and their reported results

- **Accidental-hole mining.** Four mineral-hole leads became about 265 MW (calc: McGinness, Blue Mountain, Don A. Campbell, Tungsten Mountain).
- **Regional TG grids at 90-300 m.** Desert Peak, Salt Wells, Lightning Dock.
- **2 m probes.** 5 of 5 confirmations; anomalies +12 to +19 °C over 6 km² or more.
- **Play fairway (Faulds 2019).** 10 layers, tested on 34 benchmark systems ≥130 °C. About 375 favourable settings over 12% of the area. Phase 3 drilled 2 sites: Gabbs hit 124 °C at 152 m; Granite Springs 96 °C at 250 m, with the upflow still unlocated.
- **ML heat-flow model (Zanskar).** Public record of 3 drilled sites and 3 announced discoveries. No published dry-hole count, so the true hit rate is unknown (Z02).
- **MT clay-cap conceptual models (arcs).** Industry standard in Indonesia, Kenya, the Philippines and NZ. No public pooled hit rate (unverified).

## Datasets used in the case histories

- NBMG grey literature: Phillips, Anadarko and AMAX TG reports (https://data.nbmg.unr.edu/public/Geothermal/GreyLiterature/).
- Mining drill logs and company reports (Anaconda, FMC, Newcrest, Nassau), mostly scanned.
- State water-well logs (NV State Engineer, NM OSE). Faulds found only ~10% of NV records carry temperature.
- Faulds well database: 47,263 records, only 16-17% with both depth and temperature.
- SMU heat-flow database; USGS NGDS and GDR play-fairway submissions (Gabbs, Granite Springs #969).
- 2 m probe surveys (NBMG, OpenEI wiki pages).
- Airborne magnetics, gravity, HTEM (Don A. Campbell), MT (Gabbs, Kenya, Indonesia).
- Nevada Division of Minerals geothermal drilling permits (early warning of others' TG holes).
- Water and gas chemistry; GDC (Kenya) and ESDM (Indonesia) data are state-held.

## What this means for our model design

- **Make "borehole temperature from a non-geothermal hole" the top-weight channel.** It was the first signal in 8 of 11 cases and decisive in 10 of 11. AI extraction of scanned mining, water and O&G logs feeds it directly.
- **Model outflow and upflow as two linked latent fields.** A hot shallow hole should raise the system-exists probability over a wide footprint, and push the upflow location up-gradient along mapped faults and the hydraulic gradient. Do not score the hot hole's own cell as the target.
- **Give each source a depth-of-sight term.** Accidental holes mostly see the top 300 m. A cell with no shallow anomaly but no deep data is "unseen", not "cold". This is the Big Blind and Pumpernickel class.
- **Back-test on the ladder.** For each of the 11 cases, freeze data at the year before the first signal and again before the decisive signal. Score the rank of the true cell. Desert Peak 1973, McGinness 1982, Don Campbell 1990, Gabbs 2015, Big Blind 2023 are a ready time-split set.
- **Add a volcanic-arc head.** For Kenya, Indonesia and similar terrain, include an MT clay-cap geometry channel and a gas-geothermometry channel. Do not reuse Basin-and-Range weights there.
- **Score the land lag.** A cell with a public hot-hole record and no geothermal lease is the McGinness pattern (25 years idle, then a 160 MW field). Rank these first for nomination.
- **Plan confirmation with 2 m probes and TG holes, not more geophysics.** Every case was confirmed by direct temperature. The VOI engine should price a 2 m array before MT in Basin-and-Range cells.
- **Predict existence and size separately.** 88 °C first holes led to both ~30 MW and ~160 MW fields. Size tracks reservoir temperature and structural setting.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No structured discovery-sequence database.** No one has coded, for hundreds of fields, the order of signals, their values and the outcome. An LLM pass over GRC Transactions, Stanford Workshop and WGC proceedings (tens of thousands of papers) could build it. It would yield real per-signal likelihood ratios instead of the 11-case counts above.
2. **No negative case histories.** Hot mining holes that led to nothing are unwritten. Mining-hole archives plus later lease and drilling records can rebuild the failures, which is the missing denominator.
3. **No automated "hot hole with no lease" alert.** Join extracted hole temperatures to BLM LR2000/MLRS lease status and state permits each week.
4. **No outflow-to-upflow inversion at scale.** Cases show the offset, but no one inverts it from public hydraulic-head, chemistry and fault data. A physics-informed model could.
5. **No pooled MT clay-cap hit rate for arcs.** Operator reports in Kenya and Indonesia could be mined to calibrate it (unverified availability).
