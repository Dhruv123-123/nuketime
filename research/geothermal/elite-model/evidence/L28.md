# L28: Simulation-based inference and generative priors in geoscience

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: neural posterior estimation (NPE), amortised Bayesian inference, diffusion and flow models as priors, and generative subsurface models. What they offer a West-wide hidden-geothermal prospector.

## What the field knows

1. **SBI is mature as a toolkit.** Cranmer, Brehmer and Louppe set out the frame (PNAS 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.01429). You need only a simulator you can sample. A neural net learns p(theta | x) from simulated pairs. Key families are NPE (Papamakarios and Murray 2016; APT/SNPE-C, Greenberg et al. 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.07488), neural ratio estimation (NRE) and neural likelihood estimation. Open code: `sbi` (Tejero-Cantero et al. JOSS 2020; "sbi reloaded", Boelts et al. 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17337) and BayesFlow (Radev et al. 2020, https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.06281).
2. **Amortisation is the prize.** Train once, then each new site's posterior costs one forward pass. The flagship is DINGO for gravitational waves (Dax et al., PRL 2021). It cut inference from hours or days of nested sampling to seconds to minutes per event (unverified exact figure). Flow-matching posterior estimation (Wildberger, Dax et al., NeurIPS 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.17161) scales this to bigger nets.
3. **Amortised inference is old in geophysics.** Meier, Curtis and Trampert (GRL 2007) used mixture density networks to invert surface-wave data for crustal thickness across a continent. Earp and Curtis (2020) did the same for 1D surface-wave dispersion. Zhang and Curtis (JGR 2021) used invertible networks for seismic tomography. These are NPE in all but name.
4. **Seismic imaging is the most advanced use.** Herrmann's group at Georgia Tech built amortised conditional normalising flows for imaging and full-waveform inversion. Examples: Siahkoohi et al. "Reliable amortized variational inference with physics-based latent distribution correction" (Geophysics 2023); Orozco et al. on physics-based summary statistics from adjoint operators (2023-2024, "ASPIRE"); Yin et al. "WISE" (2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.06230). Gahlot et al. built an amortised "digital shadow" of CO2 storage that joins flow simulation, seismic and wells (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.01218). This is the closest analogue to our problem: a multiphysics simulator plus sparse, mixed data.
5. **Generative models are now the default geological prior.** GANs came first: Mosser, Dubrule and Blunt (2017) for porous media, Laloy et al. (WRR 2018) for training-image aquifers inverted by MCMC in the latent space. Diffusion models now beat GANs on diversity and mode cover. Di Federico and Durlofsky (2024) used latent diffusion to parameterise facies geomodels for history matching. Wang and Alkhalifah (2024) trained diffusion models to generate velocity models and used them as FWI priors. Chung et al. "Diffusion Posterior Sampling" (ICLR 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.14687) is the generic method to condition a diffusion prior on any forward model.
6. **Geothermal uses SBI-like ideas only at reservoir scale.** The Auckland group (O'Sullivan, Maclaren, Power, de Beer, Nicholson) does Bayesian calibration of TOUGH/Waiwera natural-state models: posterior-informed approximation error with MCMC (Maclaren et al. 2020), ensemble Kalman inversion (Power et al. 2024) and data-space inversion (de Beer et al.). Durlofsky's group (Jiang and Durlofsky) uses DSI with deep nets for subsurface forecasting. Zanskar's FNO surrogate (Liang et al., SGW 2026; see M13) is trained on 1,200 simulations but had no UQ yet. **No paper found, in M15 or here, uses amortised NPE for regional geothermal exploration.** That is open ground.
7. **Calibration is the known trap.** Hermans et al. "A trust crisis in simulation-based inference?" (TMLR 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.06581) showed most SBI methods are often overconfident at small simulation budgets. The fixes are ensembles, balanced NRE (Delaunoy et al. 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.13624), simulation-based calibration (Talts et al. 2018, https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788) and TARP coverage tests (Lemos et al. 2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.03026).
8. **Misspecification is the second trap.** If the real data look unlike any simulation, NPE can return confident nonsense. Schmitt et al. (2023, https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.08866) detect this with an MMD test on learned summaries. Ward et al. "Robust NPE" (NeurIPS 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.06564) add an error model that absorbs mismatch.
9. **Messy, missing inputs are now handled.** The Simformer (Gloeckler et al., ICML 2024, https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.09636) is a transformer plus diffusion model that learns all conditionals at once. You can condition on any subset of observations. That fits public geothermal data, where each cell has a different mix of wells, springs, MT and gravity.

## Best methods and reported skill

| Method | Reported result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| NPE / SNPE-C on benchmark tasks | Classifier two-sample test (C2ST) near 0.5-0.6 (near perfect) on most of 10 tasks at 10^5 simulations; sequential variants win at 10^3-10^4 | Lueckmann et al., sbibm, AISTATS 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.04653 (unverified figures) |
| NPE ensembles / balanced NRE | Restore conservative coverage where single NPE was overconfident | Hermans 2022; Delaunoy 2022 |
| DINGO (amortised NPE, GW) | Full 15-parameter posteriors in seconds to minutes; matches nested sampling after importance reweighting | Dax et al. PRL 2021; Dax et al. 2023 (unverified timing) |
| Amortised CNF for seismic imaging | Posterior samples in milliseconds after training; physics-based summaries improve generalisation to unseen field-like models | Siahkoohi 2023; Orozco 2024 (unverified) |
| Latent diffusion geomodel prior | Better match of facies statistics than PCA/GAN parameterisations; history matching ran in a low-dimensional latent | Di Federico and Durlofsky 2024 (unverified detail) |
| Diffusion prior in FWI | Fewer artefacts and better recovery in poorly lit zones than Tikhonov / TV regularisation | Wang and Alkhalifah 2024; Taufik and Alkhalifah 2024 (unverified) |
| Geothermal DSI / EKI | Posterior forecasts from a few hundred to a few thousand simulations, versus 10^5+ for MCMC | de Beer et al.; Power et al. 2024 (unverified counts) |

Plain reading: with 10^4-10^6 cheap simulations, NPE gets close to exact posteriors. The cost moves to building the simulator and checking calibration. Both are things we can buy with compute.

## Datasets used

- **sbibm** benchmark tasks (Lueckmann 2021). The standard calibration testbed.
- **OpenFWI** (Deng et al., NeurIPS 2022, https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.02926): paired velocity models and seismic gathers. **Compass** (BG Group North Sea model), **Marmousi**, **Sleipner** CO2 data: used by the Herrmann group.
- **Wairakei-Tauhara** and other NZ TOUGH/Waiwera natural-state models: Auckland group.
- **Utah FORGE** data and models: EGS reservoir studies.
- Training-image libraries (channel aquifers, Stanford VI): GAN and diffusion prior work.
- Relevant to us but not yet used for SBI: SMU heat-flow and BHT database, INGENIOUS layers (GDR), USGS 2008 assessment favourability, NGDS well logs, USGS MT (EarthScope/USArray MT), PACES gravity, NURE/USGS geochemistry, and our AI-extracted scanned TG logs (MODEL.md Stage 0).

## What this means for our model design

- **Promote GHOST-SBI from "Tier 1 checker" to the per-cell inference engine.** Train one amortised NPE on millions of 1D-to-2.5D fault-upflow plus conductive-column simulations. Input: whatever a cell has (TG gradient, BHT, spring geothermometer, MT conductance, gravity). Output: joint posterior over T(z) at 0.5-3 km, upflow existence and permeability class. This plugs straight into RNPV/acre.
- **Use a Simformer-style any-subset model, not a fixed-input NPE.** Public data are ragged. One model that conditions on any mix of observations removes the need for per-source pipelines and lets us add sources without retraining from scratch.
- **Learn a diffusion prior over 2.5D geothermal systems from the known ones.** Train on simulated systems whose parameters are tuned to the ~100-plus known Great Basin systems (geometry, upflow depth, outflow plume length). Use diffusion posterior sampling to condition on each cell's data. This gives realistic hidden-plume shapes, including blind upflows that leak sideways under a cap.
- **Make calibration a hard gate.** Every release passes SBC rank histograms and TARP coverage on held-out simulations, and coverage on a real hindcast (fields found after 2010, e.g. the Zanskar-reported Big Blind and Pumpernickel area). Ship NPE ensembles by default.
- **Add a misspecification detector before any rights purchase.** Run an MMD test on each cell's learned summary. Cells whose data look "off-simulator" get flagged for a human or a sensor visit, not a confident score. This is where surprise finds hide, so these flags are a lead list, not just a warning.
- **Use the amortised posterior to price sensors.** With inference costing milliseconds, expected information gain per sensor type can be computed for every cell in the West. This feeds VOI-Bank's EVSI/$ engine directly (see L15 on amortised experimental design).
- **Use the FNO surrogate (L12) as the simulator once it exists.** Analytic columns first; swap in the neural-operator simulator later. NPE does not care how the simulator is built.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No published amortised NPE for regional geothermal exploration.** Zanskar's public stack is LightGBM plus an FNO surrogate without UQ. A calibrated, amortised posterior over temperature-at-depth for every 500 m cell would be a first.
2. **No generative prior trained on geothermal system geometry.** All diffusion geomodel work is facies or velocity. A "foundation prior" of hydrothermal plume shapes is unclaimed. AI can build the training set: an LLM agent reads hundreds of conceptual models in GRC/SGW papers and turns them into simulator parameters.
3. **No SBI over scanned, censored legacy logs.** Old BHTs are biased low and truncated. A simulator that includes the drilling-disturbance and censoring process, and an NPE trained on it, would turn noisy logs into honest posteriors.
4. **No search-effort-aware SBI.** Nobody puts "who drilled where, and why" into the simulator. Adding a simulated explorer (thinning by past effort, as in CREAM-COX) inside SBI would let the model learn where the absence of finds is informative.
5. **No population-level SBI across the Basin and Range.** Hierarchical NPE could learn shared hyperparameters (heat flow per province, fault permeability per stress regime) from all cells at once.
6. **Misspecification as a discovery signal.** In other fields it is a nuisance. For us, a cell whose data the simulator cannot explain may be a new system type. Nobody has used this as a ranked lead source.
