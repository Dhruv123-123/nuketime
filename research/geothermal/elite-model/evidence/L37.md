# L37: From model output to land, bids, farm-outs and buyer value

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: how prospecting model outputs become nominate / bid / file / farm-out / data-room decisions, and whether the model itself can be a moat for a heat-rights company.

Method note: the shared web-search budget was used up before this review ran. No new searches were done. Facts come from prior knowledge of the papers and from local files (`../../MEMO.md`, `../../redteam/`, `../../model/`). Exact numbers I could not re-check this run are marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**1. Lease auctions are common-value auctions, and information wins.** All bidders want the same unknown thing: the heat (or oil) under the tract. The theory is Wilson (1977, *Rev. Econ. Studies* 44:511) and Milgrom and Weber (1982, *Econometrica* 50:1089). The practical warning is Capen, Clapp and Campbell (1971, "Competitive bidding in high-risk situations", *J. Petroleum Technology* 23:641). They showed that the winner is usually the bidder who overestimated most: the "winner's curse". Their fix was to shade bids hard as the number of rivals rises.

**2. The informed neighbour earns the rent.** Hendricks and Porter (1988, *American Economic Review* 78:865) studied US offshore "drainage" tracts, where a firm already owned the next-door tract and had well data. Neighbour firms earned large positive returns. Non-neighbours bid less often and earned about zero. Porter (1995, *Econometrica* 63:1) and Haile, Hendricks and Porter (2010, *Int. J. Industrial Organization* 28:390) confirm that private information, not geology alone, drives profit. Hendricks, Pinkse and Porter (2003, *Rev. Econ. Studies* 70:115) found bidders on "wildcat" tracts did shade correctly for the winner's curse. So the crowd is not naive. You only win cheap where you know something the crowd does not.

**3. Information spills over after you drill.** Hendricks and Kovenock (1989, *RAND J. Econ.* 20:164) model exploration as a waiting game: each firm wants the neighbour to drill first. Lin (2013, *Rev. Econ. and Statistics* 95:1601) estimated this offshore (unverified pages). Geothermal shows the same thing locally: land beside Pumpernickel went from $2/acre (2023) to $232/acre (2025) about a month after Zanskar disclosed (MEMO, BLM NV Oct 2025 results).

**4. Firms under-use public data.** Covert (2015 working paper, later published; Bakken fracking) found operators learned slowly from rivals' public well results and left value on the table (unverified final venue). This is the core premise of every AI prospecting company: public data is under-read.

**5. Lease terms drive timing.** Herrnstadt, Kellogg and Lewis (2024, *Econometrica*, "Drilling deadlines and oil and gas development") show drilling bunches just before primary-term expiry. Kellogg (2014, *AER* 104:1698) shows firms delay drilling when price uncertainty rises, as real-options theory says. BLM geothermal leases have a 10-year primary term and annual diligence (work or payments in lieu) after the early years (43 CFR 3207; exact schedule unverified).

**6. Prospect risking must be calibrated or it lies.** Otis and Schneidermann (1997, *AAPG Bulletin* 81:1087) describe Chevron's prospect process. After they forced explicit chance-of-success and calibrated it against outcomes, predicted and actual discovery rates lined up across hundreds of wells (unverified counts). Rose (2001, *Risk Analysis and Management of Petroleum Exploration Ventures*, AAPG) documents chronic optimism in pre-drill volumes. Bratvold, Bickel and Lohne (2009, *SPE Reservoir Eval. & Eng.* 12:630) review value-of-information use: the industry talks about VOI but rarely computes it.

**7. Farm-outs price risk-sharing.** Cozzolino (1977, *J. Petroleum Technology*, "A simplified utility framework...") gives the risk-adjusted value (RAV) formula. It yields the working interest a risk-averse firm should keep. Lerche and MacKay (1999, *Economic Risk in Hydrocarbon Exploration*, Academic Press) extend it to portfolios. The one geothermal third-party farm-in on record is Crump Geyser (2010): Ormat paid the first $15M plus $2.5M cash for 50%, with the originator floored at 20% (SEC exhibit 4-158, cited in `redteam/neutral-rights-bank-vs-zanskar-incumbents.md`).

**8. Disclosure and certification raise sale prices.** Milgrom and Weber's linkage principle says a seller who publicly reveals verifiable information raises expected revenue, because it shrinks the buyers' winner's-curse discount. Grossman (1981) and Milgrom (1981) show "unravelling": a seller who hides data is assumed to be hiding bad news. Mining solved this with codes (JORC, NI 43-101). Geothermal has its own: the Australian Geothermal Reporting Code (AGRCC, 2008; 2nd ed. 2010) and the Canadian Geothermal Code for Public Reporting (CanGEA, 2010). UNECE's UNFC has geothermal specifications (2016). These define Exploration Results, Inferred/Indicated/Measured Resources and Reserves, signed by a competent person.

**9. AI-first mineral companies already run "model as moat".** KoBold Metals assembles proprietary data, takes land, and brings in majors (BHP, Rio Tinto) as partners. VerAI Discoveries runs an AI prospect-generator model: stake ground, option to partners, keep a royalty (unverified terms). Mining "prospect generators" (EMX Royalty and peers) show that a pipeline of optioned projects plus retained royalties is a durable business. Zanskar is the geothermal version, but owner-operator only (MEMO).

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

| Method | Reported result |
|---|---|
| Neighbour-informed bidding (Hendricks and Porter 1988) | Informed firms earned large positive returns on drainage tracts; uninformed rivals about zero (exact % unverified) |
| Winner's-curse bid shading (Capen et al. 1971) | Their rule: bid a falling fraction of estimated value as rivals rise. ARCO's backtest showed naive bidding loses money in high-risk sales |
| Calibrated chance of success (Otis and Schneidermann 1997) | Chevron's predicted vs actual success matched once calibrated (unverified exact rates) |
| Cozzolino RAV for working interest | Gives a closed-form optimal share for a stated risk tolerance; used widely in farm-out screening |
| Land-first geothermal (Zanskar, BLM NV) | 16,720 acres won at $2/acre with single bids in 2023; 18 parcels, about 50k acres, about $764k bonus total (MEMO) |
| Blind-ground floor wins | Nevada 2025 median winning bid $3.50/acre, 37% at the $2 floor; Oct 2026 sale about $24/acre average, 23 of 83 parcels unbid (MEMO) |
| Repricing after disclosure | 116x within about a month at Pumpernickel (MEMO) |
| Geothermal first-well success | About 50% for first wells in a new field (IFC 2013, 2,613 wells) |
| Confirmed resource value | About $0.1M/MW without a PPA; $4.3-5.6M/MW operating (MEMO) |

## Datasets used

- BLM geothermal lease sale notices and **all-bids results** (e.g. https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/docs/2025-10/NV_Geot_20251021_Bidding_Results.pdf). Every bid, bidder and price is public.
- BLM **MLRS** (formerly LR2000) case records: nominations, leases, assignments, relinquishments.
- **GAO-26-108040** (nomination volumes: 25.8k acres in 2020 to over 1M in 2025; 27% duplicates).
- State trust land lease records: Utah SITLA, Idaho IDL, New Mexico SLO, California SLC prospecting permits, Arizona ASLD.
- County recorder memoranda of lease (private heat; often no price).
- SEC EDGAR: Ormat 10-K acreage tables, Crump farm-in exhibit, Raser and U.S. Geothermal filings.
- **BOEM offshore lease bid statistics** (all bids since 1954) and well outcomes: the standard testbed for auction econometrics. Use it to validate our bid-shading code before using it on thin geothermal data.
- IFC (2013) "Success of Geothermal Wells: A Global Study".
- Nevada NDOM well files rolling off 5-year confidentiality (labels and competitor activity).

## What this means for our model design

- **Bid on the edge, not on RNPV.** The bid number per parcel should be RNPV/acre *conditional on winning*. It needs a winner's-curse correction from a model of how many rivals bid and what they know.
- **Build an explicit "crowd model".** Fit a second posterior that uses only what rivals can see (INGENIOUS, USGS favourability, published heat flow, public announcements). Our private edge on a parcel is the gap between our posterior and the crowd's. Act only where that gap is large. This is the Hendricks-Porter neighbour rent, made computable.
- **Learn rivals from their bids.** Ingest every BLM all-bids report, nomination list and MLRS case. Fit an inverse model of what each bidder (Zanskar, Ormat, land LLCs) believes. Use it as a competition-intensity layer in the decision engine, never as a geology feature.
- **Treat nominations as leaks and price them.** Each nomination reveals a township and section 4-16 months before sale. Score each candidate block by expected leak cost: how much the crowd posterior would move if rivals saw it. Then pad and decoy to keep the target inside noise. Prefer off-auction routes (private, state first-file, leftover parcels) where the leak cost is high.
- **Put the lease clock into VOI.** Diligence deadlines and primary-term expiry change when a measurement is worth buying. Schedule VOI spend against each lease's clock (Herrnstadt et al.).
- **Emit a data-room pack, not just a map.** For each prospect, output AGRCC/CanGEA-style categories, power-density MW at P10/P50/P90, a calibration plot on held-out blind systems, and a full lineage trail. The linkage principle says verifiable disclosure lifts the price buyers will pay.
- **Optimise the farm-out per buyer type.** Use a Cozzolino-style RAV to pick retained interest, carry and override for each buyer (Ormat wants 0.5-2 km upflow; Fervo wants 2-5 km temperature). Floor the carry near Crump's 20%.
- **Log every decision with a frozen model snapshot.** A dated, out-of-sample track record (Otis-Schneidermann style) is the moat buyers will believe. The model alone can be copied; the calibrated track record cannot.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No published econometrics of geothermal lease bidding.** BOEM has decades of studies. BLM geothermal all-bids data since 2007 has none I know of. An agent can parse every sale PDF and MLRS case into one table and fit a bid model in weeks.
2. **No structural model of rivals' beliefs from bids.** Nobody infers what Zanskar's model "sees" from where it nominates and how much it bids. Inverse reinforcement or simulation-based inference over bids could do it.
3. **No link between ML posteriors and reporting codes.** AGRCC and CanGEA predate ML. No one has a rule for when a calibrated posterior counts as "Exploration Results" or an "Inferred Resource". We could write and publish one, and become the standard.
4. **No leak-aware nomination optimiser.** Decoy design against an adversarial crowd model is untouched in the literature.
5. **No geothermal farm-out comps.** One deal in 16 years. An LLM pass over EDGAR, SEDAR and Australian ASX filings (Petratherm, Geodynamics, Panax era) could build a comps set of 20-50 deals (unverified count).
6. **No calibrated track record for any geothermal ML map.** Every published map scores on hindcast, not on dated predictions. A public, timestamped prediction ledger would be new.
