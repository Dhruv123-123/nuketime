This file is about agents that fetch geodata, write and run GIS code, and run models, and about how reliable they are.

## What the field knows

1. **"Autonomous GIS" is now a named research line.** Li and Ning (2023), "Autonomous GIS: the next-generation AI-powered GIS" (arXiv 2305.06453, LLM-Geo), showed GPT-4 turning a plain request into a solution graph of steps, then Python, then maps. It worked on a handful of case studies (US county mortality, hazardous waste near tracts). The authors set out five goals: self-generating, self-organising, self-verifying, self-executing, self-growing. Self-verifying was the weakest.
2. **Data retrieval is its own hard step.** Ning et al. (2024) "LLM-Find" (arXiv 2407.21024, unverified ID) built a retrieval agent with handbooks per data source (OSM, US Census, ESRI services, OpenTopography). The key lesson: agents do far better when each source has a short human-written "handbook" with the API, the CRS, and pitfalls. Without it they invent URLs and endpoints.
3. **GIS copilots inside desktop GIS work for routine tasks.** Akinboyewa et al. (2024/2025), "GIS Copilot" (arXiv 2411.03205), embedded an LLM agent in QGIS. Reported success was high on basic tasks and fell sharply on "advanced" multi-step tasks (roughly 90%+ basic vs well under 50% advanced, unverified). GeoGPT (Zhang et al. 2023, arXiv 2307.07930) and ShapefileGPT (Lin et al. 2024) show the same pattern.
4. **Geospatial code benchmarks exist and are young.** GeoCode-Bench and the GeoAgent system (Chen et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.18792) used retrieval plus Monte Carlo tree search over code steps. GeoAnalystBench (2025, about 50 Python GIS tasks, unverified) scored workflow plans and code separately: plans looked good far more often than code ran correctly. GeoBenchX (Krechetova and Kochedykova 2025) tested tool-calling on multi-step geo tasks and found models often "solve" tasks that should be rejected as impossible. GeoLLM-Engine (Singh et al. 2024) and ThinkGeo (2025) do the same for remote-sensing agents.
5. **General data-science agents are still far from reliable on open tasks.** ScienceAgentBench (Chen et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.05080): best agents solved about 32-42% of 102 tasks drawn from real papers, several in geoscience (unverified). DiscoveryBench (Majumder et al. 2024): best about 25% on data-driven hypothesis tasks (unverified). DSBench (Jing et al. 2024): about 34% on data analysis tasks (unverified). MLE-bench (Chan et al. 2024, OpenAI): o1-preview with AIDE got a Kaggle medal in about 17% of 75 competitions (unverified). CORE-Bench (Siegel et al. 2024): reproducing a paper's results from its code was about 21% on the hard split at launch (unverified). These numbers rose fast through 2025-2026 but the shape held: easy steps work, long chains break.
6. **Reliability across repeats is much lower than one-shot success.** tau-bench (Yao et al. 2024) introduced pass^k: the chance an agent succeeds on all k repeats. For GPT-4o in retail tasks pass^1 was about 61% but pass^8 under 25% (unverified). For a pipeline that must run every week, pass^k is the real metric.
7. **Multi-agent systems fail in known ways.** Cemri et al. (2025), "Why do multi-agent LLM systems fail?" (MAST taxonomy, arXiv 2503.13657) listed 14 failure modes in three groups: bad specification, inter-agent misalignment, and weak verification. Weak or missing verification was the most common root cause.
8. **Geospatial failure modes are specific and silent.** From the benchmarks above and practice: lat/lon order swaps; mixing NAD27 and NAD83 (shifts of tens to 100+ m in the West); feet vs metres in depth; deg F vs deg C in temperature logs; raster nodata read as zero; wrong resampling; area in degrees; joins that drop rows silently; and invented dataset URLs. These do not crash. They give a clean-looking wrong map. Roberts et al. (2023), "GPT4GEO", showed strong but patchy geographic knowledge, with confident errors on coordinates.
9. **Execution feedback is the main fix.** Self-Debug (Chen et al. 2023), Reflexion (Shinn et al. 2023) and SWE-agent (Yang et al. 2024) show that running code, reading errors and retrying lifts success a lot. SWE-bench Verified went from under 20% (early 2024) to about 70-80% (2025-2026) mostly through better tools, tests and loops (unverified). The lesson: tests and checkers matter more than the model.

## Best methods and their reported skill

| Method | Setting | Reported result (all unverified) |
|---|---|---|
| Handbook-guided retrieval (LLM-Find) | Fetch public geodata | Large lift over no-handbook baseline; most failures were wrong endpoints |
| Plan-then-code with solution graph (LLM-Geo) | County-scale analyses | Worked on 5 case studies; needed human fixes for some |
| RAG + MCTS over code (GeoAgent) | GeoCode-Bench | Clear gain over plain prompting on multi-step tasks |
| Copilot in QGIS (GIS Copilot) | ~100 tasks, 3 levels | High on basic, low on advanced |
| Agent + execution loop (SWE-agent style) | SWE-bench Verified | ~70-80% by 2025-26 |
| Agent on science papers (ScienceAgentBench) | 102 tasks, incl. geo | ~32-42% best |
| Agent on Kaggle (MLE-bench) | 75 comps | ~17% medal rate, rising |
| pass^k reliability (tau-bench) | Repeated runs | pass^8 far below pass^1 |

## Datasets used

Benchmarks: GeoCode-Bench, GeoAnalystBench, GeoBenchX, GeoLLM-Engine, ThinkGeo, ScienceAgentBench, DiscoveryBench, DSBench, MLE-bench, CORE-Bench, SWE-bench Verified, tau-bench. Public geodata these agents pull: OpenStreetMap, US Census TIGER, USGS 3DEP via OpenTopography, Landsat and Sentinel via STAC (Microsoft Planetary Computer, AWS Earth Search), ESRI REST services. Geothermal data our agents would pull (named, not catalogued here): NGDS and GDR (OpenEI), INGENIOUS layers, SMU heat-flow database, USGS Quaternary fault database, BLM MLRS and lease sale notices, Nevada Bureau of Mines and Geology well files, state oil and gas commission well logs.

## What this means for our model design

- **Agents run the data plane, not the likelihood.** Agents fetch, clean, extract and re-project. The Cox count, the column likelihoods and the VOI engine stay as fixed, versioned code that agents may not edit at run time. This keeps the MAST "weak verification" failure out of the part that sets prices.
- **Write a handbook per source.** For each of our ~50-100 sources (BLM MLRS, NGDS, NBMG, Utah DOGM, SMU, USGS QFaults, STAC), keep a one-page card: endpoint, CRS, units, datum, known traps, and a tiny golden sample with known values. LLM-Find shows this is the cheapest big lift. These cards can be the SCHEMA.md entries.
- **Hard unit and datum contracts at every boundary.** Every layer is a typed object with CRS, datum, depth unit, temperature unit and nodata. A checker rejects anything that fails a sanity band (e.g. gradient 0-300 C/km, BHT under 400 C, well inside the state polygon). This catches the silent failures in point 8.
- **Score agents by pass^k on our own golden suite.** Build 50-100 tasks with known answers from our own world: reproduce the Faulds Nevada PFA favourability at 20 known sites; rebuild a SMU heat-flow value from the raw well; re-derive the BLM lease status of 30 parcels. Ship an agent pipeline only when pass^5 is above a set bar (say 95%) and log it per release.
- **Run two independent agents and diff them.** For extraction and ETL, two different models (or prompts) do the job blind, and a third step diffs. Agreement goes in; disagreement goes to a human or a tie-break run. This turns model error into a measured disagreement rate the Cox layer can use as source noise.
- **Every number carries provenance.** Each value in the store records source URL, retrieval date, agent version, code hash and checker results. The replay backtest in MODEL.md needs dated evidence; agent provenance gives it for free and makes the "what did we know when" claim auditable for buyers.
- **Use agents for always-on watching.** The highest-value agent job is not analysis. It is watching: new BLM nominations and sale lists, new state well permits, new GDR uploads, new USGS releases, new InSAR scenes. Each event triggers a small, tested pipeline and a VOI re-score. Speed on land is our edge over Zanskar.
- **Impossible-task refusal is a feature.** GeoBenchX shows agents answer when they should refuse. Add an explicit "data not available or not good enough" outcome to every agent task, and reward it in the golden suite.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No subsurface or geothermal agent benchmark exists.** All geo-agent benchmarks are surface GIS or remote sensing. A "GeoThermBench" of well logs, BHT corrections, lease parcels and fault joins would be new. We could build it as our internal test suite and later publish part of it for credibility.
2. **No one measures silent-error rate for geo agents.** Benchmarks score pass or fail. None plant datum, unit or nodata traps and count how often the agent passes them through. A trap-injected suite is easy for us to make and directly protects our money.
3. **No agent links land status to resource evidence.** GIS copilots do analysis; none join BLM/state lease records, ownership and heat evidence into a live "buyable and hot" list. That join is our product.
4. **Self-growing toolkits are untested in practice.** Li and Ning name "self-growing" as a goal. An agent that turns each solved pipeline into a tested, reusable tool (with its golden test) would compound. With plenty of compute we can let it run, gated by pass^k.
5. **No calibrated agent uncertainty feeds a Bayesian model.** Agent outputs are treated as exact. Using measured agent disagreement and trap-suite error rates as likelihood noise in the THERMOS-B column layer is, as far as I know, new.
