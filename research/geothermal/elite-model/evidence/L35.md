# L35: Cloud-native geospatial data engineering for a small team

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Scope: STAC, COG, Zarr, Pangeo, DuckDB/GeoParquet, H3 and other grids, versioned data lakes, lineage. What a 1-3 person team should run under SENTINEL-COX (`../../model/MODEL.md`).

Method note: the shared web-search budget was used up before this review ran. No new searches were done. Facts below come from prior knowledge of the primary specs and papers. Version numbers and dates I am confident of are stated plainly. Anything I could not re-check this run is marked (unverified).

## What the field knows

**1. The "analysis-ready, cloud-optimised" (ARCO) idea won.** Pangeo argued that big science data should sit in object storage in chunked formats that clients read by HTTP range request, not in download-then-process archives. The key paper is Abernathey et al. 2021, "Cloud-Native Repositories for Big Scientific Data", *Computing in Science & Engineering* 23(2):26-35, doi:10.1109/MCSE.2021.3059437. The stack is Xarray (Hoyer and Hamman 2017, *J. Open Research Software*, doi:10.5334/jors.148) plus Dask plus Zarr. Pangeo Forge (Stern et al. 2022, *Frontiers in Climate*, doi:10.3389/fclim.2021.782909) showed recipe-based conversion of legacy archives into ARCO stores. The community "Cloud-Optimized Geospatial Formats Guide" (https://guide.cloudnativegeo.org) is the best single summary.

**2. Each data shape now has one standard format.**
- 2D rasters: **COG** (Cloud Optimized GeoTIFF). Internal tiles plus overviews, so a client fetches one tile with one or two range requests. OGC adopted it as a standard in 2023 (OGC 21-026). https://www.ogc.org/standard/cog/ ; https://cogeo.org
- N-D arrays and cubes: **Zarr**. Zarr v3 spec was finalised in 2023 and zarr-python 3.0 shipped in early 2025. GeoZarr is in OGC work. https://zarr.dev
- Legacy netCDF/HDF5/GRIB without copying: **Kerchunk** and **VirtualiZarr** build byte-range "virtual" Zarr references over old files. https://github.com/zarr-developers/VirtualiZarr
- Versioned arrays: **Icechunk** (Earthmover, open source, 1.0 in 2025 (unverified date)) adds git-like commits, branches and ACID transactions to Zarr on S3. https://icechunk.io
- Vector and tables: **GeoParquet** 1.0 (Sept 2023) and 1.1 (2024, adds a per-row bbox "covering" column so readers can skip row groups). https://geoparquet.org . Native GEOMETRY/GEOGRAPHY types were added to the core Parquet and Apache Iceberg v3 specs in 2025 (unverified exact release).
- Point clouds: **COPC** (Cloud Optimized Point Cloud, a LAZ variant). USGS 3DEP lidar is on AWS as EPT/COPC. https://copc.io
- Seismic: **MDIO** (TGS, open source, Zarr-based SEG-Y replacement). https://github.com/TGSAI/mdio-python
- Magnetotellurics: **MTH5** (Peacock et al. 2022, *Computers & Geosciences* 162:105102, HDF5 archive for MT time series). https://github.com/kujaku11/mth5

**3. Catalogues use STAC.** STAC 1.0.0 was released in May 2021 (https://stacspec.org). It is a small JSON spec for "items" (one scene, one file) with geometry, time and asset links. Microsoft Planetary Computer and Element 84 Earth Search serve Sentinel-2, Landsat, Copernicus DEM and more as COGs behind STAC APIs. `stac-geoparquet` stores a whole catalogue as one Parquet file, which DuckDB can query in place.

**4. Single-node SQL beat clusters for mid-size data.** DuckDB (Raasveldt and Mühleisen, SIGMOD 2019 demo) plus its `spatial` extension reads GeoParquet straight from S3/HTTP and runs spatial joins in-process. Overture Maps ships its global map (billions of features) as GeoParquet on S3 and recommends DuckDB as the client. DuckLabs released **DuckLake** in 2025: a lakehouse format with Parquet data plus a SQL catalogue, with snapshots and time travel. Apache Iceberg and Delta Lake do the same at larger scale.

**5. Discrete global grids are the join key of choice.** Uber's **H3** (2018) is a hierarchical hexagon grid. Average cell area is about 5.16 km² at resolution 7, 0.737 km² at res 8 and 0.105 km² at res 9. H3 is *not* equal-area: cell area varies by about a factor of two within one resolution (unverified exact ratio). OGC Abstract Specification Topic 21 defines DGGS.

**6. Lineage is its own field.** W3C PROV-O defines provenance. OpenLineage (LF AI and Data) is the open event standard. Dagster's "software-defined assets" give a lineage graph for free. DVC and lakeFS version files. Feature stores (Feast) formalised point-in-time joins to stop look-ahead leakage.

## Best methods and their reported skill or results

- **COG reads:** one 512x512 tile is one range request after the header. GDAL `/vsicurl/` or `/vsis3/` reads windows without download. Typical whole-scene download is 100-1,000 MB; a window read is tens of kB (order-of-magnitude, unverified).
- **GeoParquet vs shapefile/GeoJSON:** 5-10x smaller on disk and much faster to filter, because of columnar compression and bbox row-group pruning (community benchmarks; unverified numbers).
- **DuckDB spatial:** spatial joins on tens of millions of points against polygons run in seconds to minutes on one laptop (unverified; no peer-reviewed benchmark found this run). Apache Sedona's SpatialBench (2025) compares engines (unverified).
- **Size check for our model (my arithmetic).** The Great Basin is about 540,000 km². At 500 m cells that is about 2.2 M cells. A feature table of 2.2 M rows x 500 float32 features is about 4.4 GB. The whole West (about 3 M km²) is about 12 M cells and 24 GB. This fits in RAM on one 256-512 GB machine. **Our core problem is "many small, messy sources", not "big data".** Only imagery time series (Sentinel-2, Landsat, ECOSTRESS, InSAR), lidar and the scanned-document corpus reach TB scale, and those can be reduced upstream to derived COG layers.

## Datasets used (as they exist in cloud-native form today)

- Already cloud-native: Sentinel-2 L2A and Landsat Collection 2 COGs (Planetary Computer, Earth Search; USGS requester-pays), Copernicus DEM GLO-30 COG, USGS 3DEP lidar (EPT/COPC on AWS), NASA Earthdata Cloud (ECOSTRESS LST, ASTER, OPERA DISP-S1 InSAR displacement, NISAR via ASF; access by `earthaccess`), Overture Maps (roads, buildings) as GeoParquet.
- Not cloud-native (needs conversion): DOE **GDR** submissions (zip dumps of shapefiles, xlsx, PDFs; e.g. INGENIOUS GDR 1391), USGS **ScienceBase** items, SMU/NGDS heat flow and BHT tables, USGS NGDB geochemistry, EarthScope/USArray MT transfer functions (EMTF XML/EDI), state oil and gas well files (LAS, scanned PDFs), BLM **MLRS** lease records and **PLSS CadNSDI** parcels, HIFLD transmission lines, state land-board lease lists.

## What this means for our model design

- **Use a "one bucket, three formats, one engine" stack.** Object storage (Cloudflare R2 for zero egress, or S3 us-west-2 next to NASA data). GeoParquet for every point, line, polygon and table. COG for every 2D layer. Zarr v3 on Icechunk for cubes (imagery time series, 3D T(z) fields, posterior ensembles). DuckDB as the only query engine. No Spark, no Postgres, no Kubernetes in year one.
- **Make every record bitemporal.** Each row carries `valid_time` (when the measurement describes the Earth) and `known_time` (when it became public: report date, GDR release, well-file completion plus confidentiality lag). SENTINEL-COX's discovery thinning and the replay backtest both need "what was knowable on date t". A DuckLake/Iceberg snapshot gives *our* ingest time; `known_time` gives the world's. All hindcast training uses a point-in-time join on `known_time`.
- **Model lattice = 500 m EPSG:5070 (CONUS Albers equal-area); H3 res 8 = join key; PLSS aliquots = decision unit.** The LGCP needs exact, equal cell areas and a regular grid (FFT or SPDE/INLA). H3 is convenient for joins and the web map, but its unequal areas would bias Cox intensities if used raw. Rights are bought by PLSS section and 40-acre aliquot, so keep a precomputed crosswalk table (cell, H3, PLSS aliquot, area fraction) and roll RNPV/ac up to legal parcels through it.
- **Store uncertainty as data, not as a footnote.** Every model output is a Zarr array with a `draw` (or quantile) dimension, so RNPV/ac and T(z) P10/P50/P90 are recomputable and EVSI can sample them. Each extracted measurement in Parquet carries its likelihood parameters (sigma, censoring bound, unit-conversion flag), matching THERMOS-B's per-source likelihoods.
- **Give document AI a provenance schema from day one.** Raw PDFs are stored content-addressed by SHA-256. Each extracted value is one Parquet row: `doc_sha, page, bbox, field, value, unit, depth, model_id, prompt_hash, confidence, extracted_at, known_time, reviewer`. Re-extraction with a new model writes a new snapshot; nothing is overwritten. This makes the GeoFM-West Stage 0 extraction audit and its kill tests trivial queries.
- **Use Dagster assets for lineage, git for code, Icechunk/DuckLake snapshot IDs for data.** Every model run logs (git SHA, data snapshot IDs, config hash).
- **Publish a STAC catalogue of our own holdings** (static, as stac-geoparquet). It doubles as the dataset index the four catalogue threads are building (`../../datasets/SCHEMA.md` fields map onto STAC item properties), and later as the data room for selling proven rights.
- **Compute plan for 1-3 people:** one large VM (64 cores, 512 GB RAM) for DuckDB and model fits; Coiled/Dask or odc-stac/stackstac only for imagery reductions; GPUs rented per job for document extraction. Estimated cloud spend a few hundred to low thousands of dollars per month (unverified).

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No cloud-native geothermal catalogue exists.** GDR, ScienceBase and NGDS are zip-and-PDF archives with no STAC, no unified units, no shared grid. An LLM agent fleet can read each submission, write a typed conversion recipe with unit tests, and emit GeoParquet/COG plus STAC. Hundreds of GDR submissions become one queryable lake in weeks.
2. **No geoscience archive records `known_time`.** Nobody can do honest point-in-time hindcasts of geothermal discovery. AI can extract publication, release and confidentiality-expiry dates from report front matter, OGCC well-file stamps and BLM case histories. This is the raw material for SENTINEL-COX's search-effort thinning, and competitors lack it.
3. **No standard for "claims extracted from documents" with uncertainty.** Our provenance schema above, plus model-ensemble agreement as a confidence score, could become that standard.
4. **Unit and datum errors are silent.** BHT tables mix °F/°C, feet/metres, KB/GL depth datums. LLM-written data contracts (pandera or Great Expectations rules) plus anomaly checks against physics (gradient bounds) can flag them automatically at ingest.
5. **Rights data is not joined to geoscience anywhere.** BLM MLRS case files, state lease lists and county parcel owners sit outside every prospectivity stack. An AI-maintained PLSS-to-cell crosswalk with nightly MLRS diffs turns the data lake into a live "which acre is buyable now" layer.
6. **Agentic pipeline upkeep.** With plentiful compute, agents can watch source URLs, detect schema drift, open pull requests to fix adapters, and rerun the affected Dagster assets. This lets a two-person team run a data estate that would normally need a data-engineering group.
