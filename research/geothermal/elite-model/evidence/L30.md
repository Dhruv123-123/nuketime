# L30: EGS and superhot rock resource modelling, and what our model must add for EGS

It asks one question: what changes when the buyer is an EGS or superhot developer, not a hydrothermal one?

## What the field knows

1. **EGS works commercially, in a narrow window.** Fervo's Cape Station (Beaver County, Utah) reached commercial operation on 30 Sep 2026 at 33 MW net, with Phase II (400 MW) due in 2028 (`L07`). Fervo's S-1 land criteria are about **300-625 °F (150-330 °C) at no more than about 13,000 ft (4 km) TVD**, near old deep wells with high shallow temperatures and near transmission (`L07`).
2. **The Cape/FORGE anomaly is buried basement heat under basin fill.** FORGE well 58-32 reached about 199 °C at 2.3 km. Granite top is at 780 m. Basin fill acts as an insulating blanket (k about 1.5 W/m/K). The FORGE injector 16A(78)-32 toe is about 228-230 °C at 8,559 ft TVD (unverified, commonly cited) (Allis et al. 2019, UGS MP-169-D; `M16`).
3. **Conductive maps are the EGS baseline, and they are improving fast.** SMU/Blackwell 2011 maps to 9.5 km fed the MIT 2006 report (Tester et al.) and NREL's reV supply curves. Stanford's Thermal Earth Model (InterPIGNN, Aljubran and Horne 2024, *Geothermal Energy*, doi 10.1186/s40517-024-00304-7) and LANL's In-Context Earth (O'Malley et al. 2026, https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16665) now give 0-7 km grids. USGS (Birdwell, Burns et al., GSA 2025) estimates **135 GWe** of EGS in the Great Basin at 6 km or less (`M16`).
4. **Conduction is wrong exactly where convection lives.** At FORGE the upflow zone runs about **100 °C cooler at 4-5 km** than conductive extrapolation. The native-state model had to raise its bottom boundary to 220-270 °C after the 2018 logs (Podgorney et al. 2021, Energies 14:4758). So EGS models need to know convection too, both to avoid cold traps and to find hot lids.
5. **Cornell's thread is sedimentary and low-temperature EGS.** The Cornell GPFA-AB team (Jordan, Tester, Horowitz, Whealton, Stedinger, Smith) built Appalachian Basin thermal and risk maps with GLS BHT corrections. Those corrections explained little variance (pseudo-R² 0.08-0.5) (https://gdr.openei.org/files/682/2_GPFA-AB_BHTCorrections.pdf). Cornell's Earth Source Heat drilled the CUBO borehole to about 3.1 km in Ithaca in 2022 to test district-heat EGS in crystalline basement under sediment (https://sustainable.cornell.edu/buildings-energy/earth-source-heat; depth and result unverified). Lesson: a sedimentary-basin forecast can miss by tens of °C at 3 km if the BHT bias model is wrong.
6. **Superhot (>374 °C) is real but local.** More than 25 wells worldwide passed 374 °C, nearly all shallower than 4.7 km and almost all at existing hydrothermal or volcanic fields (Reinsch et al. 2017, *Geothermal Energy* 5:16). Scott, Driesner and Weis (2015, *Nat. Commun.* 6:7837) model superhot fluid directly beneath conventional fields. Watanabe et al. (2017, *Nat. Geosci.* 10:140) find fractured granite stays permeable at 350-500 °C. Mazama's Newberry well 1 hit 331 °C; well 2 targets over 400 °C (`L08`, `M32`).
7. **For superhot, permeability, not heat, binds.** IDDP-2 hit 426 °C at 4.5 km but deep zones took under 5% of injected fluid. Kakkonda (500 °C at 3.7 km) and Venelle-2 were essentially tight (`L08`).

Two numbers matter most for us. First, national-model error grows to about 11 °C at 4-6 km where data exist, and is unknown in empty basins. Second, per-well MW, not temperature alone, drives value. So a parcel rank must carry stress and rock, not just a geotherm.

## What this means for our model design

- **Add a second target head: T at 3-6 km in drillable rock.** SENTINEL-COX predicts hydrothermal upflow at 0.5-2 km. EGS buyers want P(T ≥ 200 °C at ≤ 4 km TVD) and depth to the 150/200/250/300/400 °C isotherms, each with a calibrated interval. Both heads should share one posterior, so one data room sells to Ormat and to Fervo (`M32` point 5).
- **Model the basin-fill blanket explicitly.** Depth to basement, fill thickness and fill conductivity set the deep geotherm at Cape/FORGE. Use gravity inversions, well tops and MT to estimate basement depth per parcel, and carry fill k as an unknown (1.2-2.0 W/m/K).
- **Add basement radiogenic heat production as a latent field.** Granite heat production can shift 4 km temperature by tens of °C. Estimate it from airborne radiometrics where basement outcrops, and from spectral gamma logs in deep wells (Bücker-Rybach relation).
- **Add a stress-and-frackability layer.** MW per well is the value driver (CATF; Fervo vs FORGE). Predict stress regime, Shmin gradient and SHmax azimuth per parcel from focal mechanisms, breakouts, DFITs and fault geometry. Normal or strike-slip regimes with known azimuth are better EGS targets.
- **Convert temperature to MW per well with a surrogate.** Train a fast emulator on GEOPHIRES and ResFrac-style runs (T, depth, stress, rock, lateral length) and calibrate it to Red, Cape, FORGE, Soultz and Habanero. Feed MW per well, not temperature, into the RNPV/acre spine.
- **Use convection as a two-sided signal.** A shallow upflow can mean a cold trap at 4-5 km (FORGE) or a hot lid over superhot rock (Scott 2015). The column model must output both deep scenarios, weighted by evidence.
- **Add hazard and water masks.** Induced-seismicity risk (Pohang Mw 5.5 in 2017, Basel 2006, both from memory) near towns and makeup-water rights must cut RNPV, since they can kill a permit.
- **Superhot tier = under known fields.** Rank parcels adjacent to operating fields, Cascades volcanoes and young silicic centres for depth to 400 °C, using Curie depth, deep MT conductors, fluid-inclusion and alteration data.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **No public parcel-scale 3-6 km temperature model with honest error in data-empty basement.** Public models are conductive and dense only where wells exist. Fit a simulation-based-inference column model, trained on thousands of TOUGH/FALCON runs, and score it on FORGE by hiding the deep wells.
2. **Basement conductivity and heat production are unmapped.** Mud logs, cuttings descriptions and scanned gamma logs in state archives hold lithology for thousands of deep holes. LLM extraction can turn these into k and A priors per basement unit.
3. **Stress is sparse.** World Stress Map points are tens of km apart in the Great Basin. Scanned image logs, DFIT reports in O&G files and ComCat focal mechanisms can be extracted and interpolated with a physics-aware model. No one sells a parcel stress map.
4. **EGS productivity has under 10 labelled field tests.** That is too few for ML, but enough to calibrate a physics emulator. A transparent MW-per-well score per parcel would be new.
5. **Sedimentary EGS is unpriced.** Tight carbonates and sandstones at 175-225 °C under the Permian, DJ and Gulf Coast sit under millions of BHTs and DFITs. Cornell's GPFA-AB showed the BHT bias is the weak link. A learned, place-dependent bias model fixes that and turns oil-field acreage into EGS rights (`M25`).
6. **Superhot maps are 0.5° coarse.** A fused model (Curie depth, MT, seismic velocity, geothermometers, deep-well fluid inclusions) at 1-5 km resolution near known fields does not exist publicly.
