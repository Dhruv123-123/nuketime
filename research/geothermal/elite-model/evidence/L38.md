## What the field knows

- **2 m probes work, but only on leaky systems.** The UNR/DRI/USGS method (Coolbaugh, Sladek, Zehner, Kratt, from 2006) drives a tube to 2 m and reads an RTD after at least an hour. It found or confirmed blind systems at Teels Marsh (2008), Emerson Pass, Columbus Salt Marsh, Hawthorne and Gabbs Valley (gradient holes later hit about 120 C at 150 m). Sources: [OpenEI 2-M Probe Survey](https://openei.org/wiki/2-M_Probe_Survey), ["Boom and Bust"](https://openei.org/wiki/Boom_And_Bust_With_The_Latest_2M_Temperature_Surveys-_Dead_Horse_Wells,_Hawthorne_Army_Depot,_Terraced_Hills,_And_Other_Areas_In_Nevada), [USGS Gabbs Valley](https://www.usgs.gov/publications/discovery-blind-geothermal-system-southern-gabbs-valley-western-nevada-through). The UNR team corrects albedo with ASTER and notes contrast is lowest about two months after the coldest air ([Improvements in 2 m](https://openei.org/wiki/Improvements_in_Shallow_(Two-Meter)_Temperature_Measurements_and_Data_Interpretation)).
- **Depth beats density.** Olmsted (1977, [USGS PP 1044-B](https://app.ingemmet.gob.pe/biblioteca/pdf/USGS-PP-1044-B.pdf)) saw a 12 C anomaly at 30 m where 1 m showed 3.3-3.7 C. At 10 m the annual wave is about 1% of the surface swing; at 30 m it is gone.
- **Electric-field-only MT is a proven 10x cost cut.** Low-cost 24-bit telluric loggers (Münster EDE; LPTR, [EGU21-16275](https://meetingorganizer.copernicus.org/EGU21/EGU21-16275.html)) matched a Metronix ADU07 from 5 s up to 42,000 s periods when paired with a sparse magnetic reference. Contract MT is about $520-2,300 per station ([OpenEI MT](https://en.openei.org/wiki/Magnetotellurics)).
- **Dense passive seismic is now a commodity.** Hengill: 498 nodes, about 2 months, imaged low-velocity zones in the top 500 m and a 3-5 km intrusion ([Wu et al. 2024](https://escholarship.org/content/qt5mj7r09n/qt5mj7r09n.pdf)). Cornwall: 450 nodes gave fracture orientation and stress that matched well logs ([Hudson et al. 2024](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:9a5713b8-cfed-4ad9-baed-9ff3af6ba6bf)). Ultra-short (days) 3C nodal ambient-noise imaging works ([ESS Open Archive](https://essopenarchive.org/users/527791/articles/598744-high-resolution-ambient-noise-imaging-of-geothermal-reservoir-using-3c-dense-seismic-nodal-array-and-ultra-short-observation)).
- **Fibre turns existing infrastructure into sensors.** Brawley: 28 km of dark telecom fibre and two days of ambient noise imaged a high-Vs, low-Vp/Vs body over a blind field ([Cheng et al. 2023](https://essopenarchive.org/users/527791/articles/596714-using-dark-fiber-and-distributed-acoustic-sensing-to-characterize-a-geothermal-system-in-the-imperial-valley-southern-california); data [catalog](https://catalog-old.data.gov/dataset/imperial-valley-dark-fiber-project-continuous-das-data-a0338)). Reno dark fibre gave bedrock depth ([NSF PAR](https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10502387-subsurface-imaging-using-interferometry-distributed-acoustic-sensing-ambient-noise-measurement-along-dark-fiber-line-case-study-downtown-reno-nevada)). PoroTomo at Brady's (2016) combined 8.6 km of trenched DAS, a DTS well and 238 nodes (unverified details; GDR). DTS reads 0.25-1 m spacing at 0.01-0.1 C.
- **Drones are moving from photos to physics.** Drone magnetics and gamma (fault and alteration mapping) and drone thermal IR are routine in mining; geothermal use is mostly thermal IR of springs and steaming ground (unverified). Satellite LST cannot see a blind system (M10).
- **VOI theory exists; likelihoods do not.** Trainor-Guitton's work shows MT at Darajat was worth $11k-50k per well decision, and 2.5-4.4x more under a pessimistic prior ([LLNL deck](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2015/06/f23/19-Information%20Evaluation_LLNL.pdf); [SGW 2023](https://pangea.stanford.edu/ERE/pdf/IGAstandard/SGW/2023/Trainorguitton.pdf)). No one has published the hit/false-alarm matrix of any cheap sensor against known blind systems.

## Best methods and their reported skill (numbers)

| Method | Cost | Reported skill / result |
|---|---|---|
| 2 m probe, one-shot | $200-500/station, 10/day ([OpenEI](https://openei.org/wiki/2-M_Probe_Survey)) | About 7 anomalies in 11 areas ("Boom and Bust"); 20 of 55 INGENIOUS areas anomalous, 64% blank (M21 calc on [GDR 1391](https://gdr.openei.org/submissions/1391)); noise floor about 1.2 C; seasonal wave about ±5 C |
| Logging 2 m probe (months, 3 depths) | $50-80 parts (H01 estimate, unverified) | Fits amplitude and phase per probe; detection limit plausibly drops from about 3 C to under 1 C (calc, unverified) |
| 10-60 m push / dart | Casual use under BLM [IM 2026-024](https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2026-024) below 10 ft; deeper needs NOI | Gradient to tens of mW/m2 at 30 m (M21) |
| Gradient hole, own rig | $30-60k target (H11, unverified) vs $0.3-1.5M contract | s/q assumed 0.75/0.95 (M17, unverified) |
| E-field MT carpet | $150-250/site (M08) | Clay cap mapping; non-unique (brine, lacustrine clay, fossil cap) |
| Dense nodal seismic | $1.5-2.5k/node, often leased (H03) | Vs to 0.5-5 km, fracture stress; structure, not heat |
| Dark-fibre DAS | Interrogator rent plus carrier access | Vs to about 3 km along 28 km in 2 days (Brawley) |
| POMDP sequential drilling | n/a | Same go/no-go accuracy with fewer than half the holes; 9 vs 36 in one case ([Mern et al. 2024, arXiv 2410.10610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.10610)) |

Active-learning literature to borrow: near-optimal GP sensor placement by mutual information, a submodular greedy with a (1 - 1/e) guarantee (Krause, Singh, Guestrin 2008, JMLR; unverified citation detail). Deep Adaptive Design trains a policy network to choose experiments in milliseconds (Foster et al. 2021, ICML; unverified). Bayesian OED review: Huan, Jagalur, Marzouk 2024, Acta Numerica (unverified). Reykjavik University VOI work found 3D active seismic not worth it for large-area exploration; passive gets similar answers cheaper ([RU](https://en.ru.is/ise/research/research-projects/voi-geothermal-seismic-value-of-seismic-survey-in-geothermal-assessment)).

- USGS Great Basin MT conductance ([USGS](https://www.usgs.gov/data/electrical-conductance-maps-great-basin-usa)); Gabbs Valley MT 2020; EMC-CONUS-MT-2023 ([IRIS](https://ds.iris.edu/ds/products/emc-conus-mt-2023)); IRIS SPUD EMTF transfer functions.
- Imperial Valley dark-fibre DAS; PoroTomo Brady's DAS/DTS/nodes (GDR, unverified ID).
- Hengill and Cornwall nodal arrays; USGS HELITEM AEM and GeoDAWN magnetics/radiometrics (MODEL.md).
- OFR 99-425 TG holes; BLM IM 2026-024 and the April 2026 BLM CX as the permit "dataset" that sets what can be sensed cheaply.

## What this means for our model design

- **Treat every sensor reading as a censored observation of the same latent field, with its own detection operator.** A 2 m blank enters as "flux below about 0.5-1 W/m2 at this lid thickness", not as "cold". The operator takes lid thickness, water-table depth, season phase and spacing.
- **Model time explicitly for shallow sensors.** Fit a per-probe seasonal amplitude and phase (damping depth about 2.2 m in dry alluvium) as nuisance parameters. A logging probe then gives a residual with its own error bar, and the model can ask "leave it 3 more months?" as an action.
- **Make the acquisition score multi-fidelity.** Actions run from $0 desk extraction to $250 E-MT site, $300 2 m station, $2k node-month, $40k own-rig hole. Use a cost-aware score: expected decision value (EVSI on RNPV/acre) plus a global information term on shared parameters, divided by cost. This is the multi-fidelity Bayesian optimisation setup.
- **Spend the first field money on the likelihood table.** A calibration campaign over about 20 known blind systems and 40 cold controls, two or more sensors per cell, gives s/q per mask class and their correlation. Every later EVSI depends on it; nothing else in the literature provides it.
- **Choose sensor batches, not single readings.** Field crews move in days and trucks. Use batch (submodular greedy or determinantal) selection per mobilisation, with a travel-cost term, and re-plan after each batch as in the POMDP drill planner.
- **Add a "wrong prior" escape hatch.** Mern et al. found the agent walked away from a good prospect without a maximum-entropy null class. Keep an "unknown play type" component so sensors can surprise the model.
- **Use structure sensors to shrink the conductive model, not to detect heat.** DAS, nodes and HVSR feed basement depth and fault offset into the 1D column. Only temperature sensors update the heat channel directly.

## Gaps nobody has filled that we could exploit with AI

1. **The published reliability matrix.** No study gives sensitivity and specificity of 2 m, E-MT, ranch-well profiles or chemistry against blind systems. We measure it and own it.
2. **Amortised design.** Nobody in geothermal trains a policy network (Deep Adaptive Design style) on synthetic Great Basin worlds so that a field tablet re-plans the next probe in seconds after each reading.
3. **Season-aware probe networks.** All public 2 m data are snapshots (under 4% read twice). A learned seasonal model from a few hundred logging probes could re-score the 3,800 legacy snapshots and revive "blank" valleys.
4. **Fibre-route atlas.** No public map says which buried telecom and rail fibre crosses favourable valleys. An LLM can assemble it from franchise filings, right-of-way records and carrier maps, then rank routes by EIG.
5. **Joint inversion with learned priors.** E-MT, gravity, nodal Vs and temperature are inverted separately. A generative prior trained on simulated hydrothermal systems (TOUGH/Waiwera runs) could make sparse cheap data act like dense data.
6. **Drone emplacement plus learned siting.** Drone-dropped darts and E-nodes (P03, P13) make the action space continuous; nobody couples placement robots to an acquisition function in real time.
