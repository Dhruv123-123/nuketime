## 1. Name and summary

**PLAYBOOK** (Play-Level Assessment, Yield-curve, Bayesian Odds, Order and Ownership Kernel).

Petroleum learned to explore with three tools that geothermal never adopted. The first is the **discovery-process model**: dated finds are a sample drawn without replacement, in proportion to discoverability, from a finite pool, so the order and timing of past finds tell you how many fields are left and how big they are (Arps-Roberts 1958; Kaufman-Balcer-Kruyt 1975; PETRIMES). The second is **play-based chance-of-success**: risk splits into shared play elements and local prospect elements, each read from an auditable table, painted as Common Risk Segment (CRS) maps, and recalibrated by post-drill lookbacks (White 1993; Otis-Schneidermann 1997; Milkov 2015). The third is **portfolio ranking**: commercial chance Pc = Pg x P(size > minimum economic size), EMV per dollar, dependency-aware drilling order and a risk-adjusted frontier (Martinelli-Eidsvik 2013; Delfiner 2003). PLAYBOOK rebuilds all three for hidden hydrothermal and EGS heat, and upgrades each with ML. Discoverability is learned per **search channel** (springs, water wells, mining holes, oil wells, TG grids, airborne surveys, our sensors), so it can say why Nevada shows no classic creaming curve (M33: size versus discovery order rank correlation about 0.03) and still forecast what is left. Risk tables become **learned, monotone, calibrated element models** (heat, open pathway, fluid, cap or mask, freshness) trained on an AI-extracted ledger of pre-drill evidence and post-drill failure modes. Prospects inside a play share a latent play factor, so one cool hole re-prices every neighbour. The output is not a heat map. It is a **book**: for every play, a posterior on how many systems remain by size class and which channel can find them; for every prospect polygon and parcel, Pg by element, Pc, risked MW and RNPV/acre; and a ranked list of buy, sense, drill and sell actions with a play-concentration limit. SENTINEL-COX's spatial Cox field becomes one input (the prospect-level heat and existence prior) inside a play-and-portfolio frame that buyers already understand from oil.

## 2. Target variables

**Units of analysis (three nested levels, as in petroleum):**
- **Play** p: archetype x province x depth band. Archetypes follow L09: B1 shallow outflow, B2 range-hosted no discharge, B3 basin-buried capped, B4 cold-aquifer lid, B5 rain curtain, B6 carbonate sweep, B7 conductive sedimentary aquifer, plus EGS-conductive, magmatic and rift, plus a max-entropy "unknown play". Provinces: Great Basin sub-domains (USGS 2026 zones or the six k-means domains of Hart-Wagoner 2024), Snake River Plain, Cascades, Rio Grande rift, Salton, Basin-and-Range AZ/NM, Imperial. Depth bands 0-1, 1-2, 2-4 km. About 150-300 plays in the West (unverified count).
- **Prospect** j: a polygon. Seeds are the >1,430 Faulds favourable structural settings (median 13.4 km2), plus AI-mapped hidden FSS under basin cover, plus basin-hosted polygons for the 17% of known systems with no FSS (L02, L07). Each prospect is split into 1-12 segments on a 250 m sub-grid (Phase 2 Nevada PFA areas held 5-12 sub-targets, L01).
- **Parcel**: PLSS 40-acre aliquot or assessor parcel, through a crosswalk from a 500 m EPSG:5070 equal-area lattice (L35).

**Predicted quantities:**
- Per play: N_remaining(p) by size class (<10, 10-30, 30-100, >100 MWe), as a posterior; the forecast creaming curve E[cumulative MW | effort path, channel]; play chance P_play(element) for each shared element.
- Per prospect: Pg = P_play x P_local over five elements (heat, open pathway, fluid, cap/mask, freshness of faulting); a size posterior (lognormal-Pareto, M33 fit median about 29 MW, sigma about 0.95, tail index about 1.1, unverified), with a 0.5-0.7x haircut until our lookback exists (L17); Pc = Pg x P(MW > MEFS); the competing-risks failure distribution {no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil, success} (L40).
- Per parcel: RNPV/acre through the fixed M34 economics, with hydrothermal, EGS (P(>=200 C at <=4 km)), and closed-loop heads.

**Decision output:** a nightly book of actions with EMV, EMV per dollar, Pandora reservation value, leak cost and a frontier position. Actions: nominate or buy a right, option private heat, sense (2 m, darts, E-MT, Sentinel visit, chemistry bottle), drill TG or intermediate hole, drop, farm out, sell.

## 4. Components and methods

**[A] Discovery ledger.** An LLM agent pipeline reads GRC, SGW, OSTI, NBMG, USGS OFRs, BLM case files and company releases. For each known system it extracts: first-signal date, first-signal channel, deciding-signal date and channel, discovery depth, temperature, and size mark (EIA-860 nameplate or EIA-923 average output). L31 shows the shape: in 8 of 11 US blind cases the first signal was a hole drilled for something else. Every row carries citations, extraction confidence and an N=5 self-ensemble spread (L21). Target: about 400 dated systems in the West, 205 in the USGS Great Basin inventory (28 plants, 78 known, 99 probable).

**[B] Prospect inventory.** Petroleum ranks prospects, not pixels. We start from the Faulds polygons and add hidden ones. A U-Net fault and FSS segmenter is trained on 3DEP lidar, Sentinel-2, GeoDAWN magnetics and gravity, with the 1,430 polygons as labels. It is benchmarked on the DOE GEMS Prize test set (closes 3 Dec 2026, L07). It outputs typed polygons with a type posterior (DTZ, pull-apart, step-over, termination, intersection, accommodation zone). A second module draws basin-hosted prospects from basin-fill thickness, carbonate aquifers and warm-well clusters.

**[C] Evidence store.** Bitemporal GeoParquet plus a knowledge graph (L23, L35). Every fact has known_time, so any freeze date can be replayed without leakage.

**[D] Failure-mode ledger.** This is the geothermal Sodir FactPages that does not exist (L17). For every dry hole, cool TG hole, failed project and relinquished lease, an agent records the pre-drill evidence package, the outcome, and the failing element. Classes follow L40: no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked (cold lid), fossil, success. Seed sets: 10 cold Reese River TG wells, Argenta's 10 TG wells (24.35 C mean at 244 m), the 4 cool Gabbs holes, 54 of 82 OFR 99-425 prospects that never passed 10 F/100 ft, NDOM P&A TG holes (124), NVML 62 negatives, IFC's 2,613-well success data as a prior. Relinquished BLM leases (MLRS) enter as weak project-level negatives.

**[E] Learned risk tables.** Each element gets its own model. Inputs are evidence features plus an **evidence-quality** axis (data density, depth of sight, extraction confidence, kriging variance), exactly as a Milkov table crosses favourability with confidence. The model is LightGBM with monotone constraints (more heat flow cannot lower P(heat); older last-slip cannot raise freshness), then isotonic calibration on the failure-mode ledger. Low quality pulls the output toward the play prior, not toward zero. That is the oil rule that "no data" means "play chance", not "dry". The heat element takes the SENTINEL-COX or In-Context-Earth column posterior P(T(z) crosses the economic line) as its main input. The open-pathway element uses a Monte Carlo slip-tendency under a sampled stress tensor (L25) and a sealing clock (L40). The cap/mask element uses water-table depth, recharge, aquifer type and MT clay geometry. The fluid element uses mixing-aware chemistry posteriors (L26). The play prior P_play(e) is a hierarchical Beta across play x province, so Nevada evidence informs Idaho with shrinkage. Every cell's Pg can be printed as a five-row table that a BLM bidder or Ormat engineer can audit.

**[F] Multi-channel discovery-process model.** This is the core upgrade over petroleum. Classic Kaufman sampling assumes one channel with discoverability proportional to size^beta. M33 shows Nevada breaks this: McGinness Hills (about 160 MW) came 25+ years after the first plants. The reason is that springs found leaky systems, not big ones. So PLAYBOOK gives each channel c its own hazard:

h_ic(t) = E_c(x_i, t) x q_c x S_i^beta_c x L_i^gamma_c x g_c(depth_i, mask_i)

E_c is dated effort (NDWR driller reports by year, MRDS/MLRS mining density by decade, NDOM and DOGM spud dates, OFR 99-425 TG grids, INGENIOUS 2 m surveys, GeoDAWN flight dates). L_i is leakiness (a latent tied to mask class). beta_c is size bias; gamma_c is leakage bias; g_c is depth of sight. Expect beta near 0 and gamma large for springs; beta positive for oil-company TG grids that targeted size; depth-limited g for water wells. The likelihood is an ordered successive-sampling (Plackett-Luce) term over dated finds, times the survival term for the unfound pool. Unfound systems are integrated out with their locations drawn from the prospect Pg map, which ties [F] to [E]. Fitting uses NumPyro HMC for the per-play parameters with hierarchical pooling, and an amortised neural posterior estimator trained on 10^5-10^6 simulated discovery histories (L28) to check and speed it. The creaming curve then becomes a counterfactual tool: "if we add channel c with this effort, how much MW do we expect to find in play p over 5 years". That is the number that sets which play to buy into.

**[G] Play dependency network.** Each prospect has a latent element state that mixes a play factor z_p and a local factor u_j (Van Wees 2008; Martinelli 2013). A cool hole at j moves z_p and so lowers or raises every j' in the same play, in proportion to their shared-element weights. A hot hole does the reverse. This makes value of information non-local, as it is in oil, and it is why the engine may drill the most informative prospect first, not the best one.

**[H] Portfolio engine.** For each prospect: Pc, risked MW distribution, RNPV/acre, EMV per dollar of exposure. Bids are RNPV conditional on winning, corrected for winner's curse using a fitted crowd posterior (the same model refit on public layers only) and the count of rivals from BLM all-bids reports (L37). A Pandora reservation value per prospect (Weitzman 1979) decides when to stop sensing and lock the right. Drilling and sensing order uses depth-2 look-ahead on the dependency network, reported against a Brown-Smith information-relaxation bound (L15). The final set sits on an RNPV-versus-variance frontier with a cap on any single play's share, because the size tail is power-law and the top 3 Nevada fields hold 44% of MW (M33).

**[I] Lookback.** Every forecast is hashed and dated before a sale, a field season or a hole. After the outcome, the failing element is tagged and [E] is recalibrated. Oil's experience is the benchmark: once Chevron tracked chance of success, predicted and actual matched (Otis-Schneidermann 1997), while Sodir found volumes still 1.4-2.5x high, with only 47-58% of outcomes inside the predicted range (L17). So we track volume bias separately and keep the haircut until the lookback says otherwise.

## 6. Labels and negatives

Positives are dated finds with a channel and a size mark. Each enters [F] at its first-signal date. Unfound systems are not negatives. They sit in the survival term, so an unexplored prospect keeps its prior. Real negatives come only from the failure ledger, each with a failing element and a depth of sight. A cool 150 m TG hole is a heat failure to 150 m, not a play failure, and it reduces P_play only through z_p by its shared-element weight. Masked holes (reversals, isothermal lids) are "masked" outcomes, not "no heat". Relinquished leases are weak project-level negatives with an unknown cause, handled as a mixture. Base-rate anchors: first exploration well about 50% (IFC), project success under one-third (Wall-Dobson 2016), Zanskar's historic 1 in 550 holes. Labels are frozen by known_time in every hindcast, with a 10-20 km purge and a 2-year embargo around each test discovery (L19).

## 7. Uncertainty and validation

Uncertainty is hierarchical Bayes end to end: posterior draws over N_p, size parameters, channel biases, element chances and play factors. The size haircut and price are scenario axes, not hidden constants. Validation, in order of honesty:

1. **Simulation-based calibration** on synthetic Westerns with planted systems and known channel biases. Check that N_remaining and beta_c are recovered and that SBC rank histograms are flat.
2. **Creaming hindcast.** Freeze at 1990, 2008 and 2015. Forecast the count and MW of finds in the next period per play and per channel. Score with CSEP N-tests on counts and CRPS on cumulative MW (L27).
3. **Pg reliability.** Reliability diagrams and Brier decomposition of Pg against the failure ledger, with spatial-block (kNNDM) splits. Gate: calibration slope 0.8-1.2.
4. **Prospect capture.** Blind-only capture of post-freeze discoveries in the top 1%, 5% and 10% of area, against the 1-in-550 base rate, a distance-to-known smoother and a frozen-label GBM. The USGS 2025 map is shown, but not treated as a fair rival.
5. **Decision replay.** Replay BLM sales 2008-2025. Score proven MW and systems per $M locked against "lock the USGS top 10%".
6. **Live lookback.** Hashed forecasts before each sale and field season; conformal selection gives the buy list a stated false-discovery rate (L27).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane only; the scoring model is fixed, versioned code (L22, L39). Agent jobs: (1) build ledgers [A] and [D] from about 10^5 documents with N=5 self-ensembles and two-agent diffs; (2) date every data layer's known_time; (3) parse BLM all-bids, nominations and MLRS diffs nightly into the crowd and rival model; (4) propose new play definitions and risk-table rules from literature, kept only if the hindcast log-likelihood improves (co-scientist tournament scored by data, not Elo); (5) write the auditable five-element risk card and data-room pack per prospect. Compute: 10^6 simulated discovery histories for the NPE (a few GPU-days); HMC per province (CPU hours); 10^5 TOUGH-class column runs borrowed from the sibling designs for the heat element; nightly portfolio optimisation on one machine.

## 9. Why it beats Zanskar

Zanskar ranks pixels with LightGBM on INGENIOUS layers and reports 94.5% of hot springs in the top half of favourability (L05). It has no published count of what remains, no element decomposition, no play dependency, no channel-aware effort term and no lookback. PLAYBOOK answers different questions. Which plays are under-creamed for the channel we own? Where is a high-geology prospect cheap because every past channel was blind to it (B3 buried, B4 lidded, private ranch land)? Which element is the weakest link, so which sensor to buy? How much does one result move the other ten prospects in that play? These answers price rights before the crowd moves. Pumpernickel step-out land repriced 116x within about a month of disclosure (L37). PLAYBOOK also speaks the buyer's language: Pc, risked MW, EMV and AGRCC-style categories. That lifts sale prices through verifiable disclosure (Milgrom-Weber). And the hashed lookback track record cannot be copied.

## 10. How it drives rights buying and sensors

**Buying.** Rank prospects by (our RNPV - crowd RNPV - winner's-curse correction - leak cost) per acre. Route high-leak targets to private, state first-file or leftover parcels rather than BLM nominations. Lock when the Pandora reservation value is below the cost of locking now, and respect the play-concentration cap. Plays with large N_remaining but small effort from any channel get a land-first, sense-second strategy.

**Sensors.** Choose by the weakest element and by dependency reach. Heat-limited prospects get 2 m arrays, darts or TG holes (direct temperature decided 10 of 11 US blind cases, L31). Pathway-limited prospects get helium, seismic nodes or earth-tide loggers. Masked prospects get E-MT or deep logs. Score each action by EVSI over the whole play (through z_p) per dollar, and batch by mobilisation route. Our sensors are a new channel in [F]: the creaming curve should step up when we deploy, and if it does not, that is a kill signal.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Test: "2008 frozen Nevada creaming and play-ranking replay."** Build the Nevada discovery ledger and four dated effort channels with LLM extraction, using only data with known_time before 2009. Fit the multi-channel discovery model and a simple monotone heat and pathway risk table. Forecast finds from 2009-2025 (Blue Mountain, McGinness Hills, Don A. Campbell, Tungsten Mountain, North Valley, Dixie Meadows, Gabbs, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel and the USGS 99 probable systems as a softer set). Cost about $4-8k of LLM and compute plus 3-4 weeks of one person (unverified).

**Pass bar (all three):**
1. The 2009-2025 discovery count in Nevada falls inside the model's 90% interval (CSEP N-test), and the fitted channel biases recover the M33 finding (springs channel beta_c 90% interval includes 0).
2. The top third of plays by forecast remaining MW per unit effort contains at least 60% of the MW found after 2008, against about 33% for random.
3. Post-2008 blind finds land in the top 10% of prospects by Pc at least 2x more often than under the frozen-label GBM alone, with a bootstrap 90% interval that excludes 1x.

If test 1 fails but 2 and 3 pass, keep the risk tables and drop the count claim. If 2 and 3 fail, PLAYBOOK becomes only the economics and lookback wrapper for another design.

## 14. Biggest risks

- **Small n.** About 60-120 dated Nevada systems spread across many plays and channels. Mitigation: collapse to 3-4 channels, hierarchical pooling, global priors.
- **No creaming in geothermal.** M33 found size and order nearly unrelated. Mitigation: that is a parameter (beta_c near 0), not a failure, but count forecasts then lean on effort data, whose quality is the weak point.
- **Effort proxies are wrong.** Driller reports and mining density are crude. Mitigation: Fithian-style shared effort field across springs, mines, oil and water wells (L18), and our own designed survey cells.
- **Play definitions are subjective.** Mitigation: let the model learn the archetype as a latent; keep the "unknown play"; test play splits by hindcast log-likelihood.
- **Failure-mode labels are noisy.** Most dry-hole reports never say why. Mitigation: mixture over causes; confidence flags; post-drill tagging on our own holes.
- **Feature-vintage leakage.** Surveys done because of a find. Mitigation: bitemporal store, purge and embargo, red-team agent.
- **Dependency misspecified.** Over-strong play factors over-react to one cold hole. Mitigation: learn shared-element weights from the ledger; cap update size until the lookback calibrates them.
