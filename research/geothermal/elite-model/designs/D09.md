## 1. One-paragraph summary

KEYSTONE treats prospecting the way a senior geothermal geologist does it, but at the scale of the whole western US and with honest probabilities. It has three layers. (1) A **bitemporal geothermal knowledge graph** (GeoKG) holds every fact we know: wells, readings, springs, samples, faults, settings, surveys, leases, parcels, actors, documents and dated discoveries. Each fact carries provenance, known-time, location error, extraction noise and the search channel that produced it. (2) A **library of play-type conceptual models**, written as **probabilistic programs**. Each program is a small structural causal model of one kind of system: heat source, upflow pathway, fluid, cap and masking, outflow geometry, and how each sensor would see it. There are templates for B1 shallow outflow, B2 range-hosted with no discharge, B3 basin-buried capped, B4 cold-aquifer lid, B5 rain curtain, B6 carbonate sweep, B7 conductive sedimentary aquifer, EGS-conductive basement, magmatic arc and rift, plus a maximum-entropy "unknown play". (3) A **hierarchical inference engine** conditions these programs on the graph. It uses neural emulators for the heavy physics, amortised inference networks for speed, learned soft rules for expert knowledge, and a small gated neural residual for whatever the rules miss. The existence layer stays an effort-thinned log-Gaussian Cox process, as in v1. The difference is that every number has a **case file**: the posterior over which play this is, the weakest link among heat, pathway, fluid and cap, the exact log-likelihood contribution of each piece of evidence (traceable to a page and bounding box), and counterfactuals such as "if this hole had read cold, P(system) would fall from 0.31 to 0.07". The case file drives the VOI engine (which hurdle to test next, with which sensor) and the data room we sell from. AI agents build and audit the graph, propose rules and new templates, and write case files. They never change the scorer directly. A rule or template enters only if it lifts held-out, point-in-time log-likelihood.

## 2. Target variables

**Grids.** Existence intensity on a 2 km equal-area mesh (EPSG:5070). Temperature columns and value on 500 m cells, refined to 125 m inside favourable structural settings (FSS) and optioned parcels. Values roll up to PLSS 40-acre aliquots and assessor parcels by a precomputed crosswalk (L35). H3 res 8 is a join key only.

**Physical latents per cell (posterior draws):**
- `a`: play archetype (categorical, 11 classes including "unknown").
- `psi`: P(an upflow lies within 2 km), split by archetype.
- Four mineral-systems fields: heat `H`, open pathway `P`, fluid `F`, cap/seal `C`. Existence intensity is log-additive in them (L16, L23).
- Mask state `M` (lid, sweep, rain curtain, none) shared by all detectors.
- `u`: upflow location relative to the warm evidence, placed up hydraulic gradient along structure (L31, L40).
- T(z) at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6 km as ordinal bins.
- Failure-mode ladder: {no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil, success} (L40).
- Size mark in MWe, P10/P50/P90, by archetype (Wilmarth-Stimac power densities for >200 C extensional fields; M33 fit below that; a lower ceiling for arcs; L09). A 0.5-0.7x haircut applies until our own lookback exists (L17).

**Value heads (fixed economics, not learned):** v1 hydrothermal RNPV/acre on the sloped economic line (about 109 C at 1 km and 152 C at 3 km at $100/MWh); an EGS head, P(>=200 C at <=4 km TVD) plus isotherm depths; a superhot head, P(>=374 C by 6 km); and P_det, the chance the system is already known.

**Crowd posterior.** The same programs conditioned only on facts flagged public at time t. This is natural in the graph, because every fact has a known-time and an access flag.

**Decision output.** A nightly ranked action list per cell and per portfolio: nominate, option ranch heat, file a state lease, buy a leftover BLM parcel, sense (desk extraction, 2 m line, dart, E-MT, nodes, TG hole), drill, drop, farm out, sell. Each action carries EV per dollar, the hurdle it tests, leak cost and a lease-clock deadline. Every buy comes with its case file.

## 4. Components and methods

**4.1 GeoKG.** A typed property graph stored as GeoParquet tables (nodes and edges) queried by DuckDB, with an RDF export using GeoSciML 4.1, USGS GeMS and CGI vocabularies (L23, L35). Facts are append-only and bitemporal. Each edge carries `source_sha256`, `page`, `bbox`, `model_id`, `prompt_hash`, `sigma`, `loc_err_m`, `channel` (spring survey, O&G, mining, water well, TG, our sensor), `known_time`, `public_flag`. Negative facts are first-class: "Hole X, 150 m, 29 C, explicitly cold"; "driller report, temperature absent" (L18, L21). Entity resolution agents link one hole across NBMG, NDOM, SMU and GRC records. Graph contradictions, such as a warm well next to a cold TG hole, are queued as cheap, high-value VOI targets (L23).

**4.2 Template library.** Each play type is a probabilistic program of about 200-600 lines in NumPyro (JAX) with Pyro-style effect handlers, so we can intervene (`do`) and condition. A template states the causal DAG explicitly:

`basal flux, conductivity -> conductive T(z)`; `FSS type, stress tensor, time since slip -> P(open)` (sealing clock, L40); `recharge, aquifer, hydraulic gradient -> mask M and outflow vector`; `H, P, F, C -> upflow exists`; `upflow, M -> each observation`.

Templates share sub-programs (the column, the stress Monte Carlo, the mixing chemistry), so B3 and B4 differ only in cap and lid modules. Initial parameters come from the literature: DTZ enrichment about 4.8x, pull-aparts 2.2x, step-overs about 0.85x (L01, L02, L09); fault-age multiplier x0.1 to x10 as a prior on a monotone learned curve (L01); 1-2 m probe noise floor about 1.2 C and seasonal wave about +/-5 C (L38); cold lid at Kimama, 4.5 C/km to about 950 m then 72-75 C/km (L40). Every template must pass BEL-style prior falsification (L14): it must be able to generate data like the real data before it is used.

**4.3 Rule bank.** Expert and literature rules become weighted soft factors (a semantic-loss or Markov-logic style term) or monotone constraints. Examples: "sinter or alteration with no live signal raises P(fossil)"; "helium R/Ra raises P only when thermal evidence is present" (L26); "temperature inversions are allowed only under an outflow archetype" (L10); boiling-point-with-depth as a hard cap. Each rule has a learned weight with a shrinkage prior toward zero. LLM agents mine SGW, GRC and NBMG papers for candidate rules. A rule is kept only if it improves held-out point-in-time log-likelihood under kNNDM CV (L19). This is the anti-"Cursed?" gate: adding a rule or layer must earn its place (L23).

**4.4 Neural parts (the "neuro").**
- Forward emulators: a shared Basin-and-Range fault, cap and basal-heat FNO trained on about 5,000-20,000 TOUGH3/Waiwera runs, with a local high-resolution correction near faults (L11, L12). It outputs temperature, resistivity and spring chemistry, so MT and chemistry enter physically.
- A mixing-aware chemistry inference net trained on PHREEQC/GeoT runs, giving reservoir T and hot fraction per water sample (L26).
- An MT operator that takes 50-200 RTO or trans-D inversion members per station, with false-conductor classes (basin clay, brine, fossil cap) tied to `M` (L13).
- An In-Context-Earth-style transformer gives the conductive T(z) prior mean (L10).
- A **gated neural residual** on log-intensity (LightGBM or a small MLP on AlphaEarth PCs and INGENIOUS layers). It has a tight shrinkage prior. Its contribution is reported in every case file as "unexplained by any play". If the residual carries more than about 25% of a cell's log-odds, the cell is flagged for a new template, not trusted.
- Amortised inference: inference compilation (Le, Baydin and Wood 2017, unverified details) trains a network on traces from the templates. It proposes archetype and latent values for any subset of observations, Simformer-style (L28). SMC then corrects the proposals, so the explanation remains exact for the program.

**4.5 Existence and effort.** As in v1, existence is a log-Gaussian Cox process. KEYSTONE adds: per-archetype label frequency (PULSNAR-style, L19); a shared Fithian-style effort field across springs, mines, oil wells, water wells and mineral occurrences (L18); Diggle preferential sampling for well locations; an Arps-Roberts discovery-efficiency term per channel (L17). Effort layers enter only the thinning term. Label-proxy features (springs, KGRA lines, lease history) are banned from the existence term (L19).

**4.6 Explanation engine.** Because the likelihood factorises over facts, the log posterior odds of `psi` for a cell decompose exactly into a prior term, plus a sum of per-fact log Bayes factors, plus a residual term. The case file lists the top positive and negative facts with KG citations; the archetype posterior; the weakest of H, P, F, C; and counterfactuals computed by re-running inference with one fact removed or flipped (`do` operations). The explanation is the model, not a post-hoc SHAP approximation.

**4.7 Decision engine.** The VOI-Bank spine from v1, upgraded. EVSI is computed per hurdle: heat-limited cells get TG holes or 2 m lines; pathway-limited cells get nodes, helium or earth-tide loggers; lidded cells get MT or deep logs (L40). A depth-2 look-ahead portfolio POMDP chooses joint sensing and land actions (L15). Because the play term is shared, one cool hole moves every cell in the same FSS and play, so the engine orders holes by how much they teach the portfolio (L17). Bids use our RNPV conditional on winning, corrected for rival count and the crowd posterior gap (L37).

## 6. Labels and negatives

Positives are the dated systems in the KG: the 2026 USGS inventory of 205 systems, the 403 INGENIOUS systems at 37 C or hotter, Zanskar announcements (Big Blind, Pumpernickel), the Nevada PFA holes and the DOE 2026 exploration-drilling sites as they report. Each positive carries a first-signal date, discovery channel and, where the literature supports it, an archetype tag drawn from extracted conceptual-model cards.

There are no "negative cells". Negatives are observations: cold TG holes (Argenta, Reese River's 10 wells, the four cool Gabbs holes), cold oil and water wells, and driller reports with no heat mention. Each enters as a censored, depth-limited reading through the archetype's observation operator. A cold 150 m hole in a B4-lid cell barely lowers `psi`. The same hole in a B2 range-hosted cell lowers it a lot. That asymmetry is the main reason to model play type explicitly. Dry deep holes get a failure-mode tag (no heat, tight, outflow, masked, fossil) extracted from completion reports, so the template that failed is the one that gets penalised.

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

Quantified: full posterior draws; structural uncertainty by averaging over templates, with the "unknown play" component taking mass when no template fits; extraction noise from self-ensemble spread; location boxes; model misspecification flagged per cell by an MMD test between observed data and template predictive draws (L28).

Validated, in order of honesty:
1. **Simulation-based calibration** on synthetic western worlds sampled from the templates themselves. A generative model gives us planted truth for free.
2. **Prior and posterior falsification** per province (BEL, L14).
3. **kNNDM spatial CV** with random and block CV shown beside it (L19).
4. **Point-in-time hindcasts** frozen at 1980, 1990, 2005 and 2015 on the KG's known-time, with 10-20 km purge, scored on the 11-case blind ladder (L31) and on later discoveries with Molchan curves, Boyce index and CSEP N/L/S tests (L27, L36).
5. **Explanation validity**: in hindcasts, the case file's top evidence must match the published discovery narrative, and the archetype must match the expert type.
6. **Pre-registered predictions** hashed before each BLM sale and field season, starting with NBMG's Granite Springs 68-02.
7. Release gates: 85-95% coverage of 90% T(z) bands on held-out logs; SBC rank histograms flat; the buy list issued by conformal selection with a stated false-discovery rate (L27).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane and the knowledge work. They never edit the scorer at run time (L39).
- **Extraction agents** read about 132k NDWR reports, 1.11M CA OSWCR records, NBMG and DOGM scans, OSTI and GRC papers. They use N=5 self-ensembles and feed spread into sigma (L21).
- **Graph curator agents** resolve entities, enforce unit, datum and nodata contracts, and flag contradictions.
- **Conceptual-model author agents** turn SGW/GRC/WGC conceptual-model figures into structured cards, then draft new template programs. A template is admitted only after it passes prior falsification, SBC and a held-out likelihood test.
- **Rule proposer agents** run a co-scientist-style tournament, but rank by held-out log-likelihood gain, not LLM votes (L22).
- **Skeptic agents** test claims with the column simulator and hunt for post-discovery leakage in hindcasts (L36).
- **Watcher agents** track BLM MLRS, state permits, GDR and USGS releases nightly and trigger re-scores.
- **Case-file writers** turn the explanation tables into readable dossiers for deals.

Compute: about 10^5-10^6 emulator-backed simulations to train amortised inference (a few thousand GPU-hours); about 20k TOUGH3 runs for the FNO (CPU cluster weeks); nightly inference over about 12M West cells in batched JAX on 8-16 GPUs. The graph is about 10^8 facts, which fits in a DuckDB lake on one large machine (L35).

## 9. Why it beats Zanskar

- **Play-aware negatives.** GeoCore trains on 148 cold wells against 6,009 warm ones (L05). KEYSTONE turns every cold or silent hole into a play-conditioned censored observation, so tens of thousands of holes become usable, and a cold hole means different things under different plays.
- **Transfer where labels are thin.** Gradient boosting learns Nevada correlations. Templates carry physics and play logic into Oregon, Idaho, New Mexico, Utah basins and abroad, where competitors' labels run out. Hierarchical sharing across plays does the rest.
- **Blind archetypes on purpose.** Big Blind is a deep unvented B3-type (L09). KEYSTONE gives B3 a non-zero prior and lets effort thinning explain its rarity, instead of learning that "hot means near springs".
- **Auditable value.** Buyers, BLM bidders and ranch owners see a causal story with citations. Verifiable disclosure raises sale prices (L37). Zanskar's FNO and GBM stack gives scores without a chain of evidence.
- **Hurdle-specific sensing.** Knowing which leg is weakest lets us buy the right cheap test first, so we spend less per confirmed system.
- **A lookback loop.** Every pre-drill forecast is hashed and every failure is tagged by mode, building the calibration record geothermal lacks (L17).

## 10. Rights buying and sensor deployment

Each night the engine ranks cells by (our RNPV/acre minus crowd RNPV/acre minus expected leak cost) per dollar. Cells with a large gap on private or state land go to ranch-heat options and state first-file. Leftover BLM parcels are bought outright. High-leak targets avoid nomination. Sensor plans are built per hurdle and batched per mobilisation, with travel cost and lease clocks in the objective (L38). After each batch, the posterior updates, case files refresh, and contradictions in the graph become the next targets. A max-entropy "unknown play" keeps sensors able to overturn a wrong template (L38).

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Test: the "Nevada 2005 case-file hindcast".** Cost: about $5-8k of compute and API, two people, eight weeks. Build the Nevada graph from keyed data plus the NBMG scan extraction, frozen on known-time at 2005. Fit four templates plus "unknown". Score the post-2005 blind and hidden finds (Tungsten Mountain, Don A. Campbell, Gabbs Valley, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel and the other ladder cases, L31), and compare with three baselines on the same graph: a flat LightGBM, a distance-to-known smoother, and the 1-in-550 base rate.

**Pass if all of these hold:**
1. Blind capture in the top 5% of area is at least equal to the flat LightGBM, and the log score under kNNDM CV is better by a bootstrap interval that excludes zero.
2. Cold-hole asymmetry works: for the 12 Nevada PFA holes plus Argenta and Reese River, the model's predictive log-likelihood beats the flat model.
3. Explanations hold up: in at least 8 of 11 ladder cases, the case file's top three facts include the signal the literature names as decisive, and the assigned archetype matches the expert type in at least 70% of 50 held-out known systems.
4. SBC: the archetype and `psi` posteriors are calibrated on synthetic Nevadas (90% intervals cover 85-95%).

If item 1 fails but 2-4 pass, KEYSTONE still ships as the explanation and negative-evidence layer over a stronger ranker. Untested lift alone does not kill it.

## 14. Biggest risks

1. **Confident but wrong structure.** If the templates miss a real play, the posterior can be sharp and wrong. Fixes: the "unknown play" component, per-cell MMD misspecification tests, the gated residual, and agent-drafted new templates.
2. **Inference on discrete latents is hard.** Archetype times mask times upflow position mixes poorly. Fixes: amortised proposals plus SMC, and per-cell independence given regional fields.
3. **Graph errors propagate into explanations.** A wrong geocode becomes a confident citation. Fixes: self-ensemble sigma, location boxes, two-agent diffs, and pass^k gates (L39).
4. **Expert bias baked in as rules.** The Nevada PFA fault-age multipliers are expert guesses (L01). Fixes: rules are weighted priors with shrinkage and must earn held-out lift.
5. **Too few labels per archetype.** B3 and B6 may have only a handful of examples. Fixes: synthetic worlds, global analogues and hierarchical pooling.
6. **Speed of build.** Writing 11 templates well is slow. Fixes: shared sub-programs and agent drafting, starting with four.
7. **Black boxes rank better.** A flat GBM may win on pure ranking. Fix: keep it as the residual and as a baseline. KEYSTONE's value is negatives, transfer, sensor choice and auditable deals.
