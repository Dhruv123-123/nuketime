# D01 · SENTINEL-COX v2 ("SCX2")

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Architect: D01 agent. Builds on `../../model/MODEL.md` (SENTINEL-COX v1), `../../MEMO.md` and literature digests `../evidence/L01`-`L40`. It does not restate v1. It says what changes and why. No new web searches were run; numbers come from the L-files, which cite primary sources. My own arithmetic and unchecked claims are marked (unverified).

## 1. Summary

SCX2 keeps v1's two best ideas: the effort-thinned Cox count, and the decision-first RNPV/acre spine. It replaces almost everything between them. v1 had seven weaknesses: one Great Basin likelihood with hand-set shapes; a single 4-state mask class; a 1D column only; heat and permeability folded into one term; a one-step, per-cell VOI; no model of rivals or what the crowd already knows; and no point-in-time data store, so its hindcasts could leak. SCX2 rebuilds it on four new foundations. (1) A **bitemporal evidence graph**: every fact carries valid time, known time, provenance, location error and search channel. (2) A **mineral-systems product of four latent fields**, heat x open pathway x fluid x cap, under a **categorical archetype latent** (B1-B7, EGS-conductive, magmatic, rift, plus a max-entropy "unknown play"), so each parcel shows its weakest link. (3) An **amortised, simulation-based column engine**. A Simformer-style network is trained on 10^6 multi-fidelity hydrothermal simulations. It reads ragged token sets of raw observations and returns posteriors on T(z), upflow offset, permeability class and archetype in milliseconds. It falls back to v1's censored 1D likelihood when inputs fall outside its training range. (4) A **portfolio POMDP decision engine**. It competes against a fitted **crowd posterior**, the map rivals can build from public data alone, and it buys rights only where our edge minus winner's-curse and leak costs is positive. The model widens from the Great Basin to the whole western US through hierarchical province pooling, and to the world through Moeck play-type sharing. It ships hydrothermal, EGS, superhot and closed-loop value heads off one posterior. It is validated by point-in-time hindcasts, kNNDM CV, CSEP tests and a hashed pre-registration ledger.

## 2. Target variables

**Grids.** The existence field sits on a 2 km equal-area mesh (EPSG:5070 in the US; local equal-area projections abroad). Temperature columns and parcels sit on 500 m cells, refined to 125 m inside favourable structural settings (FSS) and optioned parcels. Values roll up to PLSS 40-acre aliquots and assessor parcels through a precomputed crosswalk. H3 res 8 is used only as a join key, because it is not equal-area (L35).

**Physical latents per cell (posterior draws, not point estimates):**
- psi: P(a system's upflow lies within 2 km), split by archetype a.
- T(z) at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 km, as 128 ordinal bins (In-Context Earth style, L10).
- Upflow offset vector: where the upflow sits relative to the warm evidence (L31, L40).
- Open-pathway class (Libbey's four permeability classes) and P(critically stressed) per fault segment.
- Failure-mode ladder (competing risks, L40): {no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil, success}.
- Size mark: MWe at P10/P50/P90, with a 0.5-0.7x haircut until our own lookback exists (L17).

**Value heads (fixed, auditable economics, scenario axes for price, ITC and recovery factor):**
- Hydrothermal RNPV/acre, using the v1 sloped economic line (about 109 C at 1 km and 152 C at 3 km at $100/MWh).
- EGS head: P(>=200 C at <=4 km TVD), depth to the 150/200/250/300 C isotherms, basement depth, and a stress/frackability score. Fervo's screen is 300-625 F above about 13,000 ft (L06, L30).
- Superhot head: P(>=374 C by 6 km).
- Closed-loop head: conduction-only MW per loop, from a rock-conductivity posterior.
- P_det(2026): the chance that someone already knows about the system.
- Crowd psi: what a public-data-only model would say.

**Decision output:** a nightly ranked action list per cell and portfolio. Actions are buy, nominate, option a ranch, file a state lease, sense (desk extraction, Sentinel, 2 m, dart, E-MT, nodes, TG hole), drill, drop, farm-out or sell. Each action carries expected value per dollar, leak cost and a lease-clock deadline. The buy list is issued with a conformal false-discovery guarantee (L27).

## 4. Components and methods

**E0. Bitemporal evidence graph.** All data go into one lake: R2/S3 storage, GeoParquet for points and tables, COG for rasters, Zarr/Icechunk for cubes and posterior draws, and DuckDB as the only engine (L35). Each fact has `valid_time` and `known_time`. Hindcasts frozen at 1980, 1990, 2000, 2008, 2015 and 2020 join only on `known_time <= t*`. That also covers features: a gravity survey run because a system was found must not feed a freeze earlier than that find (L19). The graph uses GeoSciML/GeMS/CGI terms (L23). Dry holes, cold wells and lease status are first-class nodes. Contradictions, such as a warm well next to a cold TG hole, are flagged and sent to VOI as cheap targets.

**E1. Representation layer.**
- *Structure.* A U-Net/transformer segmenter on 3DEP lidar, GeoDAWN and GeoFlight flight-line magnetics, gravity gradients and drainage. It is trained on Faulds' 1,430+ FSS polygons and scored on the DOE GEMS Prize test set (L07). It outputs typed FSS polygons with probabilities, including under basin cover. A GNN passes messages over the fault graph: tips, step-overs and intersections are nodes, and segments with slip rate and age are edges (L16, L29). Expert fault-age multipliers (x0.1 to x10) and slip-rate multipliers (x1 to x2) become monotone learned curves, with the expert values as priors (L01).
- *Stress and sealing clock.* All public western waveforms since about 2000 are re-picked with PhaseNet/EQTransformer plus template matching. The target is roughly 10x more events and an Mc about 1 unit lower (L25, unverified). Focal mechanisms from the new catalogue are inverted for a Bayesian SHmax field on a 10-20 km grid. An FSP-style Monte Carlo then gives P(critically stressed) per segment and depth. P(open) decays with time since last slip and resets with Quaternary offset, microseismicity, InSAR/NISAR strain and dynamic-triggering sensitivity (L40).
- *Conductive prior.* ICE-West: the In-Context Earth transformer (4.7 C US MAE; L10) is fine-tuned with typed tokens (raw BHT, shut-in time, era, extraction sigma). The Burns 2024 and Sui 2025 models serve as prior means. Variance grows steeply below the deepest nearby calibration well (L13).
- *MT.* Every public EDI (SPUD/EarthScope, USGS, GDR) gets a 1D trans-D Bayesian inversion, amortised by an NPE. That gives a West-wide atlas of conductor probability and depth spread, with false-conductor classes (basin clay, brine, fossil cap) (L13).
- *Chemistry.* A mixing-aware NPE trained on PHREEQC/GeoT forward runs gives reservoir T, hot fraction and cold end-member for about 50,000 samples. Lab and era bias terms cover NURE, 1970s USGS and modern ICP-MS (L26). Helium enters only as an open-pathway signal (L26).
- *Embeddings.* 8-16 principal components of AlphaEarth embeddings at 500 m. A learned "built infrastructure" direction is projected out and sent to the effort term (L20).

**E2. Column engine.** v1 used hand-set plume kernels on a 1D column. SCX2 learns those shapes from physics. A multi-fidelity simulator bank does the work: 10^6 analytic Tier 1 runs; about 2x10^4 2.5D Waiwera/TOUGH3 natural-state runs over FSS type, dip, permeability, basal flux, aquifer sweep, cap and recharge; and about 5,000 3D runs that train an FNO plus a local DeepONet correction near faults (L11, L12). Simulated observations include drilling disturbance, BHT censoring, 2 m seasonal waves, location error and extraction noise. A Simformer (L28) is trained over this bank. It conditions on any subset of a cell's tokens, so ragged public data needs no per-source pipeline. Each release must pass SBC rank tests and TARP coverage. NPE ensembles of 5-10 networks counter overconfidence. An MMD misspecification test, plus a check for ensemble disagreement, routes out-of-range cells to the v1 censored likelihood and onto a "visit to learn" list. Temperature is split into a conductive mean plus a convective residual, governed by the archetype and flow regime. Hard limits stop impossible inversions and boiling-curve breaches.

**E3. Existence and discovery (CREAM-COX v2).**
- *Four-field product.* log lambda_a(s) sums four log-fields: heat (H), open pathway (P), fluid (F) and cap/preservation (C), each a Hilbert-space GP plus covariates. This follows the Hawaii PrH x PrP x PrF structure (L08), with weights learned hierarchically across provinces instead of set by experts.
- *Archetype latent.* A softmax prior over province, FSS type, fill thickness, recharge and aquifer type, initialised at DTZ about 4.8x and pull-apart about 2.2x (L01, L09). It has separate intensity offsets for 120-160 C and >160 C targets.
- *Effort.* The Fithian approach: one shared effort field, fit jointly on springs, mines, oil wells, water wells and MRDS occurrences (L18). Catch rates q vary by channel, archetype and province, pooled hierarchically. That lets SCX2 use more than v1's three channels without losing identifiability. A Diggle preferential-sampling term corrects the warm bias in drilled valleys. The GDR 616 "Degree of Exploration" layer, BLM bid history and AEF footprint enter only here (L01, L06).
- *Size and creaming.* A Kaufman-Balcer size-biased successive-sampling model over the dated US discovery sequence gives an independent N_hidden check per province (L17, L18).
- *Shared play risk.* A random effect per (FSS type x basin) means one cool hole lowers the odds of its neighbours (L17).

**E4. Value heads.** These are open GETEM/SAM forks. Constant drilling success is replaced by the posterior and flow class (L32). Well cost is a lognormal draw (±50%) from the Lowry curves. Flow and drawdown risk is its own wide term, so a sharp thermal posterior cannot inflate value (L06). Output follows the ThermoGIS contract: per-cell P90/P50/P10 MW and LCOE, plus risk and land status (L34).

**E5. Crowd posterior and rivals.** The same pipeline is refit with public, keyed layers only: INGENIOUS, USGS 2025/2026, GDR, announcements, and no Stage 0 extraction or sensors. The result approximates what Zanskar or a BLM bidder can know. An inverse model of rival beliefs is fit from BLM all-bids reports, nominations (more than 1M acres in 2025, 27% duplicates; L37) and MLRS cases. These feed competition intensity only, never geology.

**E6. Portfolio POMDP.** The state is the E3 posterior draws plus our holdings, rival activity and lease clocks. Planning runs POMCPOW at depth 2-3 online, reported against a Brown-Smith information-relaxation upper bound (L15). A BetaZero-style value network, trained offline on millions of synthetic campaigns in GHOST worlds, ranks every western cell nightly. Each parcel gets a Weitzman/Pandora reservation value, which decides when to stop measuring and just lock the right. EVSI is computed over the shared play terms, so the planner can choose the most informative hole first. Bid = E[RNPV | win] - winner's-curse correction - expected leak cost. Leak cost is how far the crowd posterior would move on the public signal. High-leak targets go to private, state first-file or leftover-parcel routes (L37). The final buy list uses conformal selection, Benjamini-Hochberg on spatially weighted conformal p-values by province and archetype, with a stated FDR (L27).

## 6. Labels and negatives

- Positives are dated and channel-tagged. Sources: the USGS 205-system inventory, the 2008 identified systems, the 11-case blind ladder (L31), Zanskar's announced finds, and weak positives parsed from filings. The 99 USGS probable systems are a held-out set with a learned confirmation rate.
- Negatives are graded, censored observations, never unlabelled cells. Examples: the 10 cold Reese River and Argenta TG wells (46 C/km against a regional 56; L02, L04), the Nevada PFA cool Gabbs holes, NDOM P&A TG holes, failed company grids, and the 4,293 flow-disturbed wells USGS discards (L03). A cold shallow hole lowers P(shallow anomaly). It does not zero P(reservoir at 800 m) where a recharge aquifer is mapped.
- Every dry hole gets a failure-mode tag (no heat, tight, outflow, masked, fossil). An AI pass over completion reports and mud logs builds the first public failure-mode table (L40).
- Every extracted reading has a certain or uncertain flag and a learned confusion rate (Royle-Link; L18).
- A stratified-random, design-based cell sample is part of the field budget. It is the only unbiased accuracy estimate (L36).

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

Quantified:
- About 4,000 joint draws carry shared parameters, so regional sums are not falsely narrow.
- NPE ensembles plus area-of-applicability dissimilarity give cells far from data wide posteriors.
- Variance is split into count/detection, masking/depth, size/permeability and economics. The first three can be shrunk by sensors. Economics is a scenario axis handed to the buyer.

Validated (all are release gates):
1. **SBC and TARP** on 50 synthetic western worlds with real effort geometry. Planted N must fall inside its 90% interval 85-95% of the time.
2. **Point-in-time hindcasts** at the six freezes, with purge (10-20 km) and embargo (2 years). Report blind-only area capture at 1/5/10% of area, the Boyce index, and Molchan diagrams. Each must beat three baselines: the 1-in-550 base rate, a distance-to-known smoother, and the USGS XGBoost rerun under spatial blocks (L03, L36).
3. **kNNDM CV**, with the target nearest-neighbour curve taken from unexplored cells. Random and block CV are shown beside it. Leave-one-province-out. An adversarial check confirms latitude and longitude alone cannot predict labels.
4. **CSEP N, L and S tests** on the 2010 freeze against 2010-2025 finds (L27).
5. **Temperature:** PIT KS under 3% and 85-95% coverage of 90% bands on 10 km blocks, reported by depth band.
6. **Transfer:** Western Anatolia OOD test with designed negatives (L34).
7. **Prospective:** hashed, timestamped forecasts before every BLM sale, NBMG release (Granite Springs 68-02) and field season, scored later.
8. **Decision replay:** proven MWe and systems per $M locked, against "lock USGS top 10%", with bootstrap CIs.

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane only. The scorer is fixed, versioned code that agents cannot edit at run time (L22, L39). Roles:
- **Extractors.** Five independent model and prompt runs per document. The median is the value; the spread is the extraction sigma. High-spread items go to a human. Targets: NBMG's 2,002 scans, about 132k NDWR reports, CA OSWCR's 1.1M, NDOM and UT DOGM files, and UNU-GTP/NEDO/MTA/CFE multilingual archives.
- **Linker.** Joins the same hole across NBMG, NDOM, SMU and GRC sources.
- **Watchers.** Track BLM MLRS, state permits, GDR, USGS and the 16 DOE 2026 drilling sites.
- **Prior-claimant agent.** Checks leases and claims for each target.
- **Physics-skeptic agent.** Tests ML hotspots against the simulator.
- **Hypothesis tournament.** Proposes conceptual models per basin. Concepts are ranked by held-out log-likelihood gain, not by LLM Elo.
- **Red-team leakage agent.** Hunts for post-discovery data before any result is published.
- **Engineering rules.** Handbook cards per source; unit, datum and nodata contracts; pass^5 above 95% on a planted-trap golden suite.

Compute: about 10^6 analytic plus 2x10^4 Waiwera runs is roughly 50k CPU-hours (unverified). Training the FNO, Simformer and value network is about 2-5k GPU-hours. Seismic re-picking over 25 years of western waveforms is about 20-50k GPU-hours (unverified). Nightly inference on 12M western cells is minutes on 8 GPUs, because the posteriors are amortised. Extraction is about 2-5M pages x 5 reads, roughly $50-150k of VLM calls (unverified).

## 9. Why it beats Zanskar

- **Labels.** GeoCore trains on 6,009 warm wells against 148 cold ones (L05). SCX2 turns tens of thousands of shallow, cold and silent wells into censored observations and graded effort. It adds a failure-mode table no one else has.
- **Effort.** Zanskar has no discovery-process term. SCX2 can say a cell is unexplored rather than empty, which is exactly where blind systems are.
- **Physics.** Zanskar is moving to FNO surrogates (L11, L12), but with no amortised inference and no masking physics. SCX2 trains on masked and lidded worlds and outputs the upflow offset.
- **Skill reporting.** Zanskar reports 94.5% of springs in the top half of its map. SCX2 reports blind-only capture, calibration and a dated record. That is what buyers and AGRCC-style resource reports need.
- **Game theory.** The crowd posterior and leak cost let us act only where we know more, and route around public nominations.
- **Geography and play type.** SCX2 is built for private and state land, basin-hosted plays (the 17% with no FSS), arcs, rifts, EGS and superhot. GeoCore is Great Basin federal land.

## 10. Rights buying and sensor deployment

- **Buy** when the Pandora reservation value says further sensing is worth less than locking now and the bid clears E[RNPV | win] with an edge over the crowd posterior. In practice that means ranch options and state first-file on private and state land, and leftover BLM parcels at $1.50-3.50/acre.
- **Sense by hurdle.** Heat-limited cells get re-logs, darts or TG holes. Pathway-limited cells get earth-tide loggers, He samplers or nodes. Capped or lidded cells get E-MT or deep logs (L40). In Basin and Range cells, 2 m arrays and TG holes come before MT (L31).
- **Calibration first.** The first field spend is a calibration campaign: about 20 known blind systems, 40 cold controls and two detectors per cell. It measures the sensor reliability matrix per archetype (L38).
- **Batching.** Visits are batched per mobilisation, and the plan is redone after each batch.
- **Clocks.** Lease primary terms and diligence deadlines are part of the state, so spend is timed before expiry.
- **Selling.** Data-room packs contain AGRCC/CanGEA categories, calibration plots and lineage. Farm-out terms come from a Cozzolino RAV optimiser, with the carry floored near Crump's 20% (L37).

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**"Freeze-2008 Nevada duel."** Cost: about $3-6k of compute and VLM calls, in 3-4 weeks. No simulator bank and no field work.
1. Build the point-in-time NV store at known_time 2008.
2. Fit SCX2-lite: the four-field Cox with an archetype prior from FSS type, shared multi-source effort, and censored column likelihoods using the analytic Tier 1 only. Inputs are keyed data plus NBMG scan extraction.
3. Fit two rivals on the same freeze: (a) the USGS-style ordinal XGBoost; (b) a crowd model on public keyed layers only.
4. Score on post-2008 blind or hidden finds and confirmations with first signal after 2008: Gabbs Valley, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel, San Emidio SW, plus the 99 probable systems as a pooled secondary set, and the Reese River and Argenta cold holes as designed negatives.

**Pass bar:**
- SCX2-lite captures at least 2x as many post-2008 targets in the top 5% of area as the XGBoost baseline, pooled with probable systems, with the bootstrap CI above 1.
- It ranks the cold Reese River and Argenta holes below the 60th percentile while XGBoost ranks them in the top 20%.
- CSEP N-test p > 0.05.
- Temperature 90% coverage is 85-95% on held-out logs.

A miss does not kill the model. It points to which component to fix first.

## 14. Biggest risks

1. **Simulator misspecification.** The amortised posterior is only as good as the simulated worlds. If real Great Basin systems lie outside the prior, the NPE is confidently wrong. Mitigations: MMD gating, the 1D fallback, and the archetype library extracted from conceptual-model figures.
2. **Too few blind positives to tell models apart.** There are about 10 dated blind finds. Mitigations: pool warm-well evidence, use designed negatives, run the design-based field sample and the prospective ledger. Skill claims stay wide for 1-2 years.
3. **Effort identifiability.** Even with shared effort, q_c and N_hidden may trade off. The two-detector survey is mandatory before any regional number is quoted.
4. **Leakage through feature vintage** in hindcasts. Mitigations: the bitemporal store and the red-team agent. Dating 1970s-80s surveys may still be fuzzy.
