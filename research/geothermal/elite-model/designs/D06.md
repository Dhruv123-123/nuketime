# D06 · HEARTHNET: a global token-fusion network with effort-aware PU learning and conformal buy lists

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Architect: D06 agent. Builds on `../../model/MODEL.md` (SENTINEL-COX v1), `../../MEMO.md`, `../../datasets/SCHEMA.md` and the digests `../evidence/L01`-`L40`. ## 1. Summary

HEARTHNET (Heat Evidence Attention over Raw Tokens, Hydrology and Faults) learns the whole observation model end to end from every labelled geothermal outcome on Earth. Every raw reading becomes a token (a BHT, a gradient-hole curve, a spring analysis, an MT sounding, a fault tip, a lease, a dry hole) carrying location, depth, value, error, censoring, source and the date it became public. A Perceiver-style transformer reads the tokens near each 500 m cell, and a graph network along mapped faults adds structure. Modality dropout lets it work with whatever data a cell has. It is pretrained by masked-token prediction on about 10^7 real tokens worldwide plus 10^5 synthetic "Westlets" with known truth. It is fine-tuned with three heads: censored ordinal temperature-at-depth; existence as positive-unlabelled (PU) learning, where the label frequency c(x, t) is a separate, firewalled search-effort network (the SENTINEL-COX thinning rewritten as a neural PU constant); and archetype plus failure mode. A 16-member spatial-bootstrap ensemble with distance-aware heads gives uncertainty, spatially weighted conformal calibration fixes it, and conformal selection issues the buy list with a stated false-discovery rate. Draws feed the existing VOI-Bank spine. Because the network is generative over tokens, it can imagine the reading an undeployed sensor would return, which prices sensors. If it cannot beat the physics-first core on a point-in-time hindcast, it becomes a feature generator inside it.

## 2. Target variables

**Grid.** 500 m equal-area cells (EPSG:5070 in the US, local equal-area abroad), refined to 125 m inside the >1,430 Faulds favourable structural settings and inside any parcel we option. Existence is also reported on a 2 km mesh so it plugs into the SENTINEL-COX count. Values roll up to PLSS 40-acre aliquots and assessor parcels by crosswalk. H3 res 8 is a join key only (L35).

**Per-cell outputs, each as samples from the ensemble:**
- **T(z)** at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6 km as 128 ordinal bins (In-Context Earth style, L10). Censored readings and boiling-curve caps fit naturally.
- **lambda_exist(x)**: intensity of system upflows, split by archetype a in {B1 shallow outflow, B2 range-hosted no discharge, B3 basin-buried capped, B4 cold-aquifer lid, B5 rain curtain, B6 carbonate sweep, B7 conductive sedimentary, magmatic, rift, EGS-conductive, unknown play} (L09). psi = 1 - exp(-integral of lambda over 2 km).
- **c(x, t)**: the probability that a system here would already be labelled by date t. This is the PU label frequency and the Cox P_det.
- **Open-pathway class** (Libbey's four classes) and P(critically stressed) per fault segment.
- **Failure-mode ladder** (L40): {no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil, success}.
- **Upflow offset vector**: where the upflow sits relative to the warm evidence.
- **Size mark**: MWe P10/P50/P90 with a 0.5-0.7x haircut until our own lookback exists (L17).

**Value heads.** The fixed M34 economics turn these into hydrothermal RNPV/acre on the v1 sloped line (about 109 C at 1 km, 152 C at 3 km at $100/MWh), plus an EGS head (P(>=200 C at <=4 km)), a superhot head (P(>=374 C by 6 km)) and a closed-loop head (conductive MW per loop).

**Decision output.** (1) A conformal buy list with a stated false-discovery rate, split by province and archetype. (2) EVSI per dollar for each sensor type in each cell, from generated pseudo-readings. (3) An edge map: our posterior minus a "crowd" HEARTHNET trained only on public layers.

## 4. Components and methods

**C0. Point-in-time evidence store.** GeoParquet plus Zarr, DuckDB as the engine (L35). Every value has known_time, so hindcasts are point-in-time joins. Extracted values carry page, bbox, model id, extraction spread and a location-error box (±400-800 m for TRS points, L21).

**C1. Tokeniser.** One token per raw reading: (x, y, z, type, value, sigma, censor bound, source, era, extraction confidence, location error, known_time). A full T(z) log stays one sequence, so reversals and isothermal segments are visible. Rasters become 2 km patches. MT soundings enter as conductor-depth quantiles from 50-200 randomise-then-optimise inversions (L13), so the model sees spread.

**C2. Modality encoders.** SetConv/DeepSets for point sets (L29), 1D conv plus attention for curves, a ViT patch encoder pretrained as a masked autoencoder on the West (GFM4MPM recipe, L16) for rasters. AlphaEarth embeddings enter as 8-16 PCs with the built-infrastructure direction projected out (L20).

**C3. Fault graph network.** Nodes are fault tips, step-overs, intersections, displacement transfer zones and pull-aparts, from Qfaults, NSHM 2023, Faulds FSS polygons and a lidar U-Net fault detector (Hermant 2025, PR-AUC 0.595, L04). Edges are segments with age, slip rate and samples of P(critically stressed) from a Monte Carlo stress field (L25). GATv2 message passing gives each cell a structural embedding. The FSS-type enrichments (DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x, step-over about 0.85x; L01, L02, L09) initialise the prior on the archetype head's logits, then the data update them.

**C4. Perceiver fusion.** Each 500 m cell query cross-attends to tokens within 20 km, with learned bias on distance, depth and fault-graph distance. Missing modalities get a learned ABSENT token, never mean imputation. HeMIS pooling and modality dropout (p about 0.3) prevent the collapse plain multimodal transformers show when a modality is missing (Ma et al. CVPR 2022, L29). A conductive prior mean (USGS Great Basin 3D model, InterPIGNN) is the residual base, so the network learns only the convective departure (L10).

**C5. Heads.**
- H1, temperature. 128 ordinal bins per depth. Loss is the censored ordinal likelihood: an equilibrium log scores its bin; a BHT scores "at least x" with a learned, era-dependent bias; a cold TG hole scores "below x to its depth only". Hard monotone and boiling-curve limits are applied in the decoder, which fixes the impossible inversions seen at Granite Springs (L10).
- H2, existence. Log-intensity by archetype.
- H3, archetype. Softmax with a max-entropy "unknown play" class (L38, IP v2).
- H4, permeability and failure ladder. A competing-risks head trained on AI-extracted completion reports (lost circulation, flow tests, reversals).
- H5, label frequency c(x, t). See C6.

**C6. Effort-aware PU learning (the core idea).** Classical PU assumes labels are "selected completely at random". Geothermal labels are not: systems get labelled because they leak (L19). HEARTHNET writes the PU problem as a thinned Poisson process: observed labelled systems ~ Poisson(lambda_exist(x) x c_a(x, t)), where c_a is the chance that a system of archetype a would be labelled by time t. The background integral over unlabelled cells replaces negatives. c_a comes from a **separate effort encoder** that sees only effort tokens: well density by decade, roads, mines, O&G holes, 2 m surveys, leases, data presence, and the human-footprint direction of AlphaEarth. It is shared across several "species" found by the same effort (hot springs, mines, water wells, mineral occurrences), which makes effort identifiable (Fithian et al. 2015, L18). A per-archetype label frequency (PULSNAR, L19) lets leaky B1 systems have c near 0.9 in settled valleys while capped B3 systems stay near 0.1. **Firewall:** effort tokens never enter E3, and geology tokens never enter E5. An adversarial probe checks that z_cell cannot predict effort, and that latitude and longitude alone cannot predict labels (L03). Surface manifestations (springs, sinter, KGRA lines) enter only the detection side, so the map can score blind systems (L04).

**C7. Pretraining.** Three stages. (a) Masked-token modelling on about 10^7 real tokens worldwide: hide 30% of tokens and predict them. (b) Synthetic pretraining on 10^5 "Westlets", 50 x 50 x 10 km worlds made from kinematic structure (Noddyverse style), TOUGH3/Waiwera fault-hosted upflows, masking aquifers, BHT bias and a simulated search history. In these worlds every head has a known truth (L12, L33). (c) Fine-tuning on real labels, Great Basin first, with province-hierarchical adapters (LoRA per province and per country).

**C8. Generative sensor pricing.** The masked-token model samples the reading an unbuilt sensor would give (an MT sounding, a 150 m TG hole, a ranch-well bottle). Conditioning on each sample and re-running the cell gives EVSI on RNPV/acre in one forward pass (L28, L29).

**C9. Crowd model.** A second HEARTHNET trained only on what rivals can see. Our edge is posterior minus crowd posterior; the engine acts only where it is large (L37).

## 6. Labels and negatives

**Positives.** About 280 confirmed US systems (USGS 28 plant + 78 known, plus other states) and about 700 producing fields and explored systems worldwide (unverified count), each dated, archetype-tagged and with a discovery channel. The 99 USGS probable systems are held out with a learned confirmation probability. Weak positives come from filings and DOE awards.

**Negatives are not "unlabelled cells".** HEARTHNET has three graded kinds:
1. **Background integral.** Unlabelled space enters only through lambda x c, so a never-searched cell is unknown, not cold.
2. **Censored temperature negatives.** Tens of thousands of shallow cold wells and TG holes score "T below x to depth d" in H1 only. This turns GeoCore's 148 cold wells into a large, depth-honest negative set (L05). Argenta (ten TG wells averaging 24.35 C at up to 244 m, L02) and Reese River (ten cold TG wells, L04) are the template: they lower P(shallow anomaly) and say little at 800 m where a recharge aquifer is mapped.
3. **Failure-mode labels.** AI extraction of completion reports tags each dry or disappointing hole as no heat, tight, outflow, masked or fossil (L40). This gives the first public failure-mode label set.

**Worldwide labels.** Global heat-flow and well temperatures (IHFC 2024, KGS, Alberta, OZTemp, UK) train H1. Global fields train H2/H3 under country adapters. Multilingual document AI over UNU-GTP, NEDO, MTA, CFE and Pertamina reports adds volcanic-play labels and dry wells (L34).

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

**Quantified.**
- A 16-member deep ensemble. Members differ by 50 km spatial-block bootstrap, feature-subset dropout and random seed. Ensembles degrade least under shift (Ovadia 2019, L27).
- Each member has an SNGP spectral-normalised Gaussian-process output layer, so cells far from training data in latent space get wide posteriors automatically.
- An area-of-applicability dissimilarity index per cell (Meyer and Pebesma 2021, L19), published with the map.
- Aleatoric versus epistemic split per cell. The epistemic part becomes the VOI bonus for cheap sensors in out-of-AOA, high-prior cells.

**Calibrated.**
- T(z): conformalised quantile regression with spatial weights, calibrated on blocks larger than the autocorrelation range, Mondrian by province and archetype, and reported inside and outside the AOA. Gate: 85-95% coverage of 90% bands and PIT KS under 3% (L10).
- Existence: PU probabilities are calibrated with POC plots against designed cold controls and our own sensor visits (Phillips and Elith 2010, L27).
- Buy list: conformal selection (Jin and Candès 2023) with Benjamini-Hochberg on conformal p-values gives a list with a stated FDR, for example "at most 30% of these parcels lack a >=120 C system at <=1.5 km".
- Online: adaptive conformal re-tunes interval width as each field reading lands (Gibbs and Candès 2021).

**Validated, in order of honesty.**
1. Synthetic Westlets with planted blind systems: coverage, FDR and PU class-prior recovery against known truth.
2. Point-in-time hindcasts frozen at 1980, 1990, 2005, 2010 and 2015. Purge labels within 10-20 km of each test find. Embargo two years. Score the 11-case blind ladder (Desert Peak, McGinness, Don Campbell, Gabbs, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel and others, L31).
3. kNNDM cross-validation matched to the distance from unexplored cells to their nearest label, beside random and fixed-block CV (L19).
4. Leave-one-country-out and leave-one-province-out for transfer, with Western Anatolia as an out-of-domain test with designed negatives (L34).
5. CSEP N, L and S tests on the post-2010 discoveries; Molchan and blind-only area-capture at 1%, 5% and 10%; Boyce index (L27, L36).
6. A hashed, timestamped pre-registration of the map before every BLM sale and field season. The first is Granite Springs 68-02 (about 503 m), before NBMG releases it (L01).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

**Agents run the data plane only.** They fetch, extract, reproject and watch. They never edit the model at run time (L39).
- Extraction swarm: VLM header reading plus SAM curve tracing for T-logs; N=5 self-ensembled reads per value; the median becomes the token value and the spread becomes its sigma (L21). Targets: NBMG 2,002 scans, NDWR 132k unkeyed reports, CA OSWCR 1.1M, NDOM, DOGM, CalGEM, KGS (as scorecard), UNU-GTP and NEDO in many languages.
- Source handbook cards with unit and datum contracts and golden traps, gated at pass^5 above 95% (L39). Watchers on MLRS, permits, GDR and ScienceBase turn new items into tokens nightly. A red-team agent hunts hindcast leakage (L36).

**Compute.** Pretraining a model of about 100-300M parameters on 10^7 real tokens plus 10^5 Westlets: about 2,000-6,000 A100-hours (unverified). Generating 10^5 Westlets with a multi-fidelity mix (mostly analytic Tier 1, a few thousand TOUGH3 runs): about 10^5-10^6 CPU-hours (unverified). Fine-tuning the 16-member ensemble: about 1,000 GPU-hours. West-wide inference at 500 m (about 12M cells, L35) for 16 members: a few GPU-hours. EVSI by generated tokens for the top 100k cells: about a day on 8 GPUs.

## 9. What makes it beat Zanskar

- **Labels.** GeoCore trains on 6,009 warm wells against 148 cold ones (L05). HEARTHNET uses every cold or shallow reading as a censored, depth-honest negative, plus the world's fields and heat-flow data through pretraining.
- **The missing denominator.** GeoCore has no search-effort term. HEARTHNET's firewalled c(x, t) tells "unexplored" from "barren", which is exactly where blind systems on private and state land sit.
- **Raw data, not rasters.** Tokens keep full T(z) curves, MT posterior spread, GeoDAWN 200 m flight lines and spring solutes. Rasterised layers throw these away, and L29 shows the algorithm on rasters barely matters.
- **Calibrated, audited output.** Zanskar reports 94.5% of hot springs in the top half of its map. HEARTHNET reports blind-only capture at 1% and 5% of area, reliability diagrams, coverage and a conformal FDR on its buy list, all from point-in-time hindcasts and a public pre-registration ledger.
- **Transfer and decisions.** Leave-one-country-out tests back a worldwide claim; the crowd model and generated-sensor EVSI aim money at the gap between our knowledge and rivals'.

## 10. How it drives rights buying and sensor deployment

1. Nightly RNPV/acre draws per parcel; conformal selection keeps parcels passing the FDR target; the crowd model drops parcels public data already flags.
2. Per parcel, the best sensor's EVSI/$ is compared with locking the right now. Cheap rights (ranch heat, state leases, leftover BLM at $2-4/acre, L37) are locked first where edge and nomination leak cost are high.
3. Sensors by generated-token EVSI: 2 m arrays and TG holes first in Basin and Range (direct temperature decided 10 of 11 blind cases, L31); E-MT where a capped B3 is favoured; chemistry bottles where the fluid leg is weakest.
4. Batches per mobilisation with a travel-cost term, re-planned after each. Every reading is pre-registered and lands as a token next night.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Test (about $3-8k of compute and API, weeks 1-10).** Nevada only, frozen at 2010 with point-in-time joins. Train HEARTHNET-small (about 20M parameters, no world pretraining, 10^4 Westlets) and two baselines on the same tokens: LightGBM on INGENIOUS layers with random negatives (the GeoCore recipe) and a LightGBM PU model with effort weighting. Score:
1. Censored T(z) log-likelihood on kNNDM folds and on 10 km blocks.
2. The rank of the true cell for post-2010 blind finds: Gabbs (2015), Granite Springs (2016), San Emidio SW, Big Blind (2023 signal) and Pumpernickel (2025), plus Argenta and Reese River as known cold outcomes.
3. Coverage of 90% T bands, and FDR of a conformal buy list on 20 synthetic Nevadas.

**Pass bar.**
- Blocked T(z) log-likelihood beats both baselines by at least 0.1 nats per reading, with a bootstrap CI above zero.
- The median post-2010 blind find ranks in the top 5% of Nevada area, against the top 10% or worse for the GeoCore-style baseline, and Argenta and Reese River fall below the 50th percentile on P(>=120 C at <=500 m).
- Coverage is 85-95%, and the realised synthetic FDR is at or below nominal.
- The adversarial probe cannot predict effort from z_cell (AUC <=0.6).

If it fails on (1) and (2), HEARTHNET is not killed. Its encoders become feature generators for the SENTINEL-COX intensity, and its conformal layer wraps the Cox output.

## 14. Biggest risks

1. **Too few positives.** About 280 US positives can be overfit. Fix: pretraining, synthetic worlds, small heads, ensemble plus SNGP.
2. **Effort leaking into geology.** If the firewall fails, the map learns "where people looked". Fix: separate encoders, adversarial probe, no label proxies.
3. **Synthetic-to-real gap.** Real blind archetypes (B3) are nearly absent from labels. Fix: per-cell MMD misspecification tests and an "unknown play" class.
4. **Global transfer may hurt** Basin and Range predictions. Fix: country adapters; keep a source only if leave-one-country-out shows lift.
5. **Conformal guarantees bend under spatial shift.** Fix: weighted, Mondrian and online conformal, coverage reported inside and outside the AOA.
6. **Extraction errors** create false hot spots. Fix: self-ensembled sigma, contracts, two-agent diff.
7. **Opacity to buyers.** Fix: weakest-leg attribution, a Milkov-style risk table (L17), the public pre-registration ledger.
8. **Small validation n** (about 10 dated blind finds). Fix: pool synthetic worlds and censored readings; lean on the prospective ledger.
