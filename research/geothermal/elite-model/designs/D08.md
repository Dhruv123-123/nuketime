## 1. Summary

Weather AI won mainly because a reanalysis (ERA5) already existed: a dense, physically consistent state built by assimilating sparse observations into physics (L33). The subsurface has none, so we build one. UNDERCAST holds a **3D latent state of temperature, permeability, fluid flux and phase, plus the rock properties sensors see** (resistivity, density, magnetisation, velocity, lithology), West-wide to 10 km. Its **generative prior** is a GenCast-style 3D latent diffusion model pre-trained on about 10^6 synthetic "Westlets" (50 x 50 x 10 km worlds with Basin-and-Range faults, upflows, outflow tongues, cold lids and masking aquifers) simulated at three fidelity tiers. Every real observation enters through **its own forward operator** on that shared state. **Diffusion posterior sampling** conditions the prior on all of them at once, giving a 64-member ensemble reanalysis, **SRA-West**, rebuilt nightly and frozen at past dates for honest hindcasts. Small fine-tuned **heads** read it: P(system), T(z) bins, permeability class, archetype, MW/km2 and EGS isotherms. A fixed economics layer turns these into RNPV per acre. Because the model is generative, it can **simulate any measurement before we buy it**, so EIG per dollar comes from the same model for every sensor and cell. SENTINEL-COX's effort-thinned Cox count stays as the existence head, so search bias is still modelled. The world model is the engine; the Cox count and VOI-Bank are the steering wheel.

## 2. Target variables

**Latent state.** One state per known-time t. Voxels are 1 km horizontal West-wide, nested to 250 m inside favourable structural settings (FSS) and candidate parcels; vertical 100 m to 3 km, 250 m to 6 km, 1 km to 10 km. That is about 3 x 10^8 coarse voxels, the size of one ERA5 snapshot (L33 calc). Per voxel: T, a log-permeability tensor (fault-parallel, fault-normal, vertical), porosity, Darcy flux, fluid phase and salinity, clay fraction, resistivity, density, susceptibility, Vs, lithology. 2D fields: water table, recharge, basal heat flow, basin-fill thickness, smectite-cap depth. Discrete objects: hydrothermal upflow bodies, each with an archetype (B1-B7, EGS-conductive, magmatic, "unknown play"; L09).

**Predictions per 500 m cell.** T at 0.5/1/1.5/2/3/4/6 km as 128-bin ordinal distributions (L10); P(upflow body within 2 km) with an upflow-offset vector, so an outflow hit points to the up-gradient parcel; permeability class (Libbey and Murphy SGW 2026) and P(critically stressed); archetype; depth to the 150/200/250/300 C isotherms (EGS head); P(T > 374 C at <= 6 km).

**Decision output.** RNPV per acre per legal parcel via MODEL.md's fixed M34 economics, fed by joint draws; EVSI/$ for buy, option, 2 m line, E-MT, TG hole, slim hole, wait and sell; a nightly ranked buy and sensor list. Headline map: P(T >= 150 C at <= 2.5 km AND permeable), scored by blind-only area capture at 1% and 5%.

## 4. Components and methods

**E. Encoders.** Each reading is a token, never a raster pixel (L29): position, depth interval, valid and known time, type, value, error class, location-error box, source and search channel. Per-modality set encoders feed cross-attention. Missing modalities get learned "absent" tokens, and modality dropout (HeMIS, ModDrop) prevents collapse. Gridded products (USGS favourability, INGENIOUS) are low-weight tokens, never truth.

**P. Generative prior.** A 3D VAE compresses each tile to latent tokens; a latent diffusion transformer (300M-1B parameters, unverified sizing) learns p(Z). Training: (1) synthetic Westlets from a Noddyverse-style kinematic generator seeded with Faulds' FSS templates and enrichments (DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x; L01, L09), gravity-based basin fill and NWIS-like heads; (2) physics at three tiers: 10^6 analytic column-plus-plume runs (GHOST Tier 1, about 1 ms), 10^5 FNO-surrogate 3D runs, and 5,000-20,000 TOUGH3/Waiwera natural-state runs with two-phase flow and sealing (multi-fidelity cut data cost about 80% in CO2 work; L12); (3) masked-modality pre-training on real data (MultiMAE), which teaches real noise without labels.

**H. Hybrid physics core.** A differentiable steady conduction-advection-Darcy solver with a learned residual (NeuralGCM-style). Hard limits: boiling-point-with-depth, energy balance against basal flux, and no inversion without a lateral flux to explain it. That fixes the impossible inversions seen at Granite Springs (L10). Near faults, a local U-Net corrects the FNO's blurring of sharp upflow fronts (L12).

**D. Forward operators.** Each sensor is a decoder on the one state.
- Temperature: equilibrium logs (sigma 0.5-1 C); BHT as a skewed lower bound with per-operator, per-era bias and a 2-5% junk mixture; TG holes as censored T(d) < x; driller discharge as interval readings; 2 m probes with seasonal amplitude and phase nuisances (L38).
- MT: learned Archie-plus-clay rock physics with false-conductor classes (basin clay, brine, fossil cap), then an MT emulator; trans-D ensembles enter as distributions (L13).
- Gravity and magnetics: exact Fourier forward, using GeoDAWN flight lines, not just grids.
- Chemistry: mixing-aware geothermometer forward (reservoir T, hot fraction, cold end-member, CO2 loss; L26). Helium R/Ra decodes from deep fault permeability, not heat.
- Surface: thermal IR via surface energy balance; alteration as a live-or-fossil mixture (L24).
- Seismicity: a Cox process on pore pressure and permeability, thinned by network Mc (L25).

**A. Assimilation.** Diffusion posterior sampling (Chung 2023; L28) pulls each reverse-diffusion sample toward all observations through D. An ensemble score filter adds new data nightly. Output: 64 members x 5 backbones = 320 joint states per tile. All runs are point-in-time on known time (L35), so "SRA-West as of 2015" holds only data public by 2015. A per-tile MMD misspecification test sends failing tiles to SENTINEL-COX's 1D column fallback and to the sensor lead list.

**K. Heads.** Fine-tuned on the frozen backbone (Aurora's split). The existence head is the SENTINEL-COX Cox count: intensity takes world-model features; thinning uses dated effort by channel and a depth-of-sight term, so a cell with only 50 m wells is "unseen below 50 m", not cold (L31). Effort and data presence never enter the intensity (L19).

**V. Decision.** RNPV/ac from fixed economics on joint draws. A **crowd posterior** is the same model assimilated on public, easy layers only; we act where ours beats it after winner's-curse and leak costs (L37). EIG per sensor: sample the unseen measurement through its operator, re-assimilate, measure the RNPV change. That works for sensors not yet built. The planner starts as one-step EVSI/$ plus a global-information term and moves to a POMCPOW/BetaZero portfolio POMDP trained on synthetic campaigns (L14, L15).

## 6. Labels and negatives

The main training signal is **every held-out observation**, not a system label. The model predicts readings it was not shown: the deep half of a log, a neighbour's MT, a chemistry sample. So Burns 2024's 22,535 wells, about 146k NDWR driller reports, 10,991 NBMG borehole temperatures, MT stations and tens of thousands of chemistry samples all become supervision, against GeoCore's 148 cold wells (L05).

System labels enter only through the existence head: USGS 28 plant + 78 known systems as dated, channel-tagged finds; the 99 probable systems held out. Negatives are physical: cold equilibrium logs in favourable settings (OFR 99-425 cold prospects, Argenta, Reese River's 10 TG wells, NDOM P&A TG holes) are censored temperatures to their own depth only. Reversal and lid profiles point up-gradient. The 4,293 flow-disturbed wells USGS discards (L03) become convective evidence. Surface manifestations feed detection only in blind runs. Synthetic worlds add unlimited perfect labels with planted blind systems.

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

**Quantified.** Data noise from operators; posterior spread from 64 DPS samples; model uncertainty from 5 backbones trained on different synthetic mixes and block bootstraps. Variance grows with depth below the deepest nearby calibration well (L13). OOD tiles get the 1D fallback and wide bands.

**Validated, in order of honesty.**
1. SBC and TARP on 1,000 held-out synthetic Westlets with planted blind systems.
2. Held-out-observation CRPS under kNNDM CV (L19), beside random and 20-50 km block CV, by depth band.
3. Coverage gate: 85-95% inside 90% bands, PIT KS < 3%, inside and outside the area of applicability.
4. Point-in-time hindcasts at 1990/2008/2015 scored on later wells and blind finds (Big Blind, Pumpernickel, Gabbs, Granite Springs; McGinness from 1982 data): area capture at 1/5/10%, Molchan curves, CSEP N/L/S. Baselines: 1-in-550 base rate, distance smoother, frozen-label LightGBM, kriging, ICE-style transformer, SENTINEL-COX v1.
5. Hashed pre-registration before each BLM sale and field season, scored on DOE exploration wells (L07) and Granite Springs 68-02 (L01).
6. Conformal selection with a stated FDR on the buy list (L27).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane and the simulation factory; they never edit the model at run time (L39).
- **Extraction agents** read NBMG, NDWR, CalGEM, NDOM, DOGM and SMU scans (VLM headers plus curve tracer), as 5-run self-ensembles whose spread becomes token sigma (L21).
- **Simulation agents** build TOUGH3/Waiwera decks per archetype from extracted conceptual-model figures, run about 10^5 jobs and check convergence.
- **Operator-builder agents** fit and test the forward operator for each new data type, so a modality is added in days.
- **Red-team agents** hunt leakage (post-discovery surveys, LLM memory of answers, chosen freeze dates).
- **Watch agents** track MLRS, nominations, permits, GDR and USGS releases and trigger re-assimilation.
- **Hypothesis tournament**: conceptual models per basin scored by held-out log-likelihood, never by LLM vote (L22).

Compute (unverified): about 10^5 CPU-hours for TOUGH3 (about $5k); diffusion prior 20-40k H100-hours ($60-120k); nightly assimilation of about 2,500 tiles x 320 members on 64 GPUs in about 6 hours. Year one about $0.5-1M, small next to one $11-30M test campaign (L32).

## 9. Why it beats Zanskar

Zanskar's public stack is 2D LightGBM on rasterised INGENIOUS layers (148 cold wells; 94.5% of springs in the top half) plus site-scale FNOs without UQ (L05, L11). UNDERCAST is (1) **3D and depth-aware**: Big Blind at about 820 m sits below every INGENIOUS TG hole, and only a 3D state can say "unseen below 250 m"; (2) **supervised by every raw reading**, hundreds of thousands of tokens; (3) **pre-trained on planted blind systems**; (4) **physically legal**, so lids and outflow are reasoned about; (5) able to **value any sensor by simulating it**; (6) **point-in-time and pre-registered**, so its skill number is honest and sells; (7) **crowd-aware**. Zanskar will move this way (a co-founder co-authored the FNO paper; L11), so speed and the extracted archive are the moat.

## 10. How it drives rights buying and sensor deployment

- Nightly, each parcel gets RNPV/ac (P10/P50/P90), the crowd's RNPV/ac and the edge between them.
- **Lock rule:** buy or option where edge-adjusted RNPV/ac exceeds 3x the 10-year holding cost and the conformal FDR target holds. Route high-leak targets to private ranch heat, state first-file and leftover BLM parcels rather than nominations (L37).
- **Sensor rule:** for each candidate action, simulate its outcome through the world model and compute EVSI/$ plus a global-information bonus. Batch by truck route and drilling season. Heat-limited cells get TG holes. Permeability-limited cells get helium, tidal loggers or geophones. Lidded cells get E-MT or deeper logs (L40).
- **Sell rule:** a data-room pack per prospect: ensemble cross-sections, AGRCC resource categories, calibration plots and lineage. After a slim hole, a calibrated natural-state model from the same physics core.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Test: "Does the 3D generative prior beat kriging and ICE at depth in Nevada?"** About 4-6 weeks, 2 people, about $15k compute (unverified).
- Train a small latent diffusion prior (about 50M parameters) on 10^5 tier (a) and 3,000 tier (c) Nevada Westlets.
- Condition by DPS on public temperature data only: Burns 2024 wells, NBMG BoreholeTemperature, SMU heat flow, OFR 99-425, INGENIOUS 2 m.
- Task 1: under kNNDM CV, predict held-out well readings, with emphasis on the deep half of each log hidden.
- Task 2: freeze data at 2015 and score later logs, including Gabbs Valley and Granite Springs TG holes.
- Task 3: SBC on 200 synthetic Nevadas with planted blind systems.

**Pass bar.** (1) CRPS at least 15% better than the best of universal kriging, an ICE-style transformer and the USGS conductive model in the 0.5-2 km band. (2) 90% interval coverage between 85% and 95% in every depth band. (3) SBC ranks uniform (KS p > 0.05) and recovery of 70% or more of planted blind systems in the top 5% of area. (4) In the 2015 freeze, Gabbs Valley and Granite Springs cells sit in the top 5% of P(T >= 100 C at <= 300 m). Fail on (1) and (2) means the generative prior adds nothing at depth: fall back to SENTINEL-COX with In-Context Earth as the column prior. A fail on (1) alone with a pass on (3) means the synthetic gap is the problem: re-weight the simulator mix and re-test.

## 14. Biggest risks and AI-leveraged answers

1. **Sim-to-real gap.** Answer: masked real-data pre-training, per-tile MMD gates, robust NPE fallback, and agents that tune the Westlet generator until posterior-predictive checks pass.
2. **Sparse deep data, so the state below 1 km echoes the prior.** Answer: publish a per-voxel "data-driven share", let VOI price deep holes there, and extract every deep oil, mining and geothermal log.
3. **Overconfident diffusion posteriors.** Answer: 5-backbone ensembles, SBC/TARP release gates, conformal wrapping.
4. **Permeability weakly observable.** Answer: many indirect channels (seismicity, helium, stress, lost circulation) and a wide flow-risk term in RNPV.
5. **Hindcast leakage.** Answer: bitemporal known-time joins and a red-team agent.
6. **Build complexity.** Answer: SENTINEL-COX v1 keeps running; UNDERCAST replaces its column layer only after passing the first test.
7. **Small blind evaluation set.** Answer: held-out-observation CRPS gives thousands of test points; pre-registration grows the record each season.
