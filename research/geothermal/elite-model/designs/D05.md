# D05 · HELM: a decision-first heat-rights engine

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Architect: D05 agent. Builds on `../../model/MODEL.md` (SENTINEL-COX, whose spine is VOI-Bank), `../../MEMO.md`, `../../datasets/SCHEMA.md` and the digests `../evidence/L01`-`L40`. The shared web-search budget was used up before this agent ran, so no new searches were made. Numbers come from the L-files and MODEL.md, which cite primary sources. My own arithmetic and anything not re-checked is marked (unverified).

## 1. Name and one-paragraph summary

**HELM** (Heat-rights Evidential Learning and Markov planning) treats prospecting as a sequential business decision, not a mapping problem. The question it answers each night is: "Given everything we believe, what is the next dollar of land, sensing or drilling that most increases the value of our portfolio, and when should we stop looking and just lock the right?" The scientific model is a **pluggable belief state**. Any model that can draw joint samples of the hidden world and predict the outcome of a hypothetical measurement can be plugged in: SENTINEL-COX today, a world model such as D04 TWIN-WEST later, a LightGBM-PU baseline as a control. Between belief and decision sits a **Bayesian evidential learning (BEL)** layer (Yin, Strebelle and Caers 2020, GMD 13:651). It falsifies the prior before we trust it, prunes the latent space to what moves dollars, and learns direct maps from "data we could buy" to "decision value". This makes value of information cheap enough to compute for every cell in the West. On top runs a **portfolio POMDP planner**. Its state is our belief plus our holdings, cash, lease clocks and what the crowd already knows. Its actions are nominate, bid, option, desk-extract, sense, drill, drop and sell. It uses Pandora's-box reservation values as a fast index (Weitzman 1979), depth-2 to 3 tree search for the top candidates (POMCPOW, as in Intelligent Prospector; Mern and Caers 2023), and an amortised value network trained on millions of synthetic campaigns (BetaZero-style). Every policy is scored against a Brown-Smith information-relaxation upper bound, so we know how far from optimal we are. VOI-Bank in MODEL.md is one-step and per-cell. HELM is multi-step, portfolio-wide, competition-aware and model-agnostic.

## 2. Target variables

**Primary decision output.** A ranked, dated action list for the whole portfolio, refreshed nightly: for each parcel, the best next action, its expected value gain, its cost, and its reservation value (the price at which we should stop measuring and lock). Actions are batched per field mobilisation and timed to BLM sale dates, lease primary terms, drilling seasons and permit lags.

**Primary value variable.** Portfolio risked NPV, the sum over parcels of RNPV/acre times acres, minus holding and information costs, minus the expected cost of leaking information to rivals. RNPV/acre uses the fixed M34 economics from MODEL.md (sloped Tmin line: about 109 C at 1 km, 120 C at 1.5 km, 130 C at 2 km, 152 C at 3 km at $100/MWh) with three buyer heads:
- **Hydrothermal (Ormat-type):** P(cross Tmin above 3 km) x P(flow >= 40 kg/s) x MW x capture stage.
- **EGS (Fervo-type):** P(T >= 200 C at <= 4 km TVD), depth to 150/200/250 C, basement depth, stress regime (L06, L30).
- **Closed-loop/conduction (XGS, Eavor-type):** conductive T at 3-6 km and rock conductivity.
The parcel is priced at the best head. A default 0.5-0.7x haircut applies to MW-if-exists until our own lookback exists (oil overstates volumes 1.4-2.5x; L17).

**Physical quantities read from the belief (per 500 m EPSG:5070 cell, 250 m inside the >1,430 Faulds favourable structural settings):** P(system exists within 2 km); T at 0.5/1/1.5/2/3 km as ordinal bins; permeability class (Libbey-Murphy 4 classes); archetype/mask class (B1-B7 from L09 plus "none" and a maximum-entropy "unknown play"); upflow offset vector; size mark (MWe); P_det (would it already be known). Each is a *decision-relevant summary*, not an end in itself.

**Ladder output.** For each prospect, P(reach rung k) for k = desk anomaly, hot TG hole, hot intermediate well (750-850 m, Zanskar's gate; L05), flow test, bankable. Price at each rung is the sale value at that point. The planner chooses at which rung to sell, farm out or keep.

**Regional output (secondary).** Undiscovered systems and MWe per province, F95/F50/F5, inherited from the plugged-in belief. HELM does not need it to act, but it reports it.

## 4. Components and methods

### C0. Agent data plane and crowd model
Agents fetch, extract and watch; they never edit C1-C4 at run time (L22, L39). Extraction of scanned temperature logs uses the Stage 0 split pipeline (VLM header reader plus SAM-style curve tracer; L21) run N=5 times. The median is the value; the spread is the extraction sigma in the likelihood. Every value carries known_time, so any day in the past can be rebuilt (L35).

The **crowd model** is the same belief model fed only public layers (INGENIOUS, USGS 2025 favourability, announced discoveries, public TG logs). A second agent parses every BLM all-bids report, nomination list and MLRS case into an inverse model of each rival's beliefs (L37). Zanskar's nominations and wins become a "rival knows something here" layer. The crowd model is used only in C3 (edge, bid shading, leak cost), never as a geology feature.

### C1. Belief-state interface
This is the contract that makes the science pluggable. A belief model must provide four calls:
1. `sample(n)`: joint particles over all latents, including **shared** ones (play-level risk per structural-setting type and basin, effort catch rates, sensor reliability matrices). Shared latents matter: a cold hole must lower its neighbours (L17).
2. `predict(action)`: the predictive distribution of what a measurement would return (a T-P-EC profile, a 2 m line, an E-MT station, a TG hole to 150 m, a slim hole to 800 m).
3. `update(obs)`: condition on a new observation.
4. `falsify(data)`: prior and posterior predictive p-values on held-out data.

Several beliefs run at once and are stacked by their log score on frozen-world hindcasts (Yao et al. 2018 stacking). A **maximum-entropy "unknown play" particle set** always keeps some weight, because the KoBold-Caers planner needed a null class to avoid walking away from good prospects (L38) and Intelligent Prospector v2 adds detection of "all hypotheses wrong" (L15).

Default v1 plug-in: SENTINEL-COX from MODEL.md (thinned LGCP, censored 1D columns, shared mask class). Upgrade path: the L09 archetype latent, the L10 In-Context Earth temperature prior, the L12 FNO surrogate, and D04's 3D reanalysis.

### C2. BEL layer
The BEL protocol (L14) adapted to a regional portfolio:
1. **Monte Carlo prior of worlds.** Draw 10^5-10^6 synthetic "prospect tiles" (20 x 20 km, 3 km deep) from the belief's own prior: structural setting type, archetype, basal heat flow, permeability, aquifer sweep, effort history. Forward-model every sensor through GHOST Tier 1 (analytic column plus plume, about 1 ms) and, for a subset, the FNO surrogate (L11, L12).
2. **Prior falsification.** For each basin and archetype, compare real data summaries with the prior predictive by robust Mahalanobis distance. If real data fall outside the 97.5th percentile, the prior is wrong there. The cell is flagged "off-simulator" and gets a wide belief and a sensing bonus, not a confident score (L28 MMD test does the same job).
3. **Sensitivity (DGSA).** Rank latents by their effect on RNPV/acre, not on temperature. L11 suggests basal heat flux, conductivity and permeability dominate. Keep the top 5-10; marginalise the rest.
4. **Direct forecasting.** For each sensor type, learn a map from "data that sensor would return" straight to the posterior of decision value (RNPV/acre and P(rung k)). Start with PCA plus CCA as in BEL, then conditional normalising flows (amortised NPE). The flow gives EVSI by preposterior analysis in milliseconds: sample worlds, simulate the data, push through the flow, re-solve the decision, average.
5. **Posterior falsification and calibration.** SBC rank histograms and TARP coverage on synthetic worlds, plus coverage on held-out real logs.

The point of BEL here is speed and honesty. Speed: EVSI for every action in every cell becomes a batched GPU call. Honesty: the prior is tested before the planner is allowed to exploit it.

### C3. Value layer
- **RNPV/acre** per buyer head, from a fork of GETEM/SAM in which the fixed 75-80% drilling-success constants are replaced by the belief's flow-class probabilities (L32). Well costs are lognormal draws (±50%) on Lowry 2017 curves with a Fervo cost trend term.
- **Commercial chance** Pc = P(exists) x P(MW > minimum economic size at our capture stage) (L17).
- **Bid value** conditional on winning: E[value | our bid wins], using the crowd posterior and the modelled number of informed rivals. This is the winner's-curse correction (Capen 1971; Hendricks-Porter 1988; L37).
- **Edge** = our value minus crowd value. Cheap wins happen only where we hold private information (Nevada 2025 median winning bid $3.50/acre, 37% at the floor; Pumpernickel step-out land repriced about 116x within a month of disclosure; L37).
- **Leak cost** of any public act (a BLM nomination, a public TG permit): the expected rise in the crowd posterior on our neighbouring targets, times the acres we have not yet locked. Legal descriptions go public 4-16 months before a sale and the nominator gets no preference right (GAO-26-108040; L37).
- **Risk.** Portfolio choice on an RNPV-versus-variance frontier with a play-concentration penalty (L17).

### C4. Portfolio POMDP planner
**State:** belief particles, holdings and their clocks (BLM primary term, diligence dates, option expiries), cash, public information set, calendar (sale dates, field season, permit lags).

**Actions and rough costs (unverified unless cited):** desk extraction of a cell's scans (dollars); Ranch Well Sentinel visit (hundreds of dollars); 2 m station ($200-500 each; L38); E-field MT node (about 10x cheaper than a $520-2,300 contract MT station; L38); 10-30 m darts; TG hole screen (3 holes about $0.45M; L32); two intermediate wells (Zanskar gate); private heat option ($/acre); state lease; BLM nomination and bid; drop; sell or farm out (Cozzolino RAV terms, carry floored near Crump Geyser's 20%; L37).

**Reward:** realised sale or development value of proven rungs minus all costs minus leak cost. Discounted.

**Solvers, three levels:**
1. **Index policy.** Each parcel is a Pandora's box: you pay to open it (measure) or take it (lock). Weitzman's reservation value gives the optimal order for independent boxes. Our boxes are correlated through shared play latents, so the index is computed per play group, and grouped boxes are handled by level 2. This is fast enough for 10^6 cells each night.
2. **Tree search.** For the top 500-2,000 parcels and their play groups, POMCPOW with depth-2 to 3 look-ahead, using the C2 flows as the observation model. Martinelli-Eidsvik-Hauge found depth-3 look-ahead beat naive drilling order by about 14.8% on 25 dependent North Sea prospects (L15).
3. **Amortised policy.** A value and policy network over belief summaries, trained offline BetaZero-style on 10^7 synthetic campaigns in GHOST worlds with planted blind systems, rivals and lease sales. At run time it seeds tree search and replaces it for long-tail cells.

**Batching.** Field actions are chosen per mobilisation by greedy submodular selection of EVSI per dollar with a truck-route cost, then re-planned after each batch (L38).

**Bound.** Each night, the Brown-Smith information-relaxation bound (the value of a planner told the truth, minus a penalty) gives an upper limit. The gap between our policy and the bound is reported. If it is small, better planning is not where money is.

### C5. Ledger and lookback
Every forecast is hashed and timestamped before the outcome is known (CSEP style; L36). Every hole gets a failure mode: no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil, success (L40). The ledger also checks **EVSI calibration**: did each measurement move value by about as much as predicted? A slope well below 1 means the belief is overconfident and the planner is buying the wrong data.

## 6. Labels and negatives

HELM inherits the graded label ladder from MODEL.md (G1 confirmed, G1b probable, G2 warm lower bounds, G3 hard cold logs, G4 masked, G5 shallow blanks). It adds three things.

1. **Rung labels.** Each prospect's history becomes a sequence of rungs reached and the action that reached them. This trains P(reach rung k+1 | rung k, data package), which no public study has (L32).
2. **Failure-mode labels** per dry or cool hole (L40). Reese River (10 cold TG wells; L04), Argenta (L02) and the four cool Gabbs holes (L01) are near-miss negatives with a cause.
3. **Decision outcomes.** BLM lease sales 2008-2025 with later outcomes (relinquished, drilled, producing) are labels for the planner, not for geology. Relinquished leases are project-level negatives (L32).

No cell is negative because it is unlabelled. Absence is handled by the thinned count in the belief.

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

**Quantified** at three levels:
- *Belief:* posterior particles from each plug-in, stacked, plus the maximum-entropy component. Kriged inputs enter as realisations, never as fixed layers (Caers 2025; L14).
- *Model choice:* stacking weights and BEL falsification flags. Off-simulator cells get wide beliefs.
- *Decision:* distribution of portfolio value under the policy, and the Brown-Smith gap.

**Validated**, in order of honesty:
1. **Synthetic worlds with known truth.** SBC and TARP for the belief; regret of the policy against an oracle and against one-step VOI-Bank.
2. **Frozen-world replay.** Freeze at 1990, 2005 and 2015 using known_time joins, purge labels within 10-20 km of test finds, embargo two years (L19). Run each policy with the same public observation bank. Score blind systems inside locked acres per dollar, with bootstrap CIs. Correct off-policy bias by also replaying on synthetic worlds.
3. **BLM sale replay.** For each Nevada and Utah sale since 2008, what would HELM have bid, and what did those tracts later become?
4. **Prospective.** Hashed forecasts before every sale, field season and public release (Granite Springs 68-02 results; L01). CSEP N/L tests on rates; reliability of P(rung k) on our own holes.
5. **Gates.** 85-95% coverage of 90% T(z) bands on 10 km blocks; EVSI calibration slope 0.7-1.3; conformal selection with a stated false-discovery rate on the buy list (Jin and Candes 2023; L27).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

- **Data agents:** extraction (N=5 self-ensemble), source handbooks, unit and datum contracts, pass^5 above 95% on a planted-trap golden suite (L39).
- **Watcher agents:** BLM MLRS, state lease diffs, NDOM and DOGM permits, GDR uploads, USGS releases. Each event triggers a belief update and a re-plan.
- **Crowd agents:** parse all-bids, S-1s, NEPA filings, DOE awards (16 exploration-drilling sites from DE-FOA-0003472; L07) into rival beliefs and taken-land masks.
- **Hypothesis tournament:** agents propose new conceptual archetypes per basin. Each becomes a simulator template in C2. It is kept only if it raises held-out log-likelihood (L22).
- **Red-team agent:** hunts for feature-vintage leakage before any replay result is published (L19, L36).
- **Dossier agents:** for the top few thousand parcels by EVSI per dollar, one-page deal packs for human review.

**Compute.** 10^6 synthetic tiles x 10 sensors at about 1 ms each is about 3 CPU-hours for Tier 1 (calc). FNO training on about 5,000 TOUGH3 runs is a few GPU-days (L12). BetaZero training on 10^7 campaigns is the largest job: about 1-4 weeks on 8-32 GPUs (unverified). Nightly re-plan: index for all cells in minutes, tree search for top parcels in about an hour on one GPU node.

## 9. What makes it beat Zanskar

1. **It optimises the right thing.** Zanskar's public stack (GeoCore LightGBM, fault-scenario simulation, FNO; L05) ranks where to drill. HELM ranks dollars per proven MW across land, sensing, drilling and exit together. Land timing and leak cost are where cheap rights are won or lost (L37).
2. **Private and state land.** Zanskar works mainly through BLM nominations. HELM's value layer prices ranch heat options and state leases, where nominations do not leak.
3. **Cheap sensors as first-class actions.** Pandora indices decide when a $300 visit is enough to lock a right, instead of a $0.45M TG screen.
4. **Shared play risk.** A cool hole updates the whole play group. Drilling the most informative hole first can beat drilling the best hole first (L17).
5. **Honest track record.** Hashed forecasts, rung-level lookbacks and EVSI calibration. Zanskar publishes no hit rate (L05). A dated, verifiable record raises sale prices (linkage principle; L37).
6. **Model-agnostic.** Any better science (theirs included, if public) plugs in. The decision engine and the ledger are the durable moat.

## 10. How it drives rights buying and sensor deployment

- **Lock rule.** Lock a parcel when its value exceeds its Pandora reservation value: more measurement is worth less than its cost plus the risk of losing the parcel. MODEL.md's fixed "3x holding cost" rule becomes a learned, parcel-specific threshold.
- **Channel choice.** High edge and high leak cost: buy privately or via state first-file. Low leak cost: nominate. Bid at E[value | win], not raw value.
- **Clock awareness.** Spend on a held lease is timed before its diligence date (L37). Optioned ranch ground gets sensed before option expiry.
- **Sensor choice per hurdle.** Heat-limited cells get profiles and TG holes; permeability-limited cells get pressure loggers, He samplers or geophones; lidded cells get E-MT or darts (L40). The planner picks by EVSI per dollar plus a global term for shared latents (sensor reliability, catch rates).
- **First field spend is information about the sensors.** The calibration campaign (about 20 known blind systems, about 40 cold controls, two detectors per cell; MODEL.md) has the highest global EIG because every later EVSI depends on the reliability matrix.
- **Exit.** Sell at the rung where P(next rung) x price uplift no longer pays for the next action.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Desk test, about $10-20k of compute and API (unverified), weeks 0-10.** "Does planning beat mapping?"
1. Build 200 synthetic Nevadas from GHOST Tier 1, with planted blind systems, masks, dated effort and a simple BLM sale calendar.
2. Run four policies on the same budgets ($0.5M, $2M, $5M): (a) lock the static top 10% of the belief map; (b) one-step EVSI per dollar (VOI-Bank in MODEL.md); (c) HELM index plus depth-2 search; (d) HELM with an oracle belief, to separate belief error from planning error.
3. Repeat on real Nevada frozen at 2005, drawing from the public observation bank, scoring post-2005 blind finds inside locked acreage.

**Pass bar:**
- On synthetic worlds, HELM (c) beats one-step EVSI (b) by at least 1.3x systems per dollar and static locking (a) by at least 1.5x, with bootstrap CIs above 1.
- The Brown-Smith gap for (c) is under 25% of the bound.
- On the 2005 replay, (c) is at least 1.2x over (b) with a CI above 1, and EVSI calibration slope is 0.7-1.3.
- If synthetic lift is strong but real lift is not, the belief, not the planner, is the problem; keep the planner and fix the plug-in. Untested ML lift never kills the design on its own.

## 14. Biggest risks

1. **Optimiser's curse.** A planner exploits belief errors and buys where the model is confidently wrong. Fix: maximum-entropy component, BEL falsification, ambiguity-averse value (penalise disagreement between plug-ins), EVSI calibration in the ledger.
2. **Too few real outcomes.** Rung transitions and sale prices rest on tens of cases. Fix: AI-extracted project-outcome table from MLRS, permits, GRC/SGW and SEC filings (L32), and synthetic worlds for planner skill.
3. **Off-policy replay bias.** Historical replays can only "buy" data that exists. Fix: synthetic-world replays and design-based field samples.
4. **Reward misspecification.** Exit prices per rung and buyer appetite are guessed. Fix: scenario axes, LLM forecaster ensembles on business events (L22), and early buyer conversations.
5. **Tractability.** A full portfolio POMDP is hopeless to solve exactly. Fix: index policy plus local search plus amortised value, with the Brown-Smith bound telling us when this is good enough.
6. **Crowd model errors and rival response.** If rivals copy cheap-sensor tactics, edge shrinks. Fix: re-estimate rival beliefs from every sale; speed of the loop is the defence.
7. **Legal and reputational.** Nomination strategy must stay within BLM rules; any bundling or padding of nominations needs legal review before use.
8. **Simulator bias.** Synthetic worlds can teach the planner the simulator's quirks. Fix: train on several simulators (Tier 1, FNO, TOUGH3 subset) and on archetypes from the hypothesis tournament; gate on real replays.
