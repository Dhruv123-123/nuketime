# D03 · STRATA-West: a geoscience foundation model for heat rights

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Architect: D03 agent. Builds on `../../model/MODEL.md` (SENTINEL-COX v1), `../../MEMO.md`, `../../datasets/SCHEMA.md` and the digests `../evidence/L01`-`L40`. No new web searches were run. Numbers come from the L-files, which cite primary sources. My own arithmetic and unchecked claims are marked (unverified).

Name: **STRATA-West** (Subsurface-Terrain Reanalysis And Thermal Atlas). Later **STRATA-Earth**.

## 1. One-paragraph summary

Geothermal ML is label-starved. There are about 280 positives against about 700k cells (L29). GeoCore trains on 6,009 warm wells against 148 cold ones (L05). The USGS showed that a new algorithm on the same layers adds almost nothing (L04). But the West holds billions of *unlabelled* numbers: 150k Nevada driller reports, 1.1M California well reports, GeoDAWN and GeoFlight flight lines, 400k borehole temperatures, MT transfer functions, 10 m AlphaEarth embeddings, focal mechanisms, water chemistry. STRATA-West learns the *joint structure* of all of them first, with no geothermal labels. It does this by masked any-to-any reconstruction over a shared 3D latent state of the crust to 6 km. It then fine-tunes small heads for heat, open permeability, fluid, cap/mask, existence and economics. Weather AI is the template. GraphCast and Aurora worked because ERA5 existed (L33). The subsurface has no ERA5, so STRATA builds one. It is a learned subsurface reanalysis, pretrained on about 10^6 synthetic "Westlets" with known truth, then on real data. Every sensor is a decoder on the same latent. So the model can *predict any measurement it has not yet seen*. That gives expected information gain per dollar for every cell and sensor in the West. The decision layer is unchanged in spirit from SENTINEL-COX: an effort-thinned Cox count and an RNPV/acre spine. But its inputs become calibrated posteriors from one backbone instead of hand-built per-source pipelines. The moat is the backbone, the synthetic world bank, the point-in-time data lake behind it, and a dated, hashed forecast ledger.

## 2. Target variables

**Grids.** The latent state is a 3D voxel field: 1 km x 1 km x 100 m to 6 km depth on EPSG:5070 equal-area. That is about 3x10^8 voxels for the West (L33 arithmetic). A surface latent sits at 250 m. A 125 m refinement runs inside the more than 1,430 Faulds favourable structural settings (FSS) and inside parcels we are pricing. The Cox count sits on a 2 km mesh. Parcels roll up to PLSS 40-acre aliquots.

**What the heads predict per 500 m column (draws, not points):**
- T at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4, 6 km, as 128 ordinal bins (In-Context Earth style, L10), with P10/P50/P90.
- Flow regime: conductive, upflow, outflow tongue, cold lid, swept.
- Archetype (L09): B1-B7, EGS-conductive, magmatic, rift, plus a max-entropy "unknown play".
- P(open path): critically stressed and not sealed, by depth.
- Fluid: hot fraction, reservoir T from chemistry, He R/Ra.
- Permeability class (Libbey and Murphy SGW 2026 four classes).
- Existence intensity lambda(s) and, as a *separate* head, P_det(s, t): the chance the system would already be known by year t.
- Size mark: MWe lognormal-Pareto, by archetype.
- EGS head: P(200 C or more at 4 km or less), depth to the 150/200/250/300/400 C isotherms, depth to basement.
- Predicted unseen measurements: MT apparent resistivity, 2 m temperature, TG gradient, B/Li, gravity residual. These drive EIG.

**Decision output:** RNPV/acre per parcel from the fixed M34 economics, as in v1. Add: EVSI per dollar for each action (buy, sensor, drill, drop, sell), a crowd-posterior gap (our RNPV minus the map rivals can build from public data), and a conformal buy list with a stated false-discovery rate.

**Headline map:** P(T 150 C or more at 2.5 km or less AND open), per cell, with bands.

## 4. Components and methods

**4.1 Data plane (agents build it, code owns it).** A bitemporal lake: GeoParquet for points and tables, COG for rasters, Zarr/Icechunk for cubes and model draws, MTH5 for MT, DuckDB as the only engine (L35). Every value carries `valid_time`, `known_time`, location error, unit and datum flags, extraction sigma, and search channel. Without `known_time`, a foundation model is a leakage machine, because it memorises surveys done *because* a system was found (L19).

**4.2 Tokenisation.** Every raw reading is one token, not a raster pixel (L29). Point data keep their real geometry. Rasters are cut into 1 km patches. Faults become a graph: nodes at tips, step-overs and intersections; edges carry slip rate, age and dip. MT sites keep their full apparent-resistivity and phase curves. Documents enter only after Stage 0 extraction (4.8). Each value enters with its sigma.

**4.3 Encoders and fusion.** One encoder per modality family. Fusion is HeMIS/MultiMAE-style. Each modality has a learned "absent" token. Modality dropout is used in training, and missing values are never mean-imputed (L29). A Perceiver-IO cross-attention writes tokens into voxel queries. A 3D windowed transformer mixes the state. Its relative-position bias is anisotropic along mapped faults, which fixes the Granite Springs cosimulation artefacts (L10).

**4.4 Hybrid physics core.** Following NeuralGCM (L33), the backbone carries a differentiable steady conduction-advection solve on a coarse 2 km x 250 m grid. The network learns a residual heat source and a permeability field. Samples obey hard limits: no T above the boiling curve, no unphysical inversions below a lid, and Fourier's law in conductive cells. This stops the backbone from inventing ML hotspots that no convection model can make (L11).

**4.5 Pretraining, in three stages.**
1. *Synthetic stage (truth known).* 10^6 Westlets, each 50 x 50 x 10 km. Structure comes from Noddyverse-style kinematic models (L33) plus the eight Faulds FSS templates. Heat and flow come from multi-fidelity simulation: about 10^6 analytic Tier 1 columns, 10^5 FNO-surrogate fault-flow runs (Liang et al. SGW 2026: about 0.025 relative L2 in-scenario, L12), and about 3,000 full TOUGH3/Waiwera runs. Each world also carries masking aquifers, cold lids, sealing, recharge, BHT drilling disturbance, and a *simulated search history*: who drilled where, and why. Forward operators render every sensor, with realistic noise and gaps. The backbone learns to reconstruct the true 3D state from sparse, biased, noisy observations. This is the only place it sees full truth.
2. *Real masked stage (no labels).* About 3-8 billion observation tokens across the West (unverified estimate). Masked any-to-any reconstruction: hide a modality, a region, or a depth range, then predict it from the rest. Example: hide all wells in a 20 km block and predict their T(z) from gravity, magnetics, MT, faults, chemistry and AEF. Example: hide MT and predict it from wells. Losses use each decoder's proper scoring rule (CRPS for continuous, log score for ordinal bins).
3. *Global stage (STRATA-Earth).* Add Iceland, NZ (Taupo), Japan (Kakkonda), Kenya, Western Anatolia, Alberta, Australia and the UK (L10, L34). In L10, frozen-weight In-Context Earth scored 2.2/6.2/5.4 C in Alberta/Australia/UK against 9.2/28.5/8.9 C for kriging. So transfer works and is worth the compute.

**4.6 Generative prior.** A latent diffusion model over Z, trained on synthetic and real-assimilated states. Diffusion Posterior Sampling (Chung 2023, L28) conditions it on any new observation through the sensor decoders. This gives 50-200 posterior members per region, like a GenCast ensemble. Plume shapes look like real systems instead of smooth blobs.

**4.7 Heads.** Small fine-tuned heads (LoRA or adapters) on the frozen backbone. Each head trains on its own label type (Section 6). The existence head feeds the Cox intensity. The effort head feeds P_det. These two never share label-proxy inputs (4.9). Separate per-archetype size heads use Wilmarth-Stimac power density above 200 C and the M33 fit below it (L09). An EGS head is calibrated to FORGE 58-32 (about 199 C at 2.3 km) and Cape (L30).

**4.8 Stage 0 document AI (kept from v1).** A VLM reads headers, units and location. A SAM-plus-tracer reads the curve. Five-sample self-ensembles are used, and the median plus spread becomes sigma (L21). Targets: 132k NDWR reports, 1.11M CA OSWCR, NBMG's 2,002 scans, NDOM and Utah DOGM files, and a dated pre-drill-vs-outcome table from GRC, SGW and OSTI. "Temperature absent" is a real output and feeds the effort head.

**4.9 Leakage firewall (hard-coded).**
- Data-presence masks, roads, leases, nominations, KGRA lines, springs and sinter are routed to the **effort/detection** head only (L19, L20).
- For hindcasts, one backbone is pretrained per freeze year (1990, 2005, 2015), using only tokens with `known_time` before the freeze. Compute is plentiful, so this costs money, not feasibility.
- An adversarial probe must fail to predict labels from lat/lon or from the data-presence mask alone. A "built-infrastructure" direction is projected out of the AEF tokens (L20).

**4.10 Crowd posterior.** We fine-tune the same backbone on *public, gridded* layers only: INGENIOUS, USGS 2025/2026, GeoCore-like inputs, and announcements. This is a model of what rivals can know. Our edge per parcel is our RNPV minus the crowd's RNPV (L37).

## 6. Labels and negatives

Pretraining needs no labels. That is the point. Fine-tuning uses:
- **Continuous censored thermal labels** for the heat head: every well, TG hole and log, including the 4,293 non-conductive wells USGS discards (L03), and the 10 cold Reese River/Argenta TG holes (L04). A cold shallow hole lowers P(shallow anomaly). It does not zero P(reservoir at 800 m) under a mapped recharge aquifer (L02).
- **Dated discoveries** with first-signal channel (the L31 11-case ladder; the USGS 205-system inventory) for the Cox head. 28 plant + 78 known systems are G1. The 99 probable systems are a down-weighted hold-out.
- **Failure-mode labels** extracted from completion reports (L40): no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, fossil. These train the open-path and archetype heads.
- **Synthetic labels** from Westlets: perfect truth for heat, permeability, archetype and existence. They teach the heads what a masked B3 system looks like when no real one is labelled.
- **No cell is negative because it is unlabelled.** The thinned Poisson background integral does that job, as in v1. Positive-unlabelled label frequency c(x) is estimated per archetype (PULSNAR-style, L19).

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

**Quantified.** Four layers stack:
1. Aleatoric: each decoder outputs a full predictive distribution (ordinal bins, censored likelihood).
2. Epistemic: 8-16 backbones, each with a different spatial-block bootstrap and modality-dropout seed (deep ensembles degrade least under shift, L27).
3. State: 50-200 diffusion posterior samples per region.
4. Distance: a dissimilarity index from the area of applicability (Meyer and Pebesma 2021) and an SNGP-style head. Cells far from the training data get wide bands by construction.

The Antarctic BNN result says data variance is about 9x model variance (L10), so layer 1 is never dropped.

**Validated (release gates):**
- kNNDM spatial CV, with the target distance curve taken from unexplored cells, shown beside random and fixed-block CV (L19).
- 85-95% coverage of 90% bands, and PIT KS under 3%, on 10 km blocks and a Basin-and-Range-only hold-out, reported by depth band (L10).
- SBC rank histograms and TARP on 1,000 synthetic Westlets with planted blind systems (L28).
- Point-in-time hindcasts with the frozen backbones: freeze at 2010 and score 2010-2026 blind finds, including Big Blind and Pumpernickel. CSEP N, L and S tests on Cox rates (L27). Molchan and area-capture curves at 1/5/10% of area. Boyce index.
- Three baselines on the same splits: 1-in-550 holes, a distance-to-known smoother, and a frozen-label LightGBM. Plus a rerun of USGS 2025 XGBoost with spatial blocks (L03).
- Conformal selection on the buy list, with spatially weighted calibration by province and archetype (L27).
- Every forecast is hashed and timestamped before each BLM sale and field season (L36).

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane. They never edit the model at run time (L39).
- **Fetch/ETL agents.** Each source has a handbook card (endpoint, CRS, datum, units, traps, golden sample). Two agents run blind on each source and are diffed. The disagreement rate becomes source noise. Gate: pass^5 above 95% on a golden suite with planted unit and datum traps.
- **Extraction agents.** Stage 0 over millions of pages in multiple languages (UNU-GTP, NEDO, MTA, CFE, Pertamina for STRATA-Earth).
- **Simulation agents.** Drive PyTOUGH/pywaiwera to build Westlets and per-prospect natural-state models (L11).
- **Red-team agent.** Hunts for post-discovery leakage and cherry-picked freezes before any number is published.
- **Hypothesis tournament.** Agents propose archetype or structural rules per basin. A rule is kept only if it raises held-out log-likelihood as a head constraint (L22, L23).
- **Watchers.** BLM MLRS, state permits (for example Granite Springs 68-02), GDR uploads (the 16 DOE exploration-drilling sites, L07), and NISAR releases. Each triggers a nightly assimilation step and VOI re-score.

Compute: synthetic generation is about 10^6 CPU-core-hours (unverified). Backbone pretraining is about 0.5-1.5B parameters on 3-8B tokens. That is roughly 64-128 H100s for 3-6 weeks per backbone, about $0.3-0.8M each at cloud rates (unverified). Three freeze-year backbones plus the live one cost about $1.5-3M in year one. Fine-tuning and nightly inference fit on 4-8 GPUs.

## 9. What makes it beat Zanskar

- **Labels.** GeoCore has 148 cold wells (L05). STRATA learns from every unlabelled reading, plus 10^6 synthetic worlds with truth. Label famine stops being the binding constraint.
- **Inputs.** Zanskar uses gridded INGENIOUS layers. STRATA reads raw flight lines, MT curves, full T(z) logs and documents nobody keyed. That beats the commodity GEMS-Prize fault layer coming in 2027 (L07).
- **Depth.** Big Blind sits at about 820 m, below every INGENIOUS TG hole (L02). The 3D latent and physics core reason about depth explicitly. Shallow blanks then count as "unseen", not "cold".
- **Any-sensor prediction.** Decoders give EIG for MT, 2 m, TG and chemistry in every cell. Zanskar's public stack has no search-effort term and no VOI-driven regional policy (L05).
- **Honesty as a product.** Point-in-time backbones, hindcasts and a hashed ledger give a calibrated track record. Buyers pay more for verifiable disclosure (L37).
- **Footprint.** The crowd-posterior gap points spending at private ranch and state land outside Zanskar's 250k-acre federal footprint.
- **Global reuse.** STRATA-Earth turns the same backbone into rights plays in Turkey, Kenya and Indonesia, where Zanskar has no data.

## 10. How it drives rights buying and sensor deployment

1. Each night, the heads give posterior draws. The Cox layer gives existence. M34 turns them into RNPV/acre per parcel under price scenarios.
2. Our edge = our RNPV minus crowd RNPV, minus a winner's-curse shade and a leak cost for nomination (L37).
3. **Buy** where edge is more than 3x the 10-year holding cost and the conformal FDR is under 30%. Routes: private ranch leases, state first-file, BLM leftover parcels, then nominations padded with decoys.
4. **Sense** where the posterior straddles the economic line. For each candidate action, the decoders sample its outcome, and the backbone re-conditions in milliseconds. That gives EVSI/$ per cell. Per hurdle (L40): heat-limited cells get TG holes or 2 m arrays; permeability-limited cells get He bottles, nodes or tidal loggers; lidded cells get E-MT or deep logs.
5. Batch by truck route and mobilisation with submodular selection (L38), then re-plan after each batch.
6. **Sell** with a data room: AGRCC-style P10/P50/P90 MW, calibration plots, lineage, and the ledger.

## 13. Cheapest first test and its pass bar

**Test: "Does pretraining pay?"** Cost about $10-20k (2 weeks, 8 GPUs, plus one engineer; unverified).

Build Mini-STRATA (about 50M params) on the Great Basin using only open, already-keyed data. Inputs: SMU/Burns wells, NBMG BoreholeTemperature, NDWR keyed temperatures, gravity, GeoDAWN magnetics grids, Qfaults/FSS graph, NBMG geochem, NDWR heads, and AEF PCs. Use `known_time` up to 2015. Pretrain with masked reconstruction. Then freeze it and fit two probes:
- **(a) Temperature.** Predict T at 0.5-1.5 km for held-out wells under kNNDM 20-50 km blocks. Compare against kriging, an ICE-style transformer without pretraining, and LightGBM on raw layers.
- **(b) Discovery.** Score the 2015-2026 blind and hidden finds with the frozen-2015 model. These include Big Blind, Pumpernickel, Granite Springs and Gabbs. Report area capture at 1/5/10%.

**Pass bar (all three):**
1. CRPS on block-held-out T improves 10% or more over the best baseline, and 90% bands cover 85-95%.
2. Label efficiency: the probe on 25% of the labels matches the LightGBM baseline on 100%.
3. Discovery: the frozen-2015 model ranks at least as many post-2015 finds in its top 5% of area as the frozen-label LightGBM, and the lat/lon-only adversarial probe has AUC under 0.6.

**If it fails:** the backbone is demoted, not killed. Decoders stay as the imputation and EIG engine inside SENTINEL-COX. Synthetic pretraining is retried at 10x scale before any final verdict, because untested lift is not a reason to kill.

## 14. Biggest risks (and the AI-leveraged answer)

1. **Too few independent regions for the backbone to learn transferable physics.** The Great Basin is about one tectonic "sample". Answer: synthetic Westlets with truth, plus STRATA-Earth global pretraining, plus leave-one-province-out tests.
2. **Data-presence leakage.** Surveys exist because finds exist. Answer: freeze-year backbones, the routing firewall, the red-team agent and adversarial probes.
3. **Sim-to-real gap.** Westlets may not look like real Nevada. Answer: MMD misspecification tests per cell (L28), domain-adversarial alignment, and fallback to v1 column likelihoods for off-simulator cells, which become a sensor lead list.
4. **Foundation models often fail to beat supervised UNets** (PANGAEA, L20). Answer: the gate in Section 13. Value is claimed only under kNNDM and time-split skill.
5. **Overconfident ensembles in blind cells.** Answer: distance-aware head, conformal wrappers, and the layer-1 aleatoric term that never drops.
6. **Opacity to buyers and BLM.** Answer: every head maps to the heat x path x fluid x cap product and a Milkov-style risk table (L17). Each parcel shows its weakest link.
7. **Cost and focus.** $2-4M compute could starve land buying. Answer: stage gates. The 1B backbone is built only after Mini-STRATA passes, and land locks start from v1 outputs in month 1.
8. **Extraction errors poisoning pretraining.** Answer: self-ensemble sigma, two-agent diffs and golden-suite gates before tokens enter the lake.
