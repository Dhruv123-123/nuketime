# D02: TRIBUNAL, a tool-grounded council of geologist agents with a likelihood judge

Date: 24 Oct 2026. Architect: independent design D02. No new web searches were run. Numbers come from the L01-L40 digests and MODEL.md. My own arithmetic or unchecked figures are marked (unverified).

## 1. Summary

TRIBUNAL puts a team of LLM "exploration geologist" agents to work on every prospect in the West, but it never lets an agent state a probability. Agents do what good geologists do: they read the file, pull data, draw a conceptual model, and argue about it. Each conceptual model must be written as a typed, machine-readable card (archetype, upflow location, fault, reservoir depth and temperature, outflow vector, cap and mask, size). A compiler turns each card into a simulator prior. A non-LLM judge then scores every card against the prospect's real observations by marginal likelihood, using the SENTINEL-COX column likelihoods and an amortised neural posterior. "Debate" means falsification: an agent wins only by naming a test (a data query, a simulation, a cheap field measurement) that moves the judge's weights. The posterior is a Bayesian model average over all cards plus a fixed-mass "unknown play" card, then recalibrated per archetype on a blinded, time-frozen hindcast. Every number in a dossier traces to a hashed fact in a bitemporal evidence graph. The output feeds the VOI-Bank: RNPV per acre, a buy list with a false-discovery guarantee, and the next sensor per dollar. This turns the thing that makes Zanskar's geologists valuable (conceptual reasoning per prospect) into something that runs on 3,000+ prospects a month, is calibrated, and leaves an audit trail.

## 4. Components and methods

**C1. Data-plane agents.** Fetch, extract, reproject and watch. Each source has a one-page handbook card (endpoint, CRS, datum, units, traps, golden sample), reusing SCHEMA.md entries (L39). Scanned logs go through the split pipeline from L21: a VLM reads headers and scales, and a SAM-plus-tracer reads T(z). Each value is read five times by two model families; the median goes in and the spread becomes extraction sigma. Gate: pass^5 above 95% on a golden suite with planted F/C, ft/m and NAD27/NAD83 traps.

**C2. Bitemporal evidence graph.** One row per fact with valid_time and known_time (L35), search channel (springs, mining hole, O&G, water well, TG hole, our sensor), location error box, and explicit "temperature absent" for silent driller logs (L18). Negative evidence (dry holes, cold TG holes, relinquished leases) is a first-class node (L23). Contradictions, such as a warm well next to a cold TG hole, are flagged and pushed into the prospect queue.

**C3. Regional prior.** SENTINEL-COX runs everywhere as the "prior desk". It supplies the effort-thinned LGCP intensity, the FSS-type priors (DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x, step-over about 0.85x; L01, L09), the shared mask class and P_det. The council never overrides this layer. It only refines inside a prospect, and the prior enters the judge as the prior weight of each card's existence term.

**C4. Prospect council.** One council per prospect. Roles and tools:
- *Structural geologist*: fault graph, slip and dilation tendency under a sampled stress tensor, lidar scarp freshness, FSS type, GEMS-style fault detector output.
- *Hydrogeologist / geochemist*: NWIS heads, mixing-aware geothermometry (L26), boron and He back-projection along hydraulic gradient, outflow tracing.
- *Geophysicist*: MT/gravity/magnetics ensembles (RTO or trans-D, L13), GeoDAWN flight-line magnetic lows, conductor geometry (dome vs flat tongue).
- *Thermal modeller*: In-Context Earth-style T(z) prior, BHT correction, profile shape (reversals, isothermal segments), runs the simulator.
- *Archivist*: search history, who drilled what and when, lease and nomination history, old company reports; owns P_det arguments.
- *Skeptic*: required to write the null and failure cards: fossil alteration, outflow-only, cold lid, hot but tight, false conductor (basin clay, brine).
- *Land and commercial agent*: tenure, BLM MLRS status, state leases, private ranch ownership, transmission, bid history. It writes no geology; it writes to the VOI-Bank only.

Each geology role must emit 1-4 **Conceptual Model Cards** in a typed DSL. A card holds: archetype (B1 shallow outflow, B2 range-hosted no discharge, B3 basin-buried capped, B4 cold-aquifer lid, B5 rain curtain, B6 carbonate sweep, B7 conductive sedimentary aquifer; L09), upflow polygon, controlling fault ID and dip, reservoir depth and T ranges, outflow azimuth and length, cap depth, mask parameters and size class. Each parameter is a distribution, not a point. A card that does not compile is rejected with the compiler error, and the agent retries.

Agents use different model families (for example Claude, GPT and an open-weights model) and different retrieval slices, so errors are less correlated (L22 notes debate fails when agents share the same blind spot).

**C5. Likelihood judge.** This is the heart of calibration. It has no LLM inside.
1. *Card compiler.* Turns each card into a prior over simulator parameters for GHOST Tier 1 (analytic column plus plume) and, in v2, a shared 3D Basin-and-Range FNO (L12; about 5,000 TOUGH3 runs, relative error about 0.025-0.07).
2. *Evidence.* Each card's log marginal likelihood is computed on the prospect's observations: censored T(z) columns, geothermometer samples through the mixing model, MT conductor ensembles through a learned resistivity-to-temperature likelihood, 2 m probes with seasonal nuisance terms. Engine: an amortised neural posterior / likelihood estimator trained on 10^5-10^6 simulations per archetype (L28), with ensembles for calibration and SBC/TARP checks.
3. *Model averaging.* Weights w_k proportional to prior(k) x evidence(k). A fixed-mass "unknown play" card (max-entropy, about 10% prior mass to start) keeps a wrong set of cards from producing false confidence (L15, L38, Intelligent Prospector v2).
4. *Misspecification test.* An MMD test (L28) checks whether the observed data are typical of any card. If none fit, the prospect is flagged "off-simulator" and routed to a sensor visit, not to a buy.

**C6. Falsification debate.** Rounds, not free talk. In each round any agent may file a **challenge**: "query X", "run simulation Y", or "a 2 m array at Z would split cards 2 and 4". The judge runs every cheap challenge (data and simulation) and re-weights. Field challenges go to the VOI-Bank as priced actions. A round ends when no challenge moves any weight by more than 0.02 or the token budget is spent. Agents can see weights but cannot change them by argument. This follows the evidence that free debate does not beat self-consistency, and that LLMs cannot self-correct without outside feedback (Smit et al. ICML 2024; Huang et al.; L22). It also borrows the co-scientist tournament, but ranks hypotheses by held-out log likelihood, not by LLM Elo.

**C7. Agent learning loop.** Agent prompts, retrieval policies and role mixes are versioned. Each version is scored by the judge's log score on the blinded hindcast. A version is promoted only if it improves held-out log score with bootstrap CI above zero. Agents can propose new likelihood terms or features ("helium R/Ra should enter as a permeability channel"), but these go into code only after a human review and a hindcast gate (L23 rule: keep a rule only if it lifts held-out skill).

**C8. Calibration and selection.** Per-archetype isotonic or Venn-Abers recalibration of P(exists) and of T(z) quantiles, fitted on hindcast outcomes. Buy lists come from conformal selection with Benjamini-Hochberg on spatially weighted calibration sets (Jin and Candes 2023; L27). The PU label-frequency constant c(x) comes from the Cox effort term, checked with POC plots against cold wells.

**C9. Decision engine.** VOI-Bank as a portfolio POMDP (POMCPOW, depth-2/3 look-ahead; L15). State = regional posterior + prospect card weights + our holdings + lease clocks. Actions = buy, option, nominate, bid, sense (2 m, E-field MT, nodes, TG hole), drill slim hole, drop, sell. The council proposes actions; the planner prices them. Bids use RNPV conditional on winning, minus a winner's-curse term from the crowd model (L37).

**C10. Audit layer.** Each dossier shows: the card set with weights, the facts each card used (with page and bbox), every challenge and its effect, the MMD result, the calibration curve for that archetype, and a lineage triple (git SHA, data snapshot ID, config hash). Forecast maps and buy lists are hashed and timestamped before every BLM sale and field season (CSEP-style pre-registration; L36). A red-team agent hunts for post-discovery leakage before any result is published.

## 6. Labels and negatives

- **Positives:** USGS 205-system Great Basin inventory, USGS 2008 identified systems, the M18/L31 11-case blind ladder with first-signal dates and channels, Zanskar's announced finds (Big Blind about 121 C at about 823 m; Pumpernickel about 137 C at about 762 m), Nevada PFA Gabbs (124.9 C at about 150 m).
- **Graded negatives:** every cold or warm hole as a censored column (depth-of-sight matters: a cold 244 m hole at Argenta does not rule out 800 m). Reese River's 10 cold TG wells, Argenta's 10, the four cool Gabbs holes, Granite Springs holes that missed upflow.
- **Failure-mode ladder** (L40): each dry hole is tagged no heat, hot but tight, outflow only, masked, or fossil by AI extraction from completion reports. The skeptic agent's cards are scored against these.
- **Pending labels:** the 16 DOE exploration-drilling sites (DE-FOA-0003472) and Granite Springs 68-02 are frozen as prospective tests before their logs land.
- **Design-based sample:** part of the field budget goes to stratified-random cells for an unbiased accuracy estimate (L36).

## 7. Uncertainty and validation

**Blinded hindcast.** This is the main test and the main defence against LLM memory. Frontier LLMs have read about McGinness Hills and Big Blind. So hindcast prospects are *geo-blinded*: coordinates are rotated and shifted into a local frame, place names, operator names and system names are replaced with tokens, and only facts with known_time before the freeze are retrievable. A contamination probe asks each agent to guess the real location or name. Prospects it identifies are dropped and the rate is reported. We also report blinded-vs-unblinded skill; a large gap means memory, not reasoning.

**Freezes:** 1980, 1990, 2005, 2015, 2020 (L36), with labels purged within 10-20 km of test discoveries and a 2-year embargo (L19). Cross-validation is kNNDM with the target distance curve from unexplored Great Basin cells.

**Gates for release:**
- Blind-system area capture at top 1/5/10% of area beats three baselines: 1-in-550 base rate, distance-to-known smoother, frozen-label LightGBM. Show USGS 2025 alongside.
- Council posterior beats SENTINEL-COX alone on held-out log score for the same prospects (otherwise the council is costing money for nothing).
- Reliability: Brier skill above 0 and 90% T(z) bands covering 85-95% on spatial blocks, reported by depth band and by archetype.
- SBC rank histograms and TARP coverage for the NPE.
- Upflow location: for the outflow cases, the true upflow inside the 80% density region at least 70% of the time (unverified bar; set by synthetic tests first).
- Synthetic worlds from GHOST with planted blind systems: check card-weight calibration where truth is known.

**Prospective:** CSEP N, L and S tests on hashed forecasts, scored on DOE drilling results, Zanskar announcements and our own holes.

## 8. Agents and compute at scale

- Regional prior: all of the West, nightly.
- Councils: top 3,000 prospects per month by EVSI/$, plus any prospect with a new fact or contradiction. Over a year this covers every one of the 1,430+ Great Basin FSS polygons several times, plus basin tiles and other provinces.
- Token budget per council: about 1-3M tokens (6-8 roles, 3-5 rounds, retrieval). 3,000 councils is about 3-9B tokens a month, roughly $15k-$90k a month at current prices (unverified). Small next to one TG hole.
- Simulation: 10^6 GHOST Tier 1 runs (CPU, cheap), about 5,000-20,000 TOUGH3 runs for the FNO (about 100k CPU-hours, unverified), NPE training on 8 GPUs.
- Field agents: during campaigns the planner re-scores after each batch of probes, and a council re-convenes on that prospect overnight.

## 9. What beats Zanskar

1. **Conceptual reasoning at scale.** Zanskar has a team of geologists and GeoCore LightGBM. TRIBUNAL gives every prospect a full conceptual-model review, every month, with written alternatives.
2. **Upflow, not hotspot.** Cards place the upflow up-gradient of warm wells. We buy the right acre, often before the first hole.
3. **Calibration they do not publish.** GeoCore reports 94.5% of springs in the top 50% (L05). We publish blind capture, reliability and a hashed prediction ledger.
4. **Negatives.** Censored columns turn tens of thousands of wells into evidence, against GeoCore's 148 cold wells.
5. **Failure modes.** Our skeptic and the failure ladder learn why holes fail, which is where projects die (permeability was the weak leg in 9 of 11 PFA projects; L40).
6. **Land focus.** The land agent and crowd model point us at private ranch and state ground and at gaps between our posterior and the public one, where Zanskar's BLM-heavy footprint is thin.
7. **Sellable dossier.** Buyers (Ormat, Fervo, utilities) get an auditable package with AGRCC-style categories, which lifts the sale price per acre (L37).

## 10. Driving rights and sensors

- **Buy:** conformal-selected list per province and archetype with an FDR target (for example 30% at the option stage). Bid = RNPV conditional on winning minus winner's-curse term. High-leak targets go to private or state first-file routes rather than BLM nomination.
- **Sense:** the planner prices each challenge the council files. Typical routing: heat-limited prospects get TG holes or 2 m arrays; permeability-limited get E-field MT, nodes or He sampling; lidded prospects get MT or deep logs; outflow-only gets up-gradient water-well chemistry. Batches per mobilisation with submodular selection and travel cost.
- **Drop and sell:** cards whose weight collapses trigger drop; a converged card set plus slim hole triggers EKI natural-state modelling and sale.

## 11. Team and compute

- 1 lead modeller (Bayesian/SBI), 1 simulation engineer (TOUGH3/FNO), 1 agent and data-plane engineer, 1 senior exploration geologist (writes the card DSL, audits dossiers, sets archetype priors), 0.5 land and economics analyst. Agents do the bulk of extraction and prospect review.
- Compute: one large CPU node pool for simulation, 8 GPUs, LLM API budget about $20k-$100k a month, object storage plus DuckDB.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Test:** a blinded mini-hindcast on 40 prospects, run by the council with GHOST Tier 1 as the judge. About $5k-$10k of tokens and three weeks of one engineer (unverified).
- 11 blind or hidden successes from the L31 ladder (Desert Peak, Salt Wells, McGinness Hills, Blue Mountain, Don A. Campbell, Tungsten Mountain, Lightning Dock, Gabbs Valley, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel), each frozen before its first signal and again before its deciding signal.
- 15 documented disappointments: Argenta Rise, Reese River FSS, cool Gabbs holes, other cold TG programmes.
- 14 random FSS polygons with no known system.

All are geo-blinded, with only pre-freeze facts.

**Pass bar (all four):**
1. Contamination probe identifies fewer than 10% of prospects.
2. The council posterior beats SENTINEL-COX alone and a frozen LightGBM on mean held-out log score, with bootstrap CI above zero.
3. At the deciding-signal freeze, at least 7 of 11 successes rank in the top 10 of 40, and at most 3 of the 15 disappointments do.
4. In at least 3 of the 4 outflow-first cases (Don A. Campbell, Granite Springs, Gabbs, McGinness), the card-weighted upflow density puts more mass up-gradient of the first hole than on it.

If (2) fails, the council does not add value beyond the core model, and TRIBUNAL drops to a dossier and extraction tool on top of SENTINEL-COX.

## 14. Biggest risks and AI-leveraged answers

- **LLM memory leaks the answers.** Geo-blinding, contamination probes, blinded-vs-unblinded gap reporting, and the fact that the judge, not the LLM, sets probabilities.
- **Cards the simulator cannot express.** The unknown card, MMD flags, and agents filing "missing physics" tickets that feed the FNO ensemble.
- **Judge misspecification looks like calibration.** SBC/TARP on synthetic worlds, NPE ensembles, per-archetype recalibration, and coverage reported by depth.
- **Small hindcast n.** 11 blind cases is thin. Synthetic planted worlds, the 205-system inventory and the DOE pending sites widen the test set; report CIs, never point skill.
- **Correlated agent errors.** Mixed model families and retrieval slices, a mandatory skeptic, and promotion only on held-out log score.
- **Silent geospatial errors.** Typed contracts, golden-suite pass^k, two-agent diffs fed in as source noise.
- **Cost and latency.** Councils only where EVSI/$ is high; the regional prior covers the rest.
- **Goodhart on the hindcast.** Freeze the test freezes in advance, limit promotions per quarter, and keep a sealed final freeze plus prospective CSEP scoring.
