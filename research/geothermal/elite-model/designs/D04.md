## 1. Name and one-paragraph summary

**TWIN-WEST** (Thermal-hydraulic Westlet Inference Network) treats prospecting the way weather centres treat forecasting. Weather AI won because ERA5 existed: a dense, physically consistent reanalysis made by pushing sparse raw observations through a physics model (L33). The subsurface has no ERA5. TWIN-WEST builds one. It is a 64-128 member ensemble of joint 3D states of the western US crust to 6 km: temperature, pressure, Darcy flux, permeability, porosity, resistivity, density, magnetisation, fluid chemistry and a fault-permeability field. Each member is a physically legal steady (or slowly evolving) hydrothermal solution. The ensemble is made by assimilating every raw observation we can find, each through its own forward operator, into a generative prior of "Westlets". Westlets are 20 x 20 x 6 km tiles drawn from a structural, hydrological and thermal world generator and solved with TOUGH3/Waiwera. Neural-operator surrogates (FNO plus a local near-fault corrector) make the physics cheap enough to run millions of times. Amortised simulation-based inference (a Simformer-style network that conditions on any subset of observations) gives each tile a fast posterior. Ensemble data assimilation stitches tiles into one consistent West-wide state. The dated discovery record is not used as labels. It is one more observation, with a detection operator driven by dated search effort. So the twin must reproduce both the physics we measure and the history of who found what. The decision layer reads hidden upflows straight off the members, prices them in RNPV per acre, and chooses sensors by running Observing System Simulation Experiments (OSSEs) on the twin itself. The reanalysis is both our prospecting engine and a sellable data product.

## 2. Target variables

**State grid (the reanalysis).** A 1 km x 1 km horizontal grid with 100 m vertical layers to 6 km depth over the 11 western states plus ID/MT. That is about 3.1M km2 x 60 layers, or about 1.9 x 10^8 voxels (calc). Inside each of the >1,430 Faulds favourable structural settings (FSS), and on any parcel we hold or are pricing, a conditional downscaler refines the state to 250 m x 50 m in the top 3 km. Equal-area projection EPSG:5070. Ensemble size 64 members West-wide, 256 inside refined tiles.

**Physical targets per 500 m parcel cell** (read off the members):
- T(z) at 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 4 and 6 km, as P10/P50/P90 and full samples.
- P(upflow): the share of members with a convective upflow zone (vertical Darcy flux above a set threshold and T above 120 C at 1.5 km) whose plume footprint covers the cell.
- Upflow offset vector: where the root of the plume sits relative to the cell. This tells us to buy up-gradient, not the warm well.
- Permeability class (Libbey-Murphy 4 classes) and productivity index at the reservoir depth.
- Mask/archetype class, read as an outcome of the physics, not a label: open, cold lid, aquifer-swept, outflow tongue, sealed/fossil, conductive-only.
- Depth to the 150/200/250/300 C isotherms (EGS head), and P(T >= 374 C at <= 6 km) (superhot head).

**System targets.** Each member is segmented into discrete systems (connected upflow plumes). Each system gets a reservoir temperature, a volume, a power-density MW estimate (Wilmarth-Stimac curve for > 200 C, the M33 Great Basin fit below), and a "discovered by year t" probability. Summing over members gives the number of undiscovered systems per province with F95/F50/F5, in USGS units.

**Decision outputs.** RNPV per acre for each parcel, computed by the fixed M34 GETEM-style economics layer over member samples, with a winner's-curse correction for BLM tracts. Plus a ranked action list: buy, option, sense (which sensor, where), drill, drop, sell. Plus the "edge" map: our posterior minus the crowd posterior (Section 8).

## 4. Components and methods

**A. Observation operators.** Each data type gets a forward operator h_k from the 3D state to what the sensor would read, plus a noise model. Equilibrium logs: Gaussian, sigma 0.5-1 C. BHT: a learned static-formation-temperature correction (Bassam-style ANN, L34) with a skewed lower-bound likelihood and hierarchical bias by era, mud and shut-in time; sigma 2-16 C depending on metadata (L10). TG and cold holes: censored likelihood to their logged depth only. 2 m probes: a 1D heat-flux-plus-seasonal-wave operator with per-probe amplitude and phase nuisances (L38). Water chemistry: a mixing-aware forward model (reservoir T, hot fraction, cold end-member, CO2 loss) run through an amortised PHREEQC/GeoT emulator (L26). Helium R/Ra: an operator on fault-connected deep flux, so it informs permeability, not heat. MT: the state's resistivity comes from a learned rock-physics map (temperature, porosity, salinity, smectite fraction from T history), then a 1D/2D MT forward solve; we compare against posterior ensembles of each MT inversion (RTO or trans-D, 50-200 models; L13), not one smooth model. Gravity and magnetics: standard potential-field operators on density and magnetisation, with a "hydrothermal demagnetisation" term tied to the T and alteration history. InSAR and seismicity: operators on pore pressure and fault permeability, with a detection threshold (Mc) layer. Each operator has a learned residual trained on paired state-and-reading sites (FORGE, Coso, Brady, Kakkonda, Wairakei). AI-extracted values carry their extraction spread (self-ensemble median and spread, L21) and a township-range location error box (400-800 m).

**B. Westlet world generator.** This is the prior, and it is where geology lives. It draws a tile from: a fault graph (Qfaults, NSHM 2023, Faulds FSS polygons and the 8 FSS templates, plus GEMS-Prize-style buried faults from GeoDAWN magnetics); a basin-fill thickness from gravity; a stratigraphy and conductivity column; recharge, water table and aquifer type; basal heat flux from Curie depth, Moho, CONUS-MT conductors and heat-flow data; and a fault "sealing clock" (L40) that sets fault permeability from time since last slip, slip tendency under a sampled stress tensor, and silica deposition. Conditioning variables come from real public rasters for that tile, so the prior is local. Mixing weights for FSS types start at the Faulds enrichments (DTZ about 4.8x, pull-apart about 2.2x, step-over about 0.85x; L01, L02) and are learned. A max-entropy "unknown play" component keeps the prior from ruling out something new (L38). Each draw is solved at three fidelities: analytic column-plus-plume (about 1 ms, 10^6 runs), 2.5D section TOUGH3 (minutes, 10^5 runs), and 3D TOUGH3/Waiwera tiles at 64^3 to 128^3 cells with two-phase flow (CPU-hours, 2-3 x 10^4 runs).

**C. Surrogates.** A multi-fidelity 3D FNO learns the map from generator parameters and boundary conditions to the full state and to all predicted observations. Liang et al. (SGW 2026) reached relative L2 error below 0.025 in-scenario and about 0.07 on unseen faults from 1,200 runs (L11, L12). Multi-fidelity training cut data cost by about 81% in CO2 work (L12). Because FNOs blur sharp fronts, a local DeepONet or U-Net corrector handles the top 3 km within 2 km of mapped faults, scored on peak temperature and upflow location, not mean L2. A latent diffusion model (GenCast-style; L33) learns the distribution of solved tile states. It gives a generative prior that can be conditioned on any forward operator by Diffusion Posterior Sampling (L28). An out-of-distribution check (ensemble disagreement plus an MMD test on summary statistics) flags tiles where the surrogate is outside its training range. Those tiles fall back to direct 2.5D/3D runs or to v1's 1D censored column.

**D. Inference.** Four stages.
- D1. A Simformer-style amortised posterior per tile (L28). It reads a ragged token set of raw observations (x, y, z, type, value, error class, source, year) and returns 512 posterior samples of the latent tile parameters in under a second. Trained on 10^6+ simulated (parameters, observations) pairs with realistic data gaps, BHT censoring and search effort baked in. Ensembles of NPEs, balanced training and SBC fix overconfidence (L28).
- D2. West-wide assimilation. Tiles overlap by 5 km. A localised ensemble smoother with multiple data assimilation (ES-MDA) updates all tiles together, with shared province-level hyper-parameters (basal flux scale, conductivity laws, BHT bias by era). Where tiles meet, the stitching keeps heat flux and Darcy flux continuous. D1 samples initialise D2, so D2 needs few iterations.
- D3. Refinement. For tiles holding optioned parcels, run full ensemble Kalman inversion (600-800 forward runs, 94.7% coverage in synthetic tests; L10, L11) or data space inversion (about 500-1,400 runs, 92-93% coverage of 95% bands; L11, L12) with the true simulator, not the surrogate. This becomes the data-room exhibit.
- D4. Model discrepancy. A Gaussian-process discrepancy term per observation type absorbs systematic misfit. A tile whose discrepancy stays large after assimilation is flagged "off-simulator" and ranked as a lead for a field visit, not as a confident score.

**E. Discovery layer.** This is what makes the twin a prospecting tool and not just a temperature map. In each member, connected upflow plumes are segmented into systems. For each system, the probability it was discovered by year t is 1 - exp(-sum over channels c of q_c(depth to signal, surface leakage, mask) x E_c(s, t)). The three channels are surface leakage (springs, sinter), shallow wells and mining holes, and dedicated exploration (TG holes, 2 m, our sensors), as in v1. E_c(s, t) is dated search effort from NDWR driller reports, OSWCR, NDOM/DOGM spud dates, MRDS and MLRS mining density, OFR 99-425 TG holes and INGENIOUS 2 m surveys. The dated discovery record (USGS 205-system inventory, M18 blind-case table with first-signal year) is then an observation: members that predict systems in places searched hard for decades and never found are down-weighted, and members that fail to produce the known systems are down-weighted too. The q_c are fitted jointly with the physics. Effort never enters the physics prior. This is the Diggle preferential-sampling fix (L18) done in 3D.

**F. Decision.** A MW-per-well emulator (GEOPHIRES and ResFrac-style runs, calibrated to Cape, FORGE, Red, Soultz and Habanero; L30) turns each member's temperature, flow class and productivity into MW and cost. RNPV per acre comes from the fixed M34 economics with a 0.5-0.7x lookback haircut on MW (L17). Sensor choice uses OSSEs (Section 9). Portfolio actions use depth-2 look-ahead over the ensemble, with a Brown-Smith upper bound reported (L15).

## 6. Labels and negatives

There are no class labels. Every reading is a likelihood on the physical state. This is the main strength of the physics-first route.
- A cold equilibrium log to 300 m is a direct constraint on T and heat flux down to 300 m. Below that it only constrains what physics allows: a cold lid over a hot aquifer stays possible if the generator has an aquifer there. Argenta (ten TG wells to 244 m, mean 24.35 C against 25.7 C regional; L02) becomes "shallow cold, deep conductor present", which the twin can explain with a swept lid.
- The 4,293 flow-disturbed wells that USGS discards (L03) become the richest data we have, because reversals and isothermal sections are exactly what convective members predict.
- Positive finds (plants, known systems, Big Blind, Pumpernickel) enter twice: as temperature observations at their wells, and as dated events in the discovery layer.
- Plants, mines, waterfloods and production fields are masked out of the natural-state data after their start dates. Their pre-production logs stay in.
- Failure modes come for free. A dry hole is explained in each member as "no heat", "hot but tight", "outflow only" or "masked". The posterior over these reasons is the competing-risks label L40 asks for.

## 7. Uncertainty: how it is quantified and validated

**Quantified.** The ensemble carries five sources: prior (generator), observation noise and censoring, extraction error and location error, surrogate error (FNO error is learned and added as noise), and model discrepancy (D4). Permeability uncertainty stays wide where data are thin, as Krafla shows it should (more than two orders of magnitude even with many wells; L11). Depth variance grows below the deepest nearby calibration well (L13).

**Validated, in order of honesty.**
1. Twin experiments and OSSEs. Draw a "true" synthetic West from the generator, plant blind systems, sample observations with the real data geometry and real search history, then run the full pipeline. Gate: SBC rank histograms flat, TARP coverage within 5 points, planted blind systems recovered in the top 5% of area at least 70% of the time (unverified target).
2. Held-out wells under kNNDM CV with 10-20 km purge (L19). Report CRPS of T(z) by depth band, 90% coverage (gate 85-95%), PIT KS below 3% (L10). Compare against universal kriging, In-Context Earth (4.7 C MAE nationally, 11.2 C at 4-6 km) and Stanford InterPIGNN.
3. Point-in-time hindcasts using `known_time` joins. Freeze at 1980, 1990, 2005, 2015. Score the rank of later blind finds (Desert Peak, McGinness, Don A. Campbell, Gabbs, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel) and the CSEP N, L and S tests on the count of later discoveries (L27, L36).
4. Prospective, pre-registered forecasts hashed before each field season and BLM sale. Granite Springs 68-02 (NBMG permits, May 2026; L01) is the first live test.

## 8. AI agents and compute at scale

Agents run the data plane, not the model. A document agent extracts T(z) curves, BHT metadata, lithology tops and flow tests from NBMG, NDWR, CalGEM, NDOM, DOGM and OSTI scans with a five-sample self-ensemble (L21). A setup agent builds tile inputs under unit, datum and nodata contracts (L39). A simulation agent drives PyTOUGH/pywaiwera to launch, check and repair runs; failed or non-converged runs are logged, not dropped, because silent selection would bias the prior. A falsification agent runs prior predictive checks per province: if real observations sit outside the generator's range (robust Mahalanobis, 97.5th percentile; BEL protocol, L14), it proposes generator changes, and a change is kept only if it lifts held-out likelihood. A watcher agent tracks GDR, NBMG permits, BLM nominations and the 16 DOE exploration-drilling sites (L07), and triggers nightly incremental assimilation.

Compute: about 2-3 x 10^4 3D runs at 2-20 CPU-hours each is roughly 2-6 x 10^5 CPU-hours, about $10-30k on spot (unverified). Surrogate training: 8-16 GPUs for 2-4 weeks. West-wide assimilation: about 7,800 tiles x 64 members x a few ES-MDA steps on surrogates is minutes to hours on a GPU node (calc). Refined EKI on a parcel: about 700 true runs, a day on 1,000 cores.

**Crowd posterior.** We run the same twin with only data a rival could use without extraction or our sensors: keyed public data and published maps. Our edge per parcel is the gap between our RNPV and the crowd's. We bid and option only where that gap is large and positive after a winner's-curse correction (L37).

## 9. How it drives rights buying and sensor deployment

**Buying.** Lock a parcel when our RNPV per acre beats three times the 10-year holding cost and beats the crowd RNPV by a set margin. The upflow-offset vector moves money to the up-gradient parcel, not the warm well. Private ranch heat and state leases are ranked the same way as BLM land, because land screens never touch the geology.

**Sensors.** OSSEs replace hand-set EVSI. For each candidate action (a 2 m line, ten E-field MT nodes, a 150 m TG hole, a 500-1,000 m slim hole, a ranch-well chemistry bottle, a nodal seismic array), we simulate its reading in every member, update the ensemble with the surrogate, and measure the change in decision value. Weather centres do this before they launch satellites. The same machinery prices sensors that do not exist yet, and answers 3D questions, such as whether MT can separate a flat outflow tongue from a dome over upflow. Actions are batched per field trip with a travel-cost term and re-planned after each batch.

## 10. What makes it beat Zanskar

- Zanskar's public stack is LightGBM on INGENIOUS layers (GeoCore: 148 cold wells against 6,009 warm; L05) plus prospect-scale FNOs. TWIN-WEST uses physics at regional scale, so every cold well, reversal and dry hole becomes information instead of a missing negative.
- Depth. Big Blind sits at about 820 m, deeper than any INGENIOUS TG hole (L02). A physics prior can extrapolate from shallow data to 1-3 km with honest error bars. A classifier cannot.
- Consistency. All observation types must agree in one physical state. A false MT conductor (basin clay, brine) that does not fit the temperature and gravity is down-weighted automatically.
- One posterior, many buyers: hydrothermal for Ormat-type buyers, isotherm depths for Fervo-type EGS buyers, and conduction-only output for closed-loop buyers (L06).
- Search effort in the likelihood: "not found here" is scored against how hard people looked.
- The versioned reanalysis with dated scorecards (GeoBench-West, L33) is a moat that grows with every hole we drill.

## 13. Cheapest first test and pass bar

**Nevada depth-extrapolation hindcast (about 4-6 weeks, about $8k compute; unverified).** Use only the analytic plus 2.5D generator (no 3D yet), the tile NPE and keyed public data frozen at 2005 (no AI extraction). Predict T at 0.5 and 1 km for every equilibrium log, TG hole and well deeper than 300 m that became public after 2005, and for the post-2005 blind finds.

Pass if all three hold:
1. CRPS at 0.5-1 km beats universal kriging and In-Context Earth-style nearest-well baselines by at least 15% on held-out wells deeper than the nearest training well, under kNNDM CV.
2. 90% intervals cover 85-95% of held-out readings, and coverage holds outside the area of applicability.
3. With data frozen before each first signal, at least 4 of the 7 blind cases (Desert Peak, McGinness, Don A. Campbell, Gabbs Valley, Granite Springs, Big Blind, Pumpernickel) fall in the top 5% of Nevada area for P(upflow), and Argenta and Reese River cold holes are not called hot at 300 m.

Fail means the generator is misspecified. The fix is to widen it with the falsification agent, not to kill the design.

## 14. Biggest risks and AI-leveraged answers

1. **Prior misspecification.** Real systems may sit outside the generator (fossil systems, transient pulses with about 250-year periods, carbonate sweep). Answer: max-entropy "unknown play" class, MMD off-simulator flags, a falsification agent that widens the generator, and transient (not steady) runs for a share of draws.
2. **Permeability is weakly identified.** Temperature fixes heat well but permeability poorly. Answer: give permeability its own observations (helium, seismicity, InSAR, lost circulation, flow tests extracted from scans) and let VOI target permeability-limited cells.
3. **Surrogate error near faults.** FNOs blur sharp upflows. Answer: local corrector, OOD fallback to true runs, and surrogate error in the likelihood.
4. **Prior dominance.** In most cells there is little data, so the posterior is the prior. Answer: this is honest, and the OSSE engine sends sensors exactly there; the crowd-posterior gap stops us from buying on prior alone.
5. **Engineering size.** Many moving parts. Answer: ship the 1D/2.5D twin first, keep v1's censored column as fallback, and gate 3D on month-6 lift.
6. **Count weakly tied to physics.** Segmenting plumes into "systems" depends on thresholds. Answer: report counts at several thresholds and fit the threshold through the discovery likelihood.
7. **Leakage.** Gravity and MT were often collected because a system was found (L19). Answer: bitemporal store and point-in-time joins for every hindcast; an adversarial agent hunts leaks before any result is published.
