## 1. The regulatory pathway (how a part becomes "safety-related" for a US reactor)
- 10 CFR 50 Appendix B (QA) and 10 CFR Part 21 (defect reporting). A safety-related part comes either from an Appendix B supplier ("N-stamp"/QA program) or from a commercial-grade item (CGI) that a "dedicating entity" turns into a basic component through **commercial grade dedication (CGD)** (recalled, standard).
- Guidance chain (recalled): Generic Letter 89-02 (1989) and GL 91-05; EPRI NP-5652 (1988); EPRI TR-102260 (1994 supplement); **EPRI 3002002982** (2014, Rev 1 to NP-5652/TR-102260); NRC **Regulatory Guide 1.164** (June 2017) endorses 3002002982. Part 21 was revised in 2015. NEI 14-05A lets accredited calibration/test labs (ILAC/ISO 17025) be used without a source audit. Digital items use EPRI TR-106439 and TR-107330, plus the newer EPRI digital dedication guidance.
- The CGD process is:
  1. Technical evaluation: safety function, failure modes, credible failure modes and **critical characteristics** (CCs).
  2. Select acceptance methods for the CCs: Method 1, special tests and inspections; Method 2, commercial-grade survey of the supplier; Method 3, source verification; Method 4, acceptable supplier/item performance record.
  3. Run tests on samples (sampling plans, often destructive, e.g. PMI, hardness, dimensional, functional, seismic/EQ by analysis or similarity).
  4. Prepare the dedication package and sign off under Appendix B.
  5. Keep the package for audit by NRC vendor inspection (NRC Vendor Inspection Program, NUPIC joint audits).
- Equipment qualification overlays: 10 CFR 50.49 (environmental qualification, harsh environment) and IEEE 344 (seismic). For EQ items, qualification by test (thermal/radiation ageing plus LOCA simulation) is the physics clock: months and $100k to $1M or more (recalled).
- Design-change overlay: if a replacement is not "like-for-like", the utility needs an **equivalency evaluation** (EPRI "Plant Support Engineering: Guidelines for the Technical Evaluation of Replacement Items", TR-107372 / 3002000244?, recalled) and possibly a 10 CFR 50.59 screen or evaluation. This is where obsolete parts hurt most: the original is gone, so the substitute is a design change.

## 2. Demand: spend, volumes, lead times (all recalled unless stated)
- US fleet: 94 operating reactors at about 54 sites (2024-26). Palisades restarted in 2025-26, and the restarts of Three Mile Island-1/Crane (2027-28) and Duane Arnold are announced.
- NEI generating cost: about $31-33/MWh in 2022-2023, of which O&M is about $19-21/MWh, fuel about $5-6/MWh and capital about $6-7/MWh. The fleet makes about 775-780 TWh/yr, which puts total O&M at about $15B/yr.
- Materials and parts are typically 15-25% of non-fuel O&M, i.e. **about $2-4B/yr fleetwide** in materials, parts and services from vendors. Safety-related procurement is a subset. Per site, roughly $20-60M/yr in materials (recalled/estimate).
- The cost premium of dedication (recalled, widely cited in industry): a safety-related dedicated item typically costs **2-10x the commercial catalog price**, and much more for low-value items (a $50 relay can become a $2,000-$5,000 dedicated relay). A single dedication package (technical evaluation plus testing) costs about **$5k-$50k**, while a complex or digital item costs $100k-$500k or more.
- Lead times (recalled):
  - Stocked dedicated parts from Curtiss-Wright, Paragon or Framatome take weeks.
  - A new CGD for a new item takes 3-9 months.
  - A reverse-engineered obsolete part takes 6-18 months.
  - An EQ requalification takes 12-24 months.
  - Large components (reactor coolant pump seals, transformers) take 1-3 years. Large power transformer lead times rose to 2-4 years in 2023-25.
- Obsolescence scale: EPRI and the utility obsolescence groups often say that **thousands of active plant part numbers become obsolete each year**. Utilities carry about 50k-100k+ catalogued items per site, and a meaningful fraction (often quoted at 10-30%) have no current OEM source (recalled; EPRI/NUOG figures). Analog I&C (Foxboro Spec 200, Westinghouse 7300, GE/MAC, Bailey) is the most cited obsolescence cluster.
- Industry obsolescence infrastructure (recalled):
  - EPRI Plant Engineering / Obsolescence program. EPRI holds the "Obsolescence Management" guidelines and the reverse engineering guideline (EPRI 3002003001? "Guideline for Reverse Engineering at Nuclear Power Plants", originally NP-6895).
  - **POMS** (Proactive Obsolescence Management System), utility-funded and hosted by an outside vendor.
  - **RAPID** (Readily Accessible Parts Inventory Database), from the Utilities Service Alliance: cross-utility inventory sharing.
  - **PUSH** (Pooled Utility Strategic Holdings) pools spares for large components.
  - NUOG (Nuclear Utility Obsolescence Group) holds annual conferences.
  - NUPIC (Nuclear Procurement Issues Corporation) runs joint supplier audits.
- Shrinking supplier base: there were roughly 400 N-stamp holders in the 1980s and roughly 100-200 in the 2010s-20s (ASME data, recalled). This is why CGD became the dominant route.

## 3. How much would they pay to cut dedication time? (value analysis, opinion grounded in recalled numbers)
- **Critical-path / emergent cases.** A 1 GW unit makes about 24,000 MWh/day. At $40-70/MWh replacement power plus lost capacity revenue, a **forced outage or outage extension costs about $1-2M per day per unit** (recalled; outage extensions are often quoted at $1M+/day). An emergent part that holds a refuelling outage on critical path for 5 days is worth $5-10M. Utilities already pay expediting premiums of 2-5x for these cases. Willingness to pay is per event, not per hour: $100k-$1M for a part delivered in days instead of months is rational.
- **Routine dedication.** Most dedication is planned procurement. Here the value is labour: there are only a few hundred to perhaps a thousand qualified dedication engineers across US utilities and vendors, and the workforce is ageing. A utility would pay to cut engineering hours per package from about 40-200h to about 5-20h. At a $150-250/h loaded rate that saves $5k-$40k per package, and with thousands of packages per year industry-wide the market is roughly $50-300M/yr in labour (estimate).
- **Inventory carrying.** Utilities hold large safety-related inventories ($50-150M per site, recalled) partly because dedication lead time is long. Faster dedication on demand lets them shrink inventory, which frees cash and carrying cost (about 10-20%/yr). For the whole fleet that is plausibly worth hundreds of millions per year (estimate).
- **Plant life extension.** Subsequent license renewals to 80 years (Turkey Point, Peach Bottom, Surry, North Anna, Monticello and others) plus restarts and uprates raise the obsolescence load for decades. AI-driven data-centre power purchase agreements (Constellation-Microsoft for Crane, Talen-Amazon at Susquehanna, Constellation-Meta at Clinton) raise the value of each MWh and so the cost of every lost day.

## 4. Who is already doing what (players)
- **Dedicating entities / suppliers (incumbents):**
  - Curtiss-Wright Nuclear Division: includes the former Scientech/NLI-type businesses and a large CGD/obsolescence business. The Curtiss-Wright nuclear aftermarket is roughly $0.5B+ of revenue (recalled).
  - Paragon Energy Solutions (Fort Worth; acquired NLI from AZZ, recalled).
  - Framatome, Westinghouse, GE Vernova/GE Hitachi.
  - Ultra Energy / Ultra Nuclear Sensors & Process Instrumentation.
  - Kinectrics (BWXT).
  - Engineering firms: Sargent & Lundy, Enercon, Zachry Nuclear, Nuclear Engineering Inc. (NEI-Rockwood?), Sequoia.
  - Contract test labs: Wyle/NTS for EQ, and Element.
- **AI software:**
  - **Nuclearn** (Phoenix, founded 2021, out of the Palo Verde/APS team): VERIFIED to serve "over 70 facilities in North America and the U.K." Its **Parts AI** does part discovery ("reduces research time by over 70%"), item sourcing and status reporting. **Engineering AI** automates 10 CFR 50.59 applicability checks, screenings and evaluations ("minutes instead of days"). Its site does **not** mention commercial grade dedication, equivalency or obsolescence (verified). This is the closest competitor and the obvious extender into dedication.
  - **Everstar** (Kevin Kong): VERIFIED to have a "Gordian" AI backbone and "Titan" manufacturing. It names a "Qualification" stage covering "Materials, parts, and suppliers", which are backlogged and which it says it will clear at scale. It gives no mechanism. Mostly aimed at new builds and licensing. It raised about a $4-10M seed in 2024-25 (recalled).
  - **Atomic Canyon**: the "Neutron" search over nuclear documents; Diablo Canyon/PG&E deployment; ORNL Frontier for training (recalled).
  - Others: Blue Wave AI (fuel/ops), and IBM/Accenture/Siemens consulting.
- **Additive manufacturing / qualification:**
  - Westinghouse 3D-printed thimble plugging device installed at Byron (2020), recalled.
  - ORNL/Framatome 3D-printed fuel channel brackets at TVA Browns Ferry (2021), recalled.
  - The ASME code case for AM 316L (N-845?, recalled).
  - NRC Advanced Manufacturing Technologies Action Plan (2020-), recalled.
  - EPRI AM qualification work.
- **What nobody appears to do (opinion, from incumbents' public offerings):**
  1. An automated, generative technical evaluation that turns "item + application" into "critical characteristics + acceptance methods + sampling plan" with traceable rationale, accepted by NUPIC/NRC inspectors.
  2. A fleet-wide shared, machine-readable library of dedication packages and critical characteristics. Today each utility and vendor re-dedicates the same item; packages are proprietary PDFs, and POMS/RAPID cover inventory, not engineering evidence.
  3. Predictive obsolescence: forecasting which OEM parts go EOL against plant BOMs and pre-dedicating substitutes before they are needed.
  4. Physics-test acceleration: robotic CGD labs (PMI, hardness, dimensional CT, functional test) with chain of custody, sited at a hub instead of per-vendor.
  5. EQ "by analysis" using validated ageing and physics models to cut requalification from 12-24 months, which currently needs NRC acceptance.

## 5. Binding constraints and what would change them
| Constraint | Today | What would change it |
|---|---|---|
| Physics clock: Method 1 tests | days-weeks per lot; EQ ageing months | automated test cells, CT plus ML for non-destructive CC verification, and an accepted Arrhenius/irradiation model in place of real-time ageing (needs RG/EPRI change) |
| Law | Appendix B/Part 21 puts liability on the dedicating entity | the AI house becomes an Appendix B dedicating entity itself (takes Part 21 liability), not a SaaS tool |
| Regulator acceptance | inspectors audit packages built to EPRI 3002002982 | an EPRI-sponsored pilot plus an NEI topical report ("AI-assisted technical evaluation") with NRC endorsement, like NEI 14-05A for labs |
| Liability | Part 21 defect means fleet-wide reporting | insurance (NEIL/ANI), plus traceable evidence graphs per decision |
| Data access | OEM drawings proprietary; utilities' packages siloed | a fleet consortium data deal (via EPRI/USA/NUPIC), plus reverse engineering by scanning |

## 6. Assessment for the "Approval Factory" thesis
- Nuclear CGD is structurally identical to DLA SAR/FAA PMA: knowledge is cheap, while the signature (Appendix B dedicating entity) and the accepted method are the permission.
- The market is smaller in units than aviation (94 reactors versus about 25k airliners) but has a **very high value per day of delay**, regulatory lock-in and a hard workforce cliff.
- A plausible wedge is an Appendix B-qualified "AI dedicating entity" that dedicates commercial and reverse-engineered substitutes for obsolete I&C and electrical parts in weeks. Revenue would be per part (the dedication premium), with expedite pricing for outage-critical items.
- Nuclearn is the likely fast follower on the software side. Curtiss-Wright and Paragon are the incumbents to displace or partner with (or be acquired by).
