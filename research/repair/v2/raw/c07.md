# c07: Materials informatics and AI material allowables (Citrine, Mat3ra, Polymerize, Uncountable, NASA/CMH-17/MMPDS digitization): could they produce regulator-grade allowables from fewer coupons?

## Short answer
Not today, and none of the named informatics companies are trying. Citrine, Polymerize, Uncountable and Mat3ra sell R&D formulation and simulation software. None of them mention allowables, qualification or certification on their sites; the only "certifications" they list are ISO 27001 and SOC 2. The firms working on "fewer coupons" are niche simulation and statistics players (e-Xstream/Hexagon Digimat, AlphaSTAR GENOA, Senvol ML) plus government consortia (NCAMP/NIAR, FAA JAMS, America Makes, AFRL). Nobody has an FAA-accepted ML- or AI-derived A- or B-basis allowable. The big coupon savings that regulators actually accept come from **shared databases plus equivalency** (NCAMP), not from prediction. The law already leaves an opening: 14 CFR 25.613(f) allows "other design values" with the Administrator's approval, and 25.613(e) allows higher values if each item is tested before use.

Implication for the Approval Factory. On legacy metal parts (C-130, KC-135), allowables are mostly not the bottleneck. You design to published MMPDS values for 7075-T73, 4340 and similar alloys. What costs coupons and hours there is **equivalency and conformity**: proving your part's material and process matches the OEM's (hardness, conductivity, grain flow, chemistry, comparison testing). Allowables become the bottleneck for AM replacement parts, composite repairs and substitute materials for obsolete alloys or processes. That is where a verification-side AI play sits.

## Evidence

### Baseline cost of allowables (evidence)
- CompositesWorld, "Accelerating materials insertion: Where do virtual allowables fit?" (2017-04-14), https://compositesworld.com/articles/accelerating-materials-insertion-where-do-virtual-allowables-fit
  - NCAMP's standard qualification matrix is about 1,200 coupons. The CMH-17-1G recommended matrices total about 5,000-7,000 coupons.
  - Rousseau (Lockheed Martin): 1,500-3,000 coupons per material form, about $500k of coupon testing per material, and about 18 months (at least 9) including roughly 2 months of wet conditioning. He says up to 75% reduction is possible through data pooling.
  - Assaker (e-Xstream/Digimat): a full program of 31,104 coupons across 48 laminates and about $5.7M, versus about $476.5k with virtual allowables (roughly 92% less, by my arithmetic). The vendor claims weeks for the virtual step.
  - Peer consensus reported from the 2016 American Society for Composites conference: "no use for virtual allowables in certification." That is the gate, and it is opinion.
- Metal AM (FAA JAMS / NIAR JMADD, SAMPE 2025 slides, AFRL-2025-2561), https://www.wichita.edu/industry_and_defense/NIAR/Documents/JMADD_and_JMADX-SAMPE_2025_v2.pdf
  - One alloy-machine combination (Ti-6Al-4V on the EOS M290) took: tension 1,029 specimens across 5 machines; compression 501; shear 508; bearing 397; fracture toughness 395; fatigue 412.
  - MMPDS requirements cited there: 100 x 3 orientations = 300 tension across ≥10 heats; 600 bearing (two e/D ratios).
  - Partners: Boeing, Beehive, ELCAN, ATI, AP&C, Tekna, a 100+ member advisory committee and a 33-member government steering committee. Jointly funded with OSD ManTech. Deliverables are NCAMP specs (NAPS 065, NFS 064, NAMS 064, NPCD 81064) and T90/T99 values for publication in MMPDS.
  - That is several thousand specimens and multiple years to get **one** material-machine pair into MMPDS. This is the physics-clock and coupon tax in its purest form.

### Regulatory hooks (evidence)
- 14 CFR 25.613 (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/25.613):
  - (a) values must come from "enough tests ... to establish design values on a statistical basis";
  - (b) 99%/95% for single-load-path structure (A-basis) and 90%/95% for redundant structure (B-basis);
  - (c) environmental effects;
  - (e) higher values allowed if each item is tested before use (premium selection);
  - (f) "other design values" with Administrator approval.
  - Parts 23, 27 and 29 have parallel rules. DoD uses MIL-HDBK-1530 and JSSG-2006, which also reference MMPDS and CMH-17.
- NCAMP (https://www.wichita.edu/industry_and_defense/NIAR/research/ncamp.php): the FAA accepts NCAMP specifications and design values under FAA Memo AIR100-2010-120-003, and EASA accepts them under CM-S-004. A user can adopt a qualified system and run a **reduced equivalency test** instead of a full qualification. The AGATE heritage claims about 4x faster and about 10x cheaper certification. Roughly 22 allowables and specs entries are listed.
- (Recalled) AC 20-107B (composites) allows "analysis supported by test" in the building block. CMH-17 Vol 1 Ch 8 allows data pooling across environments (ASAP/STAT17 pooled B-basis), which already cuts coupons. DOT/FAA/AR-03/19 sets the equivalency protocol (about 2 batches, reduced matrix). MMPDS also has an "S-basis" (specification minimum), which takes fewer data. CMH-17 RTM/HW software (STAT-17) is free.

### Attempts at "fewer coupons" (evidence + claims)
- **Senvol ML** (America Makes / USAF, Nylon 11 FR on polymer PBF, 2022), https://additivemanufacturing.media/news/senvol-ml-enables-new-approach-for-material-allowables-development
  - Claims to be "just as accurate" as CMH-17 with reduced data generation. No numbers are published. It produced properties "analogous to" allowables, not allowables. There is no regulatory acceptance.
  - A follow-on NCMS/AMMP OTA project compared the method against MMPDS. Coverage quotes the company saying ML for allowables is "still a work in progress" (search snippet).
- **e-Xstream Digimat (Hexagon)** and **AlphaSTAR GENOA**: "virtual allowables" by micromechanics and progressive failure analysis. AlphaSTAR lists "A- and B-basis allowables" and "certification by analysis" as application areas (https://www.alphastarcorp.com/). (Recalled) Both had FAA/NASA/AFRL-funded validation against NCAMP data in the 2010s. Neither has an FAA-accepted standalone virtual allowable. Both are used for test-matrix reduction and design.
- **Academic Bayesian approaches**:
  - UF/NASA Langley (Acar, Haftka, Kim et al.), https://web.mae.ufl.edu/nkim/Papers/conf39.pdf: Bayesian updating of analytical predictions with element tests cuts the probability of an unconservative estimate from 12.5% to 1%.
  - SAMPE papers (2008-2019, via search) claim a 60%+ coupon reduction from PFA plus Bayesian updating, and Bayesian plus constituent-variation methods for A/B-basis with limited data.
  - Wichita State (Raju) statistics work on pooled and reduced matrices: https://wichita.edu/industry_and_defense/NIAR/Documents/W1020_WSU_Raju_Stats.pdf
- **UTA / Northrop Grumman (July 2026)**: Northrop is funding Dr. Xin (Jeffrey) Liu at UT Arlington on AI for aerospace materials certification and B-basis allowables. The page returned 503 and only the search snippet confirmed it. This shows primes are paying for exactly this question now.

## What is not done (gaps)
1. **No regulator-accepted protocol for model-informed allowables.** 25.613(f) exists, but there is no AC, CMH-17 chapter or MMPDS guideline saying how a Bayesian or ML prior plus N coupons yields an acceptable A- or B-basis. Everyone builds the method, and nobody writes the acceptance standard plus the validation dataset that would get it adopted.
2. **No cross-company pooled dataset outside NCAMP and MMPDS.** OEM and prime coupon data (decades of it) is siloed. The informatics vendors host each customer's data separately and cannot learn across customers.
3. **No ML "equivalency" engine for the case that matters for PMA and DLA work.** Nothing predicts, from chemistry, microstructure, NDI and process signatures, that a reverse-engineered part's material matches the OEM's MMPDS population, so that comparison testing can shrink to a handful of confirmations.
4. **No in-situ, per-part statistical acceptance.** 25.613(e) premium selection (test each item) could be turned into "certify each part by process monitoring plus witness coupons." That would replace population allowables with per-part evidence, but it is not productized.
5. **Physics clocks are untouched.** Wet conditioning (about 2 months), fatigue (weeks), and panel fabrication and machining all still have to physically run. AI can cut N but not the clock per specimen, except through parallel high-throughput test rigs and accelerated conditioning, and those also lack an accepted protocol.

## What it would take (assume abundant intelligence and compute)
- Build the **validation corpus**. Re-derive every public NCAMP, MMPDS and JMADD allowable by leave-batches-out with an ML/Bayesian method. Show that using about 20-30% of the coupons gives basis values that are never unconservative beyond the stated confidence across all of them. This is the "regulator-grade" evidence, and nobody has assembled it publicly.
- **Write the standard.** Draft a CMH-17 chapter and an MMPDS guideline section ("Bayesian/model-informed basis values"), run them through the CMH-17 and MMPDS coordination committees (the FAA sits on both), and seek an FAA policy memo like AIR100-2010-120-003. Timeline (opinion): 3-6 years through committees. A faster route is a 25.613(f) issue paper on one program with a friendly DER/ODA or a DoD airworthiness authority (AFLCMC/EN), where military airworthiness has more discretion.
- **Data access.** Use DLA/AFRL/NCAMP public data first. Then build a consortium data trust (the NCAMP model) where members contribute coupon data in exchange for reduced-test equivalency. The network effect is the moat.
- **Liability.** A non-conservative allowable means structural failure. The method needs a built-in conservatism guarantee (calibrated tolerance bounds that degrade gracefully to the classical method) and confirmatory coupons, and the company may need to carry E&O insurance or take responsibility as the test house of record.
- **Physics clock.** Use robotic, parallel coupon labs (lights-out machining and testing, digital image correlation) plus active-learning test selection, so the coupons you do run are the most informative ones.

## AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
1. **"Equivalency engine" for repair and PMA material conformity.** Predict and statistically demonstrate that a replacement part's material sits inside the OEM/MMPDS population from minimal comparison tests, and output the test report in DLA SAR/FAA PMA format. This feeds the Approval Factory directly.
2. **Model-informed allowables as a service plus an owned robotic test lab.** Take on the coupon-heavy jobs (AM parts, composite repair materials, obsolete-alloy substitutes). Use Bayesian active learning to cut coupons by about 50-75%, with tests still physically run and witnessed. Sell outcomes (allowables, NCAMP-style specs), not software.
3. **Consortium data trust (an "NCAMP for AM and repair materials").** Pooled, anonymized coupon data across shops and primes, with AI-derived equivalency thresholds. A shop qualifies onto a shared database with about 20-60 coupons instead of about 1,000+.
4. **Per-part certification under 25.613(e).** In-process sensing plus a digital twin plus witness coupons yields a statistical acceptance for each serialized part. Most relevant to AM spares for legacy fleets.
5. **The standards-writing play.** Assemble the leave-one-out validation corpus over all public allowables data and author the CMH-17/MMPDS guideline. This is not a company on its own, but the company that writes the method owns it.
