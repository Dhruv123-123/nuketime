# f14: Remote/virtual witness testing and digital inspection evidence accepted by regulators (FAA remote inspections post-COVID, DNV remote surveys, NRC remote inspections). What became permanent?

## 1. NRC (verified, primary documents)

### IMC 2515 Appendix E, "Inspection Program Modifications During Public Health Emergencies or Other Conditions Restricting Inspector Onsite Presence"
- Issued 01/02/2024, effective 01/01/2024. URL: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2330/ML23307A009.pdf
- What became permanent is a **contingency mode**, not a new default. Remote and hybrid inspection is written into the manual and switches on when "conditions restrict inspector onsite presence", which covers pandemics and also floods and hurricanes.
- The tiered options include "Option 3: Conduct 100 percent Remote Inspection" and "Option 4: Conduct Remote Inspection with Onsite Follow-Up". Under Option 4 the inspection "remains open until an inspector can safely travel to the site", so it cannot be closed on remote evidence alone.
- On inservice inspection (ISI), inspectors "should maximize the use of record reviews, remote video or recorded inservice inspections where possible." This is the one explicit acceptance of licensee-recorded video as inspection evidence, and it applies under constrained conditions.
- Inspectors must weigh "negative impacts to safety and/or security margins when replacing onsite inspections", including the extra preparation time licensees need to support remote inspection.
- Earlier companion document: IMC 2600 App E (fuel cycle facilities), 12/15/2020, ML20350B441.

### "Final Report for the Follow-On Review of the Lessons Learned, Best Practices, and Challenges During the COVID-19 PHE" (ML22172A159, 2022)
- URL: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2217/ML22172A159.pdf
- Main conclusion: on-site, in-person inspection is "the most effective and preferred inspection method". The working group "concluded that the flexibilities to perform inspections in a hybrid application have always existed within the ROP"; they should be applied "case-by-case". The NRC therefore did not create a new remote-by-default regime.
- Methods actually used during the pandemic: remote document review, virtual entrance and exit meetings, remote interviews, and "licensee-generated photographs and video recordings of plant equipment". Physical walkdowns stayed on site.
- **The binding constraint was data access, not video.** "A significant challenge to the broad adoption of hybrid inspections is the availability of licensee documents and records and the timeliness of licensee responses." Hybrid inspections often ran past the planned 3-5 week window because of request/response delays.
- Information-sharing options it evaluated:
  - Direct licensee terminals in regional offices. Licensees opposed this.
  - Virtual desktop access to licensee databases. Referred to NRC "Future Focused Research" (Recommendation 5).
  - Third-party file-sharing platforms.
- The NRC declined to endorse any platform. Instead it set minimum functions for any platform:
  1. IT security
  2. Two-way upload and download
  3. Logging of questions, requests and responses
  4. Tracking of open and closed requests
  5. A repository of base references (Technical Specifications, UFSAR, procedures)
- "No current regulatory requirement compels any licensee to adopt a standardized methodology for electronically sharing information with NRC inspectors." Recommendation 6 is a voluntary NRC-industry standard for information sharing.
- Virtual entrance and exit meetings were more widely attended and were kept (Recommendation 4).
- **Read-across for the founder:** the NRC wrote down the spec for an inspector-facing evidence platform (requests, responses, tracking, a reference repository) and asked industry to build it voluntarily. As far as this source shows, nobody owns that layer.

## 2. Maritime class: DNV, ABS, LR, IACS (recalled; primary pages could not be fetched)
- DNV launched remote surveys around 2017-2018, before COVID. Owner or crew stream video and photos and the surveyor credits the survey (recalled). DNV has cited tens of thousands of remote surveys, of the order of more than 15,000 by 2020, and has said a large share of its occasional surveys are done remotely (recalled; numbers not verified).
- Scope that became permanent (recalled):
  - Occasional surveys, such as damage, repairs and the closing of conditions of class.
  - Some periodic items.
  - Statutory surveys only where the flag state agrees.
  - The main renewal and hull surveys, with close-up, thickness and dry-dock work, still need an attending surveyor.
- IACS Rec 42, "Guidelines for Use of Remote Inspection Techniques for surveys" (recalled, around 2016 with later revisions), covers drones, ROVs and crawlers. The surveyor may be on site with the robot as the "eyes", which removes rope access and staging.
- IACS later adopted a unified requirement on remote surveys, believed to be **UR Z29** (recalled, around 2023-2024; number and date not verified). If correct, remote class surveys moved from each society's own policy into harmonised class requirements.
- ABS published Guidance Notes on Remote Survey, and LR and BV have equivalent remote-survey products (recalled).
- Players: Flyability (Elios drones for confined spaces, used in class-accepted tank surveys); Scout Drone Inspection (Norway, autonomous tank drones developed with DNV); Gecko Robotics (US, ultrasonic crawler robots; US Navy and power-plant boilers; reported unicorn valuation in 2025, all recalled).
- **Read-across:** maritime made remote evidence the most permanent of any regulator. The reasons are that class societies are private, commercially competing firms; that remote surveys saved them money; and that the flag-state veto is exercised one case at a time.

## 3. FAA / aviation (recalled; faa.gov returned 403)
- In 2020 FAA Aircraft Certification (AIR) and Flight Standards issued COVID-era guidance letting FAA staff and designees (DERs, DARs, ODA units) use **remote/virtual means**, meaning live video and photos, for conformity inspections, test witnessing and some audits. It was explicitly at the discretion of the FAA or the designee (recalled).
- The FAA Part 21 rules never required physical presence for conformity or witnessing. Order 8110.4C and the designee orders define what must be determined, not how it is observed (recalled).
- My best understanding is that remote witnessing stayed as a **discretionary tool** in later revisions of FAA guidance, with the official's acceptance and documentation of the method. It is not a right the applicant can invoke, and physical conformity of first articles is still the norm (recalled, not verified).
- In Flight Standards, remote oversight and records review stayed in use, and "virtual" inspections for some certificate-management activities continued (recalled).
- EASA issued "remote audit" guidelines in 2020 and later folded remote-audit provisions into AMC/GM for authority requirements, covering Part 21 and Part 145 oversight. Use is conditional: on risk, on ICT capability, and generally not for an initial approval (recalled).
- Accredited certification bodies: IAF MD 4, "Use of ICT for Auditing/Assessment Purposes", is a permanent mandatory document for remote audits, so AS9100 certification bodies can audit partly remotely under IAQG rules (recalled).

## 4. Other regulators that kept remote tools (recalled)
- FDA "Remote Regulatory Assessments" (RRAs): draft guidance 2022, later finalised (recalled). These are a permanent supplement to inspections, not a substitute. Records requests under FD&C Act 704(a)(4) are a statutory route for collecting records remotely.
- DCMA (Government Source Inspection for DoD contracts) used virtual surveillance and video product examination during COVID (recalled; current permanence not verified).
- DLA Source Approval Requests (SARs): evaluation is already paper/remote by design. The Engineering Support Activity reviews the technical data package and the comparison analysis. The physical gates are First Article Test or production lot tests, plus GSI where the contract calls for it (recalled). **So for the Approval Factory's first market the bottleneck is reviewer hours and queue, not whether the witness is physically present.**

## 5. Synthesis: what became permanent

| Regime | Permanent? | Form |
|---|---|---|
| Maritime class (DNV/ABS/LR) | Yes, mainstream | Remote surveys for occasional/specific items; drones/RIT for close-up; flag-state consent for statutory |
| NRC | Contingency plus case-by-case hybrid | IMC 2515 App E (2024); remote document review, virtual meetings, licensee video for ISI; walkdowns on site |
| FAA | Discretionary (recalled) | Designee or FAA may accept video witness/conformity; no applicant right |
| EASA | Conditional remote audit (recalled) | Built into AMC; not for initial approvals |
| ISO/AS9100 certification | Yes (recalled) | IAF MD 4 |
| FDA | Yes, supplementary (recalled) | RRAs / 704(a)(4) records requests |

**The pattern:**
1. Remote **document and record review** became permanent everywhere.
2. Remote **observation** (video witness) became permanent where the verifier is a private competing firm (class societies). Where the verifier is a public regulator it was kept only as discretion or contingency.
3. Nobody accepted **evidence that verifies itself**. Every regime still relies on a human official deciding to trust a video or document. There is no standard that makes sensor-signed test data admissible on its own.

## 6. Gaps nobody fills, and what would need to be done
1. **Tamper-evident test evidence.** There is no standard for cryptographically attested test and inspection data: signed sensor streams, CMM/CT scan provenance, time and location stamps, chain of custody. What would change it: an SAE/ASTM consensus standard, then an FAA policy statement or AC accepting it as equivalent to witnessed data, piloted through an ODA holder or a DLA ESA. (Analogy: C2PA content credentials, applied to test rigs.)
2. **Continuous remote witness.** An instrumented test cell that the regulator or designee can watch live or replay, with automatic flagging of deviations from the approved test plan. The official reviews flagged segments instead of attending the whole multi-hour test. The physics clock is unchanged, but reviewer hours fall sharply.
3. **An inspector-facing evidence portal.** The NRC has already published the minimum functions (section 1) and asked industry to build it voluntarily. The same need exists at the FAA (designee packages, 8110-3 compliance findings) and at DLA ESAs (SAR packages). It needs AI pre-review against the reviewer's own checklist and an RFI tracker.
4. **Automated first-article and comparison evidence.** CT or blue-light scan of the OEM part and the reverse-engineered part, an automatic AS9102 First Article Inspection report, and statistical equivalence for material properties. Packaged so the reviewer receives a pre-argued, machine-checkable "equivalence case".
5. **Regulator-side tooling.** The FAA, DLA and NRC are short of reviewers. Tools that make reviewing faster (summaries, traceability matrices, diffs against prior approvals) would shorten queues. Selling to government reviewers is slow, but the founder could give them away free to reviewers of his own packages.
6. **Changing acceptance.** For each constraint, what it would take:
   - Law: no rule change is needed, because the rules do not require physical presence. Only guidance and individual discretion stand in the way.
   - Regulator acceptance: pilots with one ODA, one DLA ESA (for example Ogden or Tinker for C-130/KC-135) and one class society, then a published policy memo.
   - Liability: the designee signs. Give the designee better evidence than physical presence would give (full replay, a sensor record), so the incentive points toward accepting it.

## 7. Players (evidence quality noted)
- Gecko Robotics: robotic ultrasonic inspection data with a software platform; Navy and power plants; reported $1.25B valuation in 2025 (recalled). Adjacent: it produces inspection data but does not make it regulator-acceptable.
- Flyability and Scout Drone Inspection: drone remote inspection techniques accepted in class surveys (recalled). Adjacent.
- Librestream (Onsight), RealWear, Scope AR: video and AR remote-witness tools used by inspectors and class (recalled). Adjacent, a commodity video layer.
- Nominal (hardware test data platform; Series A 2024, Series B 2025, recalled) and Sift (telemetry): test-data infrastructure. Adjacent to a direct threat if they add attestation and certification packaging.
- Istari Digital (founded by Will Roper, former USAF acquisition chief): digital engineering and model-based certification for DoD (recalled). **The closest to a direct threat** on the verification side.
- First Resonance (ION factory OS: traceability and as-built records; recalled): adjacent, the digital-thread layer.
- Class societies (DNV, ABS, LR, BV): incumbents that verify; they could extend remote-survey know-how to other domains.
