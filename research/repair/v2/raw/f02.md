# f02: Model-assisted POD (MAPOD) and simulated POD for NDT: maturity, regulator acceptance, and what it would take to qualify AI inspection methods without huge physical specimen sets

Search budget used: 3 WebSearch calls. Everything else came from WebFetch, plus PDF text pulled out locally with pdftotext. Figures marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this session.

## 1. Evidence gathered (sourced)

### EPRI, "Applying AI in the Nuclear Industry" (A. Czeszumska, IAEA General Conference, 18 Sep 2025)
URL: https://restservice.epri.com/publicattachment/95346
- AI-assisted ultrasonic testing (UT) analysis: "Assist means AI flags regions for review: final decisions still rest with the qualified inspector."
- Qualification is framed as a "procedure" type. The model must flag all flawed regions, then the inspector qualifies on the flagged regions through the normal personnel qualification.
- "Model was exposed to more than 5x the amount of PDI data required for a traditional procedure qualification." It flagged craze cracking, axial, circumferential and off-axis flaws, small and large through-wall flaws, and both ID- and OD-connected flaws.
- Proposed initial qualification: the AI evaluation process is written into the procedure, and the model is "frozen" and handed to the qualification body. The body runs it on pre-recorded data and checks whether all flawed regions were flagged. The candidate then reviews the flagged areas and makes the calls, judged on the usual detection and false-call criteria.
- Conclusion on the slide: "All observations point to AI being qualifiable when paired with human analyst."
- Human-factors evidence: missed-flaw operating experience traced to fatigue, distraction and missing independent review (NUREG/CR-7295, PNNL-32505). With AI assistance a full reactor vessel head was analysed in under 3 hours.
- Deployment is a local "ML Box": data stays on site and no software install is needed.
- IAEA ISOP Taskforce 3 on AI benchmarking (data, methods, use cases).

### ENIQ RP13 Issue 1 (June 2021), "Qualification of NDT Systems that Make Use of Machine Learning" (ENIQ Report 65, SNETP)
URL: https://snetp.eu/wp-content/uploads/2021/06/ENIQ_RP13_Issue1.pdf
- Defines three roles for ML. (1) Humans do the primary analysis and ML screens it independently. (2) Humans analyse all the data and ML alerts them. (3) ML replaces part of the analysis, so humans look only at regions ML marks suspicious. Qualification of the ML model is needed in (2) and (3), and the burden grows with each step.
- "data-augmentation schemes and simulated data may be used to expand the training data... Physical reasoning may be used to justify the use of simulated signals." Parametric studies can show that simulated data is representative.
- The ML component must be "trained with a controlled, verifiable training dataset and versioned (i.e. the learning frozen)". "Once the ML system has been fully commissioned and frozen, it can be treated as any other software."
- Recommends blind trials for ML systems, rather than the usual open-plus-blind split, because the operator's role shrinks. Trials should be especially hard and can include flaws smaller than the detection target.
- Admits that POD's scalar-threshold idea does not map cleanly onto ML ("one rarely relies on scalar inputs as for POD studies. Thus the concept of detection threshold cannot be applied"). Performance may instead be shown with POD, ROC or a method specific to the justification, with confidence assessed.
- "Experience and open literature with ML in the NDT community is so far limited... more evidence may be required."
- Co-author: Iikka Virkkunen (Trueflaw).

### BINDT 2022 Technique Validation and Standards Workshop Report (UK)
URL: https://www.bindt.org/admin/Downloads/2022-Technique-Validation-and-Standards-Workshop-Report.pdf
- History: the first POD models came in the early 1980s (CNDE, Iowa State). Simulated "spot the ball" POD trials were developed in the 1990s by NNDTC for ESA, and EPRI (Greg Selby) has used the approach since. "POD modules are now standard in commercial NDE simulation software such as CIVA."
- POD began in US aerospace, with a critical airframe defect size of about 6 mm, and was central to the US Aging Aircraft programme (1989-1993). The usual metric is a90/95.
- "A new protocol has recently been developed for MAQ in the air domain" for DSTL (UK MOD): M. Wall and C. Schneider, "A new protocol for model-assisted qualification".
- Experimental trial-based qualifications are "not going to be able to meet the time and cost requirements". Simulations will play a key role (A. Ballisat, Centre for Modelling and Simulation).
- Case studies: Rolls-Royce introducing flash thermography, where there was no existing validation route (T. Barden); BAE Systems naval qualification using ENIQ with ONR (T. Barber).
- The workshop concluded that the community needed to adopt model-assisted qualification (MAQ) to bring new technology into industry.

### ASNT blog, I. Virkkunen (Trueflaw), "Machine Learning Is Here Now to Make the Inspector's Life Easier" (25 Mar 2021)
URL: https://www.asnt.org/standards-publications/blog/machine-learning-is-here-now-to-make-the-inspectors-life-easier
- eFlaw method: real flaw signals taken from mock-ups are inserted into flawless background data, which the author says gives "effectively unlimited" flaws for training, performance demonstration and POD.
- Deep models need hundreds of thousands of samples. The author argues that ML's data needs make POD easier to evaluate than for humans, because a frozen model can be tested thousands of times while a human cannot.
- Field case: DEKRA train-axle UT at the Helsinki metro depot.
- Cites MIL-HDBK-1823A (7 Apr 2009) and ASTM E2862 (hit/miss POD).

### Trueflaw (Espoo, Finland), https://www.trueflaw.com
- Sells AI automatic defect recognition trained on client data plus Trueflaw's virtual cracks and "validated with POD"; TrueflawBox Edge AI; TrueRail; FlawML web app; real thermal-fatigue-cracked samples (more than 20 years of making them); POD evaluation as a service.
- Logos shown: DEKRA, ITP Aero, KIWA, MEYER, NDT aura, Vaylavirasto, SAFRAN, Blue Origin.
- EPRI/Trueflaw dissimilar-metal-weld ML study and "virtual round robin 2", which circulates pre-acquired data files instead of shipping samples (Aalto record: https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/b8696ed1-41ed-43e2-a956-f4094aca90e5/full).
- UJV Rez and CEZ (Czech) were nominated for an EPRI technology transfer award for virtual flaws used in personnel training and qualification under ENIQ RP10 (https://www.ujv.cz/en/news/notable-achievement-epri-technology-transfer-awards-nomination-13116).

### Donecle (France), https://www.donecle.com

## 2. Recalled background (not re-verified this session)
- MIL-HDBK-1823A (2009) is the de facto statistical rulebook (hit/miss and â-vs-a). The common rule of thumb for a hit/miss demonstration is 29 of 29 detections at a90/95, or about 60 or more flaws for a full curve, plus blank specimens to measure false calls. (recalled)
- ASTM E2862 (hit/miss POD) and ASTM E3023 (â-vs-a POD) are the newer standardized versions. (recalled)
- Physical specimen sets: fatigue-cracked coupons cost roughly $1k-$10k+ each. A full POD study on a representative structure typically runs $250k to several million dollars and 6-24 months. Sandia's FAA Airworthiness Assurance NDI Validation Center (AANC) ran many such studies for the FAA. (recalled; order of magnitude)
- USAF ASIP (MIL-STD-1530D) and ENSIP (MIL-STD-1783) require NDI reliability (a90/95) to support damage-tolerance inspection intervals. JSSG-2006 covers airframes. (recalled)
- FAA AC 33.14-1 (titanium hard-alpha) and AC 33.70-1 (engine life-limited parts) build POD curves into probabilistic damage-tolerance risk assessment, for example with DARWIN (SwRI, FAA-funded). (recalled)
- MAPOD Working Group (2004 onward, AFRL, FAA, NASA, Iowa State CNDE): transfer-function MAPOD has been used by the USAF, for example eddy-current bolt-hole inspection (ECBH). Full MAPOD still needs a physical anchor dataset. (recalled)
- AFRL plus Computational Tools (J. Aldrin) and others: SBIR-funded work on ML for NDE and on the ML-POD problem. (recalled)
- Simulation tools: CIVA (CEA/Extende) has had a POD module for more than 10 years. Others: OnScale (acquired by Ansys in 2022), Pogo GPU FE (Imperial College), k-Wave, COMSOL. No regulator has accepted any of them as fully qualified for POD without physical anchoring. (recalled)
- Nuclear US route: 10 CFR 50.55a, ASME Section XI Appendix VIII performance demonstration, run by the EPRI PDI program. This is the most formal blind-trial qualification system for UT in any industry. (recalled)
- Aviation AI: the FAA "Roadmap for Artificial Intelligence Safety Assurance" v1 (2024) takes an incremental, use-case-specific approach (fetch returned 403). The EASA AI Concept Paper Issue 2 (2024) covers level 1 and 2 ML assistance. Neither gives a POD-specific method for maintenance NDT. (recalled)
- Industry ADR products: Evident (Olympus) WeldSight / AI-assisted PAUT; Zetec UltraVision with ADR; Waygate Technologies (Baker Hughes) InspectionWorks and AI borescope; GE Aerospace AI blade-inspection tools; Rolls-Royce AI borescope analysis. All are positioned as "assist", with a human making the call. (recalled)
- Gecko Robotics: robotic UT for boilers, tanks and Navy ships, about $125M Series D in June 2025 at about $1.25B valuation. A data and AI company, not a POD-qualification company. (recalled)

## 3. The verification pathway for an AI inspection method (aviation and defense framing)

| # | Step | Who does it today | Time / cost | What is not done, or done badly |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Set the detection requirement: critical flaw size from damage-tolerance analysis (DTA), then target a90/95 | OEM stress and DT engineering, USAF ASIP, FAA ACO | weeks | For orphaned or legacy parts there is often no current DTA or the original analysis is lost. Requirements get re-derived conservatively. |
| 2 | Develop the inspection procedure (method, probe, scan plan, thresholds) | NDT Level 3, OEM or depot | months | Done by hand; essential variables are under-specified. |
| 3 | Fabricate physical specimens with real or representative flaws, and establish ground truth by destructive sectioning | Specimen houses (Trueflaw, others), Sandia AANC, OEM labs | 3-12 months; $1k-10k+ per flaw (recalled) | This is the main bottleneck. Specimens sit in OEM silos and do not transfer between programs. Destructive ground truth consumes the specimen. |
| 4 | Physical data acquisition | Labs, depots | weeks | Raw data is rarely kept in a reusable, standard form. |
| 5 | Simulation and MAPOD: calibrate a physics model, then generate variability | CIVA users, AFRL, EPRI, a few academics | months of expert time | No accepted credibility standard (nothing like ASME V&V 40 for NDE). Every MAPOD is argued case by case. Full-MAPOD acceptance is rare; transfer-function MAPOD is the most accepted. |
| 6 | Data augmentation and ML training (eFlaw, simulated, GAN) | Trueflaw, EPRI, OEM R&D | months | ENIQ RP13 allows it if "physical reasoning" justifies it, but no metric of representativeness is standardized. |
| 7 | Blind trials by an independent body | EPRI PDI (nuclear US), IQBs under ENIQ (EU, UK), Sandia AANC (FAA, historically), OEM in-house for aviation | months | Aviation has no standing independent qualification body for AI NDT. Each OEM self-certifies through the ACO. |
| 8 | Statistical analysis (MIL-HDBK-1823A, ASTM E2862/E3023) | Statisticians, Level 3 | weeks | Not designed for multichannel, non-scalar ML outputs (ENIQ RP13 says so). No consensus "ML POD" metric covering image-level and region-level detection, false-call rate, or out-of-distribution behaviour. |
| 9 | Regulator acceptance | FAA ACO/DER (repairs, AMM, SRM), USAF ASIP office, NRC through ASME XI App VIII and 10 CFR 50.55a, ONR in the UK | 6-24 months | No published FAA or USAF policy on AI-made NDT calls. Every acceptance is "AI assists, human decides". |
| 10 | Personnel qualification (NAS 410 / EN 4179, SNT-TC-1A) | Employer Level 3, NANDTB | ongoing | There is no certification category for an algorithm. Liability stays with the human who signs. |
| 11 | Change control: retrain means requalify | Owner | repeat 3-9 | The frozen-model rule (ENIQ, EPRI) makes continuous learning impossible. No "predetermined change control plan" route exists, unlike the FDA PCCP for AI devices. |

## 5. What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)

1. **Simulation credibility standard for NDE**, along the lines of ASME V&V 40 or the FDA in-silico credibility framework. It would define model-risk tiers, the validation evidence each tier needs, and the physical anchor set required. Authored through ASTM E07 or SAE, with AFRL and FAA Tech Center co-sponsorship. Today every MAPOD case is argued from scratch.
2. **ML-POD statistical standard**: an extension of 1823A or E3023 for non-scalar outputs. It would cover region-level detection, false-call rate per unit inspected, out-of-distribution detection, and confidence bounds when most flaws are synthetic and only a small physical anchor set exists (Bayesian hierarchical POD with a "sim-to-real gap" term).
3. **Physical anchor library as shared infrastructure**: a standing, government-sanctioned, blind-trial specimen and data bank for aviation (a "PDI for aircraft"). The physics clock is crack growth, but robotic fatigue rigs can grow hundreds of cracks in parallel. Industrial CT gives non-destructive ground truth, so specimens are not consumed by sectioning.
4. **Independent AI-NDT qualification body for aviation and defense**, so a company does not have to self-certify through each ACO.
5. **Change-control route for learned models**, analogous to the FDA PCCP: requalify against a frozen hidden test set automatically on each retrain instead of rerunning trials 3-9.
6. **Digital-twin inspection per part number**: automatically build the CAD-derived inspection simulation for every reverse-engineered part, and inject virtual flaws at critical locations from the DTA. This yields the inspection plan and the POD dossier as by-products of reverse engineering.
7. **Regulatory test case**: get one FAA- or USAF-accepted MAPOD/ML POD with less than 20% of the usual physical specimens on a legacy C-130 or KC-135 inspection (for example, the ECBH or wing-plank programs). Precedent is the binding constraint, not the science.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side

- **"Qualification Engine"**: a software-plus-lab company that delivers a90/95 POD dossiers for new or changed inspections. It combines a physics-calibrated simulation, virtual flaw injection and a small robotic-grown physical anchor set. It sells to USAF depots (OC-ALC, WR-ALC), OEMs, MROs, and nuclear utilities through EPRI. KPI: dollars and months per qualified inspection (today roughly $250k-$2M+ and 6-24 months, recalled).
- **Embedded in the Approval Factory**: every reverse-engineered PMA or SAR part needs an inspection plan, plus for the PMA an equivalency and test case (first article tests, CT-based dimensional and material equivalency). MAPOD lets the house hand the regulator "this is how flaws in this part will be found" as a generated artifact. That shortens DER and ACO review and supports damage-tolerance substantiation for critical parts.
- **Standards authorship as a moat**: whoever writes the ASTM/SAE ML-POD and simulation-credibility standards (with AFRL and the FAA Tech Center) shapes the acceptance pathway.
- **Specimen and data bank**: robotic crack-growth farm plus a CT ground-truth data marketplace sold as a qualification utility. Hard to replicate because of the physics clock.
- **Continuous requalification ("PCCP for NDT")**: hidden-test-set governance as a service for OEM AI inspection tools (GE, Rolls-Royce, Waygate). Sells liability evidence.

## 7. Binding constraints and what would change them

- **Physics clock**: cracks must physically grow, and ground truth must be physically established. Change it with massively parallel fatigue rigs, CT ground truth, and small anchor sets justified statistically.
- **Law and regulator acceptance**: there is no FAA or USAF policy for algorithm calls, so the human signs. Change it by winning one precedent case with an ASIP office, and by moving from ENIQ role 2 to role 3 (AI screens out benign regions).
- **Liability**: AI is "assist" everywhere today. Change it with performance evidence that beats humans (human-factors NUREG/CR-7295 data) and insurance products.
- **Data access**: specimen and flaw data are locked in OEMs and the PDI. Change it by building your own bank, or through government-funded shared libraries (Sandia AANC style).

## 8. Surprises
- ENIQ (in RP13) already allows simulated and augmented data for ML qualification, as long as "physical reasoning" justifies it. Europe's nuclear framework is ahead of aviation in writing.
- EPRI says its AI model saw more than 5x the PDI data required for a traditional procedure qualification, yet it would still be qualified only as an assist to a human.
- Aviation invented POD (Aging Aircraft, 1989-93) but now lags nuclear on formal AI-NDT qualification.
- Donecle says it is listed in the Boeing and Airbus AMMs, which shows that the tool-in-AMM route is open even though algorithm certification is not.
