# c19: Testing, inspection and certification (TIC) majors and their AI moves (UL Solutions, SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, TUV SUD/Rheinland, DNV, Element, NTS). Is any of them building AI-driven qualification or certification?

Method: 3 WebSearch calls plus WebFetch. Several company sites returned 403 (businesswire, tuvsud.com, intertek.com, aerospacetestinginternational). Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this session.

## Short answer
No. None of the TIC majors is building AI-driven qualification of physical parts in the sense the Approval Factory needs: generating the evidence package, cutting test count through analysis or simulation credit, and getting a regulator (FAA, DLA ESA, EASA) to accept it. Their AI moves fall into three buckets:
1. **Certifying AI products** ("AI assurance"): UL 3115, SGS CertX, TUV AI labs, DNV recommended practices, Intertek AI assurance. They are selling trust in AI, not using AI to produce trust.
2. **AI in their own operations**: report drafting, scheduling, visual and drone inspection, and margin programs (BV says "one governing framework to deploy AI across operations").
3. **Testing for AI infrastructure**: data-center and high-speed I/O validation (SGS buying Granite River Labs, Element's "AI & Data Center Infrastructure Testing").

The closest thing to the founder's idea is Element Digital Engineering (since 2021): modelling, simulation and ML sold beside physical testing. It is an add-on service, not a product that cuts the number of tests.

## Structural reasons the TIC majors will not build the Approval Factory (opinion, grounded in the pathway)
1. **Revenue is billed per lab-hour and per test.** If certification by analysis or simulation credit cuts physical tests, it cuts their own revenue. Their AI spending goes to margin (fewer staff hours per report) and to new markets (testing AI itself), not to fewer tests.
2. **Independence rules.** A lab that designs the part, or writes the TDP, cannot also be the independent tester or certifier. ISO/IEC 17025 and 17065 impartiality rules forbid it. So they cannot own the full design-to-approval loop. That leaves room for a design-house applicant that buys their test capacity.
3. **They are not the approval authority.** For FAA PMA, the FAA ACO plus a DER is the approver. For DLA SAR, the Engineering Support Activity (the service engineering office) approves. TIC firms produce test reports that go into those packages. They are suppliers to the applicant, not gatekeepers.
4. **Their AI certification products point the other way.** They certify AI-enabled products under UL 3115, CertX, the EU AI Act and DNV-RP-0671. None certifies "an AI-generated qualification argument is equivalent to N physical tests."

## Gap map on the verification side (what nobody does)
- **Accepted credit for analysis or simulation in place of physical tests for replacement parts.** FAA policy allows "test and computation" for PMA, and AC 20-107B-style building-block approaches exist for composites. But there is no TIC-owned, regulator-accepted framework that turns a validated model into fewer tests for sole-source spares. DNV-RP-0513 is the closest template.
- **Test-plan minimization as a service.** Choosing the smallest set of physical tests that closes the equivalence argument (statistical design of experiments, Bayesian credibility). Labs run whatever plan the applicant hands them.
- **Machine-checkable compliance packages.** No lab issues structured, machine-readable test evidence tied to specific requirements (a CS/FAR or MIL-spec clause leading to the test and its result). Reports are PDFs.
- **Lab capacity scheduling and pooling.** Physical-test lead times (fatigue, environmental, flammability) set the true cycle time. No marketplace or autonomous lab exists for aerospace qualification test slots.
- **Autonomous or robotic test cells** that run fatigue, vibration and environmental tests around the clock with automated reporting. The physics clock is the same, but the queue and the labour hours collapse.

## What it would take (assume plentiful intelligence and compute)
- An **AI verification layer that the applicant owns and the regulator can audit**. For each part it produces a requirements-to-evidence graph, a minimal test plan, and simulation credibility evidence following ASME V&V 40/V&V 10 and DNV-RP-0513 logic. It then outsources the physical tests to accredited labs (Element, NTS, independent labs) to keep impartiality.
- **A regulator-acceptance campaign.** Get DLA and service ESAs, then the FAA through DERs, to accept "model-credited" test reductions on low-criticality parts (Critical Safety Item = No) first, then build the track record. Changing this takes a pilot with an ESA (Ogden or Tinker ALC for C-130/KC-135) and later an FAA issue paper.
- **Owning or partnering on an autonomous test cell** to compress queue time, which is the part of the physics clock that can be changed.
- **Possibly acquiring or partnering with a 17025 lab** in a structure that preserves impartiality, such as a separate subsidiary under firewall rules, as HEICO and others do with repair stations.

## AI plays this suggests
1. **"Qualification copilot" sold to labs and DERs.** It ingests a TDP or reverse-engineered model and outputs the minimal compliant test matrix plus a draft compliance report. Element, NTS and independent labs are customers, not competitors. It cuts their report-writing hours and suits their margin agenda.
2. **Simulation-credibility assurance for replacement parts.** Apply DNV-RP-0513 and ASME V&V 40-style credibility scoring to the FEA/CFD used in PMA and SAR packages, so a regulator can accept fewer coupons and fewer tests. This could be built with DNV or licensed from it.
3. **Structured-evidence standard plus reviewer tool.** Machine-readable compliance evidence that the reviewing ESA or ACO can check quickly. This targets reviewer hours, the second half of the KPI.
4. **Autonomous qualification lab.** Robotic test cells for fatigue, vibration, thermal and environmental tests, with AI-run test execution and reporting. It sells capacity to the Approval Factory and to everyone else.

## Surprises
- Every major's "AI certification" product points at certifying AI products. None points at using AI to cut the evidence burden for physical parts.
- DNV already has published recommended practices for trusting simulation models and digital twins as assurance evidence, an under-used template outside maritime and energy.
- Element, the most aerospace-heavy major, set up digital engineering in 2021 but still runs it as a side line next to physical testing. It reorganized in July 2026 around an Aero/Space/Defense line, so it is a natural partner or acquirer.
