## 1. Legal frame
- 10 CFR 21.3 (recalled wording): a *basic component* is a safety-related SSC. A *commercial-grade item* (CGI) is one not designed and made as a basic component. *Dedication* is an acceptance process, run under a 10 CFR 50 App. B QA program, that gives "reasonable assurance" the CGI will perform its safety function, so it can be treated as a basic component. *Critical characteristics* are the identifiable, measurable attributes that, once verified, give that assurance. The *dedicating entity* (licensee or a third-party supplier) takes on Part 21 defect-reporting liability for the item.
- Method definitions come from EPRI NP-5652 (1988) and Generic Letter 89-02. EPRI 3002002982 (2014) is Rev 1 of NP-5652 / TR-102260. NRC endorsed it, with conditions, in RG 1.164 (2017) (recalled date). Digital items follow EPRI TR-106439 (1996) and TR-107330, plus NRC RG 1.250, *Dedication of Commercial-Grade Digital Instrumentation and Control Items* (recalled ~2023). IEC 61508 SIL certification can support, but not replace, the technical evaluation (EPRI 3002029261 §3.11).
- **The NRC does not approve CGD plans.** EPRI 3002029261 §4.2.2.4, on Paragon: "The U.S. NRC does not play a significant role in Paragon's CGD activities, as it does not approve CGD plans, witness CGD activities, or regularly participate." NRC vendor inspections happened only in 2014 and 2024. The permission is the dedicator's own Appendix B / NQA-1 program, its audit standing (NUPIC), and its willingness to carry Part 21 liability. It is not a per-item regulator approval. This is a key difference from FAA PMA and DLA SAR.

## 2. Full pathway, per item
| # | Step | Who | Typical time / cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Technical evaluation: safety function, credible failure modes (FMEA), then critical characteristics (physical, performance, dependability) and acceptance criteria | Procurement or design engineer, independently reviewed | (recalled) about 8-80 engineer-hours for a new evaluation; minutes to hours when an existing template or plan is reused | EPRI survey: "identification and verification of critical characteristics are the most challenging aspects" of CGD |
| 3 | Pick acceptance method(s): M1 special tests and inspections; M2 commercial-grade survey of the supplier; M3 source verification (witnessing at the supplier); M4 acceptable supplier or item performance record (rarely used alone) | Engineer | part of step 2 | UCI uses M1 about 90% of the time; Duke and Paragon mostly use M1; M3 is used least |

Cost and time per item. Primary sources give no hard per-item figures; EPRI notes that "cost was the least influential factor" and that quality and lead time drive the decision. Recalled or opinion figures:
- Dedicated price is about 2-10x commercial list for small parts, because of fixed engineering, test and records cost per lot. Example: a $40 relay sells dedicated for $400-2,000 (recalled, typical).
- Turnaround: about 2-8 weeks when a plan exists; months for a new or complex item, especially digital or anything needing seismic qualification (recalled).
- Versus nuclear-grade procurement, CGD saves up to 80% of cost and 50% of lead time (INAC 2021 snippet).

## 3. Scale data (evidence)
- About 40% of reactors worldwide use CGD regularly, another 40% have piloted it, and 20% never use it. For more than 20 reactors, 25-50% of safety-related procurements involve CGD. 31% of licensees say use is increasing (EPRI 3002029261).
- **Westinghouse**: library of 1,000+ CGD plans and 4,000+ instructions covering about 50,000 unique part numbers. It dedicates about 50,000 CGD parts per year from a 100k sq ft facility in New Stanton, PA, with 50+ engineers and about 75 technicians. It claims to serve the entire US market and 75% of global utilities (2021 sheet).
- **Paragon Energy Solutions**: about 1,300 CGD jobs per year and 70-100 new CGD plans per month. Digital items are under 5% of jobs but rising. About 120 approved commercial suppliers.
- **UCI (United Controls International, Norcross GA)**: 2,800+ digitized CGD plans covering 270 part classes, a 21,600 sq ft facility, about 150 active plus 130 occasional M&TE pieces, and about 100 suppliers per year.
- **Duke Energy CRDF** (Harris site): handles 25-50% of fleet safety-related procurement and does 95% of M1 tests in-house. It has a tri-axial shake table, CMM arms, 3D scanning and printing, and reverse-engineering capability. "Given more resources and personnel, Duke Energy would be performing more CGD." Leadership is briefed monthly on the CGD **backlog**.
- **KHNP**: about 185 in-house dedications per year plus 515 outsourced, about 100 M&TE pieces in a 200 m² lab, and digital CGD since 2014.
- **EPRI SPEED** (Smart Procurement Engineering Evaluation Data): a collaborative online template tool that generates partially completed technical evaluations. JUTG/PEARTS produced 139 shared CGD technical evaluations early on.
- **KELPO** (Finland: Fortum, TVO, Fennovoima, with STUK observing, 2018 onward): about 80% of approval comes from joint general technical characteristics and 20% is site-specific. Records sit on a joint digital platform that utilities co-own and the regulator can access. More than 100 components bought over 3 years with no major deviations. Being extended to a Nordic KELPO.

## 4. Bottlenecks (ranked; evidence vs opinion)
1. **Critical-characteristic engineering judgment** (evidence: EPRI survey, top challenge). It is scarce and bespoke per application, and licensees often don't tell third-party dedicators the safety function (UCI). So dedicators build plans that try to cover every plausible function, which leads to over-testing.
2. **No design data from commercial OEMs** (evidence: Paragon). Data sheets aren't guaranteed, so dedicators characterize a black box. Dependability characteristics, such as software quality for digital items, are the hardest. Digital CGD is growing and is the most expensive per item (opinion).
3. **Plan libraries are fragmented by company.** Westinghouse, UCI, Paragon, Framatome and each utility keep separate libraries, and the same part number gets dedicated many times over (opinion, supported by KELPO's finding that licensees had "different requirements and approval criteria for the same SSCs").
4. **Lab throughput and personnel.** Duke has a backlog and is limited by headcount (evidence).
5. **Supplier side.** Minimum order quantities, OEM reluctance to sell to nuclear, high QA-staff turnover at commercial suppliers (evidence: Paragon), and part obsolescence.
6. **Part 21 liability.** The dedicator carries defect liability indefinitely. Breakers and relays, which have many piece-part interfaces, fail randomly in ways that standard tests don't catch (evidence: EPRI's Part 21 analysis).
7. **NRC inspection findings.** Inadequate technical evaluation or critical characteristics have historically been among the most common vendor-inspection Notices of Nonconformance (recalled).

- Incumbent dedicators: Westinghouse, Framatome, Curtiss-Wright Nuclear (recalled), Paragon (Ametek? recalled; Paragon was formerly Nuclear Logistics/AZZ NLI, recalled), UCI, GE Hitachi parts (recalled), and Nuclear Parts Corp (recalled).

- Nobody visible combines an AI-written technical evaluation, an automated M1 test cell, and an owned Appendix B program carrying Part 21 liability in one AI-native dedicating entity.

## 6. Physics, law, acceptance, liability, data: what would change each
- **Physics.** M1 tests are short. The long clocks are seismic and EQ qualification and aging. To change them you need validated digital twins or similarity analysis accepted under IEEE 344/323 (opinion), plus shared shake-table capacity.
- **Law.** No per-item approval exists, so the gate is the dedicator's QA program. Getting there means standing up an Appendix B/NQA-1 program (about 12-24 months, recalled), passing a NUPIC audit, and getting on utility approved-supplier lists. The May 2025 executive orders directed a wholesale NRC rule revision (recalled), which may open Part 21 and QA to more performance-based and graded approaches like KELPO. Worth tracking.
- **Regulator acceptance.** RG 1.164 conditions are the target. An AI-generated technical evaluation is acceptable as long as a qualified engineer reviews it under the QA program, because nothing forbids tools. For tools used in design and analysis, EPRI's 2016 guidance treats non-process software as possibly a CGI, so the AI tool itself might need to be dedicated (V&V as a critical characteristic). That is a concrete product: a "dedicated" AI technical-evaluation tool.
- **Liability.** Part 21 exposure could be priced with data, for example by using fleet failure data to set sampling plans. An insurer or warranty wrapper is possible (opinion).
- **Data.** Licensees hold the safety-function and design-basis data, and dedicators hold the plan libraries. A shared, KELPO-style evidence graph would let each part number and characteristic be verified once and reused many times.

## 7. Verification-side AI plays (opinion)
1. **AI-native dedicating entity, "Dedication Factory".** It holds an Appendix B program and Part 21 liability. LLM agents draft the safety-function analysis, FMEA, critical characteristics and sampling plan from the licensee's Q-list, UFSAR and part data; humans review. Robotic M1 cells handle dimensional, XRF/FTIR, electrical, functional and burn-in tests, with automated records. KPI: engineer-hours per new plan (today about 8-80, recalled) and turnaround days. The incumbent benchmark is Westinghouse at about 50k parts per year with about 125 staff.
2. **Cross-industry plan and evidence graph.** It would dedupe the roughly 50k Westinghouse part numbers, UCI's 2,800 plans and each utility's library, with regulator read access as in KELPO. Network effect: verify each item once.
3. **Dedication of digital items and SBOMs.** Automated firmware analysis, static analysis and fuzzing would supply dependability critical characteristics under RG 1.250. This is the fastest-growing and hardest category, with security-tech overlap.
4. **Counterfeit and lot-anomaly detection.** Spectral, X-ray and electrical fingerprints compared against a golden-sample library, also usable for DLA and FAA parts.
5. **Advanced-reactor build-out.** Kairos, X-energy, TerraPower, Oklo and NuScale builds will need tens of thousands of dedicated items under NQA-1, from a shrinking pool of N-stamp and nuclear-QA suppliers.
6. **A dedicated AI tool.** A V&V'd technical-evaluation model that licensees can use under their own QA programs, like EPRI SPEED but generative.

## 8. Relevance to the Approval Factory thesis
CGD is the nuclear analog of PMA/SAR, but the permission is a **program-level** credential (Appendix B program, NUPIC audit, Part 21 liability) rather than a per-item regulator approval. So the per-item marginal cost is mostly engineering and testing, which AI and robotics can compress. The moat is the QA program, the plan library and the utility approved-supplier lists. Incumbents already have large libraries, and Nuclearn is attacking the document layer. The open gap is the vertically integrated AI dedicator, plus the digital-item and advanced-reactor segments.
