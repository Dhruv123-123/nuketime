# c04: Additive manufacturing qualification and in-situ process monitoring companies (Senvol ML, Phase3D, Additive Assurance, Sigma Additive, EOS/Amiquam, Divergent, Velo3D, Freeform): what evidence they produce and whether regulators accept it

Method: 3 WebSearch calls plus WebFetch of company, NASA, NIST and news pages. Several sites returned 403/503 (metal-am.com, phase3d.com, amiquam.com, freeform.co, astm.org, faa.gov). Anything not taken from a fetched page is marked (recalled).

## Bottom line

- No regulator (FAA, EASA, NAVSEA/NAVAIR, DLA, NASA, NRC) has been found to accept in-situ monitoring data *in place of* the usual qualification evidence: frozen-process qualification, coupon-based allowables, and full-volume NDE/CT plus first-article inspection. Monitoring is accepted only as **supplemental** process-control evidence.
- The open standards say this outright:
  - ASTM E3353-22 says there are "not yet well-established procedures for incorporating AM process monitoring within a qualification or certification framework outside of a specific company or institution's internal use".
  - NASA-STD-6030 still requires NDE of the full surface and volume for every class except negligible-risk parts. NASA MSFC (E. Lanigan, Jan 2022, NTRS 20220000170) describes the route to monitoring credit: link monitoring indications to critical defect sizes, then show 90/95 probability of detection (POD) the way an NDE method does. NASA calls that link "the most challenging obstacle". Closed-loop/adaptive control "will require a new QMP approach", because it breaks the locked-process model.
  - NIST NISTIR 8538 (Sep 2024, for the NRC) lists "gaps in knowledge, technologies, and standards" that must close before monitoring/NDE can support AM qualification for nuclear plant parts.
- The market signal is that standalone monitoring software is worth very little and the value sits in vertically integrated producers.
  - Sigma Additive (PrintRite3D, Nasdaq SASI) sold its whole QA IP portfolio to Divergent for **$1.6M**: APA signed 6 Oct 2023, closed 16 Jan 2024. It then reverse-merged into the travel company NextTrip (closed 29 Dec 2023).
  - Money has flowed instead to integrated part makers that own their process data: Divergent, Freeform ($67M Series B, Feb 2026), Velo3D (Forge 1 campus).

## Regulatory pathway today (evidence regulators actually accept)

1. **Material/process spec.** Recalled: SAE AMS7003 (LPBF process), AMS7000-series materials, ASTM F3303, NASA MSFC-SPEC-3717 / NASA-STD-6030, NAVSEA AM technical publications. The process is frozen as a QMP (qualified material process) per machine and parameter set.
2. **Allowables.** Coupons across lots, machines and build positions. Recalled: MMPDS Emerging Technology Working Group; CMH-17-style statistics; B-basis needs dozens to hundreds of coupons per condition. Physics clock: build time plus mechanical and fatigue tests, often months.
3. **Part qualification.** First article, CT/NDE of full volume, witness coupons in each build, destructive sectioning of qualification builds.
4. **Production acceptance.** Every serial part gets NDE (CT/FPI) plus witness coupons plus build records. Monitoring data goes in the build record but earns **no credit** that cuts CT or coupons.
5. **Change control.** A new machine, new parameters, or a moved build plate can trigger requalification. This is where monitoring-based equivalence (Senvol/DLA 2018) would save the most, and no regulator has accepted it.
6. Defense: NAVSEA/NAVAIR approve AM parts case by case. Recalled: first AM parts on carriers and submarines 2017–2022, and the V-22 flight-critical part in 2016. DLA uses JAMA (Joint AM Acceptability) and AM technical data packages. FAA: no AM-specific advisory circular; it uses general 14 CFR 2x.603/.605 materials-and-processes rules with issue papers per program (recalled: GE LEAP fuel nozzle and T25 sensor housing, 2015–16, approved by point design).

## What nobody does / does well

- **POD studies for monitoring methods.** Generating the 90/95 POD dataset needs seeded-defect builds plus CT ground truth across machines. Only Additive Assurance publishes a figure, and it is vendor-claimed for its own system.
- **Indication-to-defect-to-property linkage.** No accepted protocol exists. NASA names this the main gap and wants a method-agnostic protocol.
- **Machine-to-machine and site-to-site equivalence as accepted evidence.** Each new machine still requalifies.
- **Statistical allowables with fewer coupons** (Bayesian/ML/ICME) that MMPDS, the FAA or NAVSEA accept.
- **Rules for adaptive/closed-loop control.** Regulation assumes a locked process, so Freeform-style AI control has no qualification path yet.
- **An independent evidence/dossier house.** Vendors sell sensors; primes qualify internally and keep the data private. Nobody independent turns monitoring data into a regulator-ready qualification package, and nobody sells "certified process records" across machines and OEMs.

## Binding constraints and what would change them

- **Physics clock.** Coupons and fatigue tests must physically run. Change it by running them in a massively parallel way (many small builds, automated test cells) and by Bayesian designs that cut how many are needed. Tests are not skipped; they are amortized across many part approvals.
- **Regulator acceptance.** Change it by co-writing the protocol with one authority: the NAVSEA policy route (as Senvol's Navy project is doing), NASA's 6030 monitoring roadmap, or an ASTM E3353 follow-on standard that turns the guide into a spec.
- **Liability.** Whoever signs the acceptance holds it. An independent house would need to become an approved inspection source (a Level III-like role for monitoring) or carry the signature under DLA SAR / FAA PMA as the applicant.
- **Data access.** Seeded-defect plus CT ground-truth data is proprietary and scattered. A shared, regulator-visible reference dataset would be a moat.

## Opinion: AI play on the verification side

An "AM evidence factory" inside or next to the Approval Factory. For orphaned parts, run its own qualified AM cells. Treat monitoring plus CT plus coupon data as one ML-calibrated evidence stream, and win the first regulator-accepted protocol (NAVSEA/DLA first) where monitoring earns *credit*: reduced CT sampling, machine-equivalence, fewer coupons. Then license that protocol and dataset. The weak standalone-monitoring exits (Sigma at $1.6M) say not to sell a sensor. Sell accepted evidence, or the approved part.
