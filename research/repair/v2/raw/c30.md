# c30: AI-native engineering services firms / engineering copilots (Leo AI, CoLab, Quilter, Zoo, Autodesk AI, PhysicsX, Istari, Xcert...) -- could they produce substantiation?

## Short answer
They can produce pieces of the analysis and documents, but not the substantiation itself. In FAA terms, substantiation for a PMA (14 CFR 21.303) or a DER-approved repair (8110-3) is: (a) a design data package (drawings, specs, materials, processes); (b) compliance showing "by tests and computations" against the applicable airworthiness rules (Part 25/27/29/33 rules for the part's function); (c) material and process allowables the regulator already accepts (MMPDS, CMH-17, OEM or test-derived); (d) physical tests with conformity inspection witnessed or delegated; (e) a fabrication inspection system (Part 21 Subpart K quality system); (f) a person with authority who signs (DER, ODA unit member, or an FAA ACO engineer). For DLA SARs the equivalent is a TDP plus a qualification test plan and first article, approved by the ESA (engineering support activity, e.g., Ogden/Tinker/WR-ALC for C-130/KC-135).
Engineering copilots today touch only part of (a) and (b): CAD generation, formula lookup, FEA/CFD surrogates, design-review checks. None of them, as far as public material shows, outputs a compliance matrix tied to CFR paragraphs, a test plan sized to the certification basis, or a package a DER signs. No one combines "reverse-engineer an existing part, then prove equivalence" end to end, which is a different job from designing a new part.

## Gap analysis: substantiation step -> can copilots do it? -> what's missing
1. Data acquisition of the legacy part (scan, CT, metallurgy, coating, hardness, residual stress): copilots can't. This is physical; it needs a lab plus AI interpretation (CT-to-CAD, microstructure classification). Nobody packages this for the approval workflow.
2. Design intent reconstruction (tolerances, the function and load path, the OEM spec the part was made to): copilots partially can (Leo cites sources), but the OEM specs are proprietary. Missing: a corpus of mil-specs/AMS/legacy TOs plus a tolerance-inference model.
3. Certification-basis determination (which CFR paragraphs and which amendment level, or for DLA which ESA criteria and criticality code, CSI/CAI): nobody automates this. LLMs are good at it. Xcert gestures at it.
4. Analysis (stress, fatigue, damage tolerance, thermal) with accepted methods and allowables: PhysicsX, Luminary and Leo do fast analysis, but regulators accept "computations" only with validated methods and MMPDS/CMH-17 allowables. ML surrogates are not accepted as means of compliance. To change that: method validation dossiers, or run classical FEA at scale under AI orchestration and use surrogates only for search.
5. Compliance matrix, test plan and report generation: CoLab and Xcert are adjacent. Nobody produces PMA- or SAR-format packages. LLMs can do this now; the hard part is the DER and ESA engineer accepting the format.
6. Physical test and conformity (coupon, component, fatigue, first article): physics clock. AI can minimize the test matrix (Bayesian equivalence/design of experiments) and argue for "similarity" to cut tests. Nobody does argued test minimization as a service.
7. Signature (DER/ODA/ESA): law. Today the only route is to employ or contract DERs (the FAA has about 2,000-3,000 consultant DERs, recalled) or to obtain an ODA. Copilots can't sign. The play is a house that owns the signer, or builds the AI "pre-reviewer" that makes the FAA ACO/ESA reviewer's job 10x faster.
8. Post-approval (continued airworthiness, failure reporting, Service Difficulty Reports): nobody links field data back to approvals.

## Opinion: AI plays on the verification side
- "Reviewer-side copilot": sell to the reviewer (DLA ESAs, FAA ACOs, ODA holders, DERs), or give it free, so packages are checked automatically against requirements before human review. The main bottleneck is reviewer hours: DLA SAR backlogs often run 1-3 years (recalled). Customer: government via SBIR/OTA (AFWERX, DLA R&D SBIR topics on reverse engineering already exist, recalled).
- "Equivalence engine": given the legacy part (scan plus metallurgy) and the replacement design, it auto-generates the equivalence argument, the minimal test matrix and the 8110-3/SAR package. This is the Approval Factory's internal tool; it can later be licensed to PMA houses (HEICO, Wencor, Jamco, small PMA shops).
- "Accepted-methods library": a validated, versioned analysis pipeline (classical FEA plus MMPDS allowables plus AI orchestration) with a method-validation dossier the FAA accepts once and reuses, turning "computations" into a reusable means of compliance. Changing this takes a regulator acceptance project (FAA issue paper or AC-level guidance).
- None of the copilots has a business reason to own signer liability. That is the open lane: an AI-native firm that takes the liability (it employs the DERs and holds the PMA or ODA) and uses copilots plus its own verification stack. Copilots are suppliers, not competitors.
