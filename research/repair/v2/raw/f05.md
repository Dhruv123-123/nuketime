# f05: Non-destructive "material passport" of an existing part: could CT + XRF + ultrasonic + EBSD-like methods + ML fully determine alloy, temper and microstructure without cutting? State of the art and gaps.

Search budget: 3 WebSearch calls used, plus 2 WebFetch calls (one returned 404). Figures marked (recalled) come from memory and were not checked in this session.

## Short answer
- **Fully determine, no.** Some things stay out of reach without cutting or a facility-scale beam: bulk texture and grain flow in thick sections, light and interstitial elements (O/N/H in Ti, B, and C without LIBS), the inclusion and cleanliness population (VAR vs air melt), and through-thickness residual stress. These matter for fatigue, and they are where PMA and DLA reviewers ask for destructive coupons.
- **Enough to decide a spec, mostly yes.** Approvals rarely need the OEM part's full microstructure. They need to know which published spec and temper the OEM part matches, so the replacement can be made to that spec with mill certs and its own coupons. Picking from a finite list of maybe a few hundred aerospace alloy/temper/product-form combinations is a classification problem. Fusing several NDE methods with ML could close most cases with calibrated confidence and send the rest to minimal or micro-destructive sampling.
- **There is precedent for regulators accepting this.** SAE AMS 2658 (recalled) already accepts eddy-current conductivity (%IACS) plus hardness as proof of heat treatment for wrought aluminum. No equivalent exists for steels, Ti or Ni superalloys. Writing that equivalent is a real opening.

## State of the art by property (evidence and recalled)
| Property | NDE method | Status | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| Major-element chemistry | Handheld XRF (Thermo Niton, Evident Vanta, Hitachi, Bruker) | Mature; sorts grades among Ni, Fe and Co alloys and among Al series by Cu/Zn | Weak below Mg. Cannot see Li, Be, B, C, O, N, H. Struggles to separate close grades (2024 vs 2124, which differ in Fe/Si impurity limits) |
| C, Li, B | Handheld LIBS (SciAps Z series) (recalled) | Commercial; carbon in steels (L vs non-L stainless) | Leaves micro-craters, so not strictly non-destructive; ppm-level interstitials not reliable |
| Interstitials O/N/H in Ti (ELI vs standard 6-4) | None practical NDE; inert-gas fusion is destructive | Gap | Ultrasonic velocity is weakly sensitive; nobody has calibrated it |
| Al temper | Eddy current conductivity + hardness, AMS 2658 | Regulator-accepted practice | Overlaps between T6/T73/T76 tempers; product-form dependent |
| Steel/Ti heat-treat condition | Hardness (UCI/Leeb), multi-frequency eddy current, Barkhausen noise (Stresstech) | ML classification shown in research, e.g. RBF neural net + multi-frequency EC sorting tempered 100Cr6 cams (UVa paper) | No standard, no shared datasets. A 2024 review of ML eddy current testing (Results in Engineering, doi 10.1016/j.rineng.2024.103724) notes there are no common datasets, no sample-size guidance and no uncertainty quantification |
| Grain size | Ultrasonic attenuation/backscatter; laser ultrasound (Swerim LUS: "correlates very well" with EBSD; used on hot-rolling mills) | Commercial niche | Needs calibration against micrographs for each alloy |
| Grain orientation/texture (surface) | SRAS, spatially resolved acoustic spectroscopy (Nottingham/RCNDE; Rolls-Royce and Renishaw collaborations; licensed to CPI) | Pre-commercial; maps grain size and orientation on curved, as-made surfaces | Surface only; throughput not published |
| 3D grain map | Zeiss LabDCT with Xnovo GrainMapper3D on Xradia 520/620 Versa | Commercial lab instrument | Sample volume about 8 mm^3; grains of 20-40 µm minimum. Useless for whole parts |
| Bulk texture/residual stress | Neutron diffraction (ORNL VULCAN, NIST, ISIS ENGIN-X); synchrotron HEDM | Facility-only, weeks of queue | Not scalable, though beam time is a physics/capacity limit rather than a hard limit |
| Surface residual stress | XRD sin^2-psi (Proto), cos-alpha (Pulstec µ-X360); laser-ultrasonic SAW (A*STAR: Ti-6Al-4V, about 30 µm resolution, checked against XRD, hole drilling and EBSD) | Mature at the surface | Depth profiles need layer removal, which is destructive |
| Porosity/inclusions/internal geometry | Industrial CT (Waygate/Baker Hughes, Nikon, Zeiss, North Star, Lumafield, Glimpse) | Mature | Resolution scales with part size (roughly 1/1000-2000 of field of view) (recalled); inclusions under about 10 µm invisible in real parts |
| Forging grain flow / product form (forging vs plate vs casting) | Macroetch is surface-destructive; ultrasonic texture inference is research-stage | Gap | Key to forging equivalence on fatigue-critical parts |
| Coatings/plating | XRF thickness, eddy current | Mature | - |
| Shot peen, surface process | XRD residual stress plus Barkhausen | Partial | Almen intensity is a process control and cannot be read back exactly from the part |

## Why it matters for the Approval Factory
- Today a PMA reverse-engineering job (FAA Order 8110.42, test and computation) and a DLA SAR/reverse-engineering package typically consume several OEM sample parts. They are sectioned for chemistry, hardness traverses, microstructure, grain flow and sometimes tensile coupons (recalled practice). For sole-source or orphaned parts, the samples are scarce and sometimes only one exists, so destructive analysis is itself a bottleneck and cost driver. A non-destructive passport lets you characterize the one surviving part, then return it or keep it as a reference.
- The same passport also works as an incoming inspection and counterfeit check for metal parts. DFARS 252.246-7007/7008 (recalled) covers counterfeit electronics, and there is growing concern about counterfeit and unapproved parts, e.g. the AOG Technics fake-paperwork case in 2023 (recalled).

## Physics, law and acceptance constraints, and what would change them
1. **Physics.** Light elements and interstitials don't produce usable XRF signals. The changes needed: (a) calibrate LIBS for O/N/H on Ti, accepting micro-craters as "non-detrimental" under a defined surface-damage spec; (b) use prompt-gamma or neutron activation (facility-scale); (c) fit a statistical prior from process route, since VAR Ti with a given ultrasonic signature implies an O range.
2. **Physics.** Rare-defect populations govern fatigue and can't be sampled from one part. You never need to, if the replacement is made to a spec with its own lot coupons. Frame the passport as "identify the spec and confirm no exotic processing", not "certify the OEM's properties".
3. **Regulator acceptance.** Only Al temper verification (AMS 2658) is standardized. The changes needed: a SAE AMS/AMEC (recalled committee) or ASTM E07 standard for multimodal NDE spec identification of steels, Ti and Ni, with stated false-match rates; FAA ACO/DER and DLA engineering-support-activity guidance accepting it in place of sectioning when the probability of a wrong match is below a threshold.
4. **Data access.** No public paired dataset (NDE signatures plus destructive truth plus pedigree) exists at scale. Possible sources: AMARG boneyard at Davis-Monthan (recalled, around 3,000+ aircraft), teardown companies (AerSale, GA Telesis, recalled), MRO scrap streams, and DLA disposal. Buy or obtain scrap parts, run every NDE mode, then section them all to build ground truth: the "Material Fingerprint Atlas".
5. **Liability.** If the inference is wrong and a part fails, the DER/PMA holder carries the risk. Calibrated posteriors plus a mandatory destructive check on a sample of cases would cap that risk, and an insurer could price it.

## Players
- Instruments: Zeiss/Xnovo (LabDCT), Thermo Fisher Niton, Evident (Olympus) Vanta, SciAps (LIBS), Hitachi High-Tech, Bruker, Proto Manufacturing, Pulstec, Stresstech (Barkhausen), Fischer (Sigmascope conductivity), Waygate Technologies (Baker Hughes), Nikon Metrology, North Star Imaging.
- CT startups: Lumafield (VC-backed; about $32M A in 2022 and $35M B in 2023, recalled), Glimpse (CT as a service, recalled). Both work on geometry and defects, not alloy, temper or spec.
- Research: Nottingham SRAS (licensed to CPI), Swerim LUS, A*STAR laser-ultrasonic residual stress, AFRL materials state awareness / digital thread (recalled), ORNL neutron facilities.
- Nobody found does multimodal fusion into a calibrated spec/temper/product-form call with a path to regulator acceptance. Evidence is limited by the search budget, so treat this as "not found" rather than "proven absent".

## What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)
1. Build the Material Fingerprint Atlas: about 10k+ scrap aerospace parts and coupons of known pedigree, each with XRF, LIBS, multi-frequency eddy current, hardness, ultrasonic velocity/attenuation/backscatter, SRAS, Barkhausen, XRD stress and CT, then fully sectioned (OES/combustion chemistry, EBSD, macroetch grain flow, tensile).
2. Train a Bayesian fusion model that outputs a posterior over (alloy, spec, temper, product form, melt route) plus property bounds, with an explicit "go destructive here" recommendation and the smallest sample location that answers the question.
3. Physics-based forward models (ultrasonic scattering from a simulated microstructure, eddy current response from precipitation state) so the model generalizes beyond the training data.
4. A robotic passport cell that scans whole parts automatically, with scan planning chosen by the model for maximum information per minute.
5. A standards campaign: AMS 2658 analogues for steel, Ti and Ni, with validation data from the Atlas; an FAA/DLA pilot on C-130/KC-135 parts.
6. Package the output as the materials section of the TDP/PMA submission. That turns a material-analysis line item costing several engineer-weeks plus destroyed samples into hours.

## AI plays on the verification side
- "Material passport as a service": NDE-to-spec inference with calibrated uncertainty, sold to PMA houses, DLA reverse-engineering contractors, depots (OC-ALC, WR-ALC), and the Approval Factory itself.
- A counterfeit and unapproved-part metal check at receiving inspection, using the same model.
- The Atlas as a data moat: the paired NDE-plus-destructive-truth dataset is the asset nobody has.
- Standard-writing as a moat: whoever supplies the validation data shapes the AMS standard.
