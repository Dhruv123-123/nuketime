# c02: Startups and contractors using AI for DoD reverse engineering, technical data package (TDP) reconstruction and DLA Source Approval Requests (SARs); SBIR/AFWERX/NavalX/Army awards on reverse engineering and parts obsolescence

Research date: 2026-10-20. Agent c02.

## Method
- 3 WebSearch calls used (the shared cap). After that I used WebFetch and curl only.
- **Primary dataset:** I downloaded the full SBIR.gov award file with abstracts (https://data.www.sbir.gov/mod_awarddatapublic/award_data.csv, 395 MB, 219,649 awards, refreshed monthly; the FY25 data is incomplete until March 2026 according to SBIR.gov). I filtered it to DoD/"Department of War" awards (105,822).
  - The "core" set is awards whose title or abstract mentions reverse engineering, TDP or source approval/alternate source, together with obsolescence, DMSMS, legacy, sustainment or spare parts.
  - Separate filters found AI-related awards and verification-side awards (airworthiness, qualification, first article, source approval review).
  - The SBIR.gov JSON API returned 403; the bulk CSV worked.
- **DLA primary source:** NDIA/DLA 2023 Supply Chain Alliance deck "Reverse Engineering" (https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2023/dla/Reverse_Engineering.pdf), read in full with pdftotext.
- **Blocked:** dla.mil and tinker.af.mil pages returned 403. Figures not re-verified this session are marked (recalled).

## Findings

### F1. The SBIR "reverse-engineering / TDP / SAR for sustainment" market is real but small and fragmented (data)
- 364 DoD SBIR/STTR awards in the core set, totalling $178.2M, of which 126 are Phase II. By branch:
  - DLA: 232
  - Air Force: 62
  - Navy: 29
  - Army: 19
  - MDA: 9
  - DMEA: 5
  - DARPA: 3
  - OSD: 2
  - SOCOM: 2
- DLA alone is about $99.7M from 2018 to 2025, by year:
  - 2018: $8.4M
  - 2019: $9.3M
  - 2020: $15.1M
  - 2021: $7.2M
  - 2022: $33.9M (peak)
  - 2023: $8.5M
  - 2024: $6.9M
  - 2025: $9.2M (incomplete)
- The DLA topics are recurring "SAR / complete TDP for NSNs with fewer than two sources" topics: DLA171-002 (26 awards), DLA192-001 (25), DLA222-001 (20), DLA202-001 (15), DLA231-001 (12), and others. In effect DLA SBIR is a subsidised SAR factory, where each award is a handful of NSNs.
- Top recipients in the core set (number of awards, total $). All are small engineering or manufacturing shops, not software or AI companies:
  - Parts Life Inc (Moorestown NJ): 24, $11.8M
  - Mentis Sciences: 16, $7.6M
  - Global Circuit Innovations (electronics obsolescence): 16, $7.2M
  - Mainstream Engineering: 16, $4.5M
  - Brighton Cromwell: 13, $7.9M
  - DeVal Life Cycle Support: 12, $4.2M
  - Cepeda Associates: 11, $6.1M
  - Engineering & Software System Solutions: 11, $9.6M
  - NanoSonic: 9, $5.5M
  - Beacon Industries: 9, $4.4M
  - Intellisense: 8, $4.7M
  - Form Fit and Function LLC: 7, $5.4M
  - Kaney Aerospace, Integrated Solutions for Systems, Turnaround Factor: 6 each
- The topic language fixes the unit economics. The target items are "NSNs with an Annual Demand Value (ADV) >$10K currently sourced with no current suppliers" or "only 1-2 manufacturers" (Brighton Cromwell 2019 and 2020; NanoSonic and Mainstream 2018). Each award covers between 1 and about 35 NSNs: Sunbelt researched all 35 NSNs in its 2022 topic; Turnaround Factor did 7 NSNs and 16 NSNs.
- **Speed and cost datapoints from abstracts (self-reported):**
  - Empirical Systems Aerospace (2022): "submit a completed SAR approximately 10 weeks after beginning the effort", with 213 units about 7 months in.
  - Dynovas (2024): qualifying parts "through the standard Source Approval Request process within the 6-month $100,000 Phase I" for NAVAIR PMA-202.
  - Parts Life (2018): its "SAR Process and Reverse Engineering Risk Avoidance Process (RERAP)" aims to cut the cost of one seal assembly by 20%.
  - Renaissance Services (2017): OC-ALC reported a gearbox housing with a 24-month lead time at $80,000, and $100k-750k of non-recurring tooling per casting part number.
  - Global Circuit Innovations (2024) cites AMRDEC: "70% of electronics are obsolete before a system is deployed", and one component can become obsolete up to 10 times over a system's life.
  - ORBIS (DLA SBIR success story, via the search snippet; the PDF itself returned 403): a single firm reverse engineering several NSNs showed 25% cost savings against separate firms.
- The trend is not growing: core awards by year were 2018: 37, 2019: 30, 2020: 45, 2021: 27, 2022: 70, 2023: 24, 2024: 39, 2025: 32.

### F2. AI in this space is new (2023 onwards), tiny (about 16 awards), and almost entirely on the "make the package" side (data)
Only 16 of the 364 core awards mention AI or ML. The relevant ones:

**Elevan LLC (dba Elevate Systems), San Antonio, 10 employees, SBA 8(a).** It is the most persistent AI-for-sustainment-data shop:
- 2D legacy drawing conversion (OPTIX = "Optimized Technical Information Extraction"): AF Phase I/II/TACFI in 2021-2023, about $1.55M total.
- B-1, F-16, C-5, C-130 and B-52 landing gear plastic parts via AM: $1.25M D2P2 (2022) and $1.16M TACFI (2025, 417th SCMS / 448th SCMW).
- Army A244-P057 "AI Supported Reverse Engineering Technology Platform": $250k Phase I (2025).
- DMEA PCB extraction: $97k (2024).
- AFX23E-TPCSO1 "Leveraging AI/ML to Expedite Aircraft Replacement Parts": $110k Phase I (2024). This one is the verification side; see F3.

**Mobilio Co. (Kipo AI).**
- AF D2P2 AF244-D006, $1.40M (2025): an "Automated Datasheet Extraction and DRC Suite Adapted for Reverse Engineering". It "verifies the functionality of a reverse-engineered schematic" of legacy avionics CCAs, with subcontractors Diode Inc. and JITX, and builds a 100,000-schematic labelled dataset.
- Army A244-P057, $249k (2025): an autonomous obsolete-component replacement engine for Army LEAD (Letterkenny Army Depot).

**RJ Matthews LLC / Tetrahive (Brentwood TN, 2 employees).** It builds X-ray CT based non-destructive PCB reverse engineering with ML ("3dIV"):
- AF183-021 Phase II, $750k (2020)
- Enhancement, $376k (2024)
- AF244-D007 D2P2 "Multimodal Image Fusion", $1.40M (2025)

**Cornerstone Research Group.** Navy N23B-T032, an ML tool that ingests TDPs and scores parts for AM suitability: $238k Phase I (2024) and $1.0M Phase II (2025).

**Army topic A244-P057 (2025).** This topic is effectively "AI reverse engineering for LEAD". Awards:
- XO Armor "3D Scan Enabled Machine Learning for Reverse Engineering and Parametric Modeling": $249k
- Elevate: $250k
- Merge Plot "REVISE": $250k
- Deep Analytics "Third Eye" PCB RE
- Red Balloon "TRACE" (embedded RE, security)
- Mobilio: $249k
The XO Armor abstract cites DMSMS costs "upwards of $750,000,000 per year".

**OSD254-P001 (2025).** A generative-AI open topic. Awards:
- Novineer "AI-Driven Model Generation for Rapid, Cost-Effective On-Demand Part Repair and Redesign": $150k, Army contracting
- Muir AI "Generative AI for Reverse Engineering and Supply Chain Vulnerabilities" (synthetic BoMs): $128k
- Inoviant "AI for Airworthiness Certification": $149k (see F3)
- Mile Two "Morphy-Vision": $1.2M D2P2

**DMEA23C-P001 (2024).** AI PCB reverse engineering: Aerocyonics $99k, TerraVerum "REvision" $99k, Elevate $97k.

**RGBSI Aerospace & Defense.** DLA232-001, $97k (2023): a "Digital Sustainment Platform" to resolve DLA "No Bid" sourcing.

**Adjacent geometry and identification players.**
- Physna (geometric deep learning 3D search): AF $1.04M D2P2 (2023) and $1.90M Phase II (2024) for USAF Digital Material Management. This covers identifying parts and detecting changes before they are fitted.
- Quidient (Columbia MO, "5D" photogrammetric reconstruction): about 12 AF awards from 2021 to 2025, including smartphone 3D scanners for reverse engineering, the PART-4100 reconstruction tool ($1.25M, 2023), 3D counterfeit-part detection, and aircraft surface inspection.

**Venture-backed manufacturers entering via AFWERX sustainment topics (making, not approving).**
- Divergent Technologies: AFX24E-TPCSO1 STTR with the University of Oklahoma, $106k Phase I and $1.79M Phase II (2025). It works with AFLCMC and the Rapid Sustainment Office to "reverse engineer, print, and rapidly prototype replacements for critical and/or obsolete aircraft sustainment parts".
- Machina Labs: 6 awards, $5.1M, from 2021 to 2024. A multi-year AFRL RoboCraftsman contract was announced 2025-09-19 (value undisclosed), starting with airframe skins and panels for maintenance (The Defense Post).
- Rangeview: digital investment casting, $1.4M (2025).
- Beacon Industries: 3D-printed magnesium, rapid repair and reverse engineering.

**Novineer.** Founded 2022 by Ali Tamijani (Embry-Riddle). NoviVision does photo-to-CAD (1-4 phone photos, editable STEP in about 2 minutes, mean deviation 0.72 mm on a 100 mm part, max 2.3 mm). It claims more than 80% faster MRO assessment, has US Army corporate agreements and a Stratasys partnership. This comes from a sponsored Defense Post article (2026-06-30) and is self-reported. 0.72 mm accuracy is far outside aerospace drawing tolerances (typically ±0.005 in = 0.127 mm or tighter), so this is triage and soft-tooling quality, not SAR-grade.

**Commercial AI CAD.** Backflip AI does scan, drawing and photo to parametric CAD with feature trees. Its site names no DoD contracts.

### F3. The verification / approval side: almost nobody, and the few that do are tiny (data + opinion)
The SBIR record shows a few efforts aimed at the government reviewer rather than the applicant:

**Elevate Systems, AFX23E-TPCSO1, $110k Phase I (2024), FA8649-24-P-0130.** This is the only award found that explicitly targets the reviewer side of source approval. It uses "AI/ML paired with advanced computer vision ... to save thousands of unbudgeted hours that the engineers have to spend ... reviewing and approving requests for supportability analysis resulting in source approvals for repair justification." I found no Phase II in the dataset.

**RedShred LLC (Catonsville MD, 14 employees). AIRMATE.**
- AFX23E-TPCSO1 Phase I $106k and Phase II $1.79M (2024).
- It uses LLMs to turn airworthiness certification documents such as MIL-HDBK-516C into MBSE/SysML models with "modification description alignment with relevant criteria".
- RedShred also holds a $5.75M DARPA/Navy Phase II (2022, "COACH") for liberating legacy technical data.

**Inoviant (Lincoln MA, 5 employees). OSD254-P001 "AI for Airworthiness Certification", $149k (2025).**
- Claim: airworthiness certification timelines of up to 18 months cost DoD "over $5 billion annually" (self-reported, unsourced).
- The product does MIL-HDBK-516C compliance checking, automated error detection, and a human-in-the-loop validation framework.

**Mile Two (Dayton, 125 employees).** Morphy-AI, said to be "validated in ... airworthiness certification" use cases; $1.2M D2P2 (2025).

**Istari (Charleston SC; Istari Digital).**
- 10 DoD awards totalling $12.8M from 2020 to 2024. They include physics-informed ML surrogates for the aeroelastic airworthiness bottleneck (MDA22-T003, $150k, 2023), digital certification of autonomy, and neural control certificates.
- Istari's site (2026) describes agentic engineering to "validate and certify system integrity", with the DAF "Industry Øne" announcement of 2026-05-20.
- (Recalled: Istari was co-founded by former Air Force acquisition chief Will Roper and is venture-backed.)

**Toyon + UDRI.** "AI for the Depot: Using ETAR for Digital Health Records", $1.5M D2P2 (2022). It ingests Engineering Technical Assistance Requests, NDI results and airworthiness information into a per-aircraft digital health record. That is the depot engineering-disposition queue, a close analogue of the SAR review queue.

**Nlign Analytics.**
- AF233-D016, $1.76M D2P2 (2024-03-14): "TDP Modernization for As-Built Data". It defines an Enhanced TDP carrying as-built nonconformances and repairs, using QIF for dimensional data.
- Also a $1.25M ASIP inspection-data capture award (2022).
- This is the data schema a machine reviewer would need.

**AM qualification as statistical verification.**
- Senvol (ML for AM qualification): Navy N16A-T022 Phase II, $2.28M (2018). It is a "probabilistic qualification framework that will enable additive manufacturing (AM) materials and part qualification through the use of a data-driven predictive model."
- Senvol, Army Phase II $1.2M (2023): ML for consistency and verification across sites.
- Senvol, NAVSEA Phase II $1.97M (2025): sensor fusion "developing sufficient evidence to support the acceptance" of AM parts.
- Etegent, AF Phase I (2021): "AI Enabled Rapid Qualification of AM Components" by flagging in-process defects to avoid CT.

**What is missing.** No awardee, startup or contractor found offers a system that:
- ingests a whole SAR or alternate offer (Category I-IV), checks it against the service ESA's criteria and the existing TDP or drawing, and produces a disposition-ready review for the ESA engineer; or
- carries "approval credit" (precedent) from one approved NSN to geometrically or functionally similar NSNs.

The reviewer side (DLA engineering analysts, Air Force 429 SCMS/SASPO at Tinker, NAVSUP WSS, Army AvMC SRD) has one $110k Phase I award, from Elevate.

### F4. The pathway itself (DLA 2023 deck, primary; plus the p06 prior note)
1. **Pick a target.** Find an NSN with an AMSC that allows a SAR, meaning not G or T (already competitive). Not available: source-controlled AMSC B, specification-controlled items, items where a complete approved TDP exists, some AMSC C items, and items under $10,000 anticipated annual demand. Only domestic firms are eligible. Look-ups use PUBLOG/FLIS (the "MOE Rule" tab).
2. **RPPOB request.** Under the Review, Parts Purchase or Borrow programme, request via email (Land & Maritime) or the website (Aviation). DLA returns the demand history, the cost to buy the sample, and "any available data". **Military Service concurrence is required before a Category IV (reverse-engineered) SAR for many items.**
3. Sign the contract and pay; DLA ships the part.
4. **Reverse engineer** at your own expense. This covers geometry, material, process, finish and test.
5. **Write the SAR** to the DLA SAR Guide, plus service-specific guidance (NAVAIR via NAVSUP WSS, AF via the 429 SCMS/SASPO at Tinker, Army via AvMC SRD). The package needs company manufacturing and quality information, plus "all technical data needed to demonstrate competence to manufacture the NSN to the same, or better, level of quality than the current approved source(s)". Categories: I Same Part, II Similar Part, III New Part/Manufacturer, IV Reverse Engineered.
6. **Submit to DLA.** "We forward your SAR to the appropriate Military Service for review/approval." The engineering authority is the service ESA, not DLA.
7. Approval leads to being added as an approved source, then competition on the next buy, often with first-article testing (FAT). An Alternate Offer (AO) is the variant submitted against a live solicitation.

**Timelines and thresholds (from the search snippets of DLA decks; the DLA pages themselves returned 403):**
- "Typical timeline is 12 to 18 months for full approval".
- Minimum $10K annual buy value for RPPOB.
- The DLA Aviation value-engineering flyer sets a $40,000 annual demand minimum for its Parts Catalog.
- A Joint Certification Program (JCP, for export-controlled data) may be required, and the item must be in stock.

**Statute (from p06).** 10 USC 3243 requires a "prompt opportunity" to qualify. The government bears testing and evaluation costs for small businesses when fewer than two sources exist. A 2025 amendment requires streamlined qualification of advanced-manufacturing sources.

**Who reviews today.** Service ESA engineers review each SAR by hand. Practitioners report backlogs of months to more than a year (recalled, from p06 F9; not re-verified). Elevate's abstract independently describes "thousands of unbudgeted hours" spent by AF engineers reviewing source-approval and supportability requests.

## Synthesis (opinion)
1. **Is anyone doing the "Approval Factory"?**
   - On the making side, partly. About 20-30 small engineering shops, led by Parts Life ($11.8M across 24 awards), Mentis, Brighton Cromwell, DeVal and Global Circuit Innovations, live on DLA SBIR as hand-built SAR and TDP factories, a few NSNs per award. None is AI-native or venture-scale, and none publishes engineering hours per approved SAR.
   - A wave of 2024-2025 AI tools covers parts of the package: drawings to digital (Elevate OPTIX), photo or scan to CAD (Novineer, Quidient, XO Armor, Backflip), PCB reverse engineering by CT and ML (Tetrahive, Aerocyonics, TerraVerum, Mobilio), and part search and identification (Physna).
   - Venture-backed manufacturers (Divergent, Machina Labs, Rangeview) are entering via AFWERX sustainment topics, but they are selling production capacity, not approvals.
   - Nobody combines package generation, approval throughput as the KPI, and owning the resulting source approval as the asset.
2. **The verification side is close to empty.** A $110k Phase I to Elevate on AI-assisted review of source-approval requests (2024) is the only reviewer-side award. Airworthiness-certification AI (RedShred AIRMATE $1.9M, Inoviant $149k, Mile Two, Istari) works on MIL-HDBK-516C platform certification, not part-level SAR/ESA disposition. Statistical qualification (Senvol) covers AM only. So the bottleneck the prior review found (permission, meaning ESA reviewer hours) has almost no AI investment against it.
3. **Implication for the founder.** The open position is either:
   - "the ESA's machine reviewer" (sell to, or embed in, the 429 SCMS / NAVSUP / AvMC / DLA engineering offices, measured in reviewer hours per disposition), or
   - an applicant-side factory whose packages are pre-verified in the reviewer's own format, so that reviewer time collapses.
   Both turn the scarce resource (ESA engineer attention) into the metric.

## What would need to be done (assuming abundant intelligence and compute)
- **Machine-readable SAR criteria per ESA.** Encode the DLA SAR Guide, service-specific guides, MIL-STDs and specs, and past dispositions into executable checklists. The reviewer gets a pre-filled disposition with every claim linked to evidence. This is the RedShred AIRMATE idea, applied to SARs and AOs rather than 516C.
- **A precedent and similarity graph across NSNs.** Use geometric deep learning (Physna-style) together with FLIS/PUBLOG and material and process data, so that one approved SAR justifies streamlined review of similar items. This is analogous to FAA "similarity" arguments; today each NSN restarts from zero.
- **A verified as-built evidence standard (QIF, Nlign-style Enhanced TDP).** Every measurement, CT scan (Lumafield, Tetrahive), material cert and test result is captured with chain of custody, so the reviewer checks evidence rather than prose.
- **Physics-clock compression.** Substitute validated surrogates for some FAT and qualification tests (Istari PIML surrogates, Senvol probabilistic qualification), with test-by-exception statistics agreed with the ESA. Physical tests still run, but fewer and in parallel.
- **Batch economics.** Use ORBIS's evidence that one firm doing many NSNs saved 25%, and pursue portfolio SARs: whole subsystems or all NSNs of a commodity class on a platform such as the C-130 or KC-135.
- **Legal and regulatory acceptance.** An ESA pilot under 10 USC 3243 ("prompt opportunity", with the government paying small-business qualification test costs) and the 2025 advanced-manufacturing streamlining clause, where the AI reviewer's pre-disposition is accepted as the first pass. This needs an AFWERX TACFI/STRATFI or a DLA SBIR topic sponsored by the ESA, not by DLA alone.
- **Data access.** JCP certification. RPPOB for samples. Legacy drawings (2D mylar and sepia; Elevate says DoD owns "millions of 2-D drawings"). FOIA of approved-source lists and AMSC codes, to build the target universe of all NSNs with ADV over $10k and fewer than two sources.
