## 1. Regulatory skeleton

### FAA
- **14 CFR Part 21 Subpart M is "[Reserved]"** (fetched, eCFR). The FAA has NO standalone repair-design-approval rule equivalent to EASA Subpart M. Repair data approval lives in Part 43 (maintenance), Part 43 Appendix A (major/minor classification), Part 145 (repair stations), Part 121/135 operators' CASS, plus designee orders.
- Approval routes for major repair data (fetched, SASSOFIA 2020 summary; recalled detail):
  1. **OEM/TCH data**: SRM (structural repair manual) within limits = approved data; no engineering needed.
  2. **TCH ODA**: Boeing issues **FAA Form 8100-9** under its ODA for repairs outside SRM (Boeing "Repair Engineering"/ MOM-driven requests). Airbus equivalent: RDAS under EASA DOA, accepted by FAA via bilateral (TIP).
  3. **DER**: Designated Engineering Representative signs **FAA Form 8110-3** "Statement of Compliance with Airworthiness Standards" recommending/approving technical data within his authorized area (e.g., Structures, Powerplant, Systems & Equipment, Propulsion, Flight Analyst; categories under Order 8110.37). Company DERs (employed by a TCH/MRO) vs consultant DERs (independent, serve anyone).
  4. **Non-TCH ODA** (e.g., MRO ODAs, STC ODAs) issue 8100-9 / approve repair data.
  5. **Field approval** (Form 337 block 3 by FSDO ASI, Order 8300.16) - one-off for one aircraft; increasingly discouraged.
- **Form 337** ("Major Repair and Alteration") is the record that a major repair/alteration was performed per approved data; used chiefly for non-air-carrier (Part 91, GA) aircraft. Air carriers (121) and repair stations record major repairs in their own systems (repair station form / work order) instead of 337 for many cases (recalled, AC 43-9C / Part 43 App B).
- **One aircraft vs fleet**: an 8110-3 typically approves data for a specific serial/registration unless written as part-number-generic or "fleet" data; forum practitioners advise converting prior data into P/N-specific procedures approved by a DER with procedure authority (search snippet, eng-tips; anecdotal).
- **DER limits** (recalled): DERs cannot approve data for items the FAA has retained (e.g., new TC issues, novel or unusual designs, certain AD-affected areas unless authorized, "major changes in type design"); DER repairs on PSEs (principal structural elements) of transport aircraft must address damage tolerance (14 CFR 26.43/26.45 / 121.1109, "Aging Airplane" / DT data for repairs, Damage Tolerance Data for Repairs and Alterations rule, 2007) -> three-stage approval (Stage 1 static strength to return to service, Stage 2 DT inspection program within 12 months, Stage 3 any special requirements) per AC 120-93 / AC 25.1529-1 (recalled).
- **DER population** (recalled): ~2,500-3,000 active DERs total across all categories (company + consultant); structures DERs a large fraction; median age high (>55), FAA has been moving to ODA and "Designee Management System" (DMS, 2017). Order 8100.15 for ODA.

### EASA
- **Part 21 Subpart M (21.A.431A-21.A.445)**: every repair to an EASA-certificated product needs an approved repair design (fetched summary). Classification: minor vs major (21.A.435; GM 21.A.91 criteria: affects structural performance, weight, CG, systems, etc.).
- Who approves:
  - **EASA itself** (for repairs by non-DOA applicants; fee-based, slow; months).
  - **DOA holder** with privilege 21.A.263(c)(4)/(5)? (recalled: (c)(4) minor repairs, (c)(5) major repairs only for DOA of the TCH or with specific scope; non-TCH DOAs often hold privileges for minor and certain major repairs under their "Terms of Approval").
  - **TCH** issues RDAS/RAS (Airbus Repair Design Approval Sheet, ATA 52-57 + ATA 27 interface; Technical Adaptation for systems) (fetched).
  - Since Reg (EU) 2022/201 (applicable March 2023, recalled): Subpart M allows TCH, DOA, or "Production organisation / alternative procedures" for small aircraft; Part 21 Light (Reg 2022/1358, applicable 2023-2024) introduced Part 21L declared design organisations for light aircraft.
- Non-TCH DOA for repairs must show access to TC data (21.A.433 "the applicant shall have... arrangement with TCH or own data") -> **data access is the binding gate**, same as FAA DER needing OEM loads/material allowables or doing reverse engineering (substantiation from scratch).
- **Cross-recognition**: FAA-EASA BASA TIP: EASA accepts FAA-approved repair data for US-State-of-Design products and vice versa, with exceptions (critical components, PSE repairs on non-SoD products need validation) (recalled).
- **EASA DOA population** (recalled): ~350-400 DOAs EASA-approved incl. many repair-scope "21J" independents (e.g., Lufthansa Technik EASA.21J.xxx, SR Technics, ATR, TAG/ACI, Aeroconseil/Akka, CIRA). Fees under Reg (EU) 2019/2153: EASA charges for major repair approvals billed hourly (~EUR 230-250/h, recalled).

## 2. Full pathway (typical out-of-SRM structural repair, transport aircraft)

| # | Step | Who | Time (typical) | Cost (typical) | What breaks / is undone |
|---|------|-----|----------------|----------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Damage found & characterized (NDT, measurements, photos) | Mechanic/NDT Level II-III | hours-days | $1-10k | Data quality poor; photos + hand sketches; no 3D geometry; damage mapping not machine readable |
| 2 | Check SRM/AMM limits | Maintenance engineer | hours | low | SRM is PDF; lookup manual; misreads common |
| 3 | Classify minor/major (Part 43 App A; GM 21.A.91) | Engineer / CAMO | hours-days | low | Judgment; inconsistent between operators; FAA/EASA disagree at edges |
| 4 | Request OEM disposition (Boeing MOM/ Airbus TechRequest) OR go to DER/DOA | Operator/MRO | OEM: 1 day (AOG) to 2-8 weeks routine (recalled) | OEM repair support often bundled or $5-50k per RDAS (recalled) | OEM queue = monopoly; AOG costs $10-150k/day |
| 5 | Repair design: geometry, doubler sizing, fastener pattern, material, sealing | Structures engineer | 20-200 h simple; 300-1,000+ h complex | $3k-150k | Needs OEM loads/allowables/finite element model the independent doesn't have |
| 6 | Static strength substantiation (hand calcs, FEM) | Stress engineer | 10-200 h | | Loads are the gap: reverse-derived loads -> conservative heavy repairs |
| 7 | Damage tolerance / fatigue (Stage 2), inspection program | DT specialist (scarce) | weeks; must be done within 12 months for PSEs (recalled) | $10-100k | Few DT-qualified DERs; spectra not available; Stage 2 backlog |
| 8 | Compliance documentation (8110-3 / RDAS / compliance checklist, drawing) | DER / DOA CVE | days-weeks | included | Pure paperwork; non-reusable formats |
| 9 | FAA oversight / EASA approval (if outside delegation) | ACO/EASA PCM | weeks-months | EASA hourly fees | Regulator staff shortages; retained items |
| 10 | Install, inspect, return to service (Form 337 or RS records, CRS) | Part 145 / A&P/IA | days | labor | Paper |
| 11 | Records, continued airworthiness (repair assessment programs, REGs), lease return | CAMO / lessor | years | lease return disputes $100k+ per aircraft (recalled, typical) | Repair records fragmented; lessors reject DER repairs lacking DT data; "OEM-only repair" lease clauses |

Key cost anchors (recalled, industry): consultant DER $150-300/h; simple 8110-3 for a GA/minor structural item $2-10k; transport-category major structural repair with Stage 1-3 $30-150k; OEM repair engineering cost hidden in support agreements; AOG engineering response demanded within 24-48h.

## 3. Bottlenecks (ranked)

1. **Data access (loads, FEMs, allowables, DT spectra)** - OEM proprietary; independents must reverse-engineer or be conservative. Law: 21.A.433 (EASA) and ICA/data ownership; FAA allows DER-generated data but substantiation burden. This is the permission gate.
2. **Scarce, aging signatories** - DERs and DOA CVEs (Compliance Verification Engineers) are persons; signature = liability + appointment. FAA designee appointment capped by FAA managing-office capacity. Physics doesn't bind; the appointment does.
3. **Lessor/operator acceptance** - Even valid DER repairs are often refused by lessors/insurers at lease return; "OEM-approved" premium. Market acceptance, not regulator.
4. **Damage tolerance analysis** - specialized, slow, required for PSEs; nobody automates crack-growth (NASGRO/AFGROW used manually).
5. **Non-reuse** - each 8110-3 is bespoke; prior approved repairs aren't searchable or legally reusable across tail numbers without re-approval; no shared library of approved repairs (OEM has one internally; independents do not).
6. **Regulator review** - FAA ACO/ EASA staff; retained items; field approvals discouraged.
7. **Physics clocks** - mostly analysis-only for repairs; physical tests only when novel material/process (coupon tests: weeks; full-scale fatigue: months-years, rarely needed for repairs). So repairs are dominated by human/permission time, not physics: ideal for AI.

## 5. What nobody does / what would need to be done (assume abundant compute)

1. **Reconstruct OEM-equivalent structural models without OEM data**: 3D scan + public data (TCDS, SRMs held by operators, ADs, SBs, NTSB reports, academic) -> inferred global FEM, internal loads, material allowables, for every major legacy platform (737 Classic/NG, A320ceo, 767, CRJ, ATR, C-130, KC-135). This dissolves bottleneck #1. Needs: scanning access via operator partners; validation via strain-gauge flight/ground tests (physics clock: weeks per platform, once).
2. **Machine-readable SRM + approved-repair corpus**: convert SRMs, 8110-3s, RDAS, 337s (FAA keeps 337 copies; FOIA-able for GA) into a structured library of approved repairs with geometry and substantiation -> precedent search + "family" approvals (P/N/zone-generic data) so one approval serves many tails.
3. **Automated damage tolerance (Stage 2/3)**: crack-growth (NASGRO/AFGROW) automation, inspection-interval optimization with probabilistic DT, auto-generated inspection programs. The scarcest skill.
4. **Compliance-document compiler**: auto-generate 8110-3 packages, compliance checklists vs 25.571/25.305/26.43, drawings, so the DER/CVE spends hours reviewing not weeks writing. KPI: signer-hours per approval.
5. **Verification-of-verification for regulators**: a tool FAA ACOs/EASA PCMs could use to audit designee packages (independent re-analysis). Changes regulator acceptance: if the regulator trusts a re-checker, oversight time drops.
6. **Own the signatory**: hire/appoint consultant DERs and stand up an FAA ODA (repair ODA) + EASA DOA with repair privileges 21.A.263(c); an ODA lets the company self-appoint unit members (removing the per-person FAA appointment bottleneck). Timeline (recalled): ODA application to issuance 1-3 years; EASA DOA 1-2 years.
7. **Lessor acceptance standard**: a "lessor-grade" repair dossier (DT data, digital twin, records) and maybe insurance/warranty backing the repair -> breaks the "OEM-only" lease clause. Liability: carry product liability insurance; offer residual-value guarantee.
8. **Damage capture standard**: phone/scan app that captures damage in metrology-grade 3D at the hangar -> feeds the engine; currently photos + sketches.
9. **Military analog**: USAF/ASIP (Aircraft Structural Integrity Program) engineering dispositions for C-130/KC-135 field depots (AFLCMC 202 dispositions, recalled form "AFTO 202" for technical assistance requests) - same bottleneck, government-owned data rights in some cases; SBIR/AFWERX entry.

## 6. Binding constraints and what would change them
- **Physics**: few physical tests for repairs; full-scale fatigue only for novel concepts. Model validation tests per platform once. Mostly not binding.
- **Law**: signer must be a designee/ODA/DOA; AI cannot sign. Change: become an ODA/DOA; push FAA AI policy (FAA AI Safety Assurance roadmap 2024) to accept AI-generated analysis with human verification (it already does accept software-generated FEM).
- **Regulator acceptance**: novel methods (inferred loads) need FAA issue paper/EASA CRI-like acceptance -> pilot with one ACO; publish validation.
- **Liability**: designee/ODA holder liability; GARA (General Aviation Revitalization Act 18-year) for GA; insurance.
- **Data access**: TCH data; 21.A.433; lease/IP. Change: reverse-engineered data (legal, done by PMA industry), operator-owned scans, government data rights (military).
