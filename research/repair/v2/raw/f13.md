## 1. What "similarity" is already allowed to prove today (evidence + recalled)

| Regime | What similarity is allowed to do | Scale today |
|---|---|---|
| FAA PMA, 14 CFR 21.303(a)(4) (verified) | The applicant must give "test reports and computations necessary to show that the design meets airworthiness requirements", **unless the applicant shows the design is identical** to the TC'd article. The test-and-computation route allows *comparative analysis* against the OAH part (Wikipedia/AC 21.303-4). | Each part is argued alone. The argument is the original part versus one candidate, not a population. |
| FAA/EASA means-of-compliance codes (recalled) | "Similarity" is a recognized compliance method in compliance checklists. EASA MoC numbering lists it with design review, calculation, test, etc. It needs a documented, previously certified reference design plus an analysis of the differences. | One reference per finding. |
| NASA GSFC-STD-7000 GEVS; SMC-S-016 / MIL-STD-1540 lineage (recalled) | Qualification by similarity is allowed if the hardware is the same design and comes from the same manufacturer and process. The new environments must sit inside the envelope already qualified. Any difference triggers a "delta-qualification". | Heritage hardware only. This is the strictest version. |
| MIL-PRF-38535 QML and MIL-PRF-19500 (recalled) | **Family / technology-flow qualification.** A process flow is qualified once, and new die built on that flow inherit it through generic data. | **Precedent at scale:** thousands of part numbers sit under one qualification. |
| AEC-Q100/Q101/Q200 automotive (recalled) | "Qualification family" and "generic data". A new part can use stress-test data from family members that share process, package and design rules. | Industry-wide. The data stays private to each supplier. |
| MMPDS (Battelle-run, FAA-accepted metallic allowables) and CMH-17 (recalled) | **Pooled statistical data from many sources becomes regulator-accepted evidence** (A-/B-basis allowables). | Decades old. It covers materials, not parts. |
| NCAMP at Wichita State NIAR (recalled) | A composite material system is qualified once into a shared database. A second user runs a reduced **equivalency** test set (a handful of specimens instead of a full program) and inherits the allowables. FAA accepts this through AC 20-107B and DOT/FAA/AR-03/19 (recalled). | **The closest working precedent to a "similarity graph as evidence."** It reportedly cuts per-user qualification from years and millions of dollars to months and roughly $100-200k (recalled). |
| NASA-STD-6030 / America Makes, additive manufacturing (recalled) | A Qualified Material Process plus part classification lets a new AM part use the process qualification instead of a full material program. | Emerging. |
| DLA Source Approval Request categories (recalled, verify against the DLA Aviation SAR page) | Category I: the offeror has made the *actual item* for the OEM or Government. Category II: the offeror has made a *similar item*, with a comparison of how alike they are. Category III: no actual or similar item, so a full TDP and first article are required. | **Similarity is already a formal DLA category, but it is judged by people, one item at a time, and only on the offeror's own history.** |

**Takeaway (opinion grounded in the above):** regulators already accept three forms of similarity:
- (a) one-to-one heritage (GEVS, MoC similarity, DLA Category II);
- (b) family or flow inheritance inside one supplier (QML, AEC-Q);
- (c) pooled cross-supplier databases with equivalency deltas (MMPDS, NCAMP).

Nobody yet accepts (d), cross-part, cross-supplier geometric and functional similarity across a whole fleet of approved designs, used to cut the evidence for a *new part* (as opposed to a new material). That gap is the opportunity. NCAMP is the template: it took about 15 years and an FAA-funded academic host to become accepted (recalled).

## 2. Is anyone doing (d)?

### Similarity search exists, but for sourcing, design reuse and cost, not as approval evidence
- **Physna / Thangs** (verified: "World's Leading Geometric Search Engine"). The page gave nothing else. Recalled: about $80M+ raised in total, including a $56M Series B in 2021 led by Tiger Global; DoD/Navy pilots for 3D part matching and reverse engineering. Threat: adjacent, since it has the geometric index but no qualification layer.
- **CADDi** (verified: an AI data platform for manufacturing; ingests drawings, search, AI agents; "Design Reuse Simulator"). Recalled: about $89M Series C in 2023, Japan and US, drawing-similarity search is its core. Threat: adjacent.
- **Accuris** (formerly IHS Markit engineering: Parts Management, Haystack Gold, NSN/FLIS cross-reference), **SiliconExpert** (Arrow), and **Z2Data** (recalled). These are form-fit-function cross-reference graphs for electronics and NSNs, used in DMSMS and obsolescence work. They are the nearest existing "similarity graph", but their output is a *candidate list*, not evidence. Threat: adjacent. Accuris owns much of the NSN metadata layer.
- **Senvol** (Senvol Database and Senvol ML; Navy, Army and Air Force SBIRs; recalled). Uses ML on AM process-property data to cut material qualification testing. Threat: direct in spirit, but for AM materials only.
- **NCAMP / NIAR** and **Battelle (MMPDS)**. These are the incumbent "pooled evidence" institutions. They are potential partners or channels, not competitors.
- **Siemens Teamcenter Classification, PTC Windchill, Dassault (EXALEAD OnePart)** (recalled). Part-similarity search inside one OEM's PLM. Every large OEM already has a private similarity graph of its own approved parts. HEICO's internal know-how across 10,000+ PMAs is effectively one too.

### Nobody found (with the caveat that search was unavailable)
No company or program was found that turns cross-part similarity into a submitted qualification or approval argument to the FAA or DLA. The same goes for a back-tested risk score for that argument, built from field-failure data. Confidence is medium-low because live search was not possible.

## 3. The verification pathway when similarity is the evidence

1. **Reference corpus.** Approved designs with their geometry, material, process, loads, environment and qualification data. The binding constraint is data rights.
   - Public or obtainable sources: the FAA PMA parts list (maps PMA P/N to TC P/N, public); FLIS/NSN characteristics (public via PUB LOG); JEDMICS drawings that carry Government-purpose or unlimited rights (released per SAR); QPL/QPD qualified product lists; MIL specs; MMPDS; NCAMP.
   - Labels: field outcomes from FAA SDRs (public), ADs (public), DLA/DoD PQDRs (product quality deficiency reports, not public), and GIDEP alerts (members only).
2. **Similarity metric.** Geometry + material + process + load path + environment + failure mode. Knowledge-cheap: AI does this well.
3. **Envelope argument.** Show the candidate's demands sit inside the envelope of a cluster of approved, flown parts with no failures. Show the candidate's capability is at least theirs.
4. **Delta analysis.** Identify which differences matter (for example a changed fillet radius or a new heat-treat lot) and map each one to the minimum test that closes it. This is where the physics clock lives: fatigue, environmental and first-article tests still have to run. Similarity shrinks *how many* run; it does not remove them.
5. **Metric validation (the evidence for the evidence).** Back-test: would the metric have predicted known failures (SDR/PQDR/AD history)? Nobody does this today. It is what would make a regulator trust the graph.
6. **Regulator acceptance.**
   - DLA can change SAR category criteria through agency policy, with no rulemaking. It is the cheapest lever.
   - The FAA needs an issue paper, an Order 8110.42 revision, or an accepted DER methodology. It could start through a DER using "comparative analysis" under 21.303.
   - NCAMP shows the FAA will accept a shared-database method after a long validation.
7. **Production conformity, first article, MIDO/QA.** Physical and unchanged.
8. **Continued surveillance.** Feed in-service data back into the graph, so it improves with every approval. This is a compounding moat.

### Steps nobody does today, or does badly
- Step 1 labels: SDR, PQDR and GIDEP data has never been joined to geometry or TDP data at the part level.
- Step 4: delta-to-minimum-test mapping across parts is tacit knowledge locked inside DERs and HEICO.
- Step 5: there is no published validation of any similarity metric against field failures.
- Step 6: there is no accepted "population similarity" means of compliance for parts. DLA Category II only credits the offeror's *own* similar item, not the world's.

## 4. What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)
1. **Build the graph from legally clean data.** Use the public PMA list, FLIS/NSN, Government-rights JEDMICS TDPs, MIL specs/QPLs, MMPDS and NCAMP. Add ML-inferred geometry from reverse-engineered scans that the company makes itself, since every SAR package it produces becomes a node.
2. **Join it to failure labels.** SDRs are public. Getting PQDR and GIDEP access needs a CRADA or OTA with DLA or the Services. Pitch this as DLA getting a supplier-risk model free.
3. **Publish a back-tested similarity-to-risk calibration.** Show the false-negative rate on historical failures. This is the artifact that changes regulator acceptance.
4. **Propose a "Category II-G (graph-similar)" SAR route** to DLA Aviation's engineering support activity. It would credit similarity to *any* approved, flown item with a quantified envelope and a delta-test plan. This is policy, not law, so it is changeable within 12-24 months with a champion (opinion).
5. **NCAMP-style equivalency for mechanical parts.** A reduced test matrix, statistically derived from the cluster, replaces the full qualification. Bayesian test reduction uses the cluster as a prior. The physics clock shrinks but does not vanish.
6. **Liability.** Back each similarity-based approval with warranty or insurance priced from the calibrated model. That lets the model's accuracy show up as money.
7. **FAA path.** Run it through an in-house or partner DER using 21.303 comparative analysis first, with no rule change. Then seek an FAA issue paper or policy statement once the DLA track record exists.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side
- **A. Similarity dossier engine (inside the Approval Factory).** Auto-generates the similarity, envelope and delta-test section of SAR and PMA packages. KPI: engineering hours per approval.
- **B. Reviewer-side triage for DLA and other engineering support activities.** The reviewer queue is the bottleneck (SAR reviews take months to years, recalled). A tool scores each incoming SAR against the graph of previously approved items. It sells the same graph to the regulator. It is direct, and arguably more defensible.
- **C. Field-failure-calibrated part risk model.** SDR + PQDR + AD + GIDEP joined to geometry. It is the evidence-for-evidence layer, sellable to insurers, DLA and MRO buyers.
- **D. Mechanical-parts NCAMP.** A consortium-run shared qualification database, with the startup as operator. Every user's approval adds data, which is a network effect.
- **E. Bayesian test-minimization planner.** Given the cluster prior, it computes the minimum physical test set. It does not beat the physics clock; it cuts how many clocks run.

## 6. Surprises
- Similarity-as-evidence is *already institutionalized*: DLA Category II, QML family qualification, AEC-Q generic data, MMPDS, NCAMP. The new step is crossing from one-supplier or one-material to a population of parts, not inventing the concept.
- DLA Category II credits only the offeror's own similar item. Extending it to "similar to any approved item" is a policy change, not a law change.
- Every OEM and HEICO already holds a private similarity graph. The startup's edge would be the *calibration against field failures* and the *regulator-facing* use, not the graph itself.
