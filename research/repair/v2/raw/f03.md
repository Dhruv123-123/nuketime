## 1. How the statistics work today (evidence plus recalled numbers)

- **B-basis definition**: a 95% lower confidence bound on the 10th percentile (90% of the population exceeds it at 95% confidence); A-basis is the 1st percentile. Required by 14 CFR 25.613 / 23.2260 "design values". Source: NPL report CSSC 0001 (S. Clack), https://eprintspublications.npl.co.uk/10332/1/MAT134.pdf; Acar, Haftka, Kim, Buchi 2009, https://web.mae.ufl.edu/nkim/Papers/conf54.pdf
- **CMH-17 minimum sampling** (NPL Table 1, citing CMH-17): A-basis robust 10 batches / 75 specimens, reduced 5 / 55. B-basis robust 10 / 55, reduced 3 / 18. Different methods (pooled, ANOVA, single-batch) give different B-basis values, so the method has to be stated.
- **Industry practice**: about 30-80 coupons per property per environment for a B-basis value (Acar et al. 2009). Across a full matrix (about 10 properties × 3-5 environments), a qualification runs to many hundreds or thousands of coupons. Composite qualification has commonly been quoted at roughly $1-2M+ and 1-2 years (recalled).
- **Small-sample penalty**: the one-sided normal tolerance factor for B-basis is about 2.35 at n=10, about 1.78 at n=30, about 1.53 at n=100, and 1.28 at n=infinity (recalled, standard tables). At a CV of 6%, moving from n=100 to n=10 lowers B-basis by about 5% of the mean. **The regulations already allow few tests; the cost of few tests is a lower allowable.** Fewer tests are possible today as long as the part has margin to spare.
- **MMPDS (metals)**: computing A/B-basis directly needs about 100 observations from 10 or more heats/lots. "Derived" (indirect) values use ratios to a computed property (recalled, MMPDS Ch. 9 guidelines). S-basis comes from the specification minimum.
- **CMH-17 / FAA equivalency** (DOT/FAA/AR-03/19, Tomblin et al., and FAA PS-ACE100-2002-006): a new user or a process change shows equivalency to an existing shared database (NCAMP) using a reduced subset. It typically uses 1-2 batches and about 8 specimens per key property, a test on the mean and a test on the minimum individual value at alpha about 0.05 per test. Batches and specimens are recalled; the FAA tech-center PDF https://www.tc.faa.gov/its/worldpac/techrpt/ar03-19.pdf returned 503 this session.
- **NCAMP** (Wichita State / NIAR): https://www.wichita.edu/industry_and_defense/NIAR/Research/ncamp.php. It runs a shared, public materials database. The FAA accepts NCAMP values (FAA Memo AIR100-2010-120-003) and EASA accepts them under its own memorandum. The page lists about 22 qualified systems (5 AM, 11 thermoset prepreg, 3 thermoplastic, and others). Its AGATE predecessor claims a **4× reduction in time and 10× in certification cost**. **This is the existing proof that "use a pooled prior and test a small confirmatory subset" works with regulators.** It is a frequentist, institution-run version of a Bayesian prior.

## 3. Key reframing for the Approval Factory

For most legacy-platform replacement parts (C-130/KC-135 brackets, fittings, bushings, valves), the material is a commodity spec (AMS/QQ/MIL, e.g. 7075-T7351, 4340, 17-4PH). **The allowables already exist in MMPDS. Nobody asks for new B-basis values.** The verification burden is instead:
1. Proving the part is the same: dimensions/GD&T, material and condition, process (heat treat, shot peen, plating), surface finish.
2. For critical/flight-safety parts (DLA Critical Safety Items, FAA PMA where test-and-computation is needed): **part-level tests**, mostly fatigue/endurance, proof/static, sometimes environmental, plus First Article Testing.
3. Where the process differs (new forging source, AM substitution, a different casting foundry), **material equivalency** testing.

So the statistical lever is in two places: (a) **part-level comparative tests** (replacement vs OEM sample), where fatigue tests dominate the physics clock at weeks to months per rig; and (b) process-change material equivalency. Both are "equivalence" questions, not "absolute allowable" questions. That helps: an equivalence test with a strong prior from the OEM part and fleet data can need far fewer specimens than a new allowable.

## 4. Could equivalence be proven with far fewer tests? (opinion, reasoned)

Yes, in principle by a large factor, if three things hold:
- **An informative prior that regulators accept.** A hierarchical Bayesian model can pool MMPDS raw data, NCAMP data, OEM/DLA historical test records, and the physics model (ICME microstructure-to-property, FE fatigue). It can then reach the same B-basis or equivalence confidence with something like 3-8 confirmatory specimens instead of 18-55+. The gain is entirely in the prior. Today's rules are frequentist (CMH-17 Vol 1 Ch 8, MMPDS Ch 9) and only implicitly accept priors (NCAMP pooled equivalency, "derived" MMPDS values).
- **Covariates that explain variance.** In-situ process signatures, CT porosity, microstructure (grain size, alpha-case), hardness/conductivity mapping, and residual stress (XRD) can predict strength and fatigue scatter. Once explained, scatter no longer needs averaging out by more specimens. This is the AIM-4AM / Senvol thesis applied to every part.
- **Sequential / adaptive designs.** Run specimens one at a time and stop as soon as the posterior (or SPRT) decides. With good priors, the expected sample size often falls well below a fixed-n design. Accelerated or step-stress fatigue backed by physics models can shorten the clock further.

Binding constraints and what it would take to change each:
- **Physics clock**: fatigue rigs at about 10-30 Hz, 10^6-10^7 cycles, take days to weeks per specimen. Change it with many parallel cheap rigs (a robotic "test farm"), ultrasonic fatigue (20 kHz) where it can be shown equivalent, and step-stress plus a Bayesian life model. You cannot remove the clock, but you can parallelize and shorten it.
- **Law / regulator acceptance**: 25.613 needs "design values" from "enough tests"; how many is set by guidance, not by law. So change needs FAA policy statements and AC updates, CMH-17 statistics working group and MMPDS working group adoption, and ASTM/SAE (F42, AMS-AM) standards. A DLA engineering support activity (ESA) such as AFLCMC can set its own SAR test requirements under MIL-STD/ESA discretion. **That makes it the faster first customer than the FAA.**
- **Liability**: if a Bayesian-qualified part fails, the prior gets attacked. Mitigate with frequentist coverage proofs, i.e. show the Bayesian procedure gives at least 95% coverage in blind retrospective studies, plus a conservative prior weighting (power priors).
- **Data access**: OEM test data and MMPDS raw data are held or licensed (MMPDS is run by Battelle with industry members). DLA and USAF hold decades of test and failure data on legacy platforms. Change this through CRADA/data-rights deals or a consortium "prior bank".

## 5. What nobody does today and would need doing

1. **A validated "prior bank"**: a curated hierarchical database of material and part-level test results (MMPDS raw data, NCAMP, DLA/AFLCMC SAR and FAT test results, OEM data where licensable, AM datasets), with a calibrated Bayesian model behind it.
2. **Blind retrospective calibration study**: rerun hundreds of historical MMPDS/NCAMP/SAR datasets, hiding most specimens, to show the Bayesian small-n procedure meets nominal frequentist coverage. Regulators would need exactly this. Compute is cheap; the data is the hard part.
3. **A draft guidance package**: a proposed CMH-17 / MMPDS chapter or FAA policy-statement draft for "Bayesian equivalency with informative priors", including prior-elicitation rules and conservatism knobs. No one has authored it.
4. **Part-level equivalence statistics** for reverse-engineered parts: a standard way to combine dimensional/CT/microstructure/hardness evidence with a few fatigue tests into one quantified equivalence posterior. Today it is ad hoc and engineer-judgment driven.
5. **An optimal test-plan generator**: choose which specimens and conditions give the most information (Bayesian experimental design). AIM-4AM starts this for AM material; nobody does it for replacement parts.
6. **Parallel autonomous test cells** plus step-stress/ultrasonic fatigue with physics-based acceleration, to shorten the clock that remains.
7. **Machine-checkable submission**: statistics delivered as reproducible code and data that an ESA/DER/FAA reviewer can rerun, which cuts reviewer hours too.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side

- **Equivalence engine as a service** (software plus DER/statistician sign-off): takes OEM part and replacement-part evidence, outputs a regulator-ready substantiation package with Bayesian or frequentist equivalence statistics and an optimized minimal test plan. Sell to DLA SAR applicants, PMA houses (HEICO, Wencor, and others) and MRO; or use it internally as the Approval Factory's core edge, measured in tests per approval.
- **Prior-bank data network**: aggregate test data from many small machine shops and labs that each do SARs. The network effect makes every later package cheaper. This is the moat, not the model.
- **Standards entrepreneurship**: write the CMH-17/MMPDS/ESA guidance and run the calibration study with NIAR/NCAMP or a USAF ESA (e.g. under an AFWERX/DLA SBIR). Whoever writes the accepted method gets a head start that lasts years.
- **Autonomous fatigue test farm** linked to the engine, so tests are chosen adaptively and run around the clock.

## Surprises

- The regulations do not fix a number of tests. Small n is allowed and penalized through tolerance factors, so the Bayesian lever is "keep a high allowable with fewer tests", not "be allowed to test less".
- NCAMP is already a de facto institutional Bayesian prior (shared database plus small equivalency subset), with claimed 4× time and 10× cost reductions. Regulators already accept the pattern; only the formalism is missing.
- The newest US effort (AIM-4AM, June 2026, $2M, Dyndrite/RTX) targets AM material allowables with "probabilistic risk categories". Nobody found targets reverse-engineered replacement-part equivalence.
- For most legacy spare parts, new allowables are not even needed (MMPDS covers the material). The real verification burden is part-level identity and fatigue evidence, which is less standardized and so more open to a new statistical method.
