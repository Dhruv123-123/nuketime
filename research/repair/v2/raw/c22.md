# c22: Which 2023-2026 startups have YC, a16z, Founders Fund, Lux, 8VC, Shield Capital and others funded for "AI for regulated hardware", certification, compliance, qualification, reverse engineering or parts approval?

Method: 3 WebSearch calls, then WebFetch of company and news pages. Several fetches failed (404 or DNS errors). Items marked **(verified)** were confirmed on a page fetched in this session. Items marked **(recalled)** come from memory (cutoff mid-2026); check the figures before using them.

## Bottom line

- **The gap.** No funded startup was found, verified or recalled, that does the Approval Factory loop end to end: AI reverse engineering, then building the technical data package, then winning a DLA Source Approval Request or FAA PMA, then holding the approval and selling the part. The capital has gone to the two ends of that loop and not to its middle:
  - **Upstream tools.** Test data, CT inspection, compliance traceability documents, and photo-to-CAD.
  - **Downstream capacity.** AI-automated machine shops such as Hadrian, which build to print and depend on someone else's approved drawings.
- **The closest approaches are tiny or not venture-backed:**
  - Xcert AI raised CHF 1.1M for certification workflow (verified).
  - Novineer does photo-to-CAD reverse engineering and shows no funding (verified).
  - AV8 Group and similar firms are services firms doing PMA reverse engineering (from search).
  - HEICO and AST Korea are incumbents. AST received a Korean PMA covering 6,395 items in July 2026 (from search).
- **The investor pattern.** Founders Fund and Lux have bet heavily on the hardware stack around verification: Nominal for test data and Lumafield for CT inspection (Lux is in it). They have also bet on Hadrian for production. None of them has a "we own the approval" company in the portfolio, as far as found.

## Investor-by-investor view
- **Founders Fund:** Nominal ($80M lead, Mar 2026, verified), Hadrian (recalled lead), Gecko and Freeform (recalled). Pattern: the factory plus the test-data layer. No approval-holding company.
- **Lux:** Lumafield (verified as an investor), Hadrian (recalled). Pattern: inspection and production.
- **a16z:** Ketryx (recalled lead of Series A), Diode (recalled), plus the American Dynamism portfolio. Pattern: regulated software assurance.
- **YC:** Saphira (S24, verified), Delve (W24), GovDash, Diode. Pattern: compliance software. No YC PMA or SAR house was found.
- **8VC and Shield Capital:** no relevant company verified in this window. Both lean defense, security and software (opinion, low confidence).
- **Other:** Spicehaus (Xcert), IVP/G2VP (Lumafield C), Blue Bear (Nuclearn, recalled).

## Gaps nobody funded fills (opinion, built on the evidence above)
1. **Owning the approval as an asset.** Nobody funded holds PMA or SAR approvals as an AI-native company. The incumbents are HEICO, Wencor (owned by Boeing), AST Korea and PMA consultancies.
2. **Materials and process equivalence evidence.** Reverse engineering tools produce geometry. Nobody automates proving that the material, heat treatment and process are equivalent: running the test plan, then the statistical comparison against the original part, then the DER-ready report.
3. **Reviewer-side tooling.** Nothing found sells to DLA, FAA engineering staff or service engineering support activities (ESAs) to triage and pre-check Source Approval Requests and PMA data. Navy and Army ESA reviews take at least 180 days (DLA page, from search). The reviewer queue is the clock.
4. **A replay ledger of past approvals.** No product mines decades of approved SARs, PMAs and DER 8110-3 forms to predict what a reviewer will accept.
5. **Delegated authority held by an AI-native firm.** No startup holds DER/ODA delegation and uses it to make findings of compliance at machine speed.
