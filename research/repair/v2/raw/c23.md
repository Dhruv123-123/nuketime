# c23: Electronic and PCB reverse engineering firms (TechInsights, ChipRebel, Grayscale?, Nanoscope?, Sandia/NSWC Crane RE, board-level FFF redesign houses): capability and AI use

Budget used: 3 WebSearch, about 14 WebFetch. Several .mil/.gov endpoints returned 403 or 503 (NSWC Crane, DMEA, SBIR.gov API, USPTO PDF), so the government-lab side relies partly on recalled knowledge, marked (recalled).

## 1. Who does electronic RE today: four separate worlds

### A. Chip-level RE for IP and competitive intelligence (commercial; not sustainment)
- **TechInsights** (Ottawa, founded 1989; merged with Chipworks 2016). Site claims 100K+ chip teardowns, 50K+ analyses, 1.7M+ images/reports, 650+ customers. https://www.techinsights.com/about-techinsights . Its government page sells teardowns of *current* chips, supply-chain and geopolitical intelligence, export-control assessments, and AI accelerator and chiplet analysis. It says **nothing about obsolescence, sustainment, counterfeit detection or FFF redesign**. https://www.techinsights.com/government . Ownership: Oakley Capital took a majority stake (Fund III, about $49.9M equity, date not confirmed; recalled about 2017), plus later roll-ups (VLSI Research Aug 2021, IC Knowledge about 2022, "fifth acquisition in two years"). https://obj.ca/techinsights-lands-fifth-acquisition-in-two-years-amid-surging-demand/ . The site makes no public claim of AI in the RE workflow itself. Internally, recalled: image stitching and automated layout and circuit extraction tools (the old Chipworks "ICWorks").
- **ChipRebel** (small; Germany, recalled). Die imaging of smartphone and wearable SoCs for patent Evidence of Use, competitive intelligence and failure analysis/DPA. No scale figures. https://chiprebel.com/
- "Grayscale" and "Nanoscope" (named in the question): I could not verify either as an electronics RE firm and did not spend the search budget on them. Treat them as unverified.
- The economics in this world are patent litigation and competitive intelligence. The deliverable is evidence, not a replacement part, and these firms never take on qualification or airworthiness.

### B. Government RE and trust labs (capability-rich, throughput-poor)
- **DMEA (Defense Microelectronics Activity, Sacramento).** (recalled) It runs the ATSP (Advanced Technology Support Program, ATSP4 IDIQ with a multi-billion ceiling) to re-create obsolete microcircuits through emulation, redesign and its own small foundry. It ran the older GEM (Generalized Emulation of Microcircuits) program with Sarnoff/SRI. It also runs the Trusted Supplier accreditation program. Site returned 503 (https://www.acq.osd.mil/asds/dmea/).
- **DMEA is already funding AI-ish PCB RE.** STTR Phase I HQ072724P0002 went to **Terraverum LLC (Austin, TX)**, $98,576, 1 Feb to 30 Apr 2024, "REvision: multimodal reverse engineering and device characterization", with the University of Florida as research partner. The scope is automated optical PCBA vision to produce a BOM, plus X-ray CT netlist extraction after layer-wise dewarping. Phase II/III would auto-generate netlists and **Gerbers**. https://www.highergov.com/contract/HQ072724P0002/ . This is the closest thing to a direct competitor on the capture-to-design-files step, but it is tiny (Phase I).
- **AFRL/AFMC SBIR FA857120C0023** (Air Force Materiel Command; the FA8571 office code is Robins AFB/WR-ALC, recalled). This contract produced a patent on *nondestructive CT reverse engineering of PCB inner layers*, motivated because "older methods destroy boards that may be one of the few remaining PCBs for the legacy system" and "surface probing only yields connectivity data, which does not allow reproduction ... meeting form, fit, and function." USPTO doc 12450794 (PDF returned 403; content from search snippet).
- **NSWC Crane.** (recalled) The Navy's center for trusted microelectronics, DMSMS (obsolescence) and counterfeit detection. It has an RE and failure-analysis lab and anchors the "Microelectronics Commons"/SCALE workforce efforts with Purdue. Site returned 403.
- **Sandia** (recalled). Trusted foundry (MESA), IC RE and hardware-assurance research, mostly for nuclear weapons. It does not sell to sustainment customers.
- **Air Logistics Complexes** (recalled). Warner Robins (C-130 and avionics circuit cards), Oklahoma City (KC-135) and Ogden (Hill AFB, REACT, the Reverse Engineering and Critical Tooling lab) run organic RE cells for circuit cards. The Air Force **Rapid Sustainment Office** funds RE and additive work. These cells are the government's in-house answer, and they are capacity-constrained.
- **IARPA RAVEN** (recalled, about 2016-2020). Automated IC imaging and layout extraction at advanced nodes. It shows the government has paid for "automated chip RE" before, but for intelligence, not sustainment.

### C. Board-level legacy test and RE tooling (old, deterministic, no ML)
- **Seica** flying-probe RE (Haverhill MA / Germany). A golden board is clamped and imaged. An operator clicks test points and identifies GND by hand. Fnode impedance signatures plus continuity checks learn the netlist. The parts list is entered **by hand**, then exported as EDIF schematics. Limitations: it cannot tell resistors under about 5 ohms, low-value inductors or normally-closed relays from shorts. https://militaryembedded.com/comms/test/reverse-engineering-board-test (2009, vendor-authored).
- (recalled) **Huntron** (analog signature analysis), **Diagnosys** (PinPoint, legacy board test for military depots) and **ABI Electronics** (BoardMaster). They sell to depots for troubleshooting legacy boards without documentation. None of these is AI-native.
- Board RE service shops (recalled, mostly small; for example GDCA for legacy embedded boards, and many Chinese "PCB clone" houses such as MOKO and ALLPCB that advertise RE). They clone boards for industrial customers but provide **no qualification evidence**. https://www.mokotechnology.com/what-is-the-process-of-pcb-reverse-engineering/
- **Die and IC continuation:** **Rochester Electronics** claims 15B+ devices, 12B+ die, 200K+ part numbers, authorization from 70+ OEMs, and 20,000+ device types manufactured under licence (capability for 70,000+). https://www.rocelec.com/about . (recalled) **Innovasic** and **Lansdale** re-create obsolete ICs (Innovasic uses "MILES" behavioural capture plus FPGA/ASIC re-implementation). This is the IC-level "approval by licence" model: Rochester's moat is OEM *authorization* (permission), not technique.

### D. AI-native inspection and imaging adjacent players (verification-flavoured, not RE)
- **Cybord** (Israel). AI on pick-and-place images of every component. It enforces the approved vendor list and BOM, flags counterfeits, builds component genealogy from date and lot codes, and finds single-source risk. Logos include Cisco, NVIDIA, Intel, SpaceX, Siemens and Flex; defense and aerospace are listed as markets. Funding: "$7M extended Series A". https://www.cybord.ai/
- **Lumafield.** Industrial CT (Neptune/Saturn/Triton/Mars, inline at up to 5 parts/s) plus "Voyager" AI analysis and "Quality Agent". The USMC logo appears among customers. Funding (recalled): about $85M+ total, including a 2024 Series B. https://www.lumafield.com/ . It has the CT front end but no RE or design-file reconstruction product.
- **Academic stack** (open methods that are ready to productize): UF (Forte, Tehranipoor; FICS) has CT-annotation-to-KiCad reconstruction (Sobolewski, Koblah, Forte, ISQED 2025; validated on only 3 layers of one board, with no accuracy metrics) https://isqed.org/English/Archives/2025/Technical_Sessions/51.html , automated via detection (ISTFA 2020), and multimodal X-ray plus optical netlist extraction (SPIE 13152, 2024) https://ebooks.spiedigitallibrary.org/conference-proceedings-of-spie/13152/131520Q/ . UT Dallas/TAMU (Makris) has MTBoM, which extracts BOMs from metal layers across 10 PCBs. https://personal.utdallas.edu/~yiorgos.makris/papers/dcas22a.pdf

## 2. Electronic FFF approval pathway (circuit card / LRU on legacy aircraft)

| # | Step | Who today | Time/cost (mostly recalled) | Done badly / not at all |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Obtain golden units (often 1-3 survivors) | Depot or program office | weeks to months | Destructive RE consumes the scarce exemplar; CT approaches exist only as research and SBIR work |
| 2 | Physical capture (optical, CT, delayering) | Depot RE cells, small shops, labs | days to weeks per board | Multilayer and BGA boards need CT; dewarping and segmentation are still manual |
| 3 | Netlist + BOM extraction | Flying probe (Seica) + manual | 1-4 weeks of engineering per complex board | BOM is still entered by hand; part marking and ID is manual; low-impedance ambiguities |
| 4 | Component identification + obsolescence mapping | Engineers + SiliconExpert/IHS/Rochester lookup | days | Not automated end-to-end from image to part to lifecycle to alternate |
| 5 | Behaviour capture (firmware, PAL/GAL/PROM fuse maps, analog tolerances, timing) | Specialists; Innovasic-style behavioural capture | weeks to months | **The hardest and least done step.** Read-protected MCUs and PLDs; legal grey zone (DMCA 1201, data rights) |
| 6 | Redesign (FFF) around obsolete parts: FPGA emulation, new layout | Board design houses, OEMs, ALCs | months; $100k to $1M+ per card (recalled) | Each one is bespoke; no reusable library of "emulated legacy function blocks" |
| 7 | Equivalence verification (does the new board behave identically, across temperature, voltage, ageing and edge cases?) | Engineers + legacy ATE | weeks to months | **Usually done with a handful of hand-written tests**; nobody "proves" equivalence |
| 8 | Test program set (TPS) for depot ATE (CASS, VDATS, etc.) | OEM or TPS houses | TPS development often $100k to $1M+ per card (recalled) | The TPS is itself a sole-source asset; rehosting is a major DoD cost line |
| 9 | Environmental + EMI qualification (MIL-STD-810, MIL-STD-461, DO-160 for airborne; ESS/HALT) | Test labs | physics clock: 2-12 weeks + lab queue | Cannot be skipped. Can be *reduced* by similarity arguments |
| 10 | Design assurance (DO-254 for airborne complex hardware; DO-178C if firmware) | DERs, the OEM | months; often the deal-killer for civil PMA of electronics | Very few PMA electronics because of DO-254 and the cost of a firmware data package |
| 11 | TDP / SAR package + First Article Test | Contractor, then DLA/ESA engineering review | DLA SAR review 6-18 months (recalled) | Reviewer hours are the bottleneck; packages are inconsistent |
| 12 | Production + counterfeit control (AS5553, AS6081, DFARS 252.246-7007/7008) | CM + test lab | ongoing | Evidence is paper-based; Cybord-type AI evidence is new |

## 3. Binding constraints and what it would take to move them
- **Physics.** Thermal cycling, vibration and EMI chambers must physically run. Lever: a pre-approved *similarity and equivalence* argument (new board shown behaviour-equivalent plus identical mechanical and thermal envelope) to cut the test matrix, and parallel cheap chamber capacity. Regulator acceptance of "equivalence by exhaustive differential test" would need a published DLA/AF engineering-authority policy or an MMPDS-like consensus standard (for example, an SAE G-19/G-21-style committee for "electronic FFF equivalence evidence").
- **Law.** DMCA 1201 covers firmware read-out from protected devices, and DFARS 252.227-7013/7014 data rights cover OEM-asserted limited-rights data. Lever: the government owns or has purpose rights to much legacy data. RE from hardware the government owns, for government sustainment, has precedent (recalled; DLA's competitive RE program). An Air Force or DLA "reverse engineering authorization" letter per NSN is the permission artifact.
- **Regulator acceptance.** For military work, the program engineering authority (ESA) signs. For civil airborne, the FAA plus DO-254 apply. DO-254 is the reason civil electronic PMAs are rare. Evidence tooling that auto-generates DO-254-style artifacts from an RE'd design would be novel.
- **Liability.** Whoever signs equivalence owns the failure. An insurance-backed equivalence certificate is a possible product.
- **Data access.** Golden units, depot failure data and ATE logs sit with the government. A CRADA with an ALC or NSWC Crane is the access path.

## 4. Verification-side AI plays (opinion)
1. **"Behavioural equivalence engine" for legacy electronics.** Instrument the golden board (in a bench harness or in-system) to record I/O traces across operating modes, temperature and voltage. Learn a behavioural model (for digital parts, effectively automata and protocol learning; for analog parts, transfer functions). Auto-generate coverage-maximizing test vectors, run the old and new boards side by side under differential testing, and emit a signed equivalence report with coverage metrics. Nobody sells this; today step 7 is hand-written tests. It is the electronic analogue of "reviewer hours per approval".
2. **Automatic TPS generation and rehost.** The same behavioural model writes the ATE test program (CASS/VDATS/eTPS). TPS cost is a large, measurable, sole-source-locked DoD line item, so this play monetizes even without making the part.
3. **Image to netlist to BOM to obsolescence to alternates, fully automated** (CT plus optical, and AI marking-read with lifecycle lookup). Terraverum (DMEA STTR) and UF are the only visible efforts, both at research scale. This is the "knowledge" step, which is getting cheap and is not the moat alone.
4. **Firmware and PLD fuse-map recovery plus re-implementation verification.** Formally prove an FPGA re-implementation equals the recovered logic (equivalence checking à la Cadence Conformal, but against RE-recovered netlists).
5. **Evidence-package compiler.** Assemble the TDP/SAR or DO-254 artifacts (requirements traced to tests and results) automatically from the RE and verification runs. This sells reviewer-hour reduction to DLA and ESAs themselves.
6. **AI component provenance on every built unit** (Cybord-style) as the production-side verification that closes the counterfeit loop (DFARS 7007/7008).

## 5. Surprises
- TechInsights, the biggest RE house, has a government offering entirely about *current* chips and geopolitics, with no sustainment, obsolescence or FFF work. The biggest RE capability in the world is aimed at evidence, not parts.
- The state of the art in board RE tooling (Seica flying-probe RE) still has the BOM typed in by hand.
- DMEA paid only about $98.6K for the one visible AI multimodal PCB RE STTR (2024). The government signal exists, but the money is small.
- Academic CT-to-KiCad reconstruction (2025) was validated on 3 layers of one board, with no accuracy metrics. The field is very immature.
- Rochester's IC-continuation moat is 70+ OEM authorizations, which is permission, not technique. This is consistent with the prior thesis.
