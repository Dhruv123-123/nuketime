# s03: Step: geometry capture and drawing reconstruction (CT, structured light, CMM, GD&T inference). State of the art, accuracy, time, what AI could automate fully.

Evidence tags: [web] = fetched or searched this session; (recalled) = from memory, not verified this session; [opinion] = analysis.
Search budget used: 3 of 3 WebSearch calls, plus 8 WebFetch calls (2 returned 404).

## 1. What this step has to produce

A reverse-engineered approval package (DLA SAR/TDP, FAA PMA by test and computation, Army/Navy ESA) needs much more than a 3D shape. It needs:
1. Nominal geometry: design intent, not the as-worn or as-built shape of one sample.
2. Tolerances and GD&T scheme: datums, feature control frames, fits.
3. Material, temper, heat treat, coatings, surface finish, and process specs (shot peen, plating, NDT).
4. Inspection and acceptance criteria.
5. Drawings in an acceptable format: ASME Y14.5 / Y14.100, MIL-STD-31000 TDP, or model-based definition per ASME Y14.41.

Capture covers item 1 well, items 2 to 4 poorly, and item 5 only as manual drafting labour.

## 2. Capture modalities: state of the art (most figures recalled)

| Modality | Typical accuracy | Time per part | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tactile CMM (ZEISS PRISMO/CONTURA, Hexagon Global) | MPE about 1-2 um + L/300 (recalled) | hours to program, minutes to hours to run | external features only, sparse points, needs a programmer |
| Blue-light structured light (ZEISS GOM ATOS 5 / Q, Creaform, Polyga) | about 5-20 um at small field of view (recalled) | 30 min to a few hours, including spray and alignment | line of sight only; shiny or dark parts need spray |
| Laser arm / tracker (Hexagon Absolute Arm, FARO) | about 20-50 um (recalled) | hours | external surfaces; big parts |

## 3. Mesh to CAD to drawing: state of the art

- Commercial reverse-engineering software: Geomagic Design X (wizard-driven recognition of planes, cylinders, holes and blends) [web, Develop3D]. 3D Systems sold the Geomagic line to Hexagon for about $123M, announced late 2024 (recalled). Others: PolyWorks|Modeler (InnovMetric), Creaform VXmodel, ZEISS Reverse Engineering, Volume Graphics VGSTUDIO MAX (Hexagon; CT surface determination and CAD compare), Artec Studio, which added AI "Workflows" for automatic fusion [web].
- Backflip AI: CEO and co-founder Greg Mark, founder of Markforged (recalled). About $30M in venture funding [web]. It offers a generative scan-to-parametric-CAD model that infers the sequence of CAD operations (sketch, extrude, fillet) and outputs an editable feature tree. It launched as an Onshape add-on (MMS, July 28, 2025) [web], has a Fusion 360 plugin [web], and its Head of AI is Rachel Zucker [web]. It claims minutes against hours of manual work. No accuracy benchmarks are published; customers use it for "hard-to-find replacement parts"; no aerospace or defense customers are named; no GD&T or drawing output is mentioned. https://mmsonline.com/articles/ai-creates-cad-files-from-scan-data ; https://develop3d.com/cad/exploring-ais-role-in-scan-to-cad/ [web]
- Lumafield: industrial CT plus AI cloud software (Voyager, Quality Agent). $75M Series C in March 2025, led by IVP with G2VP and Wellington plus existing investors Spark, DCVC, Kleiner, Lux and Matter [web]. $50M SVB debt facility in Nov 2025 [web]. CB Insights reports $192.5M total raised [web]. The website lists "Reverse Engineering" as an application without describing features, and shows a Marine Corps logo [web]. https://www.lumafield.com/ ; https://www.lw.com/en/news/2025/03/latham-watkins-represents-lumafield-in-us75-million-series-c-funding-round
- GD&T tools: Sigmetrix GD&T Advisor (PTC Creo only) "recommends the most appropriate control for selected features" and validates annotations. The designer still authors every annotation; the vendor claims annotation is about 4x faster. It does NOT auto-generate tolerance schemes [web]. https://www.sigmetrix.com/software/gdt-advisor . Siemens NX has a PMI/GD&T wizard (recalled). Academic work: TTRS (Clément), CLIC (Anselmetti) automated tolerance specification (recalled). Nothing infers a complete functional GD&T scheme from a scan.
- Drawing readers, the opposite direction: Werk24, CADDi Drawer (recalled) extract data from existing drawings. Text-to-CAD: Zoo.dev (recalled). Neither generates certified drawings from scans.
- Industry view (Develop3D, Feb 2026): AI fuses scans, segments surfaces, fits features and proposes CAD, but "engineers still refine". Output is "only as reliable as its input". Design-intent versus as-scanned is the core ambiguity; Backflip calls reconstruction "combinatorially hard" [web]. Jaydu (aerospace tooling article): engineers re-apply tolerances by function because a scan cannot reveal which surfaces are critical [web via search].
- Government practitioners: the RSO, the OC-ALC reverse engineering branch (dozens of B-1 parts re-blueprinted), Warner Robins 402nd EMXG REARM (avionics), Mission Support Inc. (B-52 bypass ducts via CAD/CAM) [web]. ## 4. Time and cost breakdown of this step (recalled or estimated; opinion where noted)

- Capture (structured light plus CMM on key features): 0.5-2 days per part. CT: hours, plus queue time at a scarce high-energy machine.
- Mesh to CAD: prismatic parts 4-20 h; freeform parts (blades, ducts, castings) 20-100+ h.
- Nominal and tolerance inference, GD&T scheme, drawing drafting: 20-80 h per part number. Senior engineer judgement is the bottleneck.
- Materials and process ID: 1-4 weeks elapsed, including lab queue.
- Estimate [opinion]: geometry plus drawing is about 15-30% of the 300-1,000 engineering hours in a full package. The rest goes to materials and process, the substantiation and equivalence argument, testing, and reviewer back-and-forth.

## 5. The hard core: what cannot be seen in one sample

1. Nominal versus as-built or as-worn: one sample gives one realization. Recovering intent means snapping to standard sizes (fractional inch, standard drill and thread sizes, era-specific drafting conventions such as MIL-STD-100 or AN/MS/NAS hardware) and to manufacturing-process signatures.
2. Tolerances: these are a design decision, not a measurable property. Today they come from (a) measuring N samples and building a distribution, often several units from different lots (the FAA PMA test-and-computation approach, Order 8110.42, recalled); (b) mating-part fit analysis; (c) process capability; or (d) engineering judgement.
3. Functional criticality: which surfaces are critical is unknowable from geometry alone. It requires assembly context: mating parts, load paths, failure history.
4. Material and process: composition is recoverable; exact heat treat, peening intensity, coating process and NDT acceptance are only partly inferable.
5. Measurement uncertainty: CT surface determination on dense alloys can carry tens-of-microns uncertainty. Regulators need a per-feature uncertainty budget (ISO 15530-3, VDI/VDE 2630-2.1, recalled), and today that is rarely produced automatically.

## 6. What AI could fully automate (assume intelligence and compute are abundant) [opinion, grounded in the above]

Fully automatable (software only, no new physics):
- Scan planning (pose, CT parameters, energy and filtration to minimise artefacts), multi-modal fusion (CT plus structured light plus CMM touch points), and artefact correction.
- Segmentation, feature recognition, parametric feature-tree reconstruction (Backflip-type).
- Design-intent snapping: a prior over standard sizes, era, OEM drafting habits, and standard hardware catalogs (AN/MS/NAS). Many legacy DoD drawings already exist in JEDMICS/cFolders and could serve as training data if access is granted.
- Multi-sample statistics: register N worn parts, separate wear from manufacturing variation, estimate nominal and a statistically justified tolerance band.
- GD&T scheme synthesis: from assembly context (scan the mating parts too), function (load path or seal), process capability databases, and ASME Y14.5 rule checking, generate a datum scheme and feature control frames. Then run tolerance stack-up (CETOL-like) to confirm fit.
- Drawing and MBD generation to MIL-STD-31000 / Y14.41 / Y14.100, including notes, the spec call-out table and an inspection plan (QIF output).
- A per-feature measurement uncertainty budget and conformance report generated automatically.
- Drafting the "equivalence argument" text that links every drawing value to its evidence: measured statistic, standard, or mating-fit computation.

Still bound by physics clocks: CT and CMM time (hours per part; parallelisable by buying machines); materials lab tests (days); obtaining N physical samples (logistics, often the true bottleneck for orphaned parts, since the depot may have only 1-2 units).

Bound by acceptance: whether DLA engineering support activities (ESAs) and FAA ACOs/DERs accept AI-inferred tolerances. Today there is no qualified method or standard for "computed design intent".

## 7. Who does what, and gaps

- Capture hardware is crowded and mature: ZEISS, Hexagon, Waygate, Nikon, NSI, Werth, Lumafield, Creaform, Artec.
- Mesh to CAD is being automated: Backflip ($30M), plus incumbent AI features (Artec, Geomagic/Hexagon).
- Gaps nobody fills well:
  (a) GD&T and tolerance inference with stated justification;
  (b) multi-sample nominal and tolerance recovery;
  (c) automatic uncertainty budgets tied to each drawing value;
  (d) TDP-format output (MIL-STD-31000) with an evidence trail;
  (e) a public benchmark of scan-to-drawing accuracy against ground-truth OEM drawings.
  No company was found that sells "scan to approvable TDP".

## 8. Verification-side AI plays [opinion]

1. Reconstruction-to-evidence engine: a pipeline whose product is not CAD but a traceable evidence graph. Every dimension and tolerance on the drawing links to measurements, uncertainty and the rationale used (sample statistics, standard size, mating fit). An ESA or DER reviews the graph, not raw files. KPI: reviewer hours per package.
2. Qualify the method, not each part: run a validation campaign on parts where government-owned drawings exist (JEDMICS). Blind-reconstruct hundreds of parts, publish error statistics, and seek DLA/AFMC (and eventually FAA) acceptance of the method itself, analogous to how CT metrology was qualified via VDI/VDE 2630 and how gages are qualified by MSA/Gage R&R. After that, per-part review shrinks to exceptions. This is the regulator-acceptance lever.
3. Fleet geometry library: scan every unit passing through depots (OC-ALC, WR-ALC, Tinker, Hill) and build wear and variation distributions per NSN. This solves the "N samples" problem and creates a data moat. It needs a depot CRADA.
4. Automated tolerance justification using mating-part scans and stack-up simulation, so tolerances are computed from fit and function rather than guessed.
5. An independent verification service for others' reverse-engineered TDPs (DLA receives SARs from many small vendors): AI that checks a submitted TDP against scans of the government's own sample. This is a reviewer-side product that DLA could buy.

## 9. What would have to change for binding constraints

- Regulator acceptance: a published validation dataset and a consensus standard for AI-inferred design intent (an ASME Y14 or SAE G-33/AS9100 working item; MIL-STD-31000 appendix). Route: DLA R&D / AFRL / RSO SBIR Phase II plus a NIST MBE testbed.
- Data access: government TDP repositories (JEDMICS, ETIMS, cFolders) for training and validation, which requires DoD data agreements and ITAR handling (an IL5 / CMMC L2 environment).
- Physics: CT capacity is buyable; high-energy linac CT for dense parts is scarce (Waygate, Lumafield Saturn). Samples are the real limiter; fleet scanning at depots fixes it.
- Liability: the approval holder (the Approval Factory) carries design responsibility. The verification layer must state uncertainty quantitatively so the holder can underwrite it.
