## 1. Standards bodies do not hold permission themselves. A standard binds only when someone with authority points at it.

SAE, ASTM, IEC, ASME, RTCA and NFPA write documents. They grant no approvals. A standard binds through one of seven routes, and each route has a different gatekeeper:

| # | Route | Mechanism | Gatekeeper | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Incorporation by reference (IBR) into regulation | 1 CFR Part 51. The agency must show the material is "reasonably available", a requirement tightened in the 2014 revision (recalled, 79 FR 66267) | Agency plus the Office of the Federal Register | NRC 10 CFR 50.55a takes in ASME BPVC Sections III and XI; OSHA takes in NFPA and ANSI standards |
| 2 | Accepted Means of Compliance (MoC) under a performance-based rule | The rule states the safety goal. The FAA accepts consensus standards through a Notice of Availability or an Advisory Circular | FAA (AIR) | Part 23 Amendment 64 (final rule Dec 2016, effective Aug 30 2017) let ASTM F44 standards serve as MoC (recalled). AC 20-115D recognizes RTCA DO-178C. AC 20-174 recognizes SAE ARP4754A (recalled) |
| 3 | EU harmonised standard | Citation in the OJEU gives a "presumption of conformity" under the New Legislative Framework | European Commission plus CEN/CENELEC/ETSI | Machinery Regulation 2023/1230 (applies Jan 2027); Cyber Resilience Act 2024/2847 (main obligations Dec 2027); AI Act standards via CEN-CENELEC JTC 21 (recalled) |
| 4 | Model code adopted into state or local law | An SDO publishes a model code and jurisdictions adopt it | States, cities, AHJs | NFPA 70 (the NEC); ASME boiler code enforced by state boiler inspectors commissioned by the National Board |
| 5 | Federal procurement preference | NTTAA (PL 104-113, signed Mar 7 1996, fetched) plus OMB A-119 (revised 2016, recalled) direct agencies to use voluntary consensus standards over government-unique ones. DoD adopts non-government standards through ASSIST / DoDM 4120.24 (recalled) | Agencies, DLA, program offices | AMS material specs called out on drawings in place of MIL specs |
| 6 | Contract flowdown by primes | Primes put the standard or accreditation into purchase orders | OEMs and primes | Nadcap (24 process accreditations, 4,500+ accredited suppliers, 60+ subscribing OEMs, founded 1990, fetched p-r-i.org); AS9100 through IAQG OASIS |
| 7 | Liability, insurance and market access | Tort "standard of care", insurer requirements, listing marks | Courts, insurers, retailers, OSHA NRTL program (29 CFR 1910.7) | UL listing; OSHA-recognized NRTL status (fetched: UL is an OSHA-approved NRTL) |

Key point: the money and the permission sit with whoever holds the pointer (FAA, NRC, OJEU, the prime's PO) and with whoever runs the conformity assessment the pointer requires. They do not sit with the document.

## 2. Who earns, and how much

- **SDOs**: income comes from selling documents, memberships and training. They are mid-sized nonprofits. ASTM's publication revenue is about $100M/yr and SAE's total revenue is $100-200M (both recalled, from Form 990s). That income is under legal pressure:
  - *ASTM v. Public.Resource.Org* (fetched): in 2022 the district court allowed free reproduction of 184 incorporated standards as fair use, and the D.C. Circuit affirmed in 2023.
  - *Georgia v. PRO* (2020, 5-4): annotated state law cannot be copyrighted.
  - EU: CJEU C-588/21 P *Public.Resource.Org & Right to Know v Commission* (Mar 5 2024) held that harmonised standards must be accessible because they are part of EU law (recalled). *James Elliott* C-613/14 (2016) had already said so (recalled).
  - Trend: once a standard is mandatory, the text heads toward being free. SDO paywalls are eroding, so their bargaining position weakens.
- **Conformity assessment (where the money is)**:
  - UL Solutions: 2023 revenue $2.68B, net income $276M, IPO April 2024 at about $7B valuation, still controlled by UL's nonprofit parent (fetched).
  - SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, TÜV SÜD, DEKRA each have revenue in the multiple billions of $ (recalled).
  - Element Materials Technology, an aerospace and materials test lab, was bought by Temasek in 2022 for roughly $7B EV (recalled).
- **Accreditation administrators**: PRI, an SAE-affiliated nonprofit, runs Nadcap. Its revenue is tens of $M per year from audit fees (recalled). Suppliers pay for repeat audits on each process.
- **Data custodians**:
  - MMPDS metallic allowables: Battelle runs it for the FAA and sells subscriptions (recalled).
  - CMH-17 composites handbook: run out of Wichita State NIAR and sold through SAE (recalled).
  - NCAMP: shared composite qualification data. The FAA (memo AIR100-2010-120-003) and EASA (CM-S-004) accept its values. It claims certification time cut by 4x and cost by 10x under AGATE (fetched).
  - Ansys Granta MI resells these datasets (recalled).
- **Individuals with delegated signatures**: DERs and ODA unit members (FAA), Nadcap auditors, Authorized Nuclear Inspectors (ASME), TÜV functional-safety assessors.
- **Committee seats**: the hidden moat. Primes and incumbents staff SAE AMS, ARP, AS and ASTM committees, so they write the tests their competitors must pass. A small firm can win a seat because consensus rules require balanced voting interests.

## 3. Open-source hardware as a verification authority

- OSHWA certification (since 2016) only certifies licence and documentation openness. It says nothing about safety or performance.
- CERN OHL v2 (2020) is a licence.
- DIN SPEC 3105-1/-2 (2020) is the one open-hardware standard that defines community peer review ("attestation") of documentation, run through the Open Hardware Observatory (recalled). Again, it covers documentation, not airworthiness.
- Conclusion: no open-source-hardware body acts as a verification authority for safety-critical parts. The closest working model of "open verification" is shared, regulator-accepted qualification data: NCAMP, MMPDS, and America Makes / ASTM F42 / SAE AMS-AM data for additive manufacturing.
- Verification can be shared because the FAA accepts NCAMP data. The **design** stays proprietary, but the **evidence** is pooled. This is the template that matters for the Approval Factory.

## 4. Verification pathway for a reverse-engineered or orphaned part, from the standards angle, and what is missing

| Step | What standard / authority | Who does it today | Undone or broken |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Find the governing specs on the original drawing (AMS, MIL, AS, proprietary process specs) | SAE AMS, MIL-STD via ASSIST | Engineers reading PDFs by hand | Specs are prose PDFs that machines cannot read. Superseded or cancelled MIL specs need cross-referencing by hand (e.g., MIL to AMS conversions) |
| B. Prove material equivalence | AMS material spec plus MMPDS/CMH-17 allowables | OEM or PMA applicant, test labs | No shared equivalence database exists for legacy alloys and heat-treat lots. Every applicant reruns coupons (ASTM E8/E466/E399). There is no NCAMP for metals in reverse engineering |
| C. Prove process equivalence (heat treat, NDT, plating, welding) | Nadcap checklists (AC7xxx), AMS 2750 (pyrometry) etc. | Nadcap-accredited suppliers | Audits happen once a year and run on paper and checklists. Process data that could continuously show conformance goes unused |
| D. Prove dimensional and functional equivalence | ASME Y14.5 GD&T, test methods | CMM labs, DER test plans | Each test plan is written from scratch. No standard method exists for "equivalence by reverse engineering with statistical confidence" |
| E. Software / avionics content | DO-178C/DO-254 (RTCA), ARP4754A/B, DO-330 tool qualification | Avionics houses, DERs | Any AI tool that produces evidence must be qualified under DO-330 or have its outputs independently verified. No qualified AI tool exists today (recalled) |
| F. Compliance finding and sign-off | 14 CFR 21.303 (PMA), DLA SAR, AC 20-xx | DER / ODA / FAA ACO, DLA engineering support activity | Reviewer hours are the bottleneck. Packages arrive as unstructured documents |
| G. Continued airworthiness / surveillance | AS9100, Nadcap, FAA MIDO | Registrars, auditors | Surveillance is periodic and sampled, not continuous |

## 5. What would need to be done (assume plentiful intelligence and compute)

1. **An NCAMP for reverse engineering.** Set up a consortium or company-owned qualification database for legacy alloys, forgings and processes on the C-130, KC-135, B-52 and H-60 fleets. Then get an FAA memo, plus DLA and Air Force engineering-authority acceptance, that lets applicants cite pooled allowables and equivalence data instead of rerunning coupons. NCAMP shows regulators already accept pooled data, with claimed savings of 10x cost and 4x time. Physics clock: coupons still have to be tested once, but only once for the whole industry.
2. **Write the method as a consensus standard and get it accepted as MoC.** For example: "Standard Practice for Establishing Equivalence of Reverse-Engineered Metallic Parts using Digital Inspection and Statistical Allowables", written in ASTM (F42, E07 or E08) or SAE (AMS or the AS committees). Then press for FAA acceptance through an AC or policy memo and DLA acceptance in SAR guidance. Precedent: Part 23 / ASTM F44, where industry wrote the MoC and the FAA accepted it. Whoever writes the method sets the bar their competitors must clear.
3. **Machine-readable standards.** Compile AMS, MIL, AC7xxx and ASTM documents into executable requirement graphs (requirement, then test, then acceptance criterion). ISO/IEC "SMART" standards and the IEC "Standards Machine-Readable" work are moving this way (recalled; the iso.org page returned 403). The SDOs cannot do it fast because paywalls are their business model. Since PRO v. ASTM and the CJEU Malamud ruling, licensing becomes less of a barrier for mandatory standards.
4. **A qualified AI verification tool (DO-330 TQL-5 first, aiming higher).** Qualify the AI's checking role, not its design role. A checker whose output is reviewed falls in a low tool-qualification level. Pair this with SAE G-34 / EUROCAE WG-114 (AS6983, ML in aeronautical systems, recalled) and the FAA's AI Safety Assurance Roadmap (2024, recalled).
5. **Continuous conformity evidence in place of annual audits.** Stream furnace pyrometry, NDT and CMM data into a Nadcap-style evidence ledger, and lobby the Nadcap Management Council (subscribers vote) to accept continuous evidence instead of periodic audits.
6. **Become a conformity-assessment body.** Seek ISO/IEC 17025 lab accreditation, then 17065 product certification, then designated status (DER/ODA staff, Nadcap auditor pool, OSHA NRTL for adjacent markets, EU Notified Body for the Machinery Regulation and CRA). This is where the UL / Element-scale money is.
7. **Liability wrapper.** Buy product-liability and aviation insurance priced on the AI's verification record. Insurers then become a seventh-route gatekeeper that rewards the method.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side

- **"Element/UL for the reverse-engineering era"**: an AI-native, 17025-accredited test and certification lab that owns the shared equivalence database (play 1). It earns per approval and charges subscription fees for the data.
- **Standards compiler**: turn the drawing plus the spec tree into a test plan and acceptance criteria, then into a draft compliance report shaped for DER/DLA review. Sell it to PMA houses and to DLA engineering support activities.
- **Reviewer-side copilot** for FAA ACO/MIDO, DLA ESA and Nadcap auditors. It pre-checks packages against the machine-readable standard. The regulator is the customer, and the play cuts reviewer hours per approval.
- **Consensus-standard authorship as strategy**: write the ASTM/SAE practice for AI-assisted equivalence and get it accepted as MoC. That gives a moat in permission, not in knowledge.
- **Continuous-evidence Nadcap replacement** for special processes.

## 7. Players (who already touches this)

- SAE International / PRI (Nadcap): standards plus accreditation monopoly for special processes. Adjacent; they would be a partner or a gatekeeper.
- ASTM (F42/F44/E08): writes the test methods. Adjacent.
- NIAR / NCAMP (Wichita State): shared composite qualification. Adjacent, and the closest analog to play 1.
- Battelle (MMPDS): FAA metallic allowables custodian. Adjacent.
- UL Solutions ($2.68B revenue 2023), Element, Intertek, SGS, TÜV: conformity assessment. Adjacent now, and they could become direct competitors if they move into reverse-engineering equivalence.
- Ansys Granta: materials data. Adjacent.
- Nominal (customers: Anduril, Regent, Hermeus; fetched): hardware test-data platform. Adjacent; it is the infrastructure layer, not the approval layer.
- Senvol, Phase3D (AM qualification data and in-situ inspection, recalled): adjacent, limited to AM.
- HEICO, Wencor, and the PMA houses: they do the approvals in-house, with engineers. Direct, but not AI-native.
- Public.Resource.Org: activist pressure that makes mandatory standards free. Indirect enabler.

## Surprises
- The FAA and EASA already accept pooled, non-applicant-specific qualification data (NCAMP). The law and regulator acceptance needed for a "shared verification" business largely exist for composites. Nobody has applied the model to reverse engineering of legacy metallic parts.
- The binding power sits with the pointer (CFR IBR, FAA NoA, OJEU citation, prime PO), not with the SDO. SDO revenue is small and its paywalls are legally eroding.
- Committee seats are open to small firms because consensus rules require balance, so a startup can write the MoC it will be judged by.
- UL, the canonical verification authority, is a ~$7B public company still controlled by a nonprofit, which shows a verification authority can be venture-scale.
