# f01: Certification by analysis (CbA): NASA/Boeing/Airbus CbA roadmaps, how far simulation can replace physical tests for structures, fatigue and materials by 2030, and what regulators need to accept it

## 1. Primary evidence fetched

### A. CM4QC (FAA/NASA Computational Materials for Qualification & Certification), the most current and authoritative source
- Strategy document: NASA/TM-20260001729 = DOT/FAA/TC-26/10, published March 2026. Authors: Glaessgen (NASA LaRC), Gorelik (FAA Chief Scientist, Fatigue & Damage Tolerance), Battaile (Sandia), Lamm (Lockheed), Levine (NIST), Millwater (UTSA), Peralta-Duran (Honeywell, retired), Raghavan (Boeing), Rollett (CMU), Schwalbach (AFRL). https://rosap.ntl.bts.gov/view/dot/89323 , https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260001729/downloads/NASA-TM20260001729.pdf , site https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/cm4qc
- Glaessgen/Gorelik slides, NIST in-situ metrology workshop, Apr 28 to May 1 2026: https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260003240/downloads/NIST%20041826.pdf (text extracted locally). Key content:
  - The FAA baseline it cites: "The test article should be full-scale (component or sub-component) and represent that portion of the production aircraft requiring test."
  - "Model-friendly" domains: Part 25 damage tolerance (AC 25.571-1D), where "analysis supported by test evidence" is generally accepted. Part 33 damage tolerance (AC 33.70-1), where analysis is accepted (stress, heat transfer, crack growth) but "should be validated against relevant test data", and a probabilistic approach is required as one of two elements. AC 20-146 (dynamic seat certification by analysis) is "one of the few examples of certification by analysis", and it still needs test validation. "Rational analysis" per AC 25.562-1B.
  - Steering group members: NASA, FAA, NIST, Sandia, AFRL, Army aviation, NAVAIR, ORNL, SwRI; BAE, Boeing, Lockheed, RTX/P&W, GE Aviation, Spirit, Honeywell, Northrop, Textron; UTSA, CMU, Vanderbilt, Penn State, Northwestern. Its initial outcome was 09/14/2020, followed by a multi-year implementation plan.
  - Regulatory section: "Future State: Balance between Test Data and CM-informed Q&C is Undetermined." The drivers are a gradual reduction of conventional test matrices, CM-optimized test matrices, exploring the corners of process space, and specimen-to-part similitude.
  - Maturity scale 1-5 (adapted from Cowles/Backman/Dutton 2012). Only level 5 = "Model predictions are directly applicable to Q&C activities."
  - Phased plan. Near term (1-5 yrs): use existing commercial software to *guide* conventional Q&C (design experiments, guide testing). Mid term (5-10 yrs): partnerships and incorporation into commercial tools. Long term (>10 yrs): new CM techniques.
  - Culture: "Current approaches for Q&C simply do not have procedures for making use of CM results"; "The naive expectation is that CM predictions may directly replace testing data". The stance is to augment rather than replace testing.
  - Gaps: prediction of process->microstructure->property->performance (fatigue life, DT, surface finish, defects); residual stress; build reproducibility; NDE; UQ/reliability; acceptance "incremental with rigorous validation testing"; non-proprietary I/O; public validation data (AM Bench); "resources to conduct side-by-side CM-related and conventional Q&C activities"; transitioning lab codes into maintained commercial software.
  - Next steps: a NIST-led pathfinder exercise, best practice guides, outreach to certification authorities. A conference talk at MS&T Oct 5-7 2026.
  - Opinion/inference: by the regulator's own roadmap, 2030 falls in the "near-term" phase, where simulation guides and shrinks test matrices but does not replace them for flight-critical material qualification.

### B. NASA/Boeing CbA guide
- "A Guide for Aircraft Certification by Analysis", NASA/CR-20210015404, May 2021. Authors: Mauery, Cary, Lee, Schaefer, Slotnick (Boeing), Alonso (Stanford), Malecki, Medic (P&W), Mavriplis (Wyoming). https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210015404 . Its focus is CFD, aero, propulsion, aeroelastics and stall, i.e. flight and engine test, not structures or materials.
- Aerospace America, "Flying digitally", Jun 1 2022: https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/features/flying-digitally/
  - In 2018 the FAA accepted RANS CFD in place of flight test to show that A350-certified antennas/radomes do not affect A330-200 and 737-800 (derivative/minor change).
  - Boeing (Rob Gregg): the FAA accepts CbA for minor changes; the flight-test matrix could eventually be cut 50%.
  - EASA decides case by case. For derivatives it accepts virtual tests with a model validated against flight and ground tests. EASA is pushing software developers to create their own QA standards because it lacks the resources to vet the tools itself.
  - Alonso: certification flight testing takes 12-18 months. UQ (model-form error) is the "holy grail" and is not yet practical. The target is 4-5 nines.
  - RANS vs LES: CLmax differs 10-15%. Full-aircraft LES needs 10k-100k cores. GPU/ASIC could bring a ~3000x speedup, and 100k-run ensembles could be routine in 10-15 yrs.
- NASA UQ-for-CbA paper (Boeing, DLR, NASA LaRC, Airbus coauthors): https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230016541 (not fetched).
- DLR (Markus Fischer, ILA Berlin, Aviation Week, Jun 10 2026): CbA could mature technology faster and is expected to be in the next EU research framework. https://aviationweek.com/aerospace/emerging-technologies/dlr-sees-certification-analysis-helping-mature-technology-sooner (paywalled)

## 2. Existing CbA precedents (accepted today)
| Domain | Instrument | What analysis replaces | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dynamic seats (16g) | AC 20-146A | Some sled tests for seat *derivatives*, after a validated baseline | Accepted; the canonical CbA |
| Part 25 damage tolerance | AC 25.571-1D | Crack-growth/inspection-interval derivation, "supported by test evidence" | Accepted; full-scale fatigue test still required for new TC (per 25.571 amdt 25-132 LOV rule, recalled) |
| Engine rotor DT | AC 33.70-1, AC 33.14-1 | Probabilistic DT (SwRI DARWIN, FAA-funded since 1990s, recalled) | Accepted with validation |
| Antenna/radome aero effects | Issue papers, 2018 | Flight test, via RANS CFD | Accepted, derivatives only |
| Composites | AC 20-107B building block (recalled) | Analysis at higher pyramid levels, anchored by coupon/element tests | Accepted, but coupon allowables still need physical test (CMH-17 B-basis, min ~18-30 specimens over multiple batches, recalled) |
| Metallic allowables | MMPDS (recalled) | Nothing; A/B-basis requires statistical test populations (A-basis nonparametric ~299 specimens, recalled) | No CbA |
| PMA parts | 14 CFR 21.303, Order 8110.42 (recalled) | "Test and computation" path vs identicality; analysis accepted when substantiated | Case by case, ACO/DER judgment |
| USAF ASIP | MIL-STD-1530D (recalled) | DT analysis plus individual aircraft tracking | Accepted, with full-scale durability test for new aircraft |
| Cross-domain analog | FDA guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" (Nov 2023) + ASME V&V 40 (recalled) | Some bench/animal testing | FDA has a formal credibility framework; aviation has none equivalent |

Cautionary evidence: the Boeing 777X static-test article failed in Sept 2019 at ~99% of ultimate load (recalled), so even mature OEM models miss failure loads by a few percent. That is why regulators keep the full-scale test.

## 3. How far by 2030 (assessment, opinion grounded in the above)
- Structures, static strength: analysis already does most of the work. The full-scale ultimate test survives for new TCs. For *derivatives and repairs* (the Approval Factory domain), analysis-plus-reduced-test is already routine, approved through DER judgment. By 2030 there could be a modest formalization (EASA case by case, FAA issue papers).
- Fatigue/DT: crack *growth* analysis is accepted. Crack *initiation*, surface finish, residual stress, fretting and corrosion-fatigue are not predictable to certification confidence, so S-N/coupon fatigue and full-scale fatigue remain. By 2030 the realistic outcome is a 20-50% reduction in test matrices via CM-designed experiments and Bayesian pooling (CM4QC near term), not elimination.
- Materials/process (AM, castings, forgings, reverse-engineered alloys): the hardest case. Per CM4QC, the "balance is undetermined" and the 1-5 year horizon is "guide testing". Equivalence-by-analysis for a reverse-engineered part's material is not accepted. Material testing and first article testing (FAT) remain physics clocks.
- Flight/aero: the most advanced area (antenna precedent, Boeing 50% target), but it is irrelevant to parts.

## 4. What regulators need (synthesized from CM4QC, the Alonso guide and the FAA statement)
1. Validation against relevant test data for the specific application domain ("approves the data, not the technique").
2. Quantified uncertainty (model-form + parametric + data), with statistical confidence tied to the amount of experimental data. CM4QC Section 7 gives the validation-metric rules.
3. Credibility/maturity framework: the CM4QC 1-5 level scale; the NASA-STD-7009A analog (recalled); ASME V&V 10/20/40 (recalled). No aviation-specific credibility standard is codified in an AC yet.
4. Tool qualification/QA: EASA explicitly lacks the resources to vet tools and wants vendors to self-standardize.
5. Side-by-side evidence: years of running CM in parallel with conventional Q&C to build a track record. Nobody funds this; CM4QC lists it as a gap.
6. Public validation data (NIST AM Bench), non-proprietary formats, and maintained code.
7. Workforce: FAA reviewers and DERs able to judge CM submissions.

## 5. Gaps nobody fills (opportunity space)
- **Parallel-track evidence ledger**: no one systematically runs prediction-then-test on every approval package and publishes calibration curves (predicted vs measured, with error bars) at the scale needed to earn credit. An Approval Factory produces hundreds of packages a year with physical tests anyway, so it can *be* the side-by-side dataset. That is the moat: prediction track record per part family and failure mode.
- **Credibility dossier generation**: no tool auto-assembles a V&V/UQ credibility case in the format the FAA, DLA or EASA wants (CM4QC maturity level, validation metrics, applicability domain).
- **Test-matrix minimization**: Bayesian/active-learning test planning that proves "N fewer coupons, same B-basis confidence". This is the near-term path the FAA itself blesses ("optimization of material test matrices informed by CM insights"). Senvol does a piece of it for AM; no one does it for legacy reverse-engineered metallic parts.
- **Similitude engines**: quantifying specimen-vs-part representativeness, which the FAA lists as a driver.
- **Model-form UQ** (Alonso's "holy grail"): unsolved in practice. Large simulation ensembles plus multi-fidelity ML surrogates are compute-bound, a constraint the founder says to ignore.
- **Regulator-side tooling**: EASA and the FAA lack capacity to review simulations. A reviewer-assist tool (independent re-run, automated checks) could cut reviewer hours, the second half of the Approval Factory KPI.
- **Equivalence-by-analysis for reverse engineered parts**: proving a replacement part's material/process lands inside the OEM's allowable envelope from metallography, chemistry and NDE scans, so first article testing (FAT) shrinks to confirmation tests.

## 6. Binding constraints and what would change them
- Physics clock: full-scale fatigue (months to years), coupon fatigue (weeks). What changes it: accelerated testing plus validated models granting credit; parallel rigs; DED/ultrasonic fatigue (20 kHz) acceptance for gigacycle data (recalled).
- Law/regulator acceptance: an AC or policy statement on simulation credibility (analog to the FDA 2023 guidance). Path: the CM4QC pathfinder, then an FAA policy statement, then an AC. Plausibly a 2028-2032 timeframe (opinion). A startup can accelerate it by funding the side-by-side evidence and co-authoring the pathfinder via the NIST/CM4QC channel.
- Liability: the DER/ODA holder signs. Simulation does not shift liability; the applicant still owns it. Insurance products tied to a model track record could change this.
- Data access: OEM allowables and design data are proprietary. Public sets are AM Bench, MMPDS (licensed) and CMH-17. A house that generates its own test data on every part builds a proprietary validation corpus.
