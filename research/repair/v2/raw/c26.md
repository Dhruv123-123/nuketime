# c26: Digital inventory and on-demand spares with qualification (Replique, 3YOURMIND, Craitor, Ivaldi, Spare Parts 3D, Siemens Mobility AM, Wabtec, Deutsche Bahn / Mobility Goes Additive): who owns the qualification step?

## Short answer
None of the digital-inventory platforms owns the part-level qualification step. In every case it belongs to the asset owner's or OEM's engineering authority: DB as vehicle keeper/ECM, Siemens Mobility as OEM-maintainer, NAVSEA/NAVAIR technical authorities, class societies such as DNV in maritime, and FAA/PMA holders in aero. The platforms deliberately stay on non-safety-critical parts, where the customer can self-approve. They sell part screening, a file vault, encrypted or managed print orders, and a network of process-certified printers.
- Third-party certification here is about the **process/facility** (ISO/ASTM 52920:2023, issued by TÜV NORD), not the **part**.
- Safety-relevant AM parts in rail still go through a "time-consuming individual acceptance process" with "no binding calculation basis for component design" (TÜV NORD, 2024).
- The unowned gap is part-level equivalence/approval evidence for safety-relevant replacements. That is the verification-side AI opening.

## Recalled (not re-verified this run)
- **EU rail legal owner**: under Reg (EU) 2019/779 the Entity in Charge of Maintenance (ECM) is responsible for maintenance, including substitute parts. Changes to vehicles are risk-assessed under CSM-RA (Reg 402/2013), with an independent Assessment Body. Fire safety (EN 45545-2) is the main test gate for interior polymer parts. CEN/TS 17837? is listed in my notes as a possible rail AM spec: unverified.
- **Maritime**: DNV class guideline DNV-CG-0197 on AM qualification; Wilhelmsen/Ivaldi trials around 2019-2021. Here the class society owns qualification.
- **US Navy**: NAVSEA/NAVAIR technical warrant holders approve AM parts. NAVAIR has flown flight-critical AM parts, about 2016 onward. NAVSEA's "AM Center of Excellence" (Danville VA) is run by ATDM.
- **Wabtec**: in-house AM (Pittsburgh/Erie), its first 3D-printed metal locomotive part was around 2019, and it is a DB partner in some MGA projects. Wabtec acts as OEM-qualifier.
- **Other players**:
  - Physna: geometric search; raised ~$56M Series C in 2022; DoD part-identification work.
  - Phase3D: in-situ structured-light inspection; Air Force SBIRs.
  - 1000 Kelvin: AI print correction.
  - Additive Assurance: in-situ monitoring, Australia.
  - Sigma Additive: in-situ monitoring software; assets went to Divergent around 2024.
- **Aero AM qualification**: FAA has no general "AM part" pathway. AM parts go through normal TC/STC/PMA routes, with process control under the Part 21 quality system. Reference: the FAA/EASA AM workshops, NASA-STD-6030 for spaceflight, and MMPDS/CMH-17 for allowables. AM allowables databases are thin: America Makes and the AMMS effort, plus NIST AM-Bench.

## Pathway: digital spare substitution for a legacy fleet (rail/defence)
1. **Part screening** (which SKUs are printable or worth it). Done by 3YOURMIND, DigiPART, Physna, DB in-house. Status: commoditised, AI already used.
2. **Data acquisition** (OEM drawing, or reverse engineering by scan, metrology or materials analysis). Asset owner or service bureau. Status: OK for geometry. Material and spec intent is often missing.
3. **Criticality classification** (safety-relevant or not). Asset owner engineering. Status: done manually and conservatively. Everything ambiguous defaults to "not printable".
4. **Design-for-AM or equivalence argument**: show that the AM substitute meets or exceeds the original function. Status: **broken for safety-relevant parts**. There is no binding calculation basis (TÜV), each part goes through individual acceptance, and DigiZug3D is still in research.
5. **Material/process qualification** (machine + material + parameters). DB certified Essentium; TÜV ISO/ASTM 52920 for facilities. Status: exists for facilities, but expensive per material and per machine. Allowables are thin.
6. **Part-level testing** (fire EN 45545, fatigue, vibration, first article). Physics clock: weeks to months for fatigue. Done by accredited labs.
7. **Approval/sign-off** (ECM/vehicle keeper, OEM, Assessment Body, NAVSEA TWH, class society, FAA). Status: owned by permission holders, low throughput, liability-averse.
8. **Release to digital inventory** (file plus process recipe plus inspection plan locked). Replique, Ivaldi, DB Digital Warehouse. Status: works, but "qualified file" records are proprietary and not portable.
9. **Per-print acceptance/conformity** (in-situ monitoring, CT, CoC). Printer network and inspection vendors. Status: per-print acceptance is weak for distributed printers. That is why the platforms avoid critical parts.
10. **Field feedback/configuration management** (fleet tracking, failures). Asset owner. Status: rarely linked back to the part's qualification evidence.

## Who owns qualification: answer
The asset owner or OEM owns it, by law and by liability:
- EU rail: the ECM/keeper, with an Assessment Body.
- Maritime: the class society.
- US defence: the service technical authority.
- Aero: the FAA via the TC/PMA holder.

The platforms are order and IP routers that hand qualification back to the customer. This is why their volume is stuck on seat trims, lamp holders and coffee clips. DB alone moved from ~100k parts (2024) to >200k (Dec 2025) on non-critical parts, so that market grows without any change to qualification.

## What would need to be done (nobody does it today)
- A **part-level equivalence engine**: given the original part (scan, materials analysis, inferred loads from vehicle dynamics or service history), it generates the "mathematical proof of component strength" that TÜV says does not exist. It would produce load cases, FE with AM-specific allowables, knockdown factors and a test plan that is minimal but sufficient. The output would be formatted as the regulator's dossier: CSM-RA hazard log, EN 45545 evidence, NAVSEA TWH package, DNV CG-0197 file, or FAA PMA test and computation report.
- **Open, shared AM allowables**, built probabilistically from pooled coupon data across printer networks, plus a statistical method regulators accept (MMPDS-style B-basis), so each machine-material pair does not need its own campaign.
- **Per-print acceptance from in-situ data**: certify a build from sensor data (melt pool, layer imaging) instead of CT and destructive testing. Physics-informed ML, validated against CT, would let distributed printers ship critical parts.
- **A portable "qualified digital part" record** (file + recipe + allowables + inspection plan + approval pedigree + field history) that crosses organisations. This is the digital product passport that TÜV says is being standardised.
- **A permission holder that is not the OEM**: become the Assessment Body, a qualified ECM function for maintenance changes, a NAVSEA-recognised AM qualification shop, a DER/ODA, or a DNV-approved manufacturer. Then the platform can sign rather than route.
- **Get the DigiZug3D output (due ~late 2026) into a standard** and be the first tool that implements it.

Binding constraints and what would change them:
- Physics: fatigue and fire tests still run. The fix is fewer tests through better priors and similarity-based credit.
- Law: the ECM/keeper stays responsible. Partner with a keeper, or become an ECM maintenance function.
- Regulator acceptance: needs a reference fleet case, for example DB or ÖBB through DigiZug3D.
- Liability: insurance or indemnity backed by field data.
- Data: OEM drawings. Reverse engineering plus pooled fleet data gets around this.

## AI plays on the verification side
1. **"Qualification Copilot / Equivalence-as-a-service" for rail ECMs and defence depots**: scan to inferred loads to an AM substitute design to a minimal test matrix to an auto-written approval dossier. Sell per approved part, with the KPI being reviewer hours per approval. It would start in rail safety-relevant metal parts, the market DigiZug3D is trying to open (DB, ÖBB, Hochbahn, Siemens), then move to the Navy and the DLA.
2. **Shared AM allowables plus in-situ acceptance**: pool coupon and sensor data across the networks Replique, Ivaldi and DB already run. Issue statistically based allowables and per-build acceptance certificates. This is the verification layer that would let digital-inventory platforms move up into critical parts. Senvol, Phase3D and 1000 Kelvin are adjacent, but none of them owns part approval.
3. **Become the signer**: an AI-native Assessment Body or ISO/ASTM 52920 certifier, or a NAVSEA/DLA-recognised qualification house, whose throughput comes from AI-generated evidence. This is the most direct translation of the Approval Factory into rail/maritime AM.
4. **Criticality triage at fleet scale**: auto-classify 100k+ SKUs into self-approvable, equivalence-approvable and OEM-only, with the argument written out. This expands the addressable set beyond the platforms' current "non-critical" bucket.
