## 1. Legal and policy stack (who holds the permission)
- Statute: FY2004 NDAA Sec. 802 (codified in the 10 U.S.C. 2319 note, now recodified around 10 U.S.C. 3243/3249) (recalled). It says that for aviation CSIs, the head of the **design control activity (DCA)**, the service engineering authority, must approve every source and decide what quality and qualification the item needs. DLA can buy only from DCA-approved sources. Ship CSIs were added later (FY2006 NDAA) (recalled).
- DFARS 209.270 (aviation and ship CSIs), 246.407 and 246.371 (recalled): DCA source approval, plus notification of nonconformances and quality escapes.
- The joint instruction is "Management of Aviation Critical Safety Items" (DLAI 3200.4 / SECNAVINST 4140.2 / AFI 20-106 / DA Pam 95-9 / COMDTINST 4150.5) (recalled). The **JACG Aviation CSI Management Handbook** and the **JACG Aviation Source Approval and Management Handbook** implement it. The 2011 source approval handbook names NAVAIR AIR-4.0 as the Navy aircraft airworthiness authority (search snippet).
- Airworthiness criteria: **MIL-HDBK-516C** (Dec 2014; 516D drafts circulating) (recalled). It is a criteria handbook, not a process. Compliance can be shown by analysis, test, inspection, demonstration, or similarity. The service **Technical Airworthiness Authority (TAA)** issues flight clearances and airworthiness approvals:
  - Navy/USMC: NAVAIR AIR-4.0P under NAVAIRINST 13034.1 (flight clearance) (recalled).
  - Army: DEVCOM Aviation & Missile Center (AvMC) Aviation Engineering Directorate, under AR 70-62 (recalled).
  - Air Force: AF TAA / Engineering Authority structure under AFPD 62-6 and AFI 62-601, with lead engineers in the System Program Offices. Spares SARs go through the AFSC ALCs (OC-ALC Tinker 848th SCMG / 429 SCMS SASPO, Hill, Robins) (recalled; Tinker SASPO URL confirmed in the DLA deck).
- CSI definition (recalled): a part, assembly, installation or production system with a characteristic whose failure, malfunction or absence could cause catastrophic or critical failure resulting in loss of the aircraft, serious damage, an uncommanded engine shutdown, or serious injury or death. "Critical Application Item" (CAI) covers the next tier down. A part may also carry Critical Characteristics, which need special inspection at every unit.

## 2. Full pathway for a non-OEM (Category IV reverse-engineered) CSI spare

| # | Step | Who | Time / cost (evidence or recalled) | What is broken or never done |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Target selection: NSN, AMC/AMSC code, demand, CSI flag | Entrant (PUB-LOG/FLIS, DIBBS) | days | RPPOB is barred for AMSC G/T, spec-controlled items, AMSC B source-controlled items, and items under $10k annual demand (DLA deck). Nobody publishes a ranked list of "orphaned CSI NSNs a new source could win". The data is scattered across FLIS, DIBBS, J&As and sources-sought notices. |
| 1 | Get the data or the part: request a TDP, or buy or borrow a sample via RPPOB | DLA Aviation (website), DLA L&M | weeks to months. Rotair waited about 30 days just to receive the SAR specs. | Military Service concurrence is "required prior to Cat IV SARs for many items" (DLA deck): a permission gate before any engineering starts. Domestic firms only. Samples are often worn or from mixed configurations. |
| 2 | Reverse engineering: dimensional (CMM/CT), materials (chemistry, microstructure, hardness), process inference (heat treat, forging grain flow, coatings, shot peen), drawing and TDP creation | Entrant at its own expense (DLA deck: "done at the expense of the supplier") | (recalled) $50k–$500k+ and 6–24 months for CSIs. Multiple samples are needed to set tolerances. | Process inference from a finished part is the hard technical core. Nobody keeps a shared library of "how OEM X made part class Y" fingerprints. |

Packages get rejected for form as well as substance. |
| 4 | Submit to DLA, which screens and forwards to the service ESA/DCA | DLA SAR analysts | weeks | DLA routes only and cannot approve CSIs. |
| 5 | Queue at the ESA | Service engineering | **Rotair: received Feb 3 2014, review began Apr 28 2014, about 12 weeks before anyone opened it** (GAO B-409950). GAO only requires "reasonable" time. |
| 6 | Engineering review: equivalence to OEM, critical characteristics, failure modes, whether a qualification test is required | ESA engineers (cognizant per platform) | **Rotair: Apr 28 to Aug 15 2014 (3.5 months), then put "on hold" with RFIs in 6 areas (subvendors, measurement procedures, compliance with maintenance engineering orders). Entrant had 60 days to respond or be dropped.** | Reviews are serial, iterative and document-based, with no structured data. Each RFI cycle costs months. |
| 7 | Qualification / first article test (FAT), often government-witnessed. For CSIs it may include fatigue/endurance, proof load, NDT, metallurgical sectioning, and sometimes engine/rig or flight test | Entrant pays, at gov or approved lab. ESA approves the test plan. | (recalled) months to over a year. Fatigue tests of 10^6–10^7 cycles are a physics clock. Engine parts may need engine test cell time. | Test rig capacity is scarce. Test plans are bespoke per part. Little reuse of evidence across similar parts. |
| 8 | Approval: entrant added as approved source (CAGE + P/N) for that NSN. Flight clearance/airworthiness is implicit via the ESA. A new design or configuration change may need a TAA airworthiness approval under MIL-HDBK-516 | ESA/DCA; TAA if design change | — | Approval covers that NSN/part number, that manufacturer, and often that facility. It does not transfer to a new plant or a new process. |
| 9 | Win the buy: compete in the next limited-source solicitation | DLA / service contracting | Agencies need not delay award for a pending SAR (GAO B-409950 digest #2). Rotair lost a 5-yr IDIQ for 1,224 Black Hawk drag braces (backlog of 579) to the incumbent. | **Timing mismatch: the SAR clock (12+ months) is longer than the solicitation clock (weeks).** A J&A can lock out new sources for 5 years. |
| 10 | Sustain: production lot FATs, quality escapes and PQDRs, CSI nonconformance reporting, re-approval if process or site changes | Entrant + DCMA + ESA | ongoing | One escape can lead to source disapproval. Liability sits with the source. |

Repair/overhaul CSIs (Rotair's case) follow the same SAR route, but the package is measured against maintenance engineering orders and depot work requirements rather than a drawing.

## 3. Where the bottlenecks really sit (respecting physics, law, acceptance, liability, data)
1. **Reviewer capacity at the ESA/DCA (law + acceptance).** Statute says only the DCA can approve a CSI source. The queue and the RFI loop make up most of the calendar time (Rotair: 12 wk queue + 15 wk review + RFI hold). To change it: give government reviewers tools that cut hours per review, fund contractor "engineering support" (A&AS) to pre-review, or get statutory delegation along the lines of FAA DER/ODA ("designee" model, which does not exist in DoD for CSI source approval as far as I know (opinion/recalled)).
2. **Permission to start (data access).** Cat IV needs Service concurrence for many items. Samples come via RPPOB. Data rights are held by OEMs. To change it: the 2025 right-to-repair push (Hegseth memo of 30 Apr 2025 directing right-to-repair provisions in contracts; Army Transformation Initiative; a FY2025/26 NDAA right-to-repair section was proposed and later stripped or narrowed) (recalled) changes what the government can demand in future contracts. It does not cover legacy fleets, so RE stays necessary there.
3. **Physics clocks.** Fatigue, endurance and environmental tests take months. To change them: run tests in parallel on many rigs, use accelerated tests validated against fleet data, and earn regulator acceptance of "qualification by analysis + reduced test" for similarity families (MIL-HDBK-516 already allows analysis and similarity as compliance methods).
4. **Process inference (knowledge).** This is the part AI does make cheaper: CT + metallurgy + ML to infer heat treat, grain flow, residual stress and coating spec.
5. **Commercial timing.** SAR approval does not line up with solicitations. To change it: pick NSNs with long-horizon demand, or target requirements DLA flags in sources-sought notices (May–June 2026 DLA sources-sought notices explicitly invite SARs under the JACG handbook: search snippet).
6. **Liability and quality escape.** A CSI failure is catastrophic, which makes reviewers rationally conservative. To change it: build a better evidence trail than the OEM's (per-unit CT, digital process records), so the reviewer's risk of approving is lower than the risk of the status quo.

## 4. Who is doing what (players)
- **OEM incumbents / approved sources** (Sikorsky, Overhaul Support Services, Lockheed, Boeing, GE, P&W, Rolls-Royce Allison for C-130 T56; TransDigm subsidiaries on many sole-source military spares). These hold the permission. (evidence for Sikorsky/Overhaul: GAO B-409950)
- **Traditional RE/SAR small businesses**: McNally Industries (DLA RE panelist 2023), Rotair Industries, Kitco, Barnes Aerospace, Air Industries Group (NYSE American: AIRI), many hundreds of CAGE-code machine shops. Manual, consultant-driven. They are the closest analog to an "Approval Factory", but none is AI-native (opinion).
- **HEICO**: commercial PMA leader. It does some military work via Flight Support/Electronic Technologies but is not focused on DLA Cat IV CSIs (recalled).
- **Government in-house RE**: AFSC/OC-ALC Reverse Engineering and Critical Tooling (REACT) lab at Tinker; AF Rapid Sustainment Office (RSO) and its programs; Navy FRCs; Wichita State NIAR, which digitized whole airframes (B-1B, UH-60, T-38) under AF contracts worth roughly $100M+ (recalled). These are adjacent: they produce TDPs the government owns and could hand to entrants.
- **Verification-side tech (adjacent, not approval players)**:
  - Lumafield: industrial CT + AI analysis. Lists aerospace & defense, reverse engineering and FAI as applications; USMC logo (site fetched). Funding about $85M+ total (recalled).
  - Nominal: test data platform (Core + Connect) for hardware test and validation. Anduril, Hermeus, Regent customers (site fetched). Raised a $75M Series B in 2025 (recalled).
  - Senvol: ML for additive manufacturing data and qualification. Navy-funded project announced Jan 12 2026 (site fetched).
  - PhysicsX: AI physics simulation, Series B about $135M in 2025 (recalled).
  - Gecko Robotics: inspection robots + data, Navy/USAF. Series D $125M in 2025 (recalled).
  - Hadrian, Divergent: automated manufacturing (recalled). They build parts but do not win SARs.
  - Exiger and Govini: supply chain and industrial-base analytics, used by DLA/OSD (recalled). They could identify targets but do not do approvals.
- **Nobody found** (within the limited searches) who sells an AI tool to the **government ESA reviewers** to triage and evaluate SARs, or who runs an AI-native Cat IV CSI SAR house at scale. That is consistent with the prior review's conclusion. Evidence quality is low because of the blocked .mil sites and the search quota.

## 5. What nobody does today and would need to be done (assuming abundant compute/intelligence)
1. **Reviewer copilot inside the ESA**: ingest the SAR package, check it against the service-specific guide plus the JACG handbook plus MWR/MEOs, auto-draft RFIs, flag missing critical characteristics, and pre-compare against the OEM TDP the government holds. Sold to AFSC/NAVSUP/AvMC (SBIR → OTA). This attacks the true bottleneck: the 12-week queue and the 15-week review.
2. **Machine-readable SAR ("approval-as-code")**: a structured schema (STEP AP242 + MBD + evidence links) that DLA and the three services accept in place of three PDF formats. Requires DLA/JACG buy-in. It is a standards play.
3. **Physical-to-digital equivalence proof**: CT + metallurgy + residual stress + process inference that produces a quantified "equivalence certificate" with uncertainty, so a Cat IV can be argued like Cat I. Also needs a validation corpus of OEM vs RE parts (government holds both).
4. **Qualification-by-similarity families**: group CSIs by geometry, material and process, and qualify one test campaign per family with analysis covering the rest. Needs TAA acceptance (MIL-HDBK-516 allows it in principle).
5. **24/7 automated test lab**: robotic fatigue and proof rigs running in parallel, with test plans auto-generated and pre-approved templates per part class. Physics clocks do not compress per part, but calendar time drops through parallelism.
6. **Fleet-data-anchored acceptance**: use the services' maintenance data (removals, PQDRs, flight hours) to show the OEM part's real-world margin and set evidence-based acceptance criteria. Needs data access (REMIS/NALCOMIS/GCSS-Army).
7. **Target intelligence**: rank every CSI NSN by (sole-source, demand, backlog, J&A expiry date, RPPOB eligibility, ESA queue length), so SAR approval lines up with the next solicitation.
8. **Designee model for DoD**: lobby for FAA-DER-like delegated source-approval authority for qualified private engineering organizations. Changing the law (NDAA language) is the multiplier.
9. **Escape-proof evidence trail**: per-unit CT plus digital process records, giving reviewers lower perceived liability than the incumbent OEM gives.

## 6. Surprises
- The statute itself places CSI source approval with the government DCA. There is no private designee path like FAA DER/ODA, so throughput is capped by government headcount.
- In GAO's view, a 12-week queue followed by a 3.5-month review ending in a "hold" is "not unreasonable". Agencies need not wait for a pending SAR before awarding a 5-year IDIQ.
- Cat IV SARs for many items need Service concurrence before the entrant may even buy the sample.
- RPPOB excludes items under $10k annual demand and source-controlled (AMSC B) items, which are often the most locked-in ones.
