# s09: First article inspection and conformity inspection (AS9102, CMM, CT). Automation state and AI potential.

Search budget: I used 3 WebSearch calls plus 4 WebFetch calls. Anything marked (recalled) comes from model memory and was not re-verified here.

## 1. What this step is in the Approval Factory pathway
- **AS9102 FAI (SAE; Rev C, ~2023, recalled).** This is a full, documented verification of every design characteristic of the first production part against the design record. It uses three forms:
  - Form 1: part number accountability.
  - Form 2: product accountability, covering raw material, special processes and functional tests, with certs.
  - Form 3: characteristic accountability. Every ballooned dimension, note and GD&T callout gets a measured result.
- **DLA / military alternate source.** For an approved source, DLA or the military service may require First Article Test (FAT) samples before the source goes on the approved list (rotair.com DLA guide). Solicitations carry FAT and Production Lot Test waiver worksheets, used when the supplier has recently produced the item (DLA Aviation Patriot spares solicitation SPRRA226R0077, 2026). A DoD IG finding reported that 79 of 103 reviewed contracts had no FAT requirement (via sftt.org). GAO decisions such as B-220237.2 (1985) and the D Square Engineering decision (1982) show that DLA may take whatever time it needs to evaluate alternates and may reject unverified ones. That evaluation time is a big part of the "permission" clock.
- **FAA PMA (recalled).** The FAA or a DAR runs a conformity inspection, recorded on FAA Form 8100-1, and the applicant issues a Statement of Conformity on Form 8130-9. This happens before the FAA witnesses any test, and again on production parts. Order 8110.42 (PMA) and Order 8100.15/8100.? (ODA/DAR) govern it.
- **Reverse-engineered parts need two kinds of check:**
  - (a) Conformance: does our part meet our own derived TDP? This is classic FAI.
  - (b) Equivalence: is our TDP plus our part the same as the OEM part in form, fit, function and material? DLA's ESA and the FAA's "identicality" or "test and computation" route require this. (b) is the harder one. It is mostly argued in narrative and reviewed by humans. No product automates it.

## 2. Automation state today (evidence)
### Characteristic extraction / ballooning / FAIR paperwork: mature and commoditised
- **Ideagen (InspectionXpert).** Auto-balloons drawings, imports measurement data, and auto-fills AS9102 Forms 1-3. It claims a FAIR drops from "multiple hours to a full day" to about 1 hour (vendor claim; ideagen.com FAIR blog).
- **DISCUS Software.** Its IDA engine uses AI/ML for auto-ballooning of PDF/TIFF drawings and DISCUS 3D ballooning of CAD/MBD. It says AI is making information extraction "almost completely automated". No numbers given (discussoftware.com).
- **High QA.** One-click ballooning, CMM/ERP connectors, FAI/PPAP submissions. No AI mentioned (highqa.com).
- **Net-Inspect.** Cloud FAI/SPC/NCR supply-chain portal that links detail and sub-assembly FAIRs into assembly FAIRs (net-inspect.com). Several primes push suppliers onto it (recalled; the page does not name them).
- **Others (recalled):** 1factory, Leo AI (blog says the paperwork, not the measurement, is the hard part), Kissflow FAI templates, Siemens/PTC MBD-PMI.
- **Verdict:** The paperwork layer is solved enough. It is not a venture wedge on its own.

### Measurement-program generation (CMM): partially automated
- CMM programming in PC-DMIS (Hexagon), Calypso (Zeiss) and Metrologic is still largely manual: hours to days per new part (recalled).
- PMI/MBD-driven auto-programming exists, but only works well when the CAD carries semantic PMI. Legacy C-130 and KC-135 parts have 2D paper drawings or none at all.
- QIF (ISO 23952) is the digital-thread standard from characteristic through plan to results. Adoption is low (recalled).

### CT (volumetric): hardware is scaling, and AI analysis is starting
- **Lumafield.** Neptune scanner, Voyager cloud analysis (marketed as "AI-powered"), Atlas AI engine (from a job listing, unverified). Raised a $75M Series C in March 2025 led by IVP; investors include Spark, DCVC, Kleiner, Lux, Matter, G2VP and Wellington. Added a $50M SVB growth facility in November 2025 to deploy more scanners. Total about $152.5M (secondary source).
  - Its FAI page claims CT compares internal and external geometry to CAD nominal and "supports AS9102 compliance". It names no aerospace customer and gives no accuracy or throughput numbers (lumafield.com/applications/first-article-inspection).
- **Incumbents (recalled):** Zeiss (Metrotom), Waygate/Baker Hughes, Nikon, North Star Imaging, Comet Yxlon. Software: Volume Graphics (Hexagon) VGSTUDIO MAX.
- **Traceability gap.** CT dimensional uncertainty is standardised only recently (VDI/VDE 2630; ISO 10360-11, recalled). Many primes and regulators still want tactile CMM on critical features. Regulator acceptance of CT-only FAI is limited.

### Material / special-process verification: mostly manual
- Chemistry by OES/XRF, hardness, tensile coupons, and microstructure by metallography all have human-read micrographs. NADCAP special processes are audited, not measured.
- For reverse engineering of legacy alloys and heat treats, this is where equivalence arguments break (recalled).

## 3. What nobody does (gaps)
1. **An end-to-end loop for reverse engineering.** The chain would run: CT and CMM of N OEM samples, then derived nominal with *statistically set tolerances*, then auto-generated TDP, then auto-generated measurement plan, then FAI of our part, then a quantitative equivalence report. Today these are 4-6 separate tools plus human engineers. Nobody sells "equivalence evidence" as a product.
2. **Automated measurement-uncertainty budgets** for each characteristic, mixing CMM, CT and optical, plus decision rules (ISO 14253-1), attached to the FAIR. Reviewers would then trust non-tactile data.
3. **Reviewer-side tooling.** DLA ESA engineers, FAA ACO engineers and DAR conformity inspectors receive PDFs and have no machine-checkable view. Sending FAIR plus QIF plus CT volume as a structured package would cut reviewer hours, which is the binding KPI.
4. **Remote or virtual conformity.** The FAA used remote and virtual conformity during COVID (recalled policy memos). Making CT volumes plus tamper-evident chain-of-custody an accepted substitute for a physical inspector visit is unbuilt.
5. **AI micrograph and fractography analysis** with calibrated uncertainty, so material and heat-treat equivalence can be shown cheaply.
6. **Lot-level "digital FAI" re-use.** Delta-FAI after process changes could be computed automatically. Today it is a human judgment.

## 4. Binding constraints and what would change them
- **Physics clock.** Scan and measure time is minutes to hours per part, so it is not binding. Material coupons and fatigue tests are binding, but they belong to other steps.
- **Regulator acceptance.** CT-only dimensional evidence and AI-generated FAIRs need acceptance by the DLA ESA (Engineering Support Activity), the FAA ACO and the prime. What it would take:
  - published uncertainty validation against CMM;
  - a precedent package, starting with non-critical DLA items;
  - an FAA policy memo or AC update on virtual conformity.
- **Liability / sign-off.** Form 1 is signed by the organisation's quality authority, and conformity is signed by an FAA inspector or DAR. AI drafts, a human signs. Owning DARs or ODA-type delegation inside the company converts this into an internal step.
- **Data access.** OEM drawings are unavailable, which is the whole point. Physical OEM samples and CT give the data. Owning a fleet of scanners is a moat.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side
- **A. "Equivalence engine."** Multi-sample CT and CMM of OEM parts, then statistical nominal and tolerance inference, then an automatic comparison report for DLA SAR / FAA identicality. This is the core of the Approval Factory. Rating: direct.
- **B. "Metrology agent."** Drawing or scan in, then plan, then CMM/CT program, then AS9102 plus QIF plus uncertainty budget out. Sellable as SaaS to the about 15-20k AS9100 shops (recalled count) and used internally.
- **C. A reviewer copilot / structured submission standard.** Offer it free to DLA ESAs and DARs, which turns reviewer hours into the moat. Could be an SBIR/AFWERX entry (DLA R&D SBIR topics on reverse engineering and additive parts recur, recalled).
- **D. A certified inspection lab network.** CT plus CMM, ISO 17025 accreditation, a DAR on staff, as a service to other PMA and SAR applicants. Cash flow plus a data flywheel.
