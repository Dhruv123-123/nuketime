## 1. Spend that is not competed (original analysis, USAspending API, FY2025 = 1 Oct 2024 to 30 Sep 2025)

Query: api.usaspending.gov `/api/v2/search/spending_by_category/psc/`, contract award types A-D. "Non-competed" means extent-competed codes B (not available for competition), C (not competed) and G (not competed under simplified acquisition procedures). "Competed" means A, D and F. Delivery orders inherit the parent contract's code, so these figures are approximate.

**DLA (awarding subtier), FY2025**
- All DLA: non-competed **$10.38B**, competed $45.56B. Fuel, pharmaceuticals and food dominate the competed side.
- Breakdown by federal supply group (FSG):

- Largest single non-competed DLA product codes: 1560 airframe structural components $1.165B; 1410 guided missiles $745M; 2840 aircraft gas turbines $732M; 1680 misc aircraft accessories $397M; 5865 ECM $373M; 1650 hydraulic/de-icing $310M; 1630 wheels/brakes $209M; 5998 electronic assemblies/boards $170M.
- Reading: DLA spends about **$3.4B a year non-competitively on aviation hardware alone** (FSGs 15, 16, 28 and 29). About 60-68% of airframe, component and engine dollars go non-competed. Common hardware, valves and bearings are mostly competed (19-28% non-competed). The sole-source problem therefore sits in **engineered aviation parts**, not in commodity hardware.

**DoD-wide (toptier), FY2025**
- All DoD: non-competed **$232.2B**, competed $257.2B.
- FSG 16 aircraft components: $6.39B non-competed against $1.56B competed (80%).
- FSG 28 engines: $8.35B against $1.19B (87%).
- FSG 29 engine accessories: $1.38B against $0.31B (82%).
- FSG 15 aircraft: $46.9B against $7.8B (86%). This group includes buys of whole new aircraft.
- J0xx maintenance and repair of equipment: $11.8B non-competed against $10.55B competed (53%).
- K0xx modification of equipment: $2.72B against $0.72B (79%).
- Reading: excluding whole-aircraft and whole-missile buys, about **$15-20B a year of DoD component, engine-accessory and repair spend is non-competed**. That is the addressable pool for "second source" work. The figure is an upper bound, because only a fraction is reverse-engineerable at any one time.

Gap: AMSC code is **not a USAspending field**. Spend by AMSC C/D/P (C = source approval required by the design control activity, D = data not economically available, P = proprietary rights; also H and others) needs DLA's FLIS or WebFLIS data joined to DLA award or demand histories. WebFLIS and the public FLIS product sell AMSC per NSN, so this join can be built **(recalled)**. No public source publishes it.

## 2. Number of sole-source NSNs
- **(recalled)** DLA manages about 5 million NSNs and has annual sales and services of roughly $45-50B. DLA Aviation (Richmond) manages roughly 1.1-1.2M NSNs, many with AMSC C, D or P. I could not verify a public count of "sole-source NSNs" this session, and as far as I know none is published regularly. This is itself a gap: a FLIS pull of AMSC distribution by FSC would produce the count.
- GAO-25-107468 (Sep 29, 2025) cites a DoD data call to more than 200 programs in sustainment. Half reported data or data-rights deficiencies that blocked competition or organic sustainment, and **"a majority of justifications for sole source contracts were based on inadequate technical data, software, or license rights."** This is official evidence that the main driver of sole-source buying is missing data or rights, not a technical inability to make the part.

## 3. DoD IG and GAO reports on sole-source price spikes
- **TransDigm, DoD IG Dec 2021 (DODIG-2022-043)**, from the TransDigm FY2023 10-K: the audit covered Jan 2017-Jun 2019 contracts and recommended a voluntary refund of "at least $21 million in excess profit on 150 contracts." TransDigm disputed it and recorded no loss contingency as of Sep 30, 2023. Wikipedia gives $20.8M on 105 spare parts. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1260221/000126022123000081/tdg-20230930.htm
- **TransDigm, DoD IG Feb 2019 (DODIG-2019-060)** **(recalled)**: excess profit on 46 of 47 parts, $16.1M, with some markups above 4,000%. A 9,400% markup on a metal pin was widely cited. TransDigm refunded about $16.1M after a May 2019 House Oversight hearing.
- **TransDigm's own business model**, from the same 10-K: about **90% of net sales from proprietary products**, 56% from aftermarket, and a 25-30 year aftermarket life per platform. Defense was 39% of FY2023 sales (43% FY2022, 50% FY2021). The 10-K says customers have "reduced incentive to certify another supplier because of the cost and time of the technical design and testing certification process." The company's own filing names the permission moat.
- **Boeing C-17 spare parts, DoD IG Oct 2024** **(recalled)**: the Air Force overpaid about $1M on 46 parts, including a soap dispenser bought at about 7,943% over a fair price ($149k).
- **Earlier DoD IG work** **(recalled)**: Boeing CH-47/AH-64 spare parts (DODIG-2011-061 and others), Sikorsky and Bell sole-source spares in 2015-2020. These recur because the structural cause (no data rights, no qualified second source) persists.
- **GAO-25-107468** "Weapon System Sustainment: DOD Can Improve Planning and Management of Data Rights" (Sep 29, 2025): https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-25-107468. It found vendor lock on all five programs reviewed (F/A-18, F-35, LCS, Stryker, Virginia-class).
  - **F/A-18**: for over a decade the Navy could not buy data rights for RF cables. Reverse engineering was judged "too costly," so maintainers **cannibalize grounded aircraft**.
  - **Stryker**: a 2024 sole-source technical-support contract cost about **$534M over 5 years** because unlimited rights to the base vehicle could not be obtained.
  - **LCS**: a crane repair waited 2.5 weeks for the OEM to show up.
  - **Virginia-class**: cannibalization while waiting on OEM spares.
  - Officials named reverse engineering and additive manufacturing as workarounds but called them "less effective and efficient" than buying rights early. **Cost per reverse-engineered part is the lever.**
  - Matter for Congress: clarify treatment of detailed manufacturing or process data needed for operation, maintenance, installation and training (OMIT). Recommendations were still open as of Sep 2026.
  - The DoD IP Cadre's **AI tool to scrape previously purchased data rights from contract databases was unfunded and canceled** (footnote 37, citing GAO-22-104752).
  - The **Air Force DART "IPR Module"** analyzes contracts and technical orders to show what technical data the government already owns. On F-15 it found no restriction on a problem part. On RQ-4 it reviewed thousands of documents and saved about **$1.75M in labor hours**.
  - **NAVAIR DRSAVE** (Data Rights Sustainment and Acquisition Validation Engine) tracks data rights down to the part number. CH-53K is testing it, with launch to other NAVAIR programs planned for **summer 2026**.
  - The Section 801 IP pilot's lessons-learned collection **stopped in FY2023**.
- **GAO-22-104752** (Nov 30, 2021): IP Cadre funded for only 5 temporary positions through FY2023. Lack of IP has been a known problem for "about 3 decades."
- **GAO-23-106217** (Nov 10, 2022): only 2 of 49 aircraft met mission-capable goals in FY2021. O&S costs about $54B in FY2020; maintenance costs up $1.2B since FY2011.
- **GAO-23-105341** (Sep 21, 2023): F-35 sustainment is $1.3T of $1.7T life-cycle cost. More than 10,000 components were awaiting repair, with a mission-capable rate of about 55%.
- **GAO-25-106931** (Jan 22, 2025): 0 of 20 programs did a formal MOSA cost/benefit analysis.

## 4. Budget lines and programs that pay for second sourcing and reverse engineering
These could not be verified because .mil budget documents were blocked; all are **(recalled)**.
- **DLA Aviation reverse engineering / Engineering Support Activity**: funded through the Defense Working Capital Fund (DWCF) cost recovery rate, not a discrete appropriation. DLA runs value engineering and **"Source Development"** programs. Its SAR process is free to the applicant, but evaluation is done by the service Engineering Support Activity (ESA) and is the bottleneck. Queues run from months to years.
- **DLA R&D (PE 0603680S Manufacturing Technology, plus DLA SBIR/STTR)**: DLA SBIR topics recur on reverse engineering of obsolete parts, AM of DLA-managed parts, and "technical data package generation."
- **Air Force Rapid Sustainment Office (RSO)**, Wright-Patterson: funded via Air Force O&M and RDT&E. It runs the Reverse Engineering/AM portfolio and the "Advanced Manufacturing Program Office." The OC-ALC **REACT** (Reverse Engineering and Critical Tooling) lab at Tinker AFB serves KC-135, B-52 and E-3 parts.
- **Navy**: Fleet Readiness Centers reverse engineering; NAVSUP WSS "Obsolescence/DMSMS" funding; NAVAIR AM.
- **DMSMS** (diminishing manufacturing sources and material shortages) budgets inside each program's O&M and procurement lines, plus the OSD R&E DMSMS program.
- **Industrial Base Analysis and Sustainment (IBAS) / Defense Production Act Title III**: second sources for critical components, mostly at the supplier-capacity level rather than per part.
- **Army**: the April 30, 2025 SecDef memo on Army transformation directed right-to-repair language in new contracts. The FY2026 NDAA right-to-repair fight (Senate Warren/Sheehy provision vs. a House "data-as-a-service" alternative) gave a weaker final outcome; check the current text before relying on it.
- **SBIR**: AFWERX and NAVAIR topics repeatedly fund "AI-enabled reverse engineering", "digital TDP from legacy drawings" and "obsolete part identification." There are many Phase I/II awards but no scaled production vehicle.

## 5. Market signals (verified, SEC)
- **TransDigm buys PMA houses**: on Jan 16, 2026 it agreed to buy **Jet Parts Engineering + Victor Sierra Aviation for about $2.2B cash**. The two had about **$280M** combined revenue in CY2025, roughly 7.9x revenue. Both are nearly all commercial aftermarket PMA. Chairman Howley: "We have had a long-term and sizable PMA effort." ## 6. Synthesis: where demand is, what nobody does
- Demand is real and large. Even on a conservative cut it is about $3.4B a year at DLA and about $15-20B DoD-wide in non-competed component and repair spend. The official diagnosis (GAO-25-107468, DoD data call) is that most sole-source justifications trace to **missing data or rights**.
- The verification-side gaps that nobody fills at scale:
  1. **Rights discovery**: what data the government already owns. Air Force IPR Module and NAVAIR DRSAVE are siloed in-house tools. The DoD-wide AI version was **canceled for lack of funding**.
  2. **No public AMSC-weighted demand map**: nobody publishes spend by AMSC code, so nobody can triage which NSNs are worth reverse-engineering.
  3. **ESA engineering review capacity for SARs**: the binding queue. Reviewers are government engineers at service ESAs.
  4. **Equivalence evidence packaging**: turning measurement, material analysis and test results into the format an ESA accepts.
  5. **Lessons-learned repository**: stopped in FY2023.
- The binding constraints and what would change them:
  - **Law**: the OMIT data definition. GAO asked Congress to clarify it; doing so would make detailed manufacturing data deliverable for maintenance.
  - **Regulator acceptance**: ESAs must trust AI-generated TDPs. A pilot with DLA Aviation or OC-ALC REACT to accept AI-prepared SAR packages with a standard evidence schema would change this.
  - **Liability**: the government bears flight-safety risk on critical safety items (CSIs). Non-CSI items (AMSC C/D/P but not CSI) are the low-liability entry.
  - **Physics clocks**: qualification tests (fatigue, environmental) still have to run physically. Parallel test rigs and statistical similarity arguments shorten the queue but not the test itself.
  - **Data access**: FLIS/AMSC, DLA demand history (via DIBBS/FOIA), and legacy drawings in JEDMICS. JEDMICS access for qualified vendors exists through cFolders/ETDS.
