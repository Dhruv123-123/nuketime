# f07: Regulator-side AI and capacity -- FAA, EASA, NRC, DoD use of AI to review submissions; FAA Reauthorization Act 2024 provisions on delegation/ODA; could a private party supply reviewer tooling or become a delegated authority faster?

Search budget: 3 WebSearch calls used. Rest via WebFetch / curl of primary docs. Items marked (recalled) are from memory and were not re-verified this session.

## 1. Evidence gathered

### NRC (the furthest ahead of any regulator on reviewer-side AI)
- NRC CDO / Deputy Chief AI Officer Basia Sall, ATARC summit, 25 Jun 2026: one licensing category that took ~4 years was done in 9 months against an 18-month target set by the May 2025 executive order (EO 14300). Internal tool **SimplifAI** (built on Azure OpenAI, now v2.0) used for drafting and precedent checks. NRC is testing Claude, Azure OpenAI and Gemini through GSA OneGov, and exploring USAi. Source: WebProNews 26 Jun 2026, https://www.webpronews.com/nuclear-watchdog-cuts-license-reviews-to-months-with-ai-help/ (trade press quoting the agency; the speed-up is not independently audited).
- DOE/INL/ANL/Microsoft/**Everstar** test, Mar 2026: Everstar's "Gordian" converted a 208-page DOE-format safety analysis into NRC-equivalent sections in 1 day (normally 4-6 weeks), flagging missing information; DOE's line was "Experts design, AI accelerates, and experts validate". Same source.
- INL (Akshay Dave, 19 May 2026, GAIN + Industry visit, DOE Regulatory Framework Modernization program, with ANL): https://gain.inl.gov/content/uploads/4/2026/06/06-AI-Application-for-Accelerating-NRC-Licensing-Reviews-Akshay-Dave.pdf
  - NuScale (c. 2020): about $500M and 7 years. EPRI target: $10M. EO 14300 target: 1.5 years.
  - The authors analysed 1,200+ documents from advanced-reactor dockets: **95% of applicant-regulator interactions (RAIs, audits, RCIs) had no blocking issue.** They were information exchanges.
  - Proposal: a "Regulatory Communication Protocol" (RCP), with AI agents acting as intermediaries that settle non-blocking exchanges, the ADAMS corpus feeding agents and MCP servers, and RCP formalising communication between systems. Modelled result: **$28M and 15 months (vs. $500M and 7 years), 65% shorter timeline, up to 77% labour savings.** Adoption "requires commitment from industry and government -- political will".
- NRC AI page (https://www.nrc.gov/ai.html): AI Strategic Plan published 29 Sep 2025 (ML25269A196), an AI and Data Steering Committee, and a public AI workshop on 15 Sep 2026. The earlier plan was NUREG-2261 (FY2023-27).
- Third-party nuclear licensing AI startups, per the same WebProNews piece: Atomic Canyon (Neutron, search over ADAMS), Nuclearn (workflow), Blue Wave AI Labs, Inductive ("TurboTax for nuclear licensing"), Everstar (Gordian). 10 CFR Part 53 was finalised Mar 2026, per the same source.
- DOE/INL RFI INL-26-047, "Reviewing Smarter: AI Tools for Building International Nuclear Regulatory Capacity", closed Jul 2026. It asked for outputs that are cited, auditable and grounded in source documents. This was a regulator-side tooling procurement signal. (Seen in search snippets only; the page was behind a bot wall.)

### FAA Reauthorization Act 2024 (Pub. L. 118-63), text pulled from govinfo
- **Sec. 303, ODA best-practice sharing:** the FAA must convene a forum of ODA holders, unit members and experts at least every 2 years.
- **Sec. 304, ODA unit-member ethics training (49 USC 44736(g)):** a recurrent training programme reviewed by the FAA ODA Office; training within 60 days of appointment and annually after that. Implemented by FAA Notice N 8100.20 (FR 4 Mar 2026, Docket FAA-2025-0092). That notice states there are **75 current ODA holders**. Commenters included HEICO Aerospace Corp, Boeing, Garmin, Gulfstream, Textron, Rolls-Royce and Mammoth Freighters.
- **Sec. 306:** extends the ACSAA 2020 ODA unit-member oversight (49 USC 44741, biannual briefings through FY2028), integrated project teams, and appeals of certification decisions to 2028.
- **Sec. 310, independent study on the future state of type certification:** an FFRDC or nonprofit must assess "digital tools, techniques, and software systems that allow for efficient and virtual evaluation of an applicant design, associated documentation, and software or systems engineering products", a risk-based TC model, and foreign authorities' tools. Report due 18 months after enactment (about Nov 2025); congressional briefing 270 days after that. **This is the statutory hook for reviewer-side digital and AI tooling.** (Contractor identity and report status not verified; possibly MITRE, recalled/guess.)
- **Sec. 312:** NPRM on Transport Airplane and Propulsion Certification Modernization (RIN 2120-AL42) within 2 years.
- **Sec. 315:** a third-party evaluation of FAA aviation safety data use.
- **Sec. 348, ASIAS improvements:** "prioritize production-ready configurable solutions over custom development" and a "plan to use appropriate deployable commercial solutions". This gives statutory cover to buy commercial software, though for safety data rather than certification review.
- **Sec. 349, Instructions for Continued Airworthiness (ICA) ARC:** includes PMA holders, repair stations and DERs employed by repair stations, and considers "the availability of instructions for continued airworthiness" and "protection of proprietary information". This bears directly on repair-data access. Report due about 1 year after enactment, then rulemaking or policy within 180 days.
- **Sec. 1024:** an AI/ML technology review limited to airport operations.
- **Net:** the 2024 Act **did not expand delegation**. Post-737 MAX law (ACSAA 2020, and the 2024 extensions) tightens ODA oversight. The only delegation-friendly items are the Sec. 310 digital-tools study and the general push on certification timeliness.

### EASA
- AI Roadmap 2.0 (10 May 2023); Concept Paper Issue 2 (6 Mar 2024); a newer issue released for comment on 3 Jun 2026; "EASA Olympics for aviation AI-based systems" (7 Jul 2026). https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/research-innovation/ai
- No EASA internal AI review tool was found.
- **Xcert AI SA** (Geneva): a certification co-pilot covering requirements through verification and quality (v1.2, 15 Oct 2026). It won the EASA AI Olympics out of 50 cases, has a product line for "authorities and regulators" to streamline project reviews, raised CHF 1.1M from Spicehaus (Nov 2025), and lists ZeroAvia and ESA as customers. https://xcert.ai/ and https://www.airframer.com/news/story/xcert-ai-extends-certification-platform-into-verification
- Structural point (recalled): under EASA Part 21 Subpart J, a DOA holder can approve minor changes and minor repairs itself, and major repairs with the privilege of 21.A.263(c). Self-approval is more built into the system in Europe than through US DERs.

### DoD / DLA
- I found no public evidence of DLA or service Engineering Support Activities (ESAs) using AI to evaluate Source Approval Requests (my search returned nothing).
- (recalled) A SAR is evaluated by the service ESA (for the Air Force, the Oklahoma City, Ogden or Warner Robins ALC engineering units; for the Navy, NAVAIR/NAVSEA). Evaluation queues commonly run 6-18+ months and are bottlenecked by government engineer hours, not by DLA.
- (recalled) DoD has enterprise GenAI (GenAI.mil, launched Dec 2025), but no sustainment-engineering review tooling is public.
- (recalled) The military airworthiness authority (MIL-HDBK-516 / Air Force TAA) cannot be delegated to a private party. Services do buy contractor engineering support (A&AS, SETA) that prepares evaluations for government engineers to sign.

## 2. Can a private party supply reviewer tooling or become a delegated authority faster?

**Reviewer tooling (selling to regulators):**
- Feasible, and it is already happening in nuclear (SimplifAI is internal; DOE/INL fund the external work; the RFI asks for "cited, auditable" tooling).
- Aviation regulators are a slow, small market: FAA AIR has roughly 1,300 engineers (recalled), and buying runs through FAA AMS. The leverage is real but the revenue is small and the cycle long.
- Better: sell the same tool to the **delegated side**, meaning the 75 ODA holders, about 1,000+ DERs (recalled) and DOAs. They do most of the actual finding-of-compliance work, and they buy quickly.

**Becoming a delegated authority:**
- The FAA ODA path (Order 8100.15) requires an established applicant with a track record. In practice this takes years: first PMA approvals with DERs, then an ODA application, with FAA capacity-limited acceptance and a post-ACSAA posture that is stricter, not looser.
- Faster routes:
  - **Acquire** an existing PMA/MRA ODA holder or a DER-staffed PMA house. The permission transfers with the organisation, subject to FAA review.
  - Or employ or contract consultant DERs, who can find compliance for PMA test and computation data now.
  - In Europe, obtain a DOA (Subpart J) for repair self-approval privileges.
- DoD has no delegation equivalent. The nearest route is a government contract to prepare ESA evaluations: "we do the ESA's homework, they sign".
- NRC cannot delegate licensing decisions. The play there is applicant-side (Everstar, Inductive) plus regulator-side tooling (labs).

## 3. The verification-side gap and what would need to be done (assume compute and intelligence are abundant)

**Binding constraints:**
- **Law and liability:** a designee or ESA engineer must sign, and AI cannot hold a designation.
- **Regulator acceptance:** a novel method of compliance needs an issue paper or FAA concurrence.
- **Physics clocks:** coupon, fatigue and qualification tests must physically run.
- **Data access:** OEM ICA and TDP rights, and the fact that SAR precedent is not public.

**What nobody does today:**
1. **A machine-executable regulatory basis.** This means compiling 14 CFR 21/23/25/33, ACs, Orders 8110.42 (PMA) and 8110.4, issue papers, CRI equivalents, DLA SAR packages and service ESA checklists into executable compliance checklists that tie each requirement to the evidence type that satisfies it.
2. **An adjudicated-precedent corpus.** FAA PMA/STC approvals are public in DRS, but the reasoning is not. Making the reasoning available would need a FOIA programme and partnering with ODAs to share de-identified findings. DLA SAR approval and disapproval reasons would need to become a dataset, which needs DLA agreement or an OTA.
3. **A "pre-adjudication" layer modelled on INL's RCP.** The INL work found 95% of exchanges non-blocking. An agent would answer RAIs from the applicant's evidence before a human reviewer sees them, with calibrated confidence and citations, so that reviewer time goes only to the blocking items. **KPI: reviewer hours per approval.**
4. **A reviewer workbench for DERs/ODAs/ESAs** that auto-checks the submitted data package against the compliance basis and precedent, generates the compliance finding draft (for example the FAA 8110-3 statement of compliance), and leaves an audit trail. The aim is to multiply reviews per signer by 5-10x.
5. **Evidence-generation credit.** Get certification-by-analysis accepted in place of some physical tests, using validated simulation, through Sec. 310 and the issue-paper route. Physics clocks shrink only when a regulator accepts analysis.
6. **Standing regulator acceptance.** Run a pilot with an FAA ACO (perhaps under a Sec. 310 recommendation) or with an Air Force ALC ESA (via AFWERX SBIR/OTA), so the AI-assisted packet format becomes an accepted means.
7. **Liability wrap.** An insurer-backed performance bond on AI-pre-screened packages could make it rational for signers to rely on the tool.

**The AI play on the verification side:**
- **(a) "Approval Factory, verifier edition":** own or acquire delegated signing capacity (ODA/DER/DOA) and run AI pre-adjudication behind it. Revenue comes from approvals produced, not from seats.
- **(b) Sell the reviewer workbench to ESAs and ODAs.** It is the shovel, and it creates a precedent corpus and data moat.
- **(c) An "ESA-in-a-box" contractor for DLA/service sustainment engineering,** clearing the SAR backlog under contract.
- **(d) Cross-domain:** the same engine works for NRC, where demand is proven and funded. Aviation and defence are greenfield on the reviewer side.
