## 1. Company-by-company

### ProvenAir Technologies (Chicago, founded ~2015)
- What: AI/OCR over life-limited part (LLP) records for engines, landing gear and APUs. Ingests LLP sheets, MTS (maintenance transaction summaries), NIS (non-incident statements), airworthiness certs. Auto-classifies, builds an interactive back-to-birth (BtB) timeline, and produces exception reports, open-item lists, mini-packs and disk sheets. Source: https://www.provenair.com/
- Traction claims (site): "10,000+ human errors found", "5,000+ major assets analyzed", "28x faster than leading DMS". Logos: AAR, United, British Airways, BBAM, WLFC (Willis Lease), GA Telesis, Killick, TrueAero, WNG Capital, AELS, APS, Turbo Resources, Wyatt; testimonial from Allegiant. Member of Aviation Suppliers Association (ASA).
- Partnership: AAR marketing partnership, 5 May 2022. AAR already used it for its own parts supply and landing gear. https://avitrader.com/?p=168369
- Funding: CB Insights shows total raised $3M (https://www.cbinsights.com/company/provenair-technologies/). Crain's Grand Rapids, 22 Aug 2024: Michigan Capital Network invests in ProvenAir (amount not confirmed).
- What it verifies: that the paper chain is consistent and complete (gaps in cycles, missing back-to-birth links, mismatched serial or part numbers, open items). It checks documents against documents. It does not check the physical part.

### FLYdocs (UK; Lufthansa Technik group)
- What: digital records management, asset-trading/transition packs (BtB and gap management for LLPs), digital engineering services, lifecycle asset management, plus "AI-powered OCCM (on-condition / component) inspection" with AMOS. Source: https://www.flydocs.aero/
- Traction: "75+ customers and partners". Logos include easyJet, Jetstar, Qantas, Cathay, Qatar, Etihad, Virgin Atlantic (a 17-year relationship), Airbus and Northrop Grumman.
- Ownership: Lufthansa Technik took a majority stake around 2018 (recalled). Its own funding is not disclosed.
- What it verifies: completeness and compliance of records during lease transitions. It is the system of record, not an independent verifier.

### Honeywell GoDirect Trade (launched ~2018) (recalled; site returned 503)

### Moog VeriPart (launched ~2019) (recalled; moog.com URLs 404)

## 2. What the whole category actually verifies (evidence-based synthesis)
1. Document-to-document consistency. Do serial and part numbers, cycles, dates and operators chain without gaps back to birth (ProvenAir, FLYdocs)?
2. Compliance lookup. Is there a document showing that each AD/SB/inspection was done (Bluetail, CAMP)?
3. Record immutability after entry (Aerotrax, GoDirect blockchain, VeriPart).
4. Presence of trace in a transaction (GoDirect, ILS, PartsBase).

Nobody in this list verifies:
- (a) that a document is authentic, i.e. that the issuing 145 station or 8130-3/EASA Form 1 signatory really issued it. The AOG Technics case (2023, UK) involved forged EASA Form 1s on CFM56 parts that reached dozens of engines at multiple airlines (recalled: on the order of ~100 engines and many thousands of parts). Records software did not catch it. CFM/airline manual checks against the claimed issuer did.
- (b) that the physical part in hand is the part the documents describe. There is no material, dimensional or serial-marking fingerprint at birth.
- (c) that the part conforms to type design. That is a conformity inspection plus an engineering judgement, done by humans: FAA DMIR/DAR, ODA, DER, the DLA QAR, first article inspection (AS9102).
- (d) reasoning about airworthiness. A gap in trace becomes a "no-trace part, not usable on an LLP" decision made by humans. There is no machine-auditable rulebook.

Evidence vs opinion: points (a) and (b) are inferred from the product descriptions fetched above (none claims them). The AOG figures are recalled.

## 3. Map to the Approval Factory pathway (DLA SAR / FAA PMA)
For an AI-native reverse-engineering house, the verification bottlenecks are:
1. Source data acquisition. Obtain a known-good OEM part with clean trace. Used parts come with BtB packs. ProvenAir-type AI would cut the time spent qualifying "exemplar" parts.
2. Characterisation. Dimensional (CMM/CT), material (XRF, OES, hardness, microstructure), process inference (heat treat, coatings). The physics clock is lab time, but it can run in parallel and be automated.
3. Engineering substantiation. Comparison to the original design and test plan, done by a DER or DLA engineering support activity. Today this is the human-hour sink.
4. Conformity and first article. AS9102 FAI with DMIR/DAR or DLA QAR witness.
5. Production approval and ongoing traceability. PMA with a Fabrication Inspection System (14 CFR 21.307), CAGE/QPL listing, 8130-3 issuance.
6. Field provenance. The part is sold with an 8130-3 and enters the same back-to-birth chain these companies digitise.

The records/provenance companies only touch step 6, and in part step 1. Steps 2 to 5 have no software category. That is the verification-side white space.

## 4. What would need to be done (assume abundant compute and intelligence)
1. **Issuer-authenticated release certificates.** Sign every 8130-3 / EASA Form 1 cryptographically at the 145 station or PAH (ATA Spec 2000 Ch.16 already defines digital record signatures; AC 120-78B allows e-signatures). An AI cross-checks each certificate against the FAA/EASA certificate-holder registry, signatory lists and repair-station capability lists. Fixing the law/acceptance constraint needs FAA/EASA policy recognising digital signatures as the authoritative form, which is essentially already permitted. Uptake is the real constraint.
2. **Physical-identity binding.** Fingerprint each serialised part at birth or overhaul: surface microtexture images, CT density map, XRF alloy signature, dot-peen/laser mark. Re-identify at receiving inspection with a phone or benchtop scanner. Data access is the constraint, so start with your own produced parts and with used parts flowing through partner MROs.
3. **Counterfeit/forgery anomaly model across the USM market.** Train on every 8130-3 and Form 1 seen (marketplaces, lessors, MRO receiving), plus FAA SUP notifications, GIDEP alerts and DLA debarments. Flag impossible serial reuse, wrong-format certificates, signatories after their termination date, and implausible cycles. This is a network-effect dataset that nobody aggregates today.
4. **Machine-readable airworthiness rulebook.** Encode 14 CFR 21/43/145, ASA-100, AC 00-56B, AC 20-62E (parts eligibility) and lessor return conditions as executable checks. AI then gives a usable / not usable / needs-X verdict with citations, which a human inspector co-signs. Liability stays with the human signatory, and the AI pre-clears the case.
5. **Conformity-evidence engine for PMA/SAR (the Approval Factory hook).** Auto-generate the test plan and similarity analysis (21.303(a)), run automated CMM/CT/XRF, compare against the reverse-engineered model, and assemble the DER/DLA package with traceable raw data. KPI: reviewer hours per approval. Changing regulator acceptance would take an FAA ODA, or partnering with an ODA holder, plus a DLA pilot under the Section 2319/Source Approval process with machine-checkable evidence.
6. **Remote/virtual conformity witnessing.** Video plus sensor attestation so DMIR/DAR/QAR witness steps are done asynchronously. The FAA allowed remote inspections during COVID, and EASA studied virtualisation in its VIRTUA research project (https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/research-projects/virtua-digital-transformation-case-studies-aviation-safety-standards; page content not parsed).

## 5. Takeaways
- The category is small and underfunded. Disclosed rounds are tiny: ProvenAir ~$3M, Bluetail ~$4.3M, Aerotrax $0.5M. Incumbents own the system of record (Lufthansa via FLYdocs and AMOS, Hearst via CAMP, Honeywell via GoDirect). No venture-scale verification company exists.
- All of them verify paper against paper. None verifies authenticity of the issuer, physical identity, or conformity, and AOG Technics showed the gap is real.
- Blockchain provenance (Aerotrax, VeriPart, GoDirect chain) largely fizzled because it secures the record after entry and does nothing about whether the first entry was true.
- ProvenAir and FLYdocs are acquisition targets or partners for an Approval Factory: cheap back-to-birth qualification of exemplar parts and downstream trace for its own PMA parts.
