## 1. Bottom line

- Land-based heavy-duty and industrial gas turbines are the clearest example of a reverse-engineering parts market with **no regulator**. Nobody issues a PMA or a SAR. A part is "approved" when four parties accept it: (a) the plant owner's engineering team or its fleet-management group, (b) the property and machinery-breakdown insurer, (c) the lender's independent engineer or LTSA counterparty (where one exists), and (d) the market's reputation, built through users groups (7F Users Group, CTOTF, 501F Users Group, Western Turbine Users for aeroderivatives).
- So the gatekeeper is not a certificate. It is **fleet experience**: installed sets, fired hours and starts on a "lead unit", and clean inspections at hot-gas-path (HGP) intervals (about 24k hours / 900-1,200 starts on F-class, recalled). That is a physics clock of 2-4 years per new hot-section design. It is the real moat for PSM (Hanwha Power), Sulzer, EthosEnergy, TSL and others.
- No independent third party certifies non-OEM industrial GT parts. Insurers do not run a formal parts-approval scheme. No shared, cross-owner database ties part provenance to inspection findings. All three gaps sit on the verification side.
- Evidence of AI use by these players: PSM/Hanwha Power has an "Advanced Data Analytics & AI" team, AutoTune combustion tuning (100+ installs, 1.5M hours), and plans for digital twins of its monitored 7F fleet (psm.com). That AI is for **operations**, not for reverse engineering or qualification. None of the pages fetched mention AI in reverse engineering, scanning-to-CAD, or qualification evidence.

## 3. How non-OEM GT parts get qualified today (synthesis; mostly recalled practice, partly evidenced)

1. **Get a sample.** Buy or borrow a new or used OEM part (TSL and Sulzer practice; "purchase an original part" per the FS-Elliott blog https://www.fs-elliott.com/blog/oem-vs-non-oem-parts-explained-what-manufacturers-see-that-others-dont).
2. **Dimensional capture.** CMM, laser or blue-light scan, CT for internal cooling passages. Cooled blades are the hardest because of internal cores.
3. **Material and process ID.** Chemistry, microstructure (equiaxed / DS / SX), coatings (MCrAlY bond coat plus TBC), heat treatment. The OEM's exact spec is unknown, so it is inferred.
4. **Redesign and analysis.** FEA, CFD and heat transfer, life (LCF, creep), frequency/Campbell checks. PSM and Sulzer explicitly "improve" on the original design. That is a legal choice (design-around patents) and a performance one.
5. **Supplier qualification.** Casting and machining vendors are audited on site, plus first-article inspection (TSL). ISO 9001 is the baseline. No industry standard exists comparable to AS9100 plus FAA PMA.
6. **Customer acceptance.** The owner's fleet engineers review the package, often after a factory audit. Large IPPs (Calpine, NRG, Vistra, Constellation) keep approved-vendor lists. DTS cites a "Tier-1 F-class rating from major power producers" as its credential.
7. **Field validation (the physics clock).** Lead-unit install, then borescope at intervals and inspection at the first HGP / combustion inspection. Credibility grows with fleet-leader hours and starts, which vendors present at users groups.
8. **Insurer.** Property and machinery-breakdown insurers (FM Global, HSB/Munich Re, AEGIS, Zurich, AIG, recalled) generally do not prohibit non-OEM parts. In a loss they may examine provenance and documentation. Some underwriters ask about LTSA status and parts provenance at renewal. No formal, published insurer "approved parts" scheme was found. The EGAT (Thailand) tender asks vendors to show reverse-engineering and redesign capability, which shows that utilities qualify the vendor rather than the part (https://fprocurement.egat.co.th/storage/documents/pq/turbine/4 - Pre-Qualification Criteria (PQ-GT-01 EN).pdf).
9. **Contract and legal.** OEM LTSAs (CSAs) typically void coverage, or claim warranty exclusions, if third-party parts go in. Patent and trade-secret fights are the OEM's lever (recalled: GE v. Siemens trade secrets 2010s; Alstom/PSM patent disputes).

Comparison: the FAA AC 33-12 framework for turbine-engine replacement parts asks for statistically substantiated dimensions, materials and processes, plus tests "to confirm reverse engineering assumptions". The industrial world has no equivalent. That makes it faster (no regulator) and also less trusted, which is where the opportunity is.

## 4. What nobody does, or does well (verification side)

- **No independent qualification body or "UL for non-OEM GT parts".** Each owner re-audits each vendor, so the work is duplicated across maybe 50 large owners.
- **No standard evidence dossier.** Vendors send bespoke PDFs. No schema links the scan, material certs, FEA and life calcs to the specific serial-numbered installed part.
- **No cross-owner field-evidence pool tied to part provenance.** ORAP tracks RAM (reliability, availability, maintainability) at unit level. Borescope images and repair-shop findings sit in silos. Vendors self-report fleet-leader hours.
- **Insurer underwriting is anecdotal.** No actuarial model of non-OEM part risk exists, so insurers cannot price it and default to suspicion. Engineers, not insurers, carry the decision.
- **Shortening the physics clock.** Accelerated validation (rig and burner-rig tests, digital-twin life prediction calibrated against fleet borescope data) is not accepted as a substitute for lead-unit hours.
- **Repair-development qualification.** Each new weld or braze or coating repair is qualified per shop. Adaptive cells (Liburdi) automate execution, not qualification evidence.

## 5. What it would take (abundant compute and intelligence)

1. A neutral **Qualification Passport**: a machine-readable dossier per part number. It holds a CT/scan-derived geometry delta to the OEM, material and coating microstructure ID, a physics-based life model with uncertainty, supplier process data and first-article results. An AI reviewer checks completeness and consistency, and a human PE signs. Sold to owners and insurers as "accept once, use everywhere."
2. **Fleet evidence exchange**: federated ingestion of borescope video, HGP inspection reports, outage repair findings and ORAP-style RAM data from many owners. Vision models grade distress (oxidation, TBC spallation, cracking), keyed to part serial and vendor. This gives statistical life evidence for any vendor's part, and reduces the "2-4 years of lead-unit hours" clock to "hours aggregated across the fleet."
3. **Insurer risk model and wrap**: price non-OEM parts risk with that data, and offer a performance warranty or insurance wrap backed by a carrier. That turns the insurer from a passive skeptic into the approver. Law does not need to change; carrier capacity and a reinsurer partner do.
4. **Accelerated validation accepted by owners**: calibrated digital-twin plus spin, rig and burner-rig coupon testing to fill the gap until lead-unit hours accrue. Acceptance comes from the users groups and insurers, not a regulator.
5. **AI reverse-engineering compiler**: scan to parametric CAD to cooling-design inference to casting tooling. This cuts the engineering hours per package that PSM, Sulzer and TSL spend, but the casting slot (Howmet, PCC, Doncasters) and fleet validation remain the binding constraints.

## 6. Why now

- Gas turbine demand from data centers and gas peakers: OEM new-unit backlogs run to 2029-2030 (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy, MHI public statements, recalled). Life extension and uprate of the existing 7F/501F/9E fleet are rising, and OEM service capacity is tight. Owners need non-OEM parts, which raises the value of fast, trusted verification.
- Consolidation (APG buying EthosEnergy's generator division in 2026; Hanwha owning PSM; Doosan in the aftermarket) shows PE and strategic money flowing in. Nobody owns the neutral verification layer.
