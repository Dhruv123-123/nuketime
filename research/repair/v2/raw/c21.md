## Bottom line

- Nobody in Europe or Asia runs an AI-native "approval factory" that reverse-engineers orphaned parts AND carries them through to a regulator approval. The field splits into four layers that don't connect:
  1. **Certifying AI itself.** EASA's AI Roadmap, Daedalean, ANITI/DEEL, Confiance.ai, Fraunhofer IKS, Spleenlab. This is about getting ML *into* aircraft, not using AI to *produce approvals*.
  2. **Compliance-workflow software.** Xcert AI (Geneva, CHF 1.1M, Dec 2025) is the only pure-play startup I found that pitches AI for aerospace certification paperwork. It is tiny.
  3. **Geometry recovery and part triage.** Hexagon (scan to parametric CAD), 3YOURMIND and Spare Parts 3D (AI picks which parts are good to digitise or print), CADDi (Japanese AI for drawings and procurement). None of them owns the approval.
  4. **Verification tooling and inspection.** Rapita, AbsInt, TrustInSoft, AdaCore, LDRA, Validas (qualified DO-178C/DO-330 tools), plus Donecle and Mainblades (drone inspection written into the Airbus and Boeing aircraft maintenance manuals, AMM). They are deterministic or classic computer vision, with little generative AI.
- Approved organisations hold the permission: EASA Part 21J design organisation approvals (DOAs), Part 21G production approvals, Lufthansa Technik, Satair/Airbus. Approvals are organisational, not per-part, and that makes the DOA the moat.
- There is no EASA equivalent of FAA PMA. Under the EU-US bilateral (BASA TIP), EASA accepts FAA PMA parts for non-critical parts on US-designed products. For EU-designed products, a parts design needs a DOA change or repair approval (Part 21 Subpart D/E/M) (recalled). So an FAA PMA factory exports to Europe almost for free on US platforms like the C-130 and KC-135.

### Daedalean (Zurich): AI-in-avionics certification, now defense
- Destinus (NL) agreed in Aug 2025 to buy Daedalean for CHF 180M (~$225M) in cash and stock. The deal closed on 5 Jan 2026. Destinus will run it as an AI engineering and autonomy hub focused on navigation and perception without communications or GNSS. Sources: https://seedtable.com/exits/daedalean-ai, https://startups.one.gob.es/news/note/destinus-brings-aviation-ai-specialist-daedalean-into-its-platform-in-225m-deal-1 (search snippet; page 403).
- Aug 2025 interview: not yet certified. It was aiming for the first ML-enabled avionics certification "by end of this year", using a method developed with EASA and FAA ("we now have guidance from both regulators"). The approach: one dataset trains the model, a separate dataset verifies it, then the model is frozen, so it is deterministic and does not learn in flight. Hardware is ~4 kg and 200 W. It flew a 70 nm GNSS-denied circuit on Moog's OH-58. https://verticalmag.com/q-and-a/inside-daedaleans-push-to-certify-machine-learning-avionics-for-the-evtol-era/
- I found no 2026 confirmation of a certified ML avionics product.
- Lessons (opinion):
  - Roughly 9 years and >$100M of venture capital (recalled: ~$90M raised by 2023) bought regulator guidance, not revenue.
  - The exit came through defense repositioning.
  - This is the cautionary tale for "certify the AI": the value went to the *method agreed with the regulator* and to defense optionality.

### EASA AI programme
- AI Roadmap 2.0 was published on 10 May 2023 and is meant to be updated yearly. Concept Paper Issue 2 (6 Mar 2024) gives guidance for Level 1 and 2 ML applications. A **new issue went out for public comment on 3 Jun 2026**. The MLEAP project final report came out on 31 May 2024. The "EASA Olympics for aviation AI-based systems" is now closed (7 Jul 2026). https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/research-innovation/ai
- A secondary source says ARP6983 / EUROCAE ED-324 (the ML airworthiness process standard from the SAE G-34 / WG-114 joint committee) was slated for June 2026 (unverified). It also says EASA expected a first ML vision component approval in early 2026 (unverified). https://yage.ai/share/civil-aviation-ai-triad-en-20260804.html (body not retrievable)
- Rulemaking task RMT.0742 (AI trustworthiness) is supposed to turn the concept paper into AMC/GM (recalled; NPA expected ~2025-2026).
- **Key gap:** the concept paper governs ML *in the product*. Neither EASA nor FAA has published guidance on **AI as a tool that generates compliance evidence** (e.g. an LLM writing a substantiation report, or a generative model producing a reverse-engineered TDP). Today that falls under ordinary tool-qualification logic, DO-330 for software tools (recalled). For mechanical substantiation it falls under nothing specific: the DOA's own procedures and the CVE (Compliance Verification Engineer) signature carry it. So the opening is regulatory white space, not a prohibition.

It is building an AI platform to automate certification and compliance workflows (AI-assisted analysis, workflow automation, project management) for safety-critical aerospace systems. ## Pathway view from Europe, and gaps

| Step | Who does it in EU/Asia | What is missing | What would change it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Find orphaned or overpriced parts | 3YOURMIND, Spare Parts 3D, CADDi (triage) | Triage stops at "printable / cheaper". Nobody scores parts by *approvability*: path (minor change, repair, PMA via BASA), criticality, test burden | Train an approvability model on public EASA/FAA approval data (PMA lists, STCs, ADs) |
| Geometry and material recovery | Hexagon, Creaform, GOM/Zeiss; Fraunhofer IGD | Material and process identification (alloy, heat treat, coating), tolerancing intent, and drawing notes are still manual | Automated metallurgy (OES/XRF/microstructure ML) feeding inferred process specs |
| Compliance and substantiation package | Human DOAs; Xcert AI (workflow) | No one generates the *equivalence argument and test plan* automatically, then has the argument checked mechanically | An LLM produces the argument, a formal assurance-case checker validates it (GSN/SACM), and qualified deterministic tools compute the numbers |
| Verification (test, analysis, inspection) | Rapita/AbsInt (software), Donecle (inspection), test labs | Physical tests still run part by part. There is no "credit by analysis" library that pools data across similar parts | Credit-by-similarity databases, with regulator-accepted surrogate models validated on a few physical anchors |
| Regulator review | EASA PCMs/CVEs, national authorities, DOA privileges | Reviewer bandwidth. EASA charges by the hour (fees regulation). No machine-readable submission standard exists | Structured digital compliance packages that a regulator-side AI can pre-check. EASA's AI programme and "Olympics" show appetite |
| Production and conformity | Part 21G holders | First-article inspection and conformity are manual | Automated CT/CMM conformity checked against the digital TDP |

## AI plays on the verification side (opinion)

1. **Regulator-side and DOA-side "pre-review" engine.** It ingests a substantiation package and checks traceability to the certification basis (CS-25 / Part 25 paragraphs), the completeness of means of compliance, and consistency of loads and materials. It flags issues before human review. A DOA buys it first because it cuts CVE hours; EASA could adopt it later. Nobody does this. Xcert is the nearest and is workflow-level.
2. **Qualified "AI-generates, deterministic-checks" evidence pipeline.** The LLM writes the analysis, test procedures and reports. Qualified tools (FEA with V&V, formal assurance-case checker) recompute every claim. To be acceptable it needs DO-330-style tool qualification for the checker, not for the LLM. That is the trick: never qualify the generator, qualify the checker.
3. **Approvability scoring and portfolio selection.** An approvability model trained on FAA PMA/STC/AD data and EASA approval data predicts approval path, test burden and payback per part. It turns Hexagon-style and 3YOURMIND-style triage into an approval pipeline.
4. **Credit-by-similarity / physical-anchor library.** Pool test data across families of reverse-engineered parts so analysis can stand in for some tests. It needs regulator acceptance of the surrogate's validation envelope. Precedent exists in EASA's acceptance of the Donecle method via the AMM, and in A(M)C 20-193 practice for multicore.
5. **Own the DOA.** Buy a small EASA Part 21J cabin/repair DOA and run it AI-native, with output measured as approvals per CVE-hour. In Europe the permission is organisational. (Opinion: a cheaper path to "permission" than FAA DER networks, and BASA links it to FAA PMA output.)

## What would need to be done that nobody does today
- Publish (or get EASA to accept via a DOA procedure) a **method for using generative AI as a tool in compliance demonstration**. The basis would be the checker-qualification principle under DOA handbook procedures, so no new rule is needed. Route: an EASA Innovation Partnership Contract (IPC, recalled: EASA offers these to industry for novel methods) or a DOA procedure change approved by the authority.
- A **machine-readable compliance package standard** that maps CS paragraphs to evidence objects to checks. EUROCAE has none (recalled). A startup could draft it and push it through EUROCAE WG or SAE.
- An **open approvability dataset** joining FAA PMA lists, EASA approvals, ADs and DLA SAR outcomes.
- **Automated material and process identification** good enough to give an accepted alloy, temper and coating spec from a sample.
- A **conformity loop**: CT/CMM against a digital TDP, with the automated report accepted by Part 21G and FAA 21 Subpart K.
