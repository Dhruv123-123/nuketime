## 2. The pathway (Level I / SUBSAFE part from need to installed and certified)

Doc names are (recalled) unless cited above.

1. **Designation.** Drawing, APL or Ship's notes mark the item Level I and/or SUBSAFE, within the SUBSAFE certification boundary (NAVSEA 0924-062-0010). Material control follows NAVSEA 0948-045-7010 (Level I Material Control). The owners are the design yard (General Dynamics Electric Boat for Virginia/Columbia) and NAVSEA 05 Technical Warrant Holders.
2. **Tech data.** Drawings and specs are held by NAVSEA and the design yard. For legacy parts, data is often incomplete. For castings and forgings, the **tooling** may be missing (DLA cited above).
3. **Source approval.** For spares through DLA Maritime / NAVSUP WSS, a SAR is reviewed by the Engineering Support Activity (NAVSEA field activity, e.g. NSLC SUBSAFE Engineering Support or NSWC). For new-construction suppliers, the shipbuilder's supplier-quality group approves the source with NAVSEA concurrence. For AM, the NAVSEA TWH approves machine, process and part TDPs (Keyport example, cited above).
4. **Supplier quality system.** The supplier submits its quality system to the PCO (cited). Then come shipbuilder audits and NAVSEA/SUPSHIP audits. Special processes need qualified procedures: welding per NAVSEA S9074-AQ-GIB-010/248, fabrication per S9074-AR-GIB-010/278 or MIL-STD-1689 for castings. Welders and brazers need personnel qualification. (recalled)
5. **Material.** Mill heat and lot traceability, chemistry and mechanical test reports (MTRs), and testing by an approved lab. Level I needs Objective Quality Evidence (OQE) traceable to each heat and part (recalled).
6. **Manufacture.** Physics clock: casting tooling and patterns take months, foundry pour plus heat treat take weeks, and first-article inspection follows. Typical Navy casting and forging lead times run 1-3 years (recalled).
7. **NDT.** RT, UT, MT, PT per NAVSEA T9074-AS-GIB-010/271, with acceptance per MIL-STD-2035. Interpretation is by ASNT/NAS-410-type certified Level II/III humans (recalled). RT film or digital radiograph interpretation is a human bottleneck. Inspector shortages are widely reported (recalled).
8. **Government source inspection** by DCMA, SUPSHIP or Navy QARs (cited: GSI applies).
9. **OQE package, marking and shipping.** Paper or PDF certs and Material Identification and Control (MIC) marking (recalled).
10. **Receipt inspection** at the shipyard or naval shipyard. This includes OQE review and sometimes re-testing (positive material identification, chemistry).
11. **Installation and certification.** Work is done under re-entry controls. NAVSEA 07Q runs SUBSAFE certification audits, and the boat is certified for unrestricted operations once the OQE is complete (recalled). Program basis: USS Thresher was lost 10 Apr 1963 with 129 aboard. No SUBSAFE-certified submarine has been lost since; Scorpion (1968) was not fully certified (recalled).

## 3. Where it breaks (bottlenecks)

- **Expert review hours for TDPs and SARs**: "tens of TDPs/quarter vs goal of hundreds" (Austal). Keyport took 7 years for one machine/material procedure. The decision rests with a small number of NAVSEA TWHs and ESA engineers. This is the same shape as aviation DER and DLA ESA queues.
- **Missing tooling and data** for castings and forgings, which are 3% of NSNs but 20% of the 100 oldest DLA backorders. DLA has fragmented tooling databases (DECAF, ICON, NFTD).
- **Spec interpretation**: one clarification of foundry qualification class opened 334 NSNs. Nobody systematically mines specs and flow-down clauses for over-restriction.
- **Supplier activation**: "current pool of activated suppliers cannot meet this growing need". The quality system, special-process qualification and audit cycle are slow and done on paper.
- **OQE integrity**: certs are documents, and fraud has happened. The Bradken (Kaiser foundry, Tacoma) metallurgist falsified test results on about 240 submarine steel castings from 1985-2017. Bradken paid about $10.8M (2021). The metallurgist (Elaine Thomas) was sentenced in 2023 (recalled; verify). Detection came late and by chance, through a lab employee noticing anomalies (recalled). Current receipt inspection would not catch systematic falsification in a statistically rigorous way.
- **NDT interpretation capacity**: human-certified readers, plus no accepted AI-assist in NAVSEA tech pubs (recalled; I know of none).
- **Physics clocks**: destructive tests (tensile, impact, fatigue), pours and heat treats, and shock-qualification tests (MIL-DTL-901E barge or heavyweight shock for mission-critical shipboard equipment, which is expensive and limited by the scarce test barges; recalled).

## 5. What would need to be done (assume unlimited intelligence and compute)

1. **Machine-readable SUBSAFE/Level I rulebook.** Encode NAVSEA 0924-062-0010, 0948-045-7010, MIL-STD-2035, T9074 tech pubs, MIL-STD-1689 and flow-down clauses as executable checks. Every OQE package and TDP can then be pre-adjudicated before a human TWH sees it. Change needed: NAVSEA accepts a tool-checked package as the basis for review. This is cheap to pilot at an ESA.
2. **OQE forensics.** Statistically audit every MTR and test record across heats and suppliers for fabrication patterns (Bradken-style altered values, reused curves, impossible distributions). Cross-check against the physics of the alloy and process. Customers: NAVSEA 07Q, SUPSHIP, EB/HII receipt inspection, DCMA. Data access is the gate. Start with yard receipt-inspection data under a CRADA or OTA.
3. **TDP generator (the "Approval Factory" for AM/castings).** Turn the CT scan of the legacy part plus the drawing into a geometry model. Add alloy/process selection, simulation (solidification, residual stress), a test matrix minimized by Bayesian/ICME process-property models, and an auto-drafted TDP and SAR. The KPI is TDPs per quarter per reviewer, moving from tens to hundreds as the Navy states. The physics clock remains, so batch coupons across many parts sharing one qualified process. That is "qualify the process once, print many parts", the Keyport model, at scale.
4. **In-situ evidence as acceptance.** Fuse melt-pool and thermal sensors with the post-build CT into a per-part "digital certificate" that substitutes for some destructive and NDT steps. Keyport is already spending years on the hardware. The missing piece is the statistical case that NAVSEA can accept: a probability-of-detection study under MIL-HDBK-1823A, which is how NDT methods get accepted (recalled).
5. **AI-assisted NDT interpretation** (RT/UT/PAUT) with a human sign-off retained. Needed: a POD study plus a tech-pub change allowing AI pre-screening. Liability stays with the certified Level III.
6. **Digital tooling recovery.** Reconstruct missing casting patterns from CT of spares and print sand molds (binder-jet; ExOne/Desktop Metal, Foundry Lab). Get "tooling exists" accepted for SARs.
7. **Spec over-restriction mining.** Scan drawings and specs across NSNs for clauses that block sources. The FPS-1001 class issue opened 334 NSNs. Package group waivers or deviations (DLA "339" requests) for ESAs.
8. **Supplier activation copilot.** Auto-generate a quality system, weld procedure specifications and procedure qualification records, and audit-readiness evidence for new suppliers. This attacks "activated suppliers cannot meet need". BlueForge is the channel.

What would change each constraint:
- *Physics*: coupon batching, process-level qualification and in-situ sensing reduce the number of tests, not their duration.
- *Law/regulator*: the NAVSEA TWH is the decider. Win with a pilot at an ESA (NSLC, NSWC Philadelphia/Carderock) under an OTA, SBIR Phase III or NAVSEA CRADA. Navy SIB money is large ($2.5B in FY26 per search) and explicitly targets TDP throughput.
- *Liability*: keep the human TWH or Level III as signatory. The tool is advisory first, and acceptance is then earned through POD and trust data.
- *Data*: design yard (EB) data rights and CUI/NOFORN handling are the gate. NAVSEA is likely to require CMMC Level 2 or 3 and ITAR/NNPI controls. Naval nuclear propulsion parts (NNPI, NAVSEA 08) are a separate, harder regime: avoid it at first.

## 6. Opinion

Naval SUBSAFE/Level I is a strong, underserved verification market. The government states its own KPI (TDPs per quarter) and has budget (SIB billions), yet the review work is still human paper review by a few TWHs. Capital is flooding into the *making* side (Hadrian, Anduril, AML3D) but almost none into the *proving* side. An AI-native verification house would sell to all of those makers as well as to NAVSEA. The wedge is OQE/TDP pre-adjudication plus process-level AM/casting qualification packages, starting with non-nuclear Level I CuNi and 17-4PH fluid-system components.
