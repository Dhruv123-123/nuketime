# c33: Battery, EV and grid equipment certification (UL 9540A, UL 1741, IEEE 1547, NRTL field evaluation): any AI acceleration players; queue times at NRTLs.

Search budget used: 3 WebSearch calls. Everything else came from WebFetch, or is marked (recalled), meaning from memory and not verified this session.

## 1. The pathway (US grid-tied batteries, inverters and EV chargers)

1. **Design to the standard.** UL 1741 (inverters/DER, supplement SB = IEEE 1547.1-2020 grid-support functions), UL 9540 (ESS system safety), UL 9540A (thermal-runaway fire-propagation *test method*, which yields data, not a pass/fail), UL 2202/UL 2594 (EV chargers), UL 1973 (stationary batteries), IEEE 2030.5/CSIP comms (CA Rule 21). Done by the OEM's certification engineer. Hardware startups hire for this directly; e.g. Exowatt's "Senior/Staff Product Certification Engineer" posting lists UL 9540A and UL 1741 and asks for an NRTL track record with UL, CSA or Intertek (https://jobs.lever.co/exowatt/961ffe1e-494c-4308-bbf1-2032be5baee9).
2. **Choose an NRTL and get a quote.** OSHA recognizes the NRTLs (UL Solutions, CSA, Intertek/ETL, TUV SUD/Rheinland, SGS, QPS, etc.; roughly 18-20 of them, recalled). Each lab is recognized for a defined scope of standards and uses its own mark (https://www.osha.gov/nationally-recognized-testing-laboratory-program). Recognition of a new NRTL takes years (recalled).
3. **Engineering review and test plan.** The NRTL reviews the construction file and critical components, then writes the test plan.
4. **Testing (the physics clock).**
   - UL 1741 SB / IEEE 1547.1: IREC surveyed 11 manufacturers, 95 inverter families and 3 major NRTLs and estimated 9-12 weeks of test time per inverter family. At 9 weeks, the labs could clear 85% of families in about 54 weeks (pv-magazine 2022, https://www.pv-magazine.com/2022/08/01/us-solar-sector-prepares-to-meet-smart-inverter-requirements/). SunSpec projected about 50 companies and about 1,000 products needing 10-12 weeks each, "a huge surge in demand" on NRTLs (https://sunspec.org/getting-ready-for-the-countdown-to-ul-1741-certification-the-race-to-market/). UL said the 1547.1-2020 standard runs 263 pages, versus 54 for the first edition (QualityLogic 2021, https://www.qualitylogic.com/2021/04/19/the-race-to-get-ieee-1547-1-2020-implemented-ul-1741-sb-certified-is-on/).
   - Automation evidence: Pacific Power Source's SmartTS-PV with QualityLogic software (April 30, 2026) claims about 32 hours of actual test time, against IREC's 8-12 weeks of manual NRTL testing (https://chargedevs.com/?p=616499). So most of those weeks are lab scheduling and manual sequencing, not physics.
   - UL 9540A has four levels: cell, module, unit, installation. The 6th edition drops unit-level testing except for residential BESS and systems with an active TRPPS, and adds a large-scale fire test that assumes a post-deflagration condition (https://www.ul.com/services/ul-9540a-test-method). UL publishes no cost or lead time. Recalled: cell level costs tens of thousands of dollars; unit and installation level run several hundred thousand dollars per burn; large-scale fire tests exceed $1M and are done at few sites (UL, CSA, Intertek, DNV Spadeadam, FRA/ESRG, SwRI). Each burn destroys the hardware. Quoted lead times for unit and large-scale burn slots run months (recalled).
5. **Certificate issuance and listing.** About 1-2 weeks for the certificate. The California Energy Commission's (CEC) qualified-equipment list takes about 1 month. IREC's rule of thumb was to add about 18 weeks to test time for certificate, listing and logistics (IREC, Lydic, NY DPS deck: https://dps.ny.gov/irec-ul-1741-sb-compliance-timeline). Shipping logistics alone run 2-6 months, 3 typical.
6. **Utility interconnection and AHJ approval.** Utilities check UL 1741 SB settings profiles, and AHJs/fire marshals review the UL 9540A report against NFPA 855 and IFC spacing exceptions. Fire marshals do this case by case, and nobody does it uniformly. This is where the UL 9540A *data* gets judged.
7. **Field evaluation (NFPA 790/791).** Covers unlisted, modified or one-off equipment: a field-evaluation body (IAS AC354-accredited) inspects and labels on site. Recalled: roughly $3-15k per unit and weeks to schedule. This is common for custom BESS, imported chargers and modified switchgear.
8. **Follow-up services.** Quarterly factory inspections, plus re-certification whenever a component (cell supplier, firmware) changes, which is the big recurring drag.

## 2. Players (AI or acceleration)

- **Fuchsia** (YC Spring 2026, SF, founder Tony Gao, team of 3): "Vanta for hardware". AI agents draft technical files and test plans, track requirements and talk to partner labs. Covers EMC, environmental, functional safety, electrical and machinery, for robots, medical and IoT. No grid/BESS focus stated. https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/fuchsia (the /noetic URL resolves to the same page).
- **Hardware Compliance** (hardwarecompliance.com, launched about March 2026): an AI platform that reads standards and drafts technical files, test plans and hazard analyses, then matches products to labs, with expert review. Lists UL 3100, UL 3300, IEC 62368-1, FCC, CE, 510(k), ISO 26262, FAA and MIL-STD. No 9540A/1741 named.
- **QualityLogic, Pacific Power Source and Tektronix EA**: automated IEEE 1547.1/UL 1741 SB test hardware and software. Claims about 32 hours versus 8-12 weeks.
- **SunSpec Alliance**: Modbus and IEEE 2030.5 conformance programs, and the open-source SVP (System Validation Platform) test harness (recalled).
- **UL Solutions** (public since its 2024 IPO): incumbent NRTL. In November 2025 it launched UL 3115 AI-safety certification for AI-embedded products; first certificates went to the Qcells data-center EMS and Omniconn (Fortune, March 19, 2026). UL is positioning itself to certify AI, not to use AI to shorten its own queue.
- **Battery analytics** (adjacent; the data could feed safety cases): TWAICE, ACCURE, Zitara, Voltaiq, Electra Vehicles (recalled). They do field-fleet degradation and safety-risk analytics for insurers and owners. None, to my knowledge, produces UL 9540A-equivalent evidence an AHJ accepts.
- **Simulation**: Ansys, ThermoAnalytics, NIST FDS / FM Global (recalled). Thermal-runaway propagation modelling is used in design but is not accepted in lieu of burns.

No player found who (a) shortens the NRTL queue as a lab-side operator, (b) makes UL 9540A evidence reusable or simulated in a form that AHJs and NRTLs accept, or (c) automates AHJ/fire-marshal review of 9540A reports.

## 3. What nobody does, or does poorly

- **Change-driven recertification.** A cell-supplier swap or firmware change re-triggers testing. There is no accepted "delta certification" method that uses modelling plus a reduced test set.
- **Reuse of UL 9540A data.** Results are tied to a specific cell, module and enclosure. Labs hold the data; there is no shared library of cell thermal-runaway data across OEMs.
- **AHJ review.** Thousands of fire marshals interpret NFPA 855 differently, with no tool and no consistency.
- **Lab scheduling and automation.** The test itself takes about 32 hours, against 9-12 weeks calendar plus about 18 weeks of administration. Most NRTL time is queue and manual work.
- **Field evaluation.** Fragmented, manual and local. Paper reports.
- **UL 1741 SB grid-settings conformance across utilities.** Each utility has its own profile; checking is manual.

## 4. What would need to change (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)

- **Physics clock.** Burns must physically happen for the first article. To change that: build a validated thermal-runaway propagation model, calibrated on hundreds of public and private burns, then get the NRTL and the standards technical panel (UL STP 9540) to accept "test once at cell level and model the configurations", the way FAA accepts analysis plus a reduced test set. That needs a corpus of burn data. Nobody owns one outside the labs, so a company would have to fund and run its own burns (owning a fire-test site is plausible: about $10-30M, recalled).
- **Law and acceptance.** NFPA 855, the IFC and the AHJ are the binding acceptors. Get a "performance-based design by analysis" pathway written into NFPA 855 or the IFC (code cycles run 3 years; the 2029 edition proposals are the target).
- **Liability.** An NRTL mark carries liability. A new entrant either becomes an NRTL (OSHA recognition, multi-year) or a field-evaluation body (IAS accreditation, faster), or partners with an existing one as an AI-run "lab within a lab".
- **Data access.** Cell OEMs (CATL, BYD, LG) guard their thermal-runaway data. A neutral data trust tied to insurers (FM Global, AXA XL), who are motivated by BESS fires such as Moss Landing in January 2025, could break this.

## 5. Opinion: the AI plays on the verification side

1. **AI-native field-evaluation body or NRTL for energy hardware.** Get IAS AC354 accreditation, then OSHA NRTL scope for UL 1741, 9540, 2202 and 1973. Use automated test rigs (the 32-hour benches) and AI-written reports, and sell weeks instead of quarters. Approvals here are a capacity bottleneck held by permission, which is the same shape as the Approval Factory, and this market has sharper demand (data-center BESS, V2G, FEOC-driven cell-supplier swaps that force recertification).
2. **Delta-certification engine.** Model-based impact assessment for component changes, to cut recert scope. Sell it to OEMs and NRTLs.
3. **9540A burn-data library and propagation surrogate.** Run your own burns, build a model, and push for code acceptance.
4. **AHJ copilot.** Review 9540A reports and site plans against NFPA 855 for fire marshals; it could be free to AHJs and paid by developers.
5. **UL 1741 SB / utility-profile conformance as a service.**

Competition: Fuchsia and Hardware Compliance sit on the paperwork and lab-matching layer. That layer is commoditising, and they don't hold permission. Nobody is on the lab or acceptance layer.

Relation to the Approval Factory: this is the same thesis (permission is scarce, the KPI is hours per approval). The difference is that the acceptor is a private NRTL plus a local AHJ, not the FAA or DLA. The private body is faster to become (field-evaluation accreditation takes months to a year; NRTL takes years, recalled). Market size is a guess: global testing, inspection and certification (TIC) for energy storage and inverters is low single-digit $B. UL Solutions' total revenue is about $2.9B for 2024 (recalled).
