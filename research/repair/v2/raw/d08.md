### Regulator fees (what the government itself charges)
- **NRC: $337/professional staff-hour** (10 CFR 170.20(a)). Advanced reactor applicants and pre-applicants get **$154/hr** (170.20(b),(c), ADVANCE Act; the pre-applicant clause sunsets 2030-09-30). https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/10/170.20. FY2026 fee rule published in FR 2026-06-16 (https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/regulatory/licensing/fees.html). Topical reports and vendor reviews are billed hourly at this rate. A topical report commonly runs hundreds to thousands of NRC hours, i.e. $0.3-3M+ (recalled order of magnitude).
- **FAA: charges no user fees** for type certification, STC, PMA or repair approvals (recalled; statutory). The price of FAA approval is paid entirely in *time and private engineering*: DER/ODA labor and the queue for FAA project managers. That is why DER/ODA labor has pricing power: it is the only way to buy speed.
- **EASA: does charge**, under Commission Implementing Regulation (EU) 2019/2153 (flat fees per STC/major change/repair by aircraft class plus an hourly rate). The EUR-Lex fetch returned empty, so no numbers. (recalled) Hourly rate is around EUR 250-300. Major repair and STC fees range from a few thousand EUR to six figures depending on aircraft weight class.

## 4. Interpretation (opinion)
1. **The margin ladder follows how mandatory and how scarce the sign-off is, not how much engineering goes into it.** At the bottom, commoditized certified labor (Mistras) earns ~12% at ~9.5x. Diversified TIC earns 15-18%. Then come mark owners with mandated recognition (UL Industrial ~35%), and finally owners of the approved design or part itself (HEICO FSG 24% operating margin, OEM aftermarket ~50%). The approval factory should aim to *own the resulting approval* (PMA/SAR/TDP), not to sell approval labor. The DER-hours business is a Mistras-like services margin. Owning the PMA is a HEICO-like franchise.
2. **Recurring "keep-valid" revenue is the hidden prize.** UL's Ongoing Certification Services (~33% of revenue) is paid for staying approved: follow-up audits and label fees. Aviation and nuclear analogs include continued airworthiness (ICA upkeep), PMA quality-system audits, NUPIC audits, CGD re-dedication when a supplier changes, and Part 21 evaluations. An AI-native verifier that makes continuous compliance cheap could capture this annuity.
3. **FAA charges nothing, so the scarce input is reviewer time.** In the US the price of an approval is set by DER/ODA labor and FAA queue time. Whoever compresses review time owns the margin. At the NRC the government bills $337/hr, so applicants pay directly for every hour of reviewer confusion. A cleaner, machine-checkable submission saves real dollars, and the saving is measurable (hours billed in fee-recovery invoices are public per docket).
4. **CGD is the starkest pricing-power case.** Markups of 3-20x on commercial parts exist purely because the dedicator accepts 10 CFR Part 21 liability and holds an App. B program. The same physical part plus a verification dossier and an assumed liability is the whole product. That maps directly onto an AI verification play: generate the dedication plan (critical characteristics, acceptance methods), run the tests, and assemble the dossier. The licence and the liability stay with a human-signed QA program you own.

## 5. What it would take to change the binding constraints
- **Law:** FAA delegation (14 CFR 183, ODA under 183 Subpart D) allows an organization to hold approval authority. An AI-native ODA is legally possible today: the human unit members sign and the AI does the substantiation. NRC Part 21 and App. B do not forbid automation; they require a controlled QA program. Change needed: FAA/NRC guidance accepting validated software tools inside the substantiation (analogous to DO-330 tool qualification) as qualified engineering tools.
- **Regulator acceptance:** NRC billing records per docket show how many hours a review consumed. Build a public dataset of "hours per review type" and show that AI-prepared packages cut RAIs (requests for additional information). For FAA, track the issue-paper count and the time from application to approval.
- **Liability:** CGD and NRTL margins exist because someone signs. To get those margins you have to carry the liability (insurance plus a QA program), not just sell software to the people who do.
- **Physics clocks:** fatigue, EQ, fire and large-scale 9540A tests take weeks. Capacity is scarce and priced at $150-400/lab-hour. Running a lab with simulation-driven test minimization (fewer specimens, test-to-validate-model) is a margin lever.
- **Data access:** OEM drawings are unavailable, so you reverse-engineer. NRTL test data is proprietary to the lab. FAA PMA approvals are public in DRS/PMA database, which allows targeting.

## 6. Who sits where (verification side)
- UL Solutions, CSA, Intertek, TÜV, SGS, BV, Element, NTS: own test capacity and marks. Adjacent threat; they could become acquirers or partners.
