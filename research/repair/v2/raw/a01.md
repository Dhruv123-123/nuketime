## 2. What was actually automated
1. **Evidence collection.** Read-only API connectors to AWS, GCP, Azure, Okta, Google Workspace, GitHub, Jira, HR systems (Rippling, Gusto) and MDM agents on laptops. These replace screenshots and spreadsheets that an engineer used to assemble by hand.
2. **Continuous control monitoring.** Tests run daily, for example "MFA on for all users", "S3 buckets not public" and "offboarding within N days". Failures appear as tasks, so the 3-12 month Type II observation window is passed with fewer exceptions.
3. **Templated policies and control sets** mapped to the AICPA Trust Services Criteria (2017 TSC). One control can map to many frameworks, so each extra framework costs little.
4. **Workflow for the human-only items**, such as security training, background checks, vendor reviews, and policy acceptance by employees.
5. **The auditor portal.** The audit firm gets read-only access to the same evidence, with populations and timestamps. Fieldwork moves from email back-and-forth to a portal.
6. **Downstream "trust" outputs.** Trust centers and security-questionnaire answering (AI-heavy since 2023). This is the expansion revenue.

What was NOT automated: the attestation opinion itself. A licensed CPA firm still signs the SOC 2 report under AICPA attestation standards (SSAE 18 / AT-C 105 and 205) (recalled). Vanta sells "audit-ready", not "audited".

## 3. Why auditors accepted it (mostly inference from structure; partly recalled)
- **No rule change was needed.** SOC 2 has no government regulator. The market (enterprise procurement) sets demand and the AICPA sets the standard. The company designs its own controls, and the auditor only has to judge whether evidence is sufficient and appropriate. System-generated evidence pulled by API is arguably *more* reliable than screenshots. The auditor still has to assess the reliability of the evidence the client produces, and tool vendors answer that with their own SOC 2 reports and API provenance.
- **Auditors gained more than they lost.** The platforms built auditor partner networks and sent customers to them. Audits got cheaper per engagement (recalled: about $5-15k for a Type I at partner firms, against $20-50k+ before), but volume rose sharply and fieldwork hours fell. Mid-tier and boutique CPA firms got deal flow. The tool made the auditor more productive without taking the signature.
- **The tool vendor carries no attestation liability.** Liability stays with the CPA and the management assertion. The vendor's risk is reputational.
- **Buyers accept the report, not the process.** Procurement teams check that a SOC 2 Type II exists and who signed it. Few check depth.
- **Risk now visible: race to the bottom.** Cheap "audit mill" firms and very fast, AI-generated evidence have drawn criticism in the industry over quality. This is the failure mode to avoid in safety-critical hardware (opinion; specific allegations unverified in this session).

The core dynamic: **the approval stayed human and licensed. The product removed about 80-90% of the applicant's preparation hours and a large share of the reviewer's fieldwork hours, then bundled itself with a channel to reviewers.** That is exactly the "engineering plus reviewer hours per approval" KPI.

## 4. Mapping to hardware certification (FAA PMA/DER, DLA SAR)

| SOC 2 element | Hardware analog | Gap |
|---|---|---|
| Company designs its own controls against the TSC | Applicant shows compliance with 14 CFR 21 Subpart K (PMA) and airworthiness standards (Part 25/33 etc.) through a compliance matrix | Similar |
| CPA firm (private, paid by the applicant, licensed) | FAA DER/ODA (private, paid by the applicant, delegated under FAA Orders 8100.8/8110.37 (DER) and 8100.15 (ODA), recalled). For DLA SAR the Engineering Support Activity is a **government** engineer, not paid by the applicant | Good analog for the DER route; poor for DLA, where the reviewer is a capacity-limited civil servant |
| Evidence pulled by API from cloud systems | Evidence is physical: material certs, dimensional inspection (CMM), NDT, coupon tests, fatigue and endurance runs, conformity inspections (FAA Form 8130-9), first-article inspection (AS9102) | **Evidence is not natively digital or tamper-evident.** Biggest gap |
| Continuous monitoring over the Type II window | Physical test clocks: fatigue cycles, environmental qualification (DO-160), engine endurance | The clock is physics and can't be cut, but it can run in parallel and be instrumented |
| Templated control sets | Templated compliance plans and test plans per part family (e.g., PMA by test and computation vs. identicality) | A large reuse opportunity across part families |
| Auditor portal | DER/ESA review package: TDP to MIL-STD-31000, drawings, analysis reports | Today a PDF and email; no structured review portal |
| Auditor network as channel | Consultant DER network (several thousand DERs, recalled) | The DER channel exists and is fragmented: no marketplace |
| Market acceptance (procurement) | Regulator acceptance (FAA ACO, DLA ESA) plus OEM and airline engineering acceptance | Higher bar; liability under products law; Part 21 production approval |

## 6. What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute)
1. **Evidence connectors for physical tests.** Direct capture from CMM, CT scanners, spectrometers, tensile and fatigue rigs and NDT stations, with cryptographic signing and chain of custody. This makes physical evidence as trustworthy as an API pull, so a DER can trust the data without witnessing every test.
2. **A machine-readable compliance case.** Requirements to analysis to test to conformity as a graph, in place of PDF stacks. Output in DER/ACO and DLA ESA formats (MIL-STD-31000 TDP, FAA 8110-3 forms).
3. **A pre-review agent** that reads the package the way an ESA/DER would and flags gaps before submission. It is trained on rejection reasons. Data access needed: DLA SAR disapproval letters and DER review comments, which are not public.
4. **A reviewer-side product** for DLA ESAs and FAA ACO engineers: a structured review queue that cuts reviewer hours. The regulator's capacity, not the applicant's, is the bottleneck for SAR. The SOC 2 analog is the auditor portal, sold or given to the government.
5. **A DER marketplace and channel**, as Vanta did with CPA firms, so DERs get deal flow and the platform gets distribution.
6. **Part-family templates.** One test-and-computation logic reused across hundreds of similar parts (bushings, brackets, seals). This is the source of cost per approval falling over time.
7. **Changing the rules:** DLA's ESA backlog, and whether ESAs would accept digitally signed evidence in place of witnessed First Article Tests. Path: SBIR/OTA pilots with DLA R&D, AFSC or the Rapid Sustainment Office, and an FAA ODA, or an applicant becoming its own ODA holder over time.

## 7. Opinions / implications for the founder
- The Vanta lesson is not "AI does the audit". The lesson is to automate the applicant's preparation, give the licensed reviewer a better portal, and route reviewer volume through yourself. The signature stays human; the hours collapse.
- In hardware, the moat analog is the evidence-integrity layer: signed physical test data. Templates and LLM drafting are easy to copy; trusted capture from test equipment, accepted by DERs and ESAs, is not.
- Revenue math: SOC 2 tools get about $19k ARR per customer across 16k customers. PMA/SAR packages are worth $100k+ each, but the market is thousands of approvals a year, not tens of thousands of companies. So either own the approvals (the Approval Factory, which earns part margin) or sell tooling to the DER/PMA houses (HEICO, Wencor, many small PMA shops) and to reviewers. Hybrid: use the tool internally first, as Thoropass bundles the audit.
