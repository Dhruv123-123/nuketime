# c01: Startups using AI to produce FAA PMA, DER repair or STC substantiation packages (compliance reports, test plans, drawings). Every company 2022-2026, funding, traction.

Date of research: 2026-10-20. Method: 3 WebSearch calls (all returned nothing directly on point), then Google News RSS (many queries), Hacker News Algolia API, YC directory pages, company sites, WordPress JSON APIs of trade outlets. Figures marked (recalled) are from memory, not re-verified today.

## Bottom line (evidence-based)

**I found no startup, 2022-2026, that sells or produces FAA PMA, DER (8110-3) repair or STC substantiation packages with AI as its core business.** Many targeted queries ("AI" + PMA / Parts Manufacturer Approval / DER / Designated Engineering Representative / Form 8110-3 / STC / source approval / DLA reverse engineering) returned zero relevant startup news. The incumbents doing PMA/DER work (HEICO, Wencor, Jet Parts Engineering, Chromalloy, Aerospace Design & Compliance, JDA, TxAero, Metrea, Amphenol CIT) show no announced AI-native approval workflow. A negative search result is not proof that nobody is doing this. A stealth team could exist. But nobody has a public product, round or traction.

What does exist falls into five adjacent clusters, none of which owns the approval:
1. **Generic AI "certification copilots" for aerospace documentation** (Xcert AI). They are tiny, Europe-based and focused on new programs/space, not PMA/DER.
2. **AI for testing/inspection/certification bodies and auditors** (Calibre). This is the reviewer side, not aviation-specific.
3. **AI for manufacturing quality paperwork** (GroundControl AS9102 FAI, F4 Industries GD&T/FAI, Rapta in-station inspection). These cover production conformity, which is a downstream piece of a PMA (the QMS/FAI), not design approval.
4. **AI reverse engineering of geometry** (Novineer NoviVision; Hexagon scanners; Physna (recalled)). This is the "knowledge" step that gives the TDP its geometry, but it stops at CAD.
5. **Well-funded hardware V&V/data platforms** (Nominal $1B valuation, Sift, Flow Engineering $750M valuation, Istari Digital, PhysicsX). These build the evidence and traceability layer that certification depends on, and none does aftermarket approvals.

## Company-by-company

### Xcert AI (Geneva, CH), the closest direct match
- Founded 2024 by Marc-Elian Begin (CEO) and Lawrence Blakeley (Board Chair). Has 12 employees.
- Raised CHF 1.1M from Spicehaus Partners, announced 28 Nov 2025 (Startupticker: https://www.startupticker.ch/en/news/xcert-ai-secures-chf-1-1-million-to-advance-aerospace-ai-certification-platform).
- Product: an "AI Co-Pilot for Certification". It generates and manages compliance documentation, requirements, planning and test procedures, with V-model flow-down, test-procedure generation and traceability matrices (https://www.xcert.ai). The site does not mention FAA, DO-178 or PMA. EASA appears only in event news.
- Traction: ESA demo (requirements for mission control systems), ZeroAvia collaboration on Xcert 1.2 (EIN Presswire 12 Oct 2026) and "a small number of key aerospace-ecosystem customers". The Air Current covered it on 16 Feb 2026 ("wants to save human experts time, not replace them").
- Claim: up to half of aircraft and spacecraft development time and cost goes to certification and compliance (the company's claim).
- Threat to the Approval Factory: adjacent. It is a tool vendor, not an approval holder.

### Calibre (London), the reviewer/auditor side
- Ex-Palantir founders Gautham Senthilnathan and Steve Thomas. Raised $3.3M (GBP 2.4M) pre-seed on 18 May 2026, led by Vicus Ventures and CIV, with I2BF, 9 Yards and Jigeum, plus Nikesh Arora as an angel (https://www.trendingtopics.eu/calibre-founded-by-ex-palantirs-in-london-raises-3-3m-for-ai-testing/).
- Builds AI agents embedded in TIC (testing, inspection, certification) organizations. They review documents, generate audit reports against standards and match requirements to norms. Product: "AuditorOS" for independent auditors, with general availability planned for June 2026.
- Calibre claims the TIC market is over $200B and that certification takes 3-6 months.
- Relevance: this is the only startup found that targets the *reviewer* (verification) seat rather than the applicant. It is not aviation-specific.

### GroundControl (YC Spring 2025, SF)
- Automates AS9102 First Article Inspection reports. Claims more than 70 manufacturing facilities and almost 1,000 reports per month. Team of 8. Founders Mehul Shah (CEO) and Matthew Noseworthy (CTO, previously founded and exited inspectAR). No funding disclosed beyond YC. Plans to expand into operations, engineering and sales. Sources: https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/groundcontrol and AeroXplorer 28 Jul 2026.
- Relevance: production-conformity paperwork. A PMA needs a fabrication inspection system and FAI, so this is the downstream piece only.

### F4 Industries (YC Summer 2025, SF)
- GD&T drawing checks, tolerance stack-ups, and generation of stack-up tables and FAI reports. It deliberately uses deterministic analysis with no AI. Founders are ex-Tesla and ex-SpaceX. Team of 5. No funding disclosed (https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/f4-industries, https://f4.dev).
- Relevance: drawing-quality verification, part of the TDP, but not approval.

### Rapta
- Raised an $8M seed (Sep 2026, Defense Post / AI Insider / Dealroom). It runs camera and sensor in-station inspection against a known-good build and archives a photo record per unit. Claims 5 of the top 10 defense contractors use it. Logos include Elbit and Knight's Armament (https://www.rapta.ai, vendor claims).
- Relevance: conformity evidence.

### Novineer (Daytona Beach area; founded 2022 by Dr. Ali Tamijani, Embry-Riddle professor)
- NoviVision produces an editable STEP file from 1-4 smartphone photos in about 2 minutes. Validated average deviation is 0.72mm (max 2.3mm) on a 100mm part. NoviPath is integrated in Stratasys GrabCAD Print Pro (Dec 2025). NoviDesign does generative redesign of obsolete parts. It has a partnership with AM Craft (Apr 2026) and "corporate agreements" with the US Army. The founder's research drew more than $2.5M in federal funding (NSF, AFOSR, NASA, ONR) (Defense Post op-ed by the CEO, 30 Jun 2026: https://thedefensepost.com/2026/06/30/novineer-ai-reverse-engineering-sustainment/).
- The founder states it himself: the open question is "how quickly organizations can update the workflows, approvals, and technical data governance processes". In other words, they do geometry and leave the permission problem unsolved. Army TAPS requires approved technical data.
- The 0.72mm average error is far too coarse for flight-critical PMA tolerances. It fits quoting and fit checks only.

### Saphira AI
- Its AI agents turn failure investigations and risks into traceable requirements and validation plans (FMEA, HARA, TARA). Customers are robotics firms (1X, Raise, RobCo, Dexterity). It went through the CDL Seattle accelerator (Jun 2025 graduate). No aviation work. Show HN Nov 2024 (https://www.saphira.ai).

### SysModeler.ai
- AI-generated SysML v2 models with requirement traceability. Safety analysis (FMEA/STPA/FTA) is still on its roadmap. No funding info. Show HN Jun/Aug 2025.

### Flow Engineering (SF)
- Raised a $50M Series B at a $750M valuation (30 Sep 2026), led by Valor and Atreides with Sequoia. Sequoia led the Series A in Oct 2025. Its agents align CAD with requirements, simulation and tests. Customers: Anduril, Rivian, Joby, GM PPU, RV Tech, Stoke (TechCrunch 30 Sep 2026).
- Relevance: requirements-to-verification traceability for OEM new designs. Not aftermarket and not approvals.

### Nominal (LA)
- Raised a $75M Series B (Sep 2025, Sequoia) and an $80M B-2 at a $1B valuation (Mar 2026, Founders Fund), about $155M in 10 months. It is a test and evaluation software platform. It says it landed 4 of the top 5 defense primes, plus Anduril and Antares (TechCrunch 5 Mar 2026).
- Relevance: the test-evidence layer. This is the most likely company to move up into "certification evidence".

### Sift (El Segundo)
- Ex-SpaceX founders. Raised $7.5M seed (Nov 2023), $17.5M Series A (Jun 2024) and $42M Series B (Mar 2026). It is a telemetry and test-data platform (SpaceNews, TechCrunch).

### Istari Digital (Arlington VA)
- Raised a $22.6M Series B (Jul 2026) toward a $40M target. It had a $19M Air Force contract in 2023 for the "first digitally certified airplane", plus $9M added in 2025, with Lockheed Skunk Works on the X-56A (2024). It launched "Industry One" with the Department of the Air Force (May 2026). Pitch: "validate and certify system integrity" through digital engineering.
- Relevance: the only funded company explicitly attacking certification-by-model with a regulator (USAF airworthiness). This is the right idea, but for new military airframes, not PMA/DER.

### PhysicsX (London)
- AI simulation for engineering, a "deeptech unicorn" that opened a Singapore hub in Oct 2026. Raised $135M Series B in Jun 2025 (recalled). Relevance: surrogate analysis that could replace some substantiation analysis.

### SkySelect (Estonia)
- AI parts procurement for airlines and MROs. Raised $9M in Mar 2026. It sits on the demand side and does not create approvals.

## Demand signals (evidence)
- **UPS (IFS Unleashed, Oct 2026):** "Alternative parts has been a big focus for the last year." UPS is exploring AI to find alternatives but has not said whether that means PMA. Lessors sometimes require PMA parts to be removed at lease return (IFS outlook, Dec 2025). Source: https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ups-explores-ai-to-find-alternative-aircraft-parts-amid-maintenance-shortages/
- **Oliver Wyman 2026 MRO survey:** MRO spend exceeded $136B in 2025 (+8%). Material inflation ran 100-200bp above expectations. About 90% of respondents saw tariff impacts.
- **FAA delegation:** Boeing's airworthiness-certificate delegation was restored in Sep 2025 and Jul 2026 (HN and CNBC). This shows that delegation is the scarce lever.

## The verification-side gap (opinion, derived from the above)
Every funded player sits either on the *applicant authoring* side (Xcert, Flow), on the *evidence generation* side (Nominal, Sift, Rapta, GroundControl) or on the *geometry recovery* side (Novineer). **Nobody sits on the reviewer/approver side of FAA/DLA aftermarket approvals.** The reviewer seats are:
- the FAA ACO engineer
- the DER signing an 8110-3
- the ODA unit member
- DLA/ESA engineering-support-activity reviewers of SARs

Calibre attacks the analogous seat in general TIC and Istari attacks it for USAF new airframes.

Concrete things nobody does today:
1. **An AI pre-review / "shadow DER" that checks a substantiation package against the certification basis** (14 CFR 25.xxx / 23 / 33 by part and by aircraft type certificate data sheet) and against FAA Order 8110.42 (PMA) / 8110.37 (DER) / AC 21-43. It would output a findings list in the format the ACO uses. You could sell it first to applicants (fewer review cycles) and then propose it to the FAA as a reviewer tool.
2. **A machine-readable certification basis**: the TCDS, the amendment level of each CFR section, ADs, SBs and FAA policy memos compiled into a queryable graph keyed by part number and next-higher assembly. Today a human DER reconstructs this for every package.
3. **Test-by-similarity / test-by-analysis evidence libraries**: a reusable, regulator-accepted dataset of material allowables, coupon tests and prior PMA test-by-analysis precedents, so each package cites prior accepted evidence instead of re-running tests. Physics clocks remain for fatigue, burn and environmental tests. The lever is to share and accept results across packages (MMPDS/CMH-17-style pooled data, already precedent).
4. **Credentialed AI-assisted delegation**: an ODA (Organization Designation Authorization) that holds PMA/repair authority itself. Its internal reviewers are AI-augmented under an FAA-accepted procedures manual. This is the legal route that turns "AI reviewer" into "permission". HEICO and others already hold ODA-like delegation. A startup would need about 2-4 years and a track record of non-ODA approvals first (judgment).
5. **DLA/ESA SAR review-time compression**: SAR evaluations by engineering support activities are the bottleneck, often 6-18+ months (recalled). An AI that pre-scores SARs against the ESA's checklists for Category IIC/III/IV would shorten that. It could also be offered to DLA itself via SBIR/OTA.
6. **Conformity and traceability chain-of-evidence**: the Rapta/GroundControl-style per-unit record tied to the approved design is the PMA fabrication inspection system made automatic. This is an anti-counterfeit play: the AOG Technics forged-documents scandal (2023) exposed the paper trail as the weak point.
