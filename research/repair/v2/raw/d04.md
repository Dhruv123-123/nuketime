## 1. Nuclear power: the clearest regulated "verification pathway" outside aerospace and defense

### The law (evidence)
- 10 CFR Part 21 and 10 CFR 50 Appendix B. A commercial-grade item (CGI) can go into a safety-related application only after **dedication**: an acceptance process that gives reasonable assurance the item will perform its safety function. The dedicator verifies **critical characteristics** using EPRI methods: (1) special tests and inspections, (2) commercial-grade survey of the supplier, (3) source verification, (4) supplier performance record. GL 89-02 conditionally endorsed EPRI NP-5652. It said Method 2 cannot stand alone for suppliers with weak quality control, and Method 4 cannot stand alone unless it rests on industry-wide data and audited change control. (GL 89-02, fetched.)
- Appendix B Criterion III ("Design Control") already lets design adequacy be verified by **"design reviews, ... alternate or simplified calculation methods, or ... a suitable testing program."** (IN 2016-09, fetched.) **This matters: the law already accepts analysis, not only physical test.** The limit is the dedicator's ability to show that the analysis covers every functional requirement.
- NRC RG 1.164 (2017) endorses EPRI 3002002982 (2014) as the updated CGD guideline (recalled). EPRI TR-107372 (1998) is the "Guideline for Reverse Engineering at Nuclear Power Plants" (cited in IN 2016-09, fetched).

### The pain (evidence)
- IN 2016-09: "For various reasons, including obsolescence, cost, and extended delivery schedules, licensees and their suppliers are **increasingly using reverse engineering** techniques to manufacture replacement safety-related components." Deficiencies the NRC listed:
  - "not developing a full understanding of design requirements"
  - assuming the reverse-engineered part is identical to the OEM part
  - "assessing only the physical attributes ... without properly evaluating functional design requirements"
  - not passing requirements down to the supplier
  - not verifying by test or analysis
  - unclear responsibility split
- Concrete failures named in IN 2016-09:
  - **Callaway Unit 1**: reverse-engineered Modutronics controller cards from Nuclear Logistics Inc. had undersized rectifier bridges. Two cards failed in service and auxiliary feedwater valves were inoperable.
  - **River Bend**: 18 reverse-engineered Tobul control-rod-drive accumulators were installed. The equivalency evaluation did not verify flow, leakage, pressure, stroke time, temperature or seismic qualification.
  - **NOVA Machine Products**: about **881** safety-related HCU accumulator assemblies were reverse engineered and built with no design verification.
- The NRC's own diagnosis (fetched): "physical examination alone is typically not sufficient to identify all the functional requirements. Identifying all functional requirements necessitates a full understanding of the intended application of the component, interface requirements, environmental parameters..." **The bottleneck is requirements recovery (the design basis), not geometry.** This is the opening for an AI verification play.

### Spend and scale (recalled; verify)
- About 94 operating US reactors, now on licence extensions to 60 and 80 years. Most were designed in the 1960s-70s, and many original suppliers have left the nuclear market. ASME N-stamp holders fell from about 600 (1980s) to about 200 (2000s) (recalled).
- NEI "Delivering the Nuclear Promise" (2015) aimed to cut generating cost about 30%. Industry-average cost is about $30-32/MWh, of which O&M is the largest part (recalled).
- Per-item dedication cost is often several times the commercial price. A typical dedication package for a modest component runs ~$5k-$50k+ of engineering, testing and paperwork, and complex reverse-engineered items reach six figures (recalled/opinion). EPRI and utilities report obsolescence as a top plant-reliability issue. EPRI runs POMS/RAPID obsolescence databases and a plant-support engineering programme (recalled).
- Joint buyer infrastructure exists: **NUPIC** (Nuclear Procurement Issues Corporation) runs shared supplier audits for essentially all US utilities (recalled). This is a template for "audit once, accept everywhere".
- Additive manufacturing (AM) firsts:
  - Westinghouse 3D-printed thimble plugging device installed at Byron (2020) (recalled).
  - ORNL/Framatome 3D-printed fuel-assembly channel fasteners at TVA Browns Ferry (2021) (recalled).
  - NRC has an Advanced Manufacturing Technologies action plan and AM guidance documents (recalled).

### Who does dedication and reverse engineering today (recalled)
- Curtiss-Wright Nuclear Division (QualTech NP, Scientech): dedication, test labs, obsolescence replacements.
- Paragon Energy Solutions: obsolescence and reverse-engineered safety-related electronics. *Uncertain:* I recall Nuclear Logistics Inc. (ex-AZZ) was folded into Paragon or a similar vehicle.
- Westinghouse, Framatome, GE Hitachi: OEMs and dedicators.
- Constellation (ex-Exelon) PowerLabs: utility in-house dedication lab. Other utility labs exist.
- Ultra Energy / United Controls, Ametek and other small specialist dedicators.
- No AI-native dedication house is known. The work is done by engineers with Word templates, and the EPRI guidance is the playbook.

## 2. Gas turbines and power generation (contractual and insurer gatekeeping, not a regulator)

### Structure (recalled unless noted)
- Heavy-duty gas turbine aftermarket (parts, repairs and services) is about $20-25B/yr worldwide. GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and Mitsubishi Power hold about 80%+ through long-term service agreements (LTSAs/CSAs).
- GE Vernova's total 2025 revenue was $38.07B (fetched). Services are the majority of Gas Power segment profit (recalled).
- Non-OEM ("alternative parts") share is about 10-20% of hot-gas-path spend (recalled). It is concentrated on mature frames (GE 7FA/7EA/6B/9E, Siemens V84/V94/SGT-800, Westinghouse 501F).
- Non-OEM players:
  - **PSM (Power Systems Mfg.)**: Hanwha bought it from Ansaldo Energia in 2021 (recalled).
  - **EthosEnergy**: Wood/Siemens JV formed 2014, later owned by Wood and then sold to a PE owner (recalled; verify).
  - **Sulzer Services**: rotating-equipment repair.
  - **Chromalloy**: industrial gas turbine coatings and parts.
  - **Liburdi**: blade repair and welding.
  - **Doncasters, Allied Power Group, TurboCare, MD&A, Gas Turbine Parts & Services, Siemens/MHI cross-frame repairs**.
- **Gate:** no regulator is involved. Permission comes from:
  - the plant owner's engineering sign-off
  - the **insurer** (FM Global, HSB/Munich Re, Allianz) and lender's engineer
  - the OEM LTSA terms, which often void coverage if non-OEM parts are fitted
- Qualification evidence for a non-OEM blade or vane set:
  - metallurgy (single-crystal or directionally solidified castings, coatings)
  - CMM geometry
  - airflow, frequency and natural-frequency (ping) tests
  - often a **lead-unit field trial** of 8,000-24,000 fired hours before fleet roll-out (recalled)
- **Physics clock:** the lead-unit trial. **Liability clock:** a buyer of a $3-10M hot-gas-path set wants a warranty from a balance sheet it trusts.

### Pain
- OEM parts prices rise each year, and lead times for castings (PCC, Howmet capacity) were 12-24+ months in 2023-25 (recalled). The gas-turbine order boom from data centres (GE Vernova, Siemens Energy and MHI backlogs sold out into 2028-2030) makes the aftermarket supply squeeze worse.
- Owners of older peakers and combined-cycle plants, many now running more hours because of data-centre load, want cheaper life extension. The constraint is trust in qualification data plus insurer acceptance.

## 3. Oil & gas (standards-body plus third-party certifier gatekeeping)

### The gate (evidence and recall)
- **API Spec Q1** quality system and the **API Monogram**: "more than 4,300 organizations in 75 countries" across Monogram, APIQR and the Repair and Remanufacture programmes (fetched).
- Product specs: API 6A (wellhead), 6D (valves), 16A/16AR (BOP, including repair/remanufacture), 17 series (subsea).
- **API 20 series** supply-chain specs (recalled):
  - 20A castings, 20B open-die forgings, 20C closed-die forgings
  - 20D NDE, 20E/20F bolting
  - **20S additively manufactured metallic parts (2021)**
- **DNV-ST-B203** "Additive manufacturing of metallic parts" (2019) and DNV-CG class guidelines. DNV ran JIPs with Equinor, Aker BP, TechnipFMC and others on AM spare parts (recalled).
- Equinor:
  - installed a 3D-printed part on Troll A in 2020 (recalled)
  - stated aims to digitise a large share of its spare-parts catalogue (recalled)
  - partnered with Ivaldi and with Aker Solutions' "digital warehouse" (recalled)
- Pressure equipment repairs and alterations:
  - US: National Board **NBIC "R" stamp**; ASME BPVC with an **Authorized Inspector** from an Authorized Inspection Agency (HSB, Allianz, etc.)
  - Inspectors certified under API 510/570/653 sign off in-service vessels, piping and tanks; risk-based inspection under API RP 580/581
  - EU: PED; UK: PSSR with a competent person (notified bodies TÜV, BV, Lloyd's)

### Spend and pain (recalled/opinion)
- A typical refinery or offshore asset holds $50-150M of MRO spares. A large share has not moved in years and a large share of SKUs are obsolete.
- Majors carry multi-billion-dollar MRO inventories (Shell, Exxon and Equinor each reportedly over $1B).
- Testing, inspection and certification (TIC) is about $250B/yr worldwide (SGS, Bureau Veritas, Intertek, DNV, TÜV, Applus), with oil & gas a large vertical.
- NDT services are about $10B+. US NDT/inspection firms: Mistras (~$700M revenue), Team Inc.
- **Inspector shortage:** API 510/570 and National Board commissioned inspectors are ageing. Turnarounds cost $1-2M/day in lost margin for a large refinery, so inspection sign-off time is on the critical path.
- **Non-OEM part qualification in oil & gas is mostly owner-driven.** The owner's materials engineer writes an equivalency or "like-for-like" justification and DNV/BV/ABS witness where class requires it. There is no public registry of equivalence; every operator repeats the work for the same obsolete valve trim or pump impeller.

## 4. Process and chemical industry
- Pumps, compressors, seals: API 610/617/682; Flowserve, Sulzer, John Crane and many reverse-engineering machine shops. Obsolete controls (Honeywell TDC 3000, Foxboro I/A, Bailey Infi 90, Westinghouse WDPF) are kept alive by brokers (Radwell, EU Automation) and by migration projects.
- Safety instrumented systems (IEC 61508/61511) need "proven in use" or certified (exida, TÜV) devices, so a non-OEM replacement needs SIL evidence (recalled).
- Regulators (OSHA PSM 29 CFR 1910.119, EPA RMP) require mechanical integrity and management of change (MOC). They do not certify parts, so the gate is the owner's MOC plus the insurer.

## 5. The full verification pathway, generalised across these sectors
1. **Identify need and obsolescence** (owner reliability engineer; EPRI POMS, BOM databases). Often reactive, triggered by a failure or an outage.
2. **Recover requirements and design basis** (owner design engineering; UFSAR, purchase specs, OEM manuals, system calcs). **The NRC names this as the weakest step (IN 2016-09).** Requirements are scattered across decades of scanned PDFs.
3. **Equivalency evaluation / technical justification** (owner engineer or dedicator). Template-driven, slow, repeated plant by plant.
4. **Select critical characteristics and acceptance methods** (dedicator, EPRI 3002002982). Judgement-heavy, and NRC inspections find gaps.
5. **Reverse engineering** (machine shop or dedicator: CMM/CT scan, chemistry, hardness, microstructure). Mostly commodity work. Physical clock of days to weeks.
6. **Supplier qualification and audit** (NUPIC joint audits, API Q1, DNV/BV). Audit capacity is scarce, and travel and audit cycles add months.
7. **Testing**: environmental/seismic qualification (IEEE 323/344), EMC, pressure and leak, fatigue, field trials (Curtiss-Wright, Wyle (now part of Element), Element, NTS labs; owner lead units). **This is the physics clock.** Seismic or harsh-environment qualification runs weeks to months, and gas turbine field trials take 1-3 years.
8. **Sign-off**: dedication certificate / Certificate of Conformance with Part 21 responsibility (nuclear); AI or NB stamp (pressure); insurer letter (gas turbines). Personal and corporate liability sits here: Part 21 makes the dedicator liable for reporting defects.
9. **Install and in-service inspection** (ASME Section XI ISI, API 510, robot or manual NDT; Gecko, Mistras, Team). Data stays in silos and is not fed back into qualification.
10. **Fleet reuse**: nobody shares dedication or equivalency packages across owners. Each utility, refinery or turbine owner redoes the work, and only commercial dedicators resell their own packages.

## 6. What nobody does today (opinion, built on the evidence above)
- **A machine-readable design-basis or requirements graph per plant system.** Requirements recovery is the step the NRC says fails most, and it is pure information work, so AI can do it.
- **A cross-owner equivalence and dedication registry**: "this RE part was qualified against these critical characteristics, with these results, used at these N plants with zero failures." This is the analogue of the FAA PMA parts list, applied to nuclear CGD and oil & gas like-for-like. NUPIC already shares audits, but not dedications.
- **Analysis-credit qualification.** Criterion III allows calculation-based design verification, but in practice the industry defaults to test because the dedicator cannot defend completeness. A model that proves coverage of requirements could shift the analysis/test balance, for example by reducing seismic tests through validated FEA plus similarity.
- **Insurer-backed warranty wrap for non-OEM gas turbine parts.** No balance sheet underwrites alternative-part risk at scale apart from PSM/Hanwha and EthosEnergy. A captive or MGA that prices qualification data would unlock the 80% of the fleet under OEM-only habits.
- **Continuous in-service evidence feeding back into qualification.** Robot inspection data (Gecko, ANYbotics, Energy Robotics) never flows into supplier performance records (EPRI Method 4), although Method 4 is explicitly about performance history.
- **Counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect item (CFSI) material fingerprinting** at receipt inspection, beyond PMI guns.

## 8. Binding constraints, and what would change each
- **Physics clocks** (seismic, environmental and fatigue tests; gas turbine field trials). To change them: validated simulation plus similarity arguments so that tests drop from per-part to per-family; risk-informed categorisation under 10 CFR 50.69 (already lets low-safety-significance SSCs skip special treatment, recalled); fleet-pooled lead-unit data.
- **Law**: Part 21 and Appendix B already allow analysis-based verification, so the law is *not* the barrier. Regulator acceptance is. To change it: get NRC to endorse an AI-assisted dedication methodology via an EPRI or NEI topical report (like EPRI 3002002982 → RG 1.164), possibly through the NRC's AI strategic plan (2023) and ADVANCE Act (2024) push for efficiency (recalled).
- **Liability**: the dedicator carries Part 21 reporting liability, and gas turbine buyers need a warranty. To change it: become the dedicator of record (a licensed Appendix B house), or partner with an insurer or MGA.
- **Data access**: design-basis documents are owner-held and OEM drawings are proprietary. To change it: owner-sponsored ingestion (utilities want it), EPRI pooled data, and inspection-robot partnerships.

## 9. AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
1. **AI-native nuclear commercial-grade dedicator.** An Appendix B / Part 21 house whose engine:
   - ingests plant design-basis documents
   - derives critical characteristics
   - writes EPRI-compliant dedication plans and equivalency evaluations
   - plans the minimum test set
   - reuses packages across the 94-unit fleet

   KPI: engineering hours per dedication. Revenue: per package plus parts margin. This is the nuclear twin of the Approval Factory. It fits the founder's energy focus and avoids the slow military DLA channel. Data-centre-driven licence extensions, uprates and restarts (Palisades, TMI-1/Crane, Duane Arnold) raise demand.
2. **Requirements-recovery and design-basis graph SaaS for plants.** It addresses the step NRC inspections say is failing. Sell to utilities and the incumbent dedicators.
3. **Cross-owner equivalence registry plus insurer-backed warranty** for non-OEM gas turbine and oil & gas parts: the evidence brokerage and underwriting layer.
4. **Inspection sign-off copilot** for API 510/570, NBIC R-stamp and ASME Section XI. It turns robot and NDT data into a signable fitness-for-service (API 579) assessment and lifts throughput per authorised inspector, which is the scarce human sign-off.
5. **Analysis-credit qualification**: simulation-plus-similarity packages that replace physical seismic and environmental tests where Criterion III permits, taken to the NRC through an EPRI topical report.
