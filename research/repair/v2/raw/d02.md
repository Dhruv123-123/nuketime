## 1. The headline finding (EVIDENCE, primary documents)

**Lessors still contractually ban PMA parts and DER repairs at return in 2025-2026.** This is the main gate on airline demand, and it sits in permission, not in knowledge.

### Global Crossing Airlines (GlobalX) engine leases filed with the SEC
- EX-10.4, filed 2025-11-06. Lessor: Gryphon Trading Company, LLC. Engine: IAE V2527-A5, ESN V12844. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1846084/000119312525268615/jetmf-ex10_4.htm
  - Appendix C para 2(d): engine "shall not have installed any DER-approved repairs or PMA parts", except "non-OEM approved PMA parts already present on the Commencement Date".
  - Appendix B-2: the lessee must "Provide a statement that no Non-OEM approved Repairs were performed on the Engine" and "Provide a statement that no Non-OEM approved PMA Parts were installed".
  - Appendix B-4 Q13/Q14: a return questionnaire that asks whether non-OEM repairs or PMA parts were installed.
  - Part II item 7: the lessor disclaims "THE ACCURACY, VALIDITY OR TRACEABILITY OF ANY OF THE ENGINE DOCUMENTATION". The lessor itself will not warrant the trace.
  - Every replaced part needs an "FAA Form 8130-3 or EASA Form One". The engine must be redelivered with an 8130-3 after a "full front-to-back VBSI", meaning a borescope inspection.
- EX-10.2, filed 2026-08-13. Lessor: UMB Bank N.A. as Owner Trustee for AvCap 3349, LLC. Engine: CFM56-5B4/3, ESN 697550. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1846084/000119312526348692/jetmf-ex10_2.htm
  - It carries the same PMA/DER ban, the same no-non-OEM statements, dual FAA/EASA release, the original documents and the last shop-visit report.
- Interpretation: this is a template clause used across small-to-mid lessors. It was still being signed in August 2026, about three years after AOG Technics.

### OEM fixed-price overhaul agreements (Pratt & Whitney with Willis Lease Finance, filed 2021)
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018164/000101816421000013/ex1056-fixedpricerepairagr.htm
  - s.9.1.16: "the use or replacement of any PMA parts ... not approved by the FPA Manager" is Excess Work, billed outside the fixed price.
  - s.9.1.17: "DER repairs not acceptable to the FPA Manager" are also Excess Work.
  - s.10.3: at redelivery, "no PMA parts that have not been approved by the OEM".
  - s.9.1.8: work "required to meet any lease return conditions" is Excess Work.
- Interpretation: the OEM is the gatekeeper on its own competitors' parts. Lessor return conditions and OEM service contracts lock each other in.

### Airline-side MRO contract, the counter-example (Volaris with Aveos, 2013)
- https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1520504/000119312513265084/d500205dex103.htm
  - s.3.6: PMA parts (Class II/III) are allowed "only if ... approved in writing by VOLARIS"; "AVEOS shall not unreasonably refuse the specified PMA Part"; "AVEOS shall use DERs and 'Extended' DER repairs".
- Interpretation: airlines that run their own fleets and keep aircraft to end of life pull PMA/DER in. Lessors and OEMs push it out. Demand splits by who holds residual-value risk.

## 3. Airline adoption (mostly RECALLED, flag)
- (recalled) Heavy PMA/DER users: United, American, Delta (Delta TechOps), Lufthansa/LHT, Southwest (CFM56), Air France-KLM E&M, JAL, Emirates, Air Canada. Flag-carrier MROs that keep aircraft to retirement use PMA most.
- (recalled) PMA is estimated at only about 2-5% of the global commercial aftermarket parts spend, which runs to tens of billions of dollars. It reaches far higher shares on mature engine programs: CFM56, V2500, PW4000 and CF6 hot-section and gas-path parts.
- (recalled) Surveys by Aviation Week, MARPA and the IATA PMA/DER guidance (2nd edition) report that most large carriers use PMA. Lessor acceptance and residual value are consistently the top cited barriers, followed by OEM warranty and service-contract terms and the burden of an EASA/FAA bilateral split.

## 4. AOG Technics, and what changed afterwards (EVIDENCE, AeroTime)
- Fraud: about $52M (GBP 39.3M) total; parts sold worth $9.3M; more than 60,000 parts sold. Director Jose Alejandro Zamora Yrala pleaded guilty at Southwark Crown Court on 2025-12-01 and was sentenced on 2026-02-23 to 4 years 8 months. https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/fake-aircraft-parts-aog-technics
- CFM update of 2023-10-04: 126 engines suspected, which is less than 1% of the 22,600 CFM56 engines in service; 95 falsified documents covering 61 CFM56 part numbers, plus 2 for CF6. Most parts were non-serialized: bolts, nuts, washers, dampers, seals, bushings. No life-limited parts were involved. Four instances entered CFM's own OEM MRO shops (16 engines); 110 engines were at non-OEM shops. https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/cfm-international-aog-technics-parts-components
- Affected airlines: Delta (less than 1% of its 2,100+ mainline engines), American, United, Southwest, TAP, Ryanair, WestJet, Virgin Australia. Safran's CEO said about 100 engines. https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/westjet-delta-aog-technics
- FAA special airworthiness information bulletin of 2023-12-19: no AD issued, and no critical parts with forged certificates found. https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/faa-aog-technics-update-airworthiness-directive
- SFO dawn raid 2023-12-06; CFM sued 2023-09-07; High Court disclosure order 2023-09-20. https://www.aerotime.aero/articles/serious-fraud-office-aog-techics-fake-parts-scandal
- Key structural fact: the fraud was a forged paper EASA Form 1 / ARC on non-serialized parts. Nobody could cheaply check a release certificate against its issuer. Safran caught it by manually noticing a fake certificate. The fraud also contaminated the OEM's own shops, so "OEM-only" is no protection.
- (recalled) Behaviour changes since: lessors and airlines demand more back-to-birth trace and more "non-incident / non-military" statements, and have tightened approved-vendor lists. Distributors push ASA-100 and AC 00-56 accreditation. The FAA, EASA and UK CAA issued unapproved-parts notices. US bills were proposed to require accredited distributors. Trace-review labour and lease-transition time went up. The GlobalX leases above show return conditions did not loosen for PMA.
- MARPA blog, 2025-26 titles (EVIDENCE, titles only): "FAA 'Sample Size' AC Has Been Released" (2026-10-05); "FAA Certification Standards Division AIR-600 Realigning" (2026-10-14); "Section 232 Aircraft Parts Investigation" (2026-07-20); "Capitol Hill Meeting ... PMA can Support Armed Services Readiness" (2026-07-13); "Selling PMAs to the U.S. Military" (2026-04-30). https://marpa.pmaparts.org/

## 6. Analysis: where the demand gate is and what would change it (OPINION)
1. The demand gate is the lessor return clause plus OEM service-contract clauses, not airline willingness. Lessors own about 50%+ of the commercial fleet (recalled). Their fear is residual value: the next lessee or financier may refuse a PMA-laden engine, and appraisers and ABS rating agencies haircut it.
2. What nobody does: price the PMA/DER residual-value effect with data. No appraiser publishes "PMA-content-adjusted" values. Nobody insures it. Nobody offers a reversion guarantee, where the PMA vendor contractually swaps parts back to OEM at return. HEICO and LHT have done ad hoc guarantees (recalled, uncertain).
3. Verification gap exposed by AOG Technics: release certificates (8130-3/Form 1) are PDFs or paper. There is no issuer-side registry, so nothing can confirm that a certificate was actually issued by the named Part 145/21G holder for that serial or lot. Non-serialized hardware has no unit identity at all.
4. Trace review is manual: a lease transition takes weeks to months and often costs $100k-$1M+ in records work per aircraft (recalled). Back-to-birth for LLPs is a labour-intensive reading problem that AI handles well. That reading only delivers value once the issuers confirm what the documents say.

## 7. What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence and compute)
- A certificate-issuer registry: every Part 145/21G/PMA holder publishes a signed hash of each 8130-3/Form 1 it issues. Any buyer can verify one in a millisecond. The binding constraint is issuer adoption. The likely route is a lessor/insurer mandate: no registry match, no acceptance. The FAA/EASA already accept e-signatures, so this needs no new law.
- An AI trace auditor that reads entire records sets: OCR, cross-checks against issuer registries, OEM shop-visit data, ADs and the cycles math. It outputs a quantified trace-confidence score that a lessor, appraiser or insurer will sign up to.
- A PMA residual-value model with a reversion guarantee or insurance product, so lessors can strike the clause. This turns "no PMA" into "PMA permitted with guarantee", and that is the unlock for Approval Factory demand.
- Physical verification for parts that cannot be serialized: spectroscopy, CT or metrology fingerprinting at incoming inspection, matched to a design data fingerprint. The time this takes is set by physics and is short for incoming inspection.
- Standard lease-clause language, with ISTAT/AWG and lessor counsel, that admits FAA-PMA/DER items carrying verified trace and a guarantee.

## 8. AI plays on the verification side
1. "Trace oracle": AI back-to-birth and certificate verification as a service. It sells first to lessors, financiers and insurers, the parties that impose the clauses. ProvenAir is closest, but it reads documents and does not verify against issuers.
2. An issuer registry, run as a network-effect business, seeded by PMA houses and independent MROs that want their parts accepted. This flips them from suspect to verified.
3. A PMA residual-value and risk underwriter that uses records plus reliability data to price and guarantee PMA content. It converts the lessor's "no" into a priced "yes".
4. An approval-package auditor: the regulator- or DER-facing side. AI pre-reviews PMA/DER and DLA technical-data packages for completeness and compliance, cutting the reviewer hours that are the Approval Factory's own KPI.
5. Strategic fit: the Approval Factory makes parts, and a verification and underwriting layer makes those parts acceptable to lessors. Either sequence works, building the verification layer first or alongside. The verification layer may be the faster revenue, and it builds the moat that lets the parts sell.
