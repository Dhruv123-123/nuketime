# c32: Building permits and code compliance AI (PermitFlow, Greenlite, CodeComply, UpCodes, Archistar, Singapore CORENET X): did AI plan review get regulator acceptance? Speed-ups achieved?

Analog question for the "Approval Factory" thesis: in building permitting, a domain where approvals are also held by PERMISSION (a building official's sign-off, a licensed person's stamp), did AI get the regulator to accept it, and through what lever?

## Short answer
- AI plan review has NOT been accepted anywhere I could verify as the legal approver. In every deployment found, the regulator accepts AI only (a) as an applicant-side or agency-side **pre-check** whose output is advisory (Archistar in Austin and Los Angeles, Singapore's CORENET X Automated Model Checker, CodeComply.Ai via CivicPlus), or (b) **inside a licensed human reviewer's stamp**, where a statute already lets a private licensed party do the official review (GreenLite under "private provider" laws).
- The speed-ups that are real came from the **legal lever** (private-provider statutes and shot clocks that let a third party replace a backlogged city queue), not from AI accuracy. AI raises throughput per licensed reviewer, but it is the law that lets a private reviewer's output count.
- There is almost no independently measured speed-up data. Every figure is a vendor claim or an anecdote.

## Evidence by player

### Archistar (Sydney; eCheck)
- Austin TX adopted eCheck in Oct 2024 after a 3-month pilot, as a **pre-check** for single-family residential zoning. "It does not review every element" and reduces what staff must examine. A second pilot with the ICC covers the IBC. No time-savings figures were published. BD+C, 17 Oct 2024: https://www.bdcnetwork.com/codes-and-standards/news/55237400/austin-texas-adopts-ai-driven-building-permit-software ; Govlaunch says the pilot is "Complete" but gives no metrics: https://govlaunch.com/projects/austin-tx-evaluates-the-impacts-of-archistars-echeck-assessment-tool-on-permit-review-times
- Los Angeles City and County, for the wildfire rebuild: Mayor Bass's Executive Directive 10 told LADBS to explore AI. The tool was funded by philanthropy (RiseLA). Its first scope was EO1 "like-for-like" zoning compliance, with a next iteration in Sept 2025 under EO8. County Public Works runs a pilot for fire life-safety checks (VHFHSZ). The city describes it as an "AI Pre-Plan Check ... to improve plan submission quality." Applicants attach the eCheck report link to their application, and humans still review it. The county says it is "checking the program for accuracy." LA County / Archistar deck, 31 Jan 2026: https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/dpw/recovery/1201690_RebuildingTogether_ArchistarDRAFTPPT12226.pdf
  - Vendor claims in that deck: it "reduces number of technical reviews from 3+ to 1, with some cities allowing 0 (i.e. full automated approval by eCheck)", and permit time "from 12 months to instant (full approval) or <1-2 months (1 check)". This is the vendor's claim. The "0 review" cities are not named. My recollection is that they are Australian councils approving simple complying developments.
- ICC became a "Premier Platinum Reseller" of eCheck on 24 Jul 2025 and integrated its Code Connect API (machine-readable I-Codes). An 11-city pilot (cities not named) reported "significant improvements" with **no figures**. ICC does **not** certify the AI. Archistar claims more than 25 governments, including Vancouver, Austin and LA. https://www.iccsafe.org/about/periodicals-and-newsroom/international-code-council-collaborates-with-archistar-to-modernize-permitting-and-accelerate-housing-development

### GreenLite (private plan review plus AI "LiteTable")
- $49.5M Series B led by Insight Partners, with Energize, Craft, LiveOak and Chicago Ventures (Sept 2025). Claims permit timelines 75% faster. Customers are nearly 100 national brands (Walgreens, O'Reilly, TD Bank). Its stated thesis is "the privatization of construction permitting". It says nearly a quarter of US states advanced private plan review laws in the past 3 years. https://www.insightpartners.com/?p=25308
- Website claims: typical permitting phase 90-120+ days versus 21-45 days with GreenLite; first review comments in 7 days; Florida restaurants permitted in as little as 8 business days; 0.5 average resubmission cycles. In private plan review, its "certified experts" (engineers, architects, code officers) review, approve and **stamp**. https://www.greenlite.com/
- East Hampton NY (27east, 22 Jul 2026): GreenLite is one of 4 outside firms hired to clear a backlog of more than 6 months. A **licensed GreenLite staff architect QA's** each review before the town acts, and the town gives final approval. The town's Building Department does not itself use AI. One builder got his review back in 10 days. Complaints: the AI flags items "not relevant or actually wrong", requires items outside the town's 100-point checklist, and misreads plans. https://www.27east.com/east-hampton-press/news/government/article_5257a813-bcf3-4176-88ac-46749e3d167d.html
- Legal basis (recalled, not verified this session because flsenate.gov returned 429): Florida Statute 553.791 lets an owner hire a licensed engineer, architect or building code administrator as a "private provider" to do plan review and inspections. The local building official must then act within a fixed number of business days, or the permit is deemed approved; 2023 amendments tightened this. Texas HB 3699 (2023) and related laws added third-party review when city deadlines lapse. Other states followed.

### CodeComply.Ai (via CivicPlus)
- Partnership with CivicPlus announced 19 Mar 2026. Covers IBC, IMC, IPC, IRC, ADA, FHA, UFC, state amendments and NFPA 101/70/72/13/14. Claims it can "cut plan review times from months to minutes" (vendor claim). The disclaimer says automated checks "do not replace professional review" and that final compliance determinations remain with qualified reviewers. No jurisdictions or metrics are named. https://www.civicplus.com/news/nn/civicplus-brings-ai-building-plan-review-codecomply-ai/

### CivCheck (Clariti)
- Sponsored the CALBO webinar on AI plan review (1 Apr 2026), framed around "transparency, accuracy, and defensibility". No metrics. https://www.calbo.org/post/ai-and-plan-review-practical-lessons-building-departments

### PermitFlow
- Raised $54M (Series B, per its homepage). It runs agents for intake, research, submission, coordination and issuance, and covers more than 7,000 AHJs, more than $20B in construction value permitted, and more than 12M data points. Claims 2.5x faster approvals and 90% less admin. Customers include Apple, Amazon, IKEA, Lennar and NVR. It is the **applicant-side expediter**. It does not do code review and has no regulator acceptance as a reviewer. https://www.permitflow.com/

### UpCodes
- A searchable code library with an AI copilot and a Revit model-checking plugin (the plugin is recalled; the homepage fetch was thin). It is design-side only and not used for official review. Funding: a Series A of about $10M+ (recalled).

### Singapore CORENET X (BCA and URA)
- Media factsheet: Qualified Persons (registered architects and professional engineers) submit **one coordinated BIM model**. Agencies (BCA, URA, LTA, PUB, NParks, NEA, SCDF, SLA, HDB, JTC, GovTech) review it together and give one consolidated response. The **Automated Model Checker** lets QPs check against the requirements of 7 main agencies **before submission**, so it is a pre-check, and the QP stays accountable. https://isomer-user-content.by.gov.sg/338/278a782d-cf72-4b0d-987f-4ee7b7e57225/corenet-x.pdf
- URA circular DC23-07 gives the timeline: soft launch 18 Dec 2023; voluntary from 1 Jun 2024; **mandatory for new projects from 1 Apr 2025**; ongoing projects from 1H 2026. There are 3 gateways (Design, Construction, Completion) plus an optional Piling gateway. IFC-SG BIM is required for projects of 5,000 m2 GFA or more. The Automated Model Checker "will be implemented progressively in later phases", with no date given. **No approval-time reduction is claimed.** https://www.ura.gov.sg/Corporate/Guidelines/Circulars/dc23-07
- History: CORENET e-PlanCheck (automated code checking on IFC) ran from the early 2000s. A 2005 review found it "not quite working yet". https://aecbytes.com/feature/2005/CORENETePlanCheck.html . So the most digitally aggressive regulator in the world has spent more than 20 years on this and still keeps the machine as a pre-check, with humans and QPs as the approvers. Its win was **process redesign** (one model, consolidated review, gateways) plus a computable code schema (IFC-SG), not machine approval.

## Synthesis for the Approval Factory
1. Regulator acceptance pattern: the machine is **advisory**, and a **licensed human signs**. Acceptance comes from (a) the regulator making its own rules computable (ICC Code Connect API, IFC-SG, the LA emergency orders scoped to rule sets a machine can check) and (b) statutes that let **delegated private licensed reviewers** stand in for the agency (private-provider laws). The FAA already has the second: DERs and ODAs are delegated private approvers. DLA does not, because engineering support activities review SARs in-house.
2. Where the money is: GreenLite (reviewer of record plus AI, a $49.5M Series B) and PermitFlow (expediter plus AI, $54M) both raised more than pure code-checkers (Archistar, CodeComply). Value accrues to whoever **holds the permission**, the stamp or the delegated authority, and uses AI internally to cut hours per approval. This matches the prior conclusion.
3. Speed-ups: credible orders of magnitude come from replacing a **queue**: 6+ months down to 10 days (East Hampton), 90-120 days down to 21-45 (GreenLite). The machine pre-check improves first-submission quality, cutting cycles from 3+ to about 1 (vendor claim). No regulator has published an audited before-and-after.
4. Failure mode to design against: AI over-flags, invents requirements and misreads drawings (East Hampton). Every acceptance includes "the human remains responsible" language. Liability stays with the licensed person.

## Verification-side AI plays implied (opinion)
- "Archistar for SARs/PMAs": a pre-submission checker that scores a SAR/TDP or PMA package against DLA engineering support activity / FAA reviewer checklists (FAA Order 8110.42, DLA SAR packages), predicts rejection reasons, and targets 0-1 review cycles. It could be sold to applicants, and later to the agency the way CivCheck and CodeComply sell to building departments.
- "GreenLite for aerospace": own the delegated approval (DER and, later, a PMA-holder ODA) and run AI-native review internally. This is the permission-holder position.
- Regulator-side: computable requirements. Convert MIL-specs, AMSC/AMC codes and DLA SAR requirements into a machine-checkable schema (the IFC-SG / ICC Code Connect equivalent), and offer it to DLA as an agency reviewer copilot.
- Policy lever: a "private provider plus shot clock" for DLA SARs, with deemed approval or third-party engineering review when the engineering support activity misses its deadline. That is the lever that unlocked GreenLite's market. Its analog in DoD would be an FAR/DFARS or NDAA provision for third-party SAR evaluation, which is plausible given DoD's "right to repair" and sole-source pressure.
