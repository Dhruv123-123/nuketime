## 1. Timeline: from "simulation works" to "simulation counts"

If tests deviate from predictions, a correction factor scales the OEM's predictions. | 2026 | UN ADS GTR/regulation targeted for adoption around mid-2026, built on NATM, including simulation credibility. | recalled, unverified |

**Elapsed time:**
- About 24 years from the first full-car simulation (1986) to the first legal permission (2010). That permission covered only trivial geometric acts.
- About 27 years (1986 to ~2013) to the first consumer-rating use. That use followed a "predict, then spot-check" model.
- About 37-40 years to HBM-based virtual assessment in a rating (2023-2026).
- **The full-scale regulatory crash test (UN R94/R95, FMVSS) is still physical in 2026.** The physical test was never replaced. It became the anchor that validates the model, and the model fills in the cases no one physically tests.

## 2. The acceptance mechanism (what made regulators say yes)
1. **Positive list of eligible acts.** The regulator decides, act by act, where simulation may substitute (Annex XVI in 2007/46, Annex VIII in 2018/858). It started with low-consequence, geometric acts.
   - Fetched: Art. 30(7) of 2018/858 allows virtual testing "at the request of the manufacturer" and "subject to the agreement of the approval authority", in accordance with Annex VIII.
   - Fetched: Art. 30(8) lets the Commission extend the list by delegated act.
   - Fetched: Annex VIII has three appendices: general conditions, specific conditions, and validation process.
2. **Validation against a physical anchor.** The model must be correlated with at least one physical test of the same or a similar configuration before its predictions count.
3. **The assessor picks the spot checks.** In Euro NCAP pedestrian, and recalled for VTC, the OEM submits predictions across the full space and the assessor physically verifies points of its own choosing.
4. **An automatic penalty for wrong predictions.** A correction factor or downgrade applies when spot checks miss the predictions. This makes honest prediction the rational strategy and removes the need to trust the OEM's model.
5. **An accredited third party.** A technical service (TÜV, DEKRA, IDIADA, UTAC, ...) must be designated as competent for virtual testing and runs or witnesses the work.
6. **Standardised models and metrics.** These are what made it scale:
   - Shared reference models: THUMS, GHBMC, Humanetics FE dummies.
   - Objective correlation metrics: ISO/TS 18571 (recalled).
   - Later, toolchain credibility frameworks (NATM CAF; EU 2022/1426). Analogous frameworks exist in other fields: ASME V&V 40 (medical, 2018), NASA-STD-7009 (2008).

**Gap:** no company sells "simulation credibility evidence as a product". That would mean an automated V&V dossier that a technical service or regulator accepts, plus the correlation, uncertainty and coverage argument. OEM CAE teams and technical-service engineers assemble these dossiers by hand.

## 4. Lessons for the Approval Factory (DLA SAR / FAA PMA)
- Aviation PMA by test and computation is already allowed in principle, and DER analysis substitutes for tests routinely. The automotive history shows that what unlocks scale is a codified "predict-everything, physically-verify-a-sample, penalise-misses" protocol, not better simulation. The FAA and DLA have no such statistical protocol. Each package is judged bespoke, which keeps the cost at roughly $100k and 300-1,000 hours per package.
- Timescales: autos needed about 24 years to win legal permission for trivial acts, and about 37 years for HBM crash. The path ran through consumer ratings (Euro NCAP, a voluntary body) rather than law. Analogues in aerospace and defense:
  - industry consensus bodies (SAE G-34/G-35; ASTM F42 for AM parts; MMPDS; CMH-17)
  - DLA engineering support activities
  - FAA AC 20-107/20-170-style advisory material
- Physics clocks are not removed; they are shrunk to an anchor test plus spot checks. On a C-130 part, that means one coupon or part test to validate the model, a regulator-chosen spot check, and a penalty or rescission if field data diverges.

## 5. What would need to be done (abundant compute and intelligence assumed)
1. **A "virtual equivalence" protocol for spare parts, written and proposed to DLA and the FAA.** It mirrors Euro NCAP pedestrian:
   - The applicant predicts every requirement in the TDP: dimensional, material, fatigue, thermal.
   - The government chooses k physical verification tests at random.
   - A correction factor or rejection applies when they miss.
   - It is shipped as a draft AC, MIL-HDBK or SAE ARP, with the statistics (sampling plan, power, consumer's risk) worked out.
2. **A per-part credibility dossier generator**, aligned to ASME V&V 40 / NASA-STD-7009 and modelled on NATM CAF:
   - ISO/TS 18571-style objective correlation for every model
   - uncertainty quantification
   - an explicit statement of the model's domain of validity
3. **Shared reference models**, the equivalent of THUMS/GHBMC for legacy platforms: open, validated reference models of common sole-source part classes (bushings, brackets, seals, harness connectors, valves). A regulator certifies the reference model once, and each part application reuses it.
4. **The independent "technical service" role for aero/defense.** An accredited third party that runs or witnesses virtual verification, playing the role of DEKRA or TÜV in EU type-approval. In the FAA world this sits close to an ODA/DER firm. Nobody runs it as an AI-native verification house.
5. **A positive list of eligible part classes.** Lobby, through the Commission-style delegated-act equivalent (DLA policy or an FAA order), for a list of low-criticality part classes where analysis plus spot-check is presumptively acceptable. Start with geometric and non-critical parts, as the EU did with Reg. 371/2010.
6. **Field-data back-validation.** In-service monitoring (NATM's 5th pillar) supplies the post-approval evidence: the parts fleet reliability data (DLA quality deficiency reports, FAA SDRs) can be fed back to rescind or confirm the approval.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side
- **"Credibility-as-a-Service":** an AI system that produces and audits V&V evidence for simulation-based approvals, sold to technical services, ODAs and regulators rather than to OEMs. Automotive has the demand but no vendor. Aero/defense has neither the protocol nor the vendor.
- **An AI reviewer copilot for DLA ESA / FAA ACO / DER reviewers.** It checks a SAR or PMA package against requirements, flags gaps and proposes the spot-check tests. This shrinks reviewer hours, which is the true bottleneck.
- **Statistical spot-check design:** choose the minimum physical tests that bound the risk of a wrong approval, the Euro NCAP grid-sampling logic generalised.
- **Surrogate-model correlation engines**, in the style of Neural Concept and PhysicsX, but packaged with a regulator-acceptable domain-of-validity argument rather than design speed.
