# c08: Counterfeit and authenticity detection for electronic and mechanical parts (Cybord, Battelle Barricade, IDEA/SMT labs, ERAI, Sentry, Chipscan, XRF/CT AI vendors): what is automated and what is not

Tags used here: (evidence) means fetched or search-confirmed this run. (recalled) means from memory and not re-verified. (opinion) means analysis.

## 1. Evidence gathered this run

- **Cybord (Tel Aviv).** Visual AI that inspects every component image as it is placed on SMT lines. It flags counterfeit, defective and tampered parts, AVL/BOM deviations, component-level country of origin, and lot/date-code genealogy. Products are ShieldScan, Component-Level Inspection and Micro-Traceability. Logos on its site: Cisco, NVIDIA, Intel, SpaceX, Siemens, Flex, Fuji, ASMPT, Yamaha, Koh Young. Funding: $4M seed (Mar 2022, per a profile), $8.7M Series A (Sep 2024, Capri Ventures lead), and a $7M extended Series A (28 Apr 2026, Capri) that opened US sales. Trade press says it holds a database of more than 4bn component images and runs on more than 60 lines. It claims 99.9% rejection accuracy (Mar 2025 launch) and 99.3% detection accuracy "outperforming X-ray" (CALCE/SMTA 2025 Counterfeit Symposium abstract). All accuracy figures are vendor claims with no published method. The site never mentions AS6171 and does not claim to replace lab testing. (evidence) Sources: https://www.cybord.ai/ ; https://www.tullahomanews.com/?p=100852 ; https://calce.umd.edu/node/1961 ; https://www.electronicspecifier.com/?p=34390
- **DMEA / CALCE / Intelligent Automation Inc: DATUM (2020).** An ML and machine-vision system that compares the original design against the device under test across radiography, acoustic tomography and spectroscopy images, plus process change notice (PCN) data. The humans still generate the images, define the defects and analyse the PCNs. It is a research programme with no sign that it was fielded. (evidence) https://calce.umd.edu/news/story/developing-anbspnew-solution-fornbspdetecting-counterfeit-parts
- **TWI ChipCheck (EU FP7, completed).** Aimed at automated X-ray inspection of components "in their original packaging". The page shows no commercial follow-through. (evidence) https://www.twi-global.com/.../chipcheck
- **USPTO 12656277**, "Artificial intelligence anomaly detection using x-ray computed tomography scan data". It states the core problem: counterfeits mimic genuine parts, so telling them apart "requires a more exhaustive, and sometimes destructive, inspection". Also **USPTO 12105857**, "System and method for detection of counterfeit and cyber electronic components". I did not resolve the assignees. (evidence: titles only)
- **GAO-12-375 (2012).** GAO bought 16 parts and all 16 were bogus. All 12 with real part numbers were rated suspect counterfeit by an independent lab using SEM, X-ray and XRF: they were re-marked, had sub-spec metal composition, and had altered date codes. Four parts with non-existent part numbers were still delivered. Of 396 responding vendors, 334 were in China. Vendors replied within a day and delivered within two weeks. (evidence) https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-12-375
- **ERAI.** Calls itself "the world's largest searchable database of counterfeit components and high risk suppliers" and has run for 25-30 years. It offers a nonconformance photo library, InterCEPT training, ERAI E$CROW and supplier screening. Its site lists **no AI product**. (evidence) https://www.erai.com/
- **Chip Scan (chipscan.us).** A DMEA Accredited Trusted Supplier. Products: ESPY (an appliance for continuous hardware assurance against third-party IP and insider threats), Deep Lift (rapid hardware reverse engineering of legacy FPGAs/ASICs), YOLO (OT) and hardware red-teaming. This is design and trojan assurance, not authentication of parts bought on the open market. (evidence) https://www.chipscan.us/
- **Mechanical / aviation paperwork fraud.** EASA confirmed a forged FAA Form 8130-3 on a Cessna landing-gear support (P/N 0745603-2), case OC-EASA-2022000331. It was **detected by a human emailing the US repair station named on the form**, which confirmed the forgery. Concord International forged 8130-3s using a deceased DAR's signature. A vendor blog (JDA Solutions) says counterfeiters reuse genuine serials from scrapped aircraft and edit PDF metadata. Guidance is FAA AC 21-29D on suspected unapproved parts. (evidence) https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/aircraft-products/suspected-unapproved-parts/forged-faa-form-8130-3 ; https://jdasolutions.aero/blog/what-the-us-and-easa-are-doing-to-interdict-sups-and-how-should-you-prepare/
- **Failed fetches:** Battelle (403 on both pages), Sentry (the site is a placeholder "GOVCON_OS" app), SAE AS6171 (empty page), idofea.com (domain lapsed or parked; the real IDEA site is idofea.org, which I did not fetch).

## 2. Recalled context (not re-verified)

- **Standards stack** (recalled): NDAA FY2012 Sec. 818, and DFARS 252.246-7007 and 252.246-7008 (2016 revision) on traceability and sourcing from authorized channels. SAE AS5553 covers counterfeit avoidance by manufacturers, AS6081 by distributors, AS6174 for materiel, and AS6171 for test methods. AS6171 assigns a risk-based test level from part criticality and supplier risk. Its slash sheets cover external visual, XRF, X-ray, delid/decap and die inspection, SAM/CSAM, electrical test, burn-in, Raman/FTIR and more. Lab accreditation is to AS6171 or ISO 17025 (DLA, via A2LA/ANAB). IDEA-STD-1010 covers visual inspection. Suspect parts are reported to GIDEP and ERAI.
- **Battelle Barricade** (recalled, low confidence): Battelle's counterfeit IC screening product, pitched as fast, non-destructive and largely automated, comparing a part to known-good signatures. I could not confirm its current status this run.
- **Lab economics** (recalled): a full AS6171 test-level-A/B lot test runs from about $1k to over $5k and takes 1-3 weeks. Decapsulation destroys samples. Burn-in and temperature cycling run on physical clocks of 48-168 hours. Labs include SMT Corp, Integra Technologies, Process Sciences, Trusted Parts Labs, NTS/Element and others.
- **AOG Technics** (recalled): a UK distributor that sold thousands of CFM56 (and some CF6/V2500) parts with forged EASA Form 1 / 8130-3 certificates. It was exposed in mid-2023 when TAP Air Portugal engineers checked a certificate back with the issuing MRO or OEM. Hundreds of engines were affected across operators including United, American, Delta, Southwest and Virgin. The director was prosecuted in the UK (details not verified). Again the detection was manual: someone called the supposed issuer.
- **Adjacent players** (recalled):
  - Physical marking and fingerprinting: Applied DNA Sciences (DNA marking that DLA mandated for FSC 5962 microcircuits from about 2012; later scaled back), SMX/Security Matters (molecular marking; Nasdaq), Alitheon (optical FeaturePrint for mechanical parts).
  - Provenance and trading: Moog VeriPart, SkyThread, Honeywell GoDirect Trade (records of trades and certificates).
  - X-ray and CT: Creative Electron and Nordson (automated X-ray component counting with some AI), Lumafield (industrial CT with cloud analysis), Zeiss, Thermo Fisher.
  - Materials analysis: SciAps, Evident/Olympus and Hitachi for XRF and LIBS alloy identification. QuantumDiamonds (magnetic imaging of chips).
  - DARPA programmes: SHIELD (2014 dielet authentication), IRIS (IC integrity), AISS.

## 3. What is automated vs. not, by step (opinion, built on the evidence above)

| Step | Today | Automated? |
|---|---|---|
| Supplier and channel screening | ERAI, GIDEP and Supplier databases with manual lookups | Partly. Database search, no inference |
| Paperwork check (CoC, 8130-3, EASA Form 1, OCM trace) | Human reads the PDF, then phones or emails the issuer | **No.** There is no authoritative registry of issued 8130-3s or Form 1s, and the FAA does not keep one |
| External visual inspection (IDEA-1010, AS6171/2) | Microscope plus inspector, with photo comparison to ERAI images | Little. Cybord automates this **on the SMT line only**, after the purchase decision |
| XRF plating/lead composition, and alloy ID for mechanical parts | The instrument is automated. Interpretation against spec is human | The instrument is. The judgement is not |
| 2D X-ray and CT (die, bond-wire, lead-frame comparison) | Human compares to a known-good "golden" sample, if one exists | Research only (DATUM, ChipCheck, patents). **The bottleneck is the missing golden-reference library**, because OCMs do not share die images or bond maps |
| SAM/CSAM, decap and die marking | Manual, destructive and sample-based | No |
| Electrical test, curve trace, burn-in | ATE is automated, but writing a test program per part number is manual. Burn-in runs on a physical clock | Partly. Program generation is manual and the clock cannot be removed |
| Final "suspect counterfeit" determination and report | Engineer judgement written as a PDF, then filed to GIDEP or ERAI by hand | **No** |
| Mechanical parts (fasteners, bearings, castings, rotables) | Dimensional/CMM check, hardness, XRF/LIBS alloy test, plus paperwork. Fatigue life and heat treat cannot be verified without destroying the part | Little. Mostly a paperwork-trust system |
| Post-detection (quarantine, fleet sweep, finding which aircraft or boards carry the lot) | Manual record searches (the EASA SUP advice is to "check if installed") | No |

**Main point (opinion).** The physical measurements are mostly automated: the XRF, X-ray and ATE instruments run on their own. What is not automated is everything after measurement:
- the **reference** to compare against (golden samples and OEM design data);
- the **provenance** of the paperwork;
- the **judgement** and its acceptance by the customer or regulator;
- the **trace-forward** to installed parts.

These are the same permission and data-rights gates the prior review found in repair monopolies.

## 4. Binding constraints and what would change them

- **Physics clocks.** Burn-in (48-168 h), environmental tests and destructive decap or fatigue tests on samples. To change this you would need validated non-destructive surrogates, such as CT plus electrical fingerprinting that correlates with decap results. A large paired dataset (non-destructive scan matched to destructive truth) would let AS6171 test levels be relaxed with evidence. Labs destroy samples every day and throw that pairing away.
- **Data access.** OCMs and OEMs will not hand over die images, bond maps or drawings, so golden references are scarce. To change this: build reference fingerprints from trusted-channel parts yourself (franchised-distributor buys, DLA QPL stock), or license OEM data as a counterfeit-protection service the OEM is paid for or benefits from.
- **Regulator and customer acceptance.** AS6171 and DFARS name test methods and lab accreditation, not AI. To change this: get an AI method written into an AS6171 slash sheet through SAE G-19, or win DLA, DMEA or NAVSEA acceptance through a pilot such as an SBIR or an OTA at DLA Land & Maritime.
- **Law and liability.** Under DFARS 252.246-7007 the contractor carries the cost of counterfeit rework, and labs carry the liability for their reports. An AI verdict needs a liable signer, such as an accredited lab that owns the AI.
- **Paperwork provenance.** No registry exists. To change this: a signed issuance ledger in which repair stations, OEMs and DARs countersign every 8130-3 or Form 1 at the moment of issue. This is a network-effects problem, not a technical one.

## 5. Gaps nobody fills today (opinion)

1. **Pre-purchase or receiving-inspection AI for the open market.** Cybord inspects at placement, after you already own the parts. Nobody sells an automated receiving-inspection cell that runs visual, XRF, X-ray/CT and curve trace, judges the results with AI, and outputs an AS6171-formatted report.
2. **A golden-reference fingerprint library** (optical, X-ray and CT, XRF and electrical) covering obsolete and legacy part numbers. This is the parts used on C-130 and KC-135, where counterfeits concentrate because those parts are bought from brokers.
3. **A registry and verifier for paperwork provenance** (8130-3, Form 1, CoC). AOG Technics and the Cessna case were both caught only by phoning the issuer.
4. **An evidence-based way to reduce test levels.** Use paired non-destructive and destructive data to justify less destructive sampling, giving each lot a probability instead of a pass/fail stamp.
5. **Trace-forward and fleet sweep.** When a lot is flagged, automatically find every installed instance. This needs MRO record data.
6. **Mechanical part authentication beyond paperwork.** This means combining alloy ID, microstructure (CT, eddy current) and dimensional checks against the type design. It is the same measurement stack as reverse engineering.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side (opinion)

- **A. An AI-native AS6171 lab, run as an "Authentication Factory".** It owns the accreditation (the liable signer). Robotic cells run optical, XRF, X-ray/CT and curve-trace tests, AI makes the determination, and a human signs. The KPI is cost and hours per lot, against today's estimate of roughly $1-5k and 1-3 weeks (recalled). Every destructive test it runs adds a non-destructive-to-destructive training pair, which builds a data moat. It sells to DLA, primes and brokers under DFARS.
- **B. Shared infrastructure with the Approval Factory.** Detecting a counterfeit and reverse-engineering a part are **the same measurement problem**: characterise an unknown part against a reference. The Approval Factory's TDP generation (dimensional, material, functional comparison) and the counterfeit lab can share one metrology and AI stack. The lab earns cash from day one and builds the part-fingerprint library that later speeds Source Approval Requests and first-article inspections.
- **C. A paperwork provenance network.** An AI forgery detector for 8130-3 and Form 1 documents (signature, DAR status, serial reuse against scrapped-aircraft records, PDF forensics), with a cryptographic issuance ledger behind it. This sits next to Rotabull, SkyThread and GoDirect, but none of them verify issuance.
- **D. Golden-reference data licensing.** Build a fingerprint database from trusted-channel buys and license it to labs, Cybord-style line inspection, and DMEA.

## 7. Players (threat to the Approval Factory: direct, adjacent or none)

- Cybord: visual AI at placement. Adjacent. About $20M raised in total (evidence plus profile).
- Battelle Barricade: counterfeit IC screening. Adjacent (recalled).
- DMEA/CALCE/IAI DATUM: research. Adjacent.
- Chip Scan: design and trojan assurance, plus reverse engineering of legacy FPGAs (Deep Lift). **Adjacent to direct** on legacy electronics reverse engineering.
- ERAI and GIDEP: reporting databases. None or adjacent; ERAI could be a data partner.
- SMT Corp, Integra, Process Sciences and other AS6171 labs: manual labs. Adjacent; they are the incumbents play A would displace.
- Applied DNA, SMX, Alitheon: marking and fingerprinting. Adjacent.
- Moog VeriPart, SkyThread, GoDirect Trade: provenance and trading. Adjacent to play C.
- Lumafield, Creative Electron, Nordson, Zeiss: CT and X-ray tool vendors. Adjacent; they would be suppliers.
