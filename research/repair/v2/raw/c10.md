# c10: AI in NDT and inspection acceptance (Gecko Robotics, Waygate/Baker Hughes ADR, Voliro, Eddyfi, Scientific Ultrasonics, Avonic, Boeing/Airbus AI borescope, Rolls-Royce IntelligentEngine): who signs, what regulators accept

## 1. Evidence gathered

### FAA-accepted AI or robot-assisted inspection: the one clean precedent
- A4A/SAE 21st NDT Innovation Award, announced 16 Sep 2025 and presented 23 Sep 2025 at the A4A NDT Forum in Fort Worth. It went to Delta Air Lines (11 staff) and Mainblades (Floris Straver) for the "first FAA-accepted, drone-assisted autonomous general visual inspection (GVI)" of aircraft surface defects: fasteners, corrosion, scratches, dents and lightning strikes. Scope is outdoor flights at ATL, MSP and DTW, covering 17 aircraft models and about 990 aircraft. **Human inspectors still review the images and make the call.** The drone replaces access, not judgment. Source: https://airlines.org/news/a4a-sae-ndt-innovation-award-honors-team-for-first-faa-accepted-automatic-drone-based-aircraft-inspection-program/
- The mechanism was not stated. It was most likely an "alternative method" accepted under Delta's FAA-accepted maintenance program / Part 121 CAMP, with Delta as the certificate holder taking responsibility (inference).

### Engine borescope ADR
- GE Aerospace and Waygate Technologies (Baker Hughes) announced on 28 Oct 2024 an AI-assisted ADR update for the Mentor Visual iQ+ borescope, for HPC inspection on GEnx and CFM LEAP in GE's MRO network. They claim +33.6% recall and +13.5% precision versus ADR model v4.1, trained on "thousands of new representative images". **There is no regulatory statement.** It is positioned as an aid to the inspector. Source: https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/ge-aerospace-waygate-technologies-deliver-new-ai-assisted-commercial-jet-engine
- Vendor pages (Roboflow, Oxmaint) say a certified inspector "retains disposition". They supply "documentation to support" Part 145 and OEM manual compliance. That is not an approval. Sources: https://roboflow.com/ai/automated-borescope-inspection , https://roboflow.com/ai/automated-ndt-inspection-ai
- (recalled) Other players: Aiir Innovations (NL, borescope AI, Lufthansa Technik and others), Lufthansa Technik AURORA robotic borescope, Rolls-Royce IntelligentEngine plus its SWARM/FLARE micro-robot concepts and remote borescope with partners, Pratt & Whitney digital borescope, Donecle (FR, drone GVI), Airbus Advanced Inspection Drone (2018). In all of these, the human signs and the OEM manual sets the limits.

### Standards and qualification
- MIL-HDBK-1823A (2009) is the POD standard for aerospace NDE. Empirical trials need roughly 30 to 100 realistic flaw specimens. Model-assisted POD (MAPOD) is a recognised research route (ISU CNDE MAPOD workshops), and TWI is building protocols on 1823A that include automated analysis. Sources: https://www.bindt.org/admin/Downloads/BINDT-Aero2019_MAQ_Wall_8Presented.pdf ; https://www.cnde.iastate.edu/mapod/2006%20October/MAPODdemo-draft06.pdf
- An MDPI review (2025, search snippet only, fetch returned 403) names the "lack of broadly accepted code provisions for AI-based accept/reject decisions" as a key barrier. It also notes that ML accuracy/F1 is not the same as qualified POD. Source: https://www.mdpi.com/2813-477X/4/3/17
- NRC uses EPRI PDI (ASME Section XI Appendix VIII performance demonstration) results to estimate human inspector POD for xLPR. Source: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1401/ML14014A344.pdf
- A Patsnap blog (opinion) argues that AI crack detection needs a quantitative POD curve feeding the damage-tolerance evaluation (AC 25.571), and that FAA Order 8110.105A and RTCA SC-216 (ML) would apply. https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/articles/ai-crack-detection-certification-60-patent-insights/
- (recalled) EASA AI Concept Paper Issue 2 (2024) sets levels 1A/1B (assistance), 2A/2B (human-AI teaming) and 3 (advanced automation), and covers maintenance and Part 145 use cases. The FAA Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance v1 (Jul 2024) is incremental, favours "learned" (frozen) models over "learning" ones, and is aimed at aircraft systems first.
- (recalled) **Nuclear steam generator eddy-current Automated Data Analysis (ADA)** is the strongest precedent anywhere for a machine making the accept/reject call. Under the EPRI PWR SG Examination Guidelines, an ADA system can be qualified per Appendix H/I examination technique specification sheets and used in place of one of the two independent analysts (primary or secondary). Examples are Zetec RevospECT and Westinghouse/Framatome ADA. Its acceptance rests on a qualified performance demonstration, not on explainability.
- (recalled) Pipe mills (ISO 10893, API 5L/5CT) have run automated UT/ET accept/reject with alarm thresholds for decades. Automated digital radiography ADR for automotive and aero castings (Waygate, Volume Graphics) is also accepted inline. These deterministic thresholded systems are accepted because they are qualified with reference standards.
- (recalled) Trueflaw (Finland) makes real thermal-fatigue cracks and "virtual flaws" for ML training. With VTT and Aalto (Koskinen, Virkkunen et al.) it published results showing ML UT data analysis meeting or exceeding human PDI-style performance on nuclear welds. EPRI has run ML-for-UT research programs.

## 2. Who signs, today

| Domain | Who makes and signs the accept/reject | What the regulator actually approves |
|---|---|---|
| FAA aviation maintenance | A certificated mechanic/repairman at a Part 145 station or air carrier (14 CFR 43, 65, 145, 121). The NDT tech is qualified per NAS 410 / EN 4179 by the employer's written practice, the Level 3 approves the procedure, and an RII inspector covers required-inspection items. | The OEM manual (AMM, EMM, NDT manual) or other "acceptable data", plus the operator's maintenance program. The FAA accepts the method. It does not certify the AI. |
| FAA design and production (PMA, repairs) | A DER, or ODA unit members, signs 8110-3 compliance data. QA signs First Article Inspection (AS9102). | The data package (design, test, inspection plan). |
| DLA SAR (military spares) | The DLA/ESA engineering support activity reviews the evidence: drawings, FAI, test reports and inspection data. | The source approval itself. |
| Nuclear ISI | ASME Section XI Appendix VIII qualified personnel, procedure and equipment via EPRI PDI. The ANII (authorized nuclear inservice inspector, employed by an insurer such as HSB) witnesses and verifies. NRC oversees. | The qualified procedure plus the performance demonstration. SG ADA counts as one analyst. |
| Pressure equipment (refining, chemicals) | API 510/570/653 certified inspector, with ASNT-qualified techs. | The owner's inspection program, plus jurisdiction or insurer. |
| Navy | NAVSEA tech pubs (e.g. T9074-AS-GIB-010/271) and NAVSEA-qualified personnel. | Procedure approval by NAVSEA. |

**The common thread:** in every regime the regulator approves a procedure and a person (and in nuclear, a demonstrated performance), never a model. Software is accepted as equipment inside a qualified procedure. The legal place for AI is "equipment + procedure", with a qualified human or organisation holding the signature. The one place software already takes a signing role is nuclear SG ADA, and it got there through performance demonstration.

## 3. The verification pathway: steps and gaps

1. **Specimen and flaw set creation.** Realistic flaws (fatigue cracks, corrosion, porosity) in representative material are the physics clock. They take weeks to months and cost $1k–$20k+ per specimen (recalled order of magnitude). Suppliers are a handful (Trueflaw, FlawTech, Sonaspection). *Gap:* no shared, statistically designed, platform-specific flaw library. Every POD study rebuilds its own.
2. **POD study (MIL-HDBK-1823A).** 30–100+ flaws, multiple inspectors, hit/miss or a-hat analysis. This takes months and is done per method, part and procedure. *Gap:* this is the bottleneck for using any new AI method in damage-tolerance-critical inspection. MAPOD exists in research but is rarely accepted in submissions.
3. **Procedure writing and Level 3 approval.** *Gap:* no standard language anywhere (NAS 410, ASTM, ASME) for a "frozen ML model as part of the equipment" with drift monitoring, re-qualification triggers or data provenance (recalled; worth verifying NAS 410 Rev 5 and ASTM E07 work items).
4. **Personnel qualification.** Employer-based, so the "qualified" entity could in principle be an organisation that owns both the model and the Level 3.
5. **Regulator or OEM acceptance.** Aviation: an OEM manual revision or alternative-method acceptance by the operator's PMI. Nuclear: PDI. DLA: ESA review. *Gap:* there is no route for a third party (not OEM, not operator) to qualify a new AI inspection method once and have many operators reuse it, the way an STC works for a design change.
6. **In-service monitoring.** *Gap:* no continuous POD tracking, drift detection or field-miss feedback loop is required or done. Field misses surface only after an incident.
7. **For the Approval Factory specifically (reverse-engineered part equivalence):** CT and CMM metrology, materials analysis (chemistry, microstructure, hardness), FAI, then qualification tests (fatigue, rig). *Gap:* every package is hand-assembled. No one sells an automated "equivalence evidence package", meaning CT-to-CAD deviation plus material fingerprint plus statistical comparison to OEM parts formatted for ESA or DER review.

## 4. What would need to be done (assume unlimited compute and intelligence)

- **Qualified-POD-as-a-service for AI methods.** Build physics simulation (CIVA-class UT/ET/RT models plus generative flaw synthesis) calibrated with a small physical specimen set. Get MAPOD accepted for transfer-function POD. Deliver MIL-HDBK-1823A-compliant POD curves for an AI+sensor procedure in weeks, not a year. *What it takes to change acceptance:* run a joint program with the FAA Tech Center (William J. Hughes, Atlantic City, the AANC Sandia NDI validation center heritage), EPRI and the AFRL/CASR POD community, so that one method gets qualified under both empirical and model-assisted routes and the agreement between them is published.
- **Write the missing standard.** Draft and push an ASTM E07 / SAE AMS-NDT / ASME Section V article for "automated/ML data analysis qualification": a frozen model with a hash, a qualified training and validation set, a POD at a stated confidence (a90/95), drift monitors and re-qualification triggers. Model it on EPRI SG ADA Appendix H, which already works. Whoever writes the standard shapes the moat.
- **A third-party "method STC".** A route where an independent organisation qualifies an AI inspection method (sensor + model + procedure) once, and operators adopt it by reference, the same way NDT manual references work. Near term, the FAA alternative-method acceptance Delta/Mainblades got (2025) can be replicated per operator. The longer-term fix is an AC or policy statement.
- **A shared flaw-specimen bank and virtual-twin library** for legacy platforms (C-130, KC-135 structure, T56 engine parts). It would be useful both for POD and for training.
- **Continuous field POD.** Every AI-flagged and human-dispositioned finding feeds a fleet-level POD and false-call estimate, which makes the evidence for regulators cumulative.
- **Evidence packages for part equivalence (the link to the Approval Factory).** Automated CT, metrology and materials fingerprinting with statistical equivalence reports, formatted to DLA SAR categories and 8110-3 expectations. This reduces reviewer hours, which is the KPI.
- **Liability and insurance wrapper.** Nuclear already uses insurer-employed ANIIs. An insurer-backed verification organisation that underwrites its AI dispositions would turn the "who signs" problem into a product.

## 5. Opinion: where the AI play is

- Robotics and data capture (Gecko, Voliro, Mainblades, Donecle) is crowded and well funded. Gecko alone is at $1.25B.
- AI defect assist is done by OEM/instrument incumbents (Waygate/GE, Olympus/Evident, Eddyfi, Zetec) plus image-AI startups. It is commoditising, and **none of them carry the signature or a qualified POD.**
- **The unoccupied layer is qualification itself.** That means turning an AI inspection method into regulator-acceptable evidence (POD, procedure, standard, continuous assurance) at low marginal cost. It sits upstream of every inspection AI and inside the Approval Factory's own bottleneck, since every reverse-engineered part needs inspection criteria and equivalence evidence. Physics clocks (specimen fabrication, a minimum physical validation set) and the habits of regulator acceptance are the binding constraints. Both can be shrunk with MAPOD acceptance and a standard, and neither is fixed by law. 14 CFR does not prohibit a qualified automated analysis inside an accepted procedure.
- Nuclear SG ADA proves that machine accept/reject can be legitimised through performance demonstration. Aviation has no equivalent yet, and that is the opening.
