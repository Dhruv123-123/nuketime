# c16: Defence sustainment software and spare-parts AI (Vannevar?, Govini, Rune, Craitor, Systecon, PTC Servigistics, Palantir in sustainment, Shift5): who targets sole-source parts and price spikes?

Method: 3 WebSearch calls (used), ~15 WebFetch attempts (several 403/empty). Items marked (recalled) are from model memory and are not verified this session.

## Bottom line
None of the named sustainment-software companies does the "Approval Factory" job (creating a second, government-approved source for a sole-source part). They split into:
1. **Demand/stock planners** (Rune TyrOS, PTC Servigistics, Systecon Opus Suite): they forecast consumption and optimise stock levels for parts that already exist. A sole-source part is just a long-lead, high-cost SKU to them.
2. **Illuminators** (Govini Ark, Palantir, Exiger, Interos, Altana): they map supplier networks and flag single points of failure, foreign dependence and sole-source exposure. They find the targets but do not make the approval.
3. **Platform data** (Shift5): it reads onboard bus data for predictive maintenance and cyber. That is useful in-service evidence, but nobody uses it for parts approval.
4. **Edge fabrication** (Craitor, and additive work generally): it can make a part, but it cannot make the part *approved*.
5. **Vannevar Labs** is open-source intelligence on foreign targets. It is not in sustainment.

The real competitors are the old reverse-engineering and PMA shops (HEICO, Parts Life Inc, small DLA SAR houses) and the government's own price-challenge programmes. Nobody owns the **verification and evidence layer**: turning scan, test and field data into a package a reviewer accepts, plus reviewer-side tooling for DLA Engineering Support Activities (ESA) and FAA DER/ACO reviewers.

## Verification/approval pathway (DLA SAR route) and gaps
1. **Pick the target.** Find NSNs coded sole-source (AMC/AMSC codes), with price history from DIBBS/FPDS and demand from FLIS.
   - Today: Govini and Palantir partly do this; it is otherwise manual.
   - Gap: nobody ranks parts by (price spike x demand x likelihood the SAR succeeds x data availability).
2. **Get technical data.**
   - Today: often unavailable, held by the OEM under limited rights; JEDMICS/cFolders hold partial TDPs.
   - Gap: rights are law-bound and no AI fixes that. Reverse engineering is the legal workaround.
3. **Reverse-engineer.** CT/CMM metrology, materials ID, process inference, tolerance stack.
   - Today: 300-1,000 engineering hours per package; tools are point solutions.
4. **Build the SAR package.** Category per DLA SAR guidance, comparison data, drawings, process specs, test plan.
   - Gap: assembling the package is manual and nobody automates it end to end.
5. **ESA/engineering review.**
   - (recalled) Months to more than a year per SAR, with a backlog at the service engineering activities. This review capacity is the binding scarce resource.
   - Gap: no reviewer-side AI is sold to the government.
6. **First article test / qualification test.** Physical; this is a physics clock.
   - Gap: simulation/ICME analysis rarely earns credit that waives tests.
7. **Win the competitive buy, then do production lot acceptance and quality escapes.**
   - Gap: in-service field data is not fed back as evidence.

## What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence and compute)
- An **auto-SAR / auto-substantiation compiler**: from CT scans, materials data and legacy drawings, produce a reviewer-format package in which every claim is linked to evidence and uncertainty.
- A **reviewer copilot sold to DLA ESAs and FAA ACOs**: pre-screen packages, check completeness, flag deviations from precedent. This attacks the capacity bottleneck directly and needs government acceptance via SBIR/OTA.
- **Precedent graph**: mine every past SAR, PMA and approval decision so that new packages argue from accepted similarity.
- **Test credit by analysis**: probabilistic, physics-based models validated against historical test results to argue for reduced or combined first-article tests. Changing this needs regulator policy, so pilot it with a single ESA.
- **Target radar**: a public-data model (FPDS, DIBBS award history, DoD IG findings, single-bid rates) ranking NSNs by savings x feasibility. Sell it to DLA as a "should-cost plus second-source" service.
- **Field evidence loop**: platform bus data (Shift5-type) and failure data as in-service equivalence evidence after approval.
