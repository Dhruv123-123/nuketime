# g10: Power generation: how gas turbine owners, insurers (HSB, FM Global) and lenders accept non-OEM parts and repairs (no regulator); what evidence is demanded; who decides.

Date: 2026-10-20. WebSearch used 3/3. Figures marked (recalled) are from memory, not fetched this session.

## 1. Bottom line

- In land-based power generation there is no government approver for gas turbine parts. There is no FAA PMA, DLA SAR or NRC 10 CFR 21 equivalent. "Permission" is spread across three private parties:
  1. The **owner's** engineering staff (or a hired consultant) qualifies the vendor.
  2. The **OEM's LTSA/warranty**. Using non-OEM parts lets the OEM void warranties or cancel service agreements.
  3. The **property/machinery-breakdown insurer**. No approved-parts list exists. Insurers act through "material change in risk", deductibles, LEG design exclusions, serial-loss clauses, prototype/unproven classification, and adjusters' causation calls after a loss.
  - Lenders sit behind both the LTSA and the insurer. Their independent engineer usually treats an OEM LTSA as the "bankable" default (opinion/recalled, not fetched).
- The key evidence comes from insurers themselves. Per IMIA WGP 64 (09) (authors from HSB, Munich Re, Swiss Re, RSA, Charles Taylor, Cunningham Lindsey):
  - "Owners are mostly responsible on their own for verification and assessment of the quality of non-OEM service providers and non-OEM spare parts suppliers. There is no independent organisation to assist the Owner."
  - "Currently there is no comprehensive turbine loss register."
  - URL: https://www.imia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IMIA-WGP-64-Combustion-Turbines-FINALa-4.pdf
- So the verification layer is not done badly. It is **missing**.

## 2. Evidence collected

### IMIA WGP 64 (09) "Combustion Turbines: Critical Losses and Trends" (Istanbul 2009)
- Working group included Michael Bove (Hartford Steam Boiler), Bernd Luckey (Munich Re) and Patrice Nigon (Swiss Re).
- **Insurers depend on the OEM:**
  - "insurers are increasingly in the hands of the manufacturers for assurance that operation and maintenance is being carried out correctly"
  - Users and insurers are "very vulnerable to substandard repairs" by third parties.
  - It warns of "extreme risk of rogue parts" and reverse-engineered parts "of dubious quality".
  - "Parts traceability is of serious concern to all the major manufacturers."
- **Spare-parts tiers:**
  - OEM parts.
  - Non-OEM new parts, "manufactured under different patents or using improved design features".
  - Grey-market and reconditioned "brown parts".
- **Reverse engineering:** model quality depends on "measurement techniques, data approximation errors, numerical methods, wear and tear of the object and 'frozen-in' errors". The paper gives a successful case: a redesigned transition piece, validated by resonance/mode testing plus material, seal and cooling changes, beat the original part's life.
- **Market shape:**
  - "Where Owners use non-OEM spare parts, the manufacturers can cancel warranties and service agreements. Therefore the spare part market is bigger for smaller turbines."
  - Non-OEM share is "much higher for combustion turbines older than 5 years".
  - Within this industry, non-OEM parts are "generally of higher quality compared to other spare part markets".
- **Insurer ambivalence:** non-OEM parts "might reduce a BI loss". But insurers "are not keen to pay for repairs and parts which might cause future severe damage".
- **LTSA effects on insurers:**
  - Parts may belong to the OEM, so insurers cannot inspect them or recover salvage.
  - Waivers of subrogation are common.
  - The OEM is often a named insured.
  - Owners who move to Independent Service Providers (ISPs) get OEM threats of warranty/LTSA cancellation.
- **Technology status:** prototype / unproven / proven / "problematical".
  - "The first 2000 hours of operation seems critical."
  - "Substantial engineering changes" can push a proven model back to unproven.
  - In 2004 the broker Marsh kept a list of models graded on this scale (reported in the EPRI CT Experience & Intelligence report 2005).
- **Root-cause analysis:** "A RCA team comprised only of the OEM's personnel ... should not be accepted by insurers". Parts and service agreements "can restrict the accessibility of the offending hardware."
- **Loss examples:**
  - $11M and $19M hot-gas-path losses. In one, the OEM quoted full replacement of undamaged items, but analysis showed many blades and vanes could be repaired.
  - "Use of non OEM or aftermarket components leading to voiding of manufacturer warranties" appears as a listed QA loss cause.
  - A repaired inlet segment had weld porosity and caused multi-blade damage.

### IMIA WGP 67 (10) "Fast developing technology underwriting and claims issues" (Berlin 2010)
- Authors included HSB London, Munich Re, Swiss Re, SCOR and HDI.
- "Gas Turbine components are mostly patented and therefore can only be manufactured and sold by the patent owners or OEM".
- Free-on-board lead times "sometimes reach 3 years". Downtime can exceed 6 months.
- **Underwriter tools:**
  - Ask the insured for "measured operating hours free of claims for a similar type of machine".
  - Munich Re's "material change in risk" definition.
  - High deductibles.
  - Insure only known components.
  - LEG1/DE1 exclusions (exclude design risk) or LEG2/DE3 (limit it).
  - Munich Re Endorsement 011 Serial Losses: a sliding scale of 100% on the first loss, then declining for repeat losses from the same design cause.
- URL: https://www.imia.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/IMIA-WGP-6710-Fast-Dev-Tech-20.6.10-2.pdf
- Other IMIA papers: WGP 13 (00) Large Gas Turbines; WGP 52 (07) Insuring Emerging Technologies.

### FM Global (Terence Lee, ESD News, 7 Feb 2020)
- FM Global insures about 7% of global power generation assets.
- Clients had about 20 gas turbine failures a year over the decade. The article gives an average cost of about US$100M, but it is ambiguous whether that is per failure or per year.
- Compressor and turbine blades cause "well over three quarters of all turbine losses".
- Insurers and owners traditionally treat a lead unit running **8,000 hours** failure-free as "mature". FM Global says this is now inadequate: there is cyclic duty, few OEM test facilities, and some tested technology still failed.
- FM Global recommends validation chosen case by case, and wants OEMs to carry more loss liability where technology is unvalidated.
- URL: https://esdnews.com.au/a-new-approach-to-managing-evolving-risks-in-gas-turbine-technology/
- FM Global property loss prevention data sheets exist for gas turbines (DS 7-79 is fire protection for gas turbines (recalled)). I found no FM rule naming acceptable non-OEM parts.

### Owner practice (Turbomachinery International, "Aftermarket parts: is there life after the OEM")
- Most attendees at one US user meeting used non-OEM parts, including 1st-stage buckets, shroud blocks, vanes and row-17 blades.
- Chromalloy advises using the OEM for designs under about 10 years old.
- **Vetting steps:**
  - Start early, because qualification takes months.
  - Check references.
  - Visit several vendor sites.
  - Hire a consultant or subject-matter expert.
  - Review quality documents.
- Service market about $16-17B/yr, up to about $20B with upgrades. F-class aftermarket split: parts 68%, repairs 18%, field service 14%.
- Non-OEM savings of 30% or more off list price.
- Third-party warranties are based on equivalent operating hours. Third parties "must go further than OEMs to win confidence".
- URL: https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/aftermarket-parts-is-there-life-after-the-oem

### LTSA terms (Power Engineering, 2001)
- Major maintenance is 50-80% of lifetime maintenance cost; 80% or more of that is parts (Dynegy).
- At the time, only OEMs offered LTSAs for F/G/H machines. Non-OEMs were weak on directionally-solidified and single-crystal blades.
- Insurers may cancel or modify policies when LTSAs extend overhaul intervals.
- The owner decides on original vs replacement parts.
- URL: https://www.power-eng.com/gas/turbines/long-term-service-agreementsweighing-the-risks-and-rewards/

### OEM as gatekeeper (GE Vernova TransAlta case study)
- GE inspects every part for non-GE hardware: dimensions, material ID and **patent reviews**.
- It runs a third-party-parts program for non-GE parts in GE units.
- URL: https://www.gevernova.com/gas-power/resources/case-studies/transalta

## 3. Who is doing it (suppliers = the "approval factory" analogue in power)

- **PSM (Hanwha Power Systems), Jupiter FL**
  - Covers GE 7F/9F/6B/7EA/9E and Siemens/MHI 501F, plus FlameSheet, LEC III, GTOP and AutoTune.
  - 350+ upgrades in 30+ countries.
  - Certifications are quality-system ones (ISO 9001, AS9100). No product-validation evidence is published.
  - Ansaldo sold PSM to Hanwha in 2021 (recalled).
  - URL: https://www.psm.com/
- **EthosEnergy**
  - Formed 2014 as a JV of Wood and Siemens. Moved to One Equity Partners in Dec 2024.
  - Its CEO says OEMs quote 24-36 months for upgrades vs EthosEnergy's 7 months (Power Engineering, 30 Oct 2025).
- **Others:**
  - Chromalloy (casting in Tampa).
  - Turbine Services Ltd (600+ components engineered since 1972).
  - Sulzer, MD&A, Allied Power Group, Liburdi (recalled).
  - OEMs acting as ISPs on each other's fleets (IMIA).
- **Data / verification adjacent:**
  - Strategic Power Systems ORAP: reliability database for gas, steam, wind and solar plants and reciprocating engines, about 40-50 years old. ORAP Parts-Trac tracks the expended life of serialized parts. Partners include EPRI, NERC and ASME. https://www.spsinc.com/
  - EPRI CT program.
  - User groups: 7F Users Group, CTOTF, WTUI, 501F Users (recalled).
  - Waygate Technologies (Baker Hughes) AI borescope (recalled).
  - Gecko Robotics: boiler inspection robots plus data, roughly $1.25B valuation in 2025 (recalled).
  - OEM remote monitoring (GE, Siemens Omnivise).
- **Nobody found** sells an independent, insurer-recognised qualification of non-OEM gas turbine parts or repairs.

## 4. Pathway (power gen) and what is broken

| Step | Who | Evidence today | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Design / reverse-engineer part or repair | ISP (PSM, Ethos, Chromalloy, Sulzer) | Scan, metallurgy, FEA/CFD, rig tests (rare), field trial unit | Each ISP repeats the work. No shared standard of "equivalence". Patents (GE patent reviews). |
| 2. Owner qualification | Owner fleet engineering or consultant | References, site audits, QA docs, ISO certs, user-group anecdotes; takes months | No independent body (IMIA 2009). Small IPPs depend on the OEM. |
| 3. Contract | OEM LTSA / warranty | n/a | OEM can void warranty or cancel the LTSA. Parts owned by the OEM. Remote-monitoring data held by the OEM. |
| 4. Insurance placement / renewal | Underwriter engineers (FM Global, HSB/Munich Re, AXA XL, Allianz, Swiss Re, Lloyd's energy) | Claims-free fleet hours (2,000 critical; 8,000 = "proven"), OEM involvement, "material change in risk" | No parts-level loss register. Prototype classification is binary and blunt. Non-OEM parts are tolerated, not certified. |
| 5. Financing / refinancing | Lenders' independent engineer (Leidos, Sargent & Lundy, DNV, Black & Veatch (recalled)) | O&M plan, LTSA, budgets | IE defaults to the OEM LTSA as bankable (opinion). |
| 6. In service | Owner / ISP | Borescope at combustion, hot-gas-path and major inspections; parts-life tracking | Data is fragmented and OEM-held. |
| 7. Loss / claim | Adjusters (Charles Taylor, Sedgwick), forensic engineers | RCA. If a non-OEM part is implicated: subrogation, exclusions | RCA is often OEM-dominated (IMIA). Hardware is inaccessible under LTSAs. |

## 5. What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence/compute)

1. **An independent qualification standard plus an issuing body for non-OEM gas turbine parts and repairs** (a "PMA for power" by private standard).
   - Contents of each part's equivalency dossier:
     - CT/CMM scan of N OEM parts to get the population geometry distribution.
     - Chemistry and microstructure.
     - Coating spec.
     - FEA/CFD life prediction under cyclic duty.
     - Frequency/mode bench test.
     - Field-trial plan.
   - Insurers (HSB, FM Global) would co-sign and give a premium/deductible credit, so that a qualified part is not a "material change in risk".
   - Constraint: insurer acceptance. Fix: launch as an underwriting tool for one insurer or MGA, then turn it into a standard (ASME/API committee or an ORAP-like consortium).
2. **A gas turbine parts loss and experience register**, the one IMIA asked for in 2009. It would hold serialized part histories (hours, starts, trips, inspection findings, failures) across OEM and non-OEM parts.
   - Inputs: ORAP data, owner CMMS, borescope video, adjuster files.
   - Use: replace "8,000 fleet-leader hours" with Bayesian reliability per design, so non-OEM parts reach "proven" faster.
   - Constraint: data access. Fix: insurers and adjusters already hold the claims files, and owners own the operating data, not the OEM.
3. **An AI-run independent RCA / forensic service for adjusters and insurers** (non-OEM), using borescope, metallurgy and operating data. IMIA says OEM-only RCA "should not be accepted".
4. **Insure the part: a warranty plus performance wrap**, sold through an MGA backed by a reinsurer.
   - Removes the "OEM voids warranty" lever, because the owner holds a better warranty from a rated carrier.
   - Historical analog: Hartford Steam Boiler (1866) was founded on inspect-then-insure.
5. **Condition-based life acceptance**: AI borescope, thermography and data turn interval-based overhaul into per-part life consumption, which is the evidence insurers want for extended intervals.
6. **Physics clocks still apply.** Field-trial hours on a lead unit and rig tests must physically run.
   - Mitigation: digital-twin life prediction validated against the register, and accelerated cyclic rig testing at shared test cells. FM Global says there are "few OEM test facilities", so an independent hot-gas test cell is itself a gap.
7. **Law: OEM patents** (GE runs patent reviews). This needs design-around (AI generative redesign that beats the original, which PSM already does by hand) and freedom-to-operate analysis per part.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side

- **"UL for turbine aftermarket"**: an AI-generated equivalency and life dossier per part or repair, insurer-recognised, paid for by ISPs and owners. Revenue per dossier plus annual surveillance.
- **Turbine loss and experience register plus actuarial model**, sold to engineering insurers and reinsurers (HSB/Munich Re, FM Global, Swiss Re, AXA XL, Allianz) for pricing and "proven" status.
- **Independent forensic RCA plus repair-scope optimiser for adjusters.** The IMIA $11M case shows the OEM quoting replacement of undamaged items, so savings land directly on the claim.
- **An MGA that insures non-OEM parts and repairs** (warranty plus breakdown wrap), using the dossier as its underwriting engine. This is the only route that directly breaks the OEM warranty-voiding lever.
- **IE / lender module**: bankability scoring of an ISP O&M plan for refinancings of merchant gas plants. Timely, because the 2025-26 turbine shortage is extending legacy fleet life.
- **Fit with the Approval Factory:** in power, the approver is private (insurer plus owner), so a startup can become the approver's tool. This is faster than DLA or FAA: no statute and no regulator queue. But because no regulator stands behind the approval, it confers weaker permission: owners can always ignore it.

## 7. Surprises

- The insurers' own trade body said (2009) that no independent verifier and no loss register exist. I could not see that anyone has filled this gap since.
- FM Global publicly says the 8,000-hour fleet-leader rule is inadequate. That leaves an opening for a better evidence standard.
- OEMs themselves act as non-OEM ISPs on rivals' fleets, so OEM safety arguments against third-party parts are partly commercial.
- PSM's public credentials are quality-system certificates only. No product-validation evidence is published.
