# s07: Production approval and quality system (PMA production approval, AS9100, AS9102 FAI). Cost and time to stand up; could it be shared across a supplier network?

Search budget: 3 WebSearch used. Figures marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this run.

## 1. What the step legally is

### FAA PMA (14 CFR Part 21 Subpart K)
- A PMA is a combined design + production approval for one replacement/modification article. 21.303(a) requires: the product it installs on, **the name and address of the manufacturing facilities**, design data (drawings, specs, dimensional/material/process info), test reports and computations, and a compliance statement. 21.303(b): the applicant must do the inspections and tests to show material conformity, conformity to design, and conformity of manufacturing processes. Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/21.303
- The holder must hold a quality system meeting **14 CFR 21.137**, which has 15 elements: design data control, document control, **supplier control**, manufacturing process control, inspecting and testing, inspection/measuring/test equipment control, inspection and test status, nonconforming product control, corrective/preventive action, handling and storage, quality records, internal audits, in-service feedback, quality escapes, issuing authorized release documents (8130-3). Source: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/14/21.137
- 21.137(c) supplier control only requires procedures that make sure each supplier-provided item meets the holder's requirements, plus a feedback path to the supplier. **The law does not require the PMA holder to make parts itself.** Fabless or "virtual" PMA holders that use controlled suppliers are allowed, and many exist (evidence: the rule text. That many fabless holders exist is recalled, not verified).
- The FAA Manufacturing Inspection District Office (MIDO) evaluates the quality system before the first PMA is issued and then surveils the holder and its suppliers. The guidance is FAA Order 8120.22 (production approval procedures) and Order 8110.42 (PMA procedures) (recalled; faa.gov returned 403).
- **The key structural fact:** the quality system is approved once per holder and facility, not per part. Each later PMA is added to the holder's PMA supplement under the same quality system. The cost of standing up production approval is therefore a fixed cost spread over every PMA that follows. This favours the Approval Factory model, where one holder carries many approvals.

### AS9100D
- AS9100 is not an FAA legal requirement. 21.137 is. Customers (OEMs, airlines, MROs, primes) demand AS9100 in commercial practice.
- Published cost for a small shop is $10k-$50k, and time is 3-12 months. The sources are mostly vendor blogs. One estimate gives $10-20k for firms under 50 people, with consultants from about $20k. An ISO 9001 shop is said to already have about 75% of AS9100D. Sources: https://seller.alibaba.com/blogs/2026/southeast-asia/aerospace/as9100-certification-guide-alibaba-b2b , https://ecisolutions.com/blog/manufacturing/jobboss2/considering-as9100 , NIST MEP case of R&M Machine Shop (12 people, certified 2021): https://ws680.nist.gov/mepmeis/ss.ashx?SSID=5950
- On top of that come annual surveillance audits and a 3-year recertification, roughly $5-15k/yr in registrar fees (recalled), plus about 1-2 quality FTEs. Certificates are listed in the IAQG OASIS database (iaqg.org/oasis returned 403).
- AS9003 (the lighter standard for simple shops) is being replaced by IA9150 from Feb 2026, per an Elsmar forum thread: https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/as-9003a-2012-acceptance-why-not.60797/

### AS9102 First Article Inspection
- AS9102 Rev C (2023, recalled) defines the FAI report: Form 1 (part number accountability), Form 2 (materials, special processes, functional tests), Form 3 (every characteristic: balloon, requirement, result). The report is required on first production and again after design, process, location or tooling changes, or a 2-year production lapse (recalled).
- Labor: GroundControl's founders cite about **8 engineer-hours per FAI report**, with one facility doing about 15 reports/day. A rejected FAIR delays payment even when the parts conform. Source: https://www.ycombinator.com/launches/NWA-groundcontrol-vertical-ai-for-highly-regulated-manufacturing
- A forum poster warns that shops often don't price FAI into bids, even though customers require AS9102 regardless of AS9100: https://elsmar.com/elsmarqualityforum/threads/as9100-certification-for-small-2-man-business.42127/

### DLA / military side
- DLA does not issue a "production approval." The gates are source approval by the Engineering Support Activity (ESA) for Critical Safety Items (procurement note H04, DFARS 252.209-7010), plus a contract-level quality clause (FAR 52.246-11 higher-level quality, often ISO 9001/AS9100 equivalent), a possible DCMA pre-award survey, and First Article Testing.
- DLAD 9.3: the buyer must justify FAT over cheaper methods (DCMA inspection, product verification, lot testing). FAT can be waived if the offeror delivered the same or a similar item within the last 5 years (3 years for CSI). Under clause E08, FAT cost is absorbed into the production price unless it has its own CLIN. A Certificate of Conformance is not allowed for CSI unless the ESA approves it. Source: https://www.acquisition.gov/index%2Ephp/dlad/subpart-9.3-%E2%80%93-first-article-testing-and-approval
- Example: a March 2026 DLA Navy pin solicitation with government FAT plus production lot testing and only two approved sources (Sikorsky, Apex Manufacturing): https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16272128 (search snippet)
- Implication: past performance (deliveries within the last 3-5 years) is itself a verification asset that waives FAT. A network that keeps a shared delivery history and conformity record can turn that into FAT waivers.

## 2. Cost and time to stand up (estimates)

| Item | Cost | Time | Physics clock? |
|---|---|---|---|
| AS9100D cert, small shop | $10-50k plus 1-2 quality FTEs | 3-12 mo | No (paper plus audit) |
| 21.137 quality system manual and procedures for a PMA applicant | $50-200k consultant/staff (recalled) | 3-6 mo writing | No |
| First MIDO production approval evaluation (new PMA holder) | internal labor | 6-18 mo, driven by MIDO queue (recalled) | No; regulator capacity is the binding clock |
| Each FAI report (AS9102) | 8 h labor (GroundControl claim) plus CMM time | days | Partly (parts must be made and measured) |
| DLA FAT | priced into the bid; often $5-50k and 90-300 days including government test (recalled) | months | Yes (parts must be built and tested) |
| Annual upkeep | registrar $5-15k/yr plus FTEs (recalled) | continuous | No |

Bottom line (opinion): on a dollar basis the quality system is not the expensive part of an approval. The design/substantiation package (~$100k, 300-1,000 h) costs more. The real cost here is **calendar time and the regulator's queue on the first approval**, and after that the recurring FAI and surveillance labor for every part, at every supplier, after every change.

## 3. Can it be shared across a supplier network?

Evidence of shared forms that exist today:
1. **Holder-as-integrator under 21.137(c):** one PMA holder's quality system covers many suppliers through supplier control. This is legal now. The holder carries the liability and has to surveil its suppliers. The FAA also surveils suppliers of production approval holders (recalled: Order 8120.23 risk-based resource targeting covers supplier surveillance).
2. **Marketplace as supplier of record:** Xometry and Fictiv are AS9100D-certified as companies and route work to partner shops, so the platform's quality system and inspection stand in front of the network (recalled; not re-fetched).
3. **Shop roll-ups and networks:** Re:Build Manufacturing (acquired shops under shared systems), Hadrian (VC-backed automated aerospace factories; raised about $260M by 2025, recalled), and Divergent. These are owned networks, not shared certification.
4. **Shared FAI data:** Net-Inspect runs a browser platform where suppliers get a free limited licence to the FAI module for a customer's data, and sub-assembly and detail FAIRs link into assembly FAIRs. Source: https://www.net-inspect.com/ (Boeing and other primes use it, recalled).
5. **AS9100 multi-site certification** (IAQG 9104/1) only covers sites under one organization with central control, not independent companies (recalled).

What is not shared today (opinion, supported by the rule structure):
- Production approval cannot be pooled across independent legal entities. Each PMA names a holder and facilities, so a cooperative of shops cannot co-hold one PMA. The workable structure is **one holder (the Approval Factory) plus a controlled supplier network**.
- FAI is re-done per customer and per supplier, even when an identical part has already been characterised elsewhere. There is no portable, verifiable "conformity passport" that a second customer, DLA, or the FAA accepts in place of re-inspection.
- Supplier surveillance means auditors sampling paperwork on site. It is not continuous and not data-driven.

## 4. Who is doing pieces (verification-side tooling)
- **GroundControl (YC; Mehul Shah, Nick Warren, Matthew Noseworthy; ex-InspectAR, acquired by Cadence):** AI FAI ballooning and AS9102 report generation, 70+ US facilities, about 1,000 reports/month (company claims).
- **Ideagen InspectionXpert, DISCUS Software, High QA, 1factory, Kissflow FAI app:** FAI ballooning and report tools (some recalled).
- **Net-Inspect:** supplier quality network with shared FAI.
- **Leo AI:** design/DFM AI; argues FAI should be assisted, not autonomous: https://www.getleo.ai/blog/ai-first-article-inspection
- **aiOla:** voice/AI for inspection workflows.
- **First Resonance (ION), Paperless Parts, Instrumental, Elementary, Cognex, plus Hexagon/Zeiss metrology software:** digital thread, quoting, visual inspection, CMM (recalled).
- **QMS software:** ETQ, MasterControl, Intelex, Qualio (recalled).
- **None of these is a PMA holder.** None sells to the FAA/MIDO or the DLA ESA as the party that accepts the evidence. All of them sell productivity to the shop.

## 5. What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence/compute)
1. **A conformity passport per serialized part.** Raw metrology (CT/CMM/scan point clouds), material cert chain (mill cert verified against the heat lot), and process parameters (heat treat, plating, NDT logs), machine-checked against the MBD/TDP and cryptographically tied to the part's 8130-3 or DD250. This replaces Form 3 as a human-typed table.
2. **An automated 21.137 / AS9100 compliance engine** that reads the live QMS (ERP, NCRs, calibration, training) and continuously proves each clause. MIDO and registrar audits become reviews of exceptions, not sampling.
3. **A PMA holder that is fabless by design**, carrying the 21.137 quality system for N contract shops. Each shop is onboarded with instrumented inspection that streams evidence into item 1, so supplier control becomes measurement rather than paperwork.
4. **Change-impact analysis** that works out from the data which changes (tooling, location, sub-tier) actually require a full or delta FAI per AS9102 rules, and auto-generates the delta.
5. **Regulator acceptance work:** propose evidence formats and a pilot to FAA AIR-6xx/MIDO (risk-based resource targeting already exists, so data-driven targeting is a natural fit) and to DLA/ESA. Ask them to accept the conformity passport plus delivery history as grounds for FAT waiver under DLAD 9.3 (waiver language already exists) and for a reduced surveillance frequency.
6. **Portable past performance:** a shared, verifiable delivery/quality record across the network so a new shop under the holder inherits the 3- or 5-year FAT waiver eligibility. Whether a new shop can inherit eligibility depends on contracting officer judgement, not physics.

Binding constraints:
- **Physics:** the part must be made and measured once. CT/CMM time is minutes to hours, so it is not binding at scale.
- **Law:** one holder per PMA (fixed); 21.137 is technology-neutral (good).
- **Regulator acceptance:** the MIDO queue and inspector discretion. To change it: a pilot and a data format the FAA can audit.
- **Liability:** the holder owns every escape. Insurers would price a data-rich holder better (opinion).
- **Data access:** the TDP/MBD to measure against is the upstream bottleneck (other steps).

## 6. AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
- **"Quality-system-as-a-service" holder:** the Approval Factory holds PMA/production approval and DLA source approvals, while contract shops make parts and the AI verifies every article. Margin comes from owning the approval, not the machining.
- **Conformity evidence compiler (FAI/FAT/8130-3 from raw data)** sold to the regulator-facing side: become the evidence format MIDO/DCMA accept. GroundControl already automates the shop-side report; the gap is the measurement-to-acceptance link and regulator trust.
- **Continuous audit / MIDO-readiness agent:** cuts the 6-18 months to a first production approval and the recurring surveillance cost.
- **Network FAT-waiver engine for DLA:** turns delivery and conformity history into FAT waivers. FAT takes months and is priced into every bid, so waivers convert directly into win rate.
