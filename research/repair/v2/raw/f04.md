# f04: Process fingerprinting and in-situ monitoring as qualification evidence ("qualify the process, not the part"; ASTM F42, America Makes, FAA AM roadmap). What it would take to make every part born-certified.

Method: 3 WebSearch calls plus about 15 WebFetch attempts. Many regulator and standards pages returned 403 or 503 (faa.gov, sae.org, amchronicle, the amcoe PDF). Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this session. Items marked (opinion) are my analysis.

## 1. Current state: evidence

- **The joint EASA-FAA AM Workshop** has been held yearly since 2015. The 2025 edition ran 21-23 Oct 2025 in Cologne. **WG3 covers In-Situ Process Monitoring (ISPM)** for quality assurance of metal AM. It is advancing **SAE ARP7068**, a guidance document on using ISPM that builds on **ARP7065**. It is also working on use cases, *barriers to data sharing* and *reliability requirements*. The roadmap's goal is for ISPM to "replace or supplement conventional inspections for critical AM parts." The page states no agreed certification-credit approach. WG1 (non-critical and low-criticality parts) is paused until the revised **EASA CM-S-008** is published. https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/node/142087
  - Takeaway: as of late 2025, the regulators have no agreed method for giving in-situ data formal credit. A guidance ARP is still being written. The rules are still open, so a newcomer could help write them.
- **ASTM AM CoE "Strategic Guide: AM In-Situ Technology Readiness"** (workshop June 2022, published July 2023). It was NASA-sponsored, with America Makes. It frames itself as a gap roadmap, not a statement that the technology is ready. NASA's Doug Wells said it informs NASA's methods for putting in-situ monitoring into qualification and certification. https://www.qualitymag.com/articles/97431-astm-international-releases-technology-readiness-report-on-am-in-situ-monitoring
- **NASA slides (NSTGRO, May 2023):** current Q&C of metallic materials, AM included, is "entirely based on test data." https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20230007109/downloads/slides_NSTGRO_202305_SH1.pdf (from the search snippet; the file was too large to fetch).
- **NASA-STD-6030**, baseline 21 Apr 2021, was due for its 5-year review on 21 Apr 2026. https://standards.nasa.gov/standard/NASA/NASA-STD-6030 . (recalled) It and MSFC-STD-3716 define the Qualified Metallurgical Process (QMP), Qualified Part Process (QPP), Equipment and Process Control Plans, and part classes A/B/C. Each qualified part process is effectively frozen. In-situ monitoring is allowed as a process-control aid but gives no acceptance credit in place of NDE and witness coupons.
- **ICAM 2026** (Orlando, 28 Sep-2 Oct 2026) has a monitoring track. It says in-situ monitoring "could reduce reliance on traditional NDE and accelerate qualification." This is still framed as a future possibility. (search snippet; the PDF returned 403)
- **America Makes** has issued several in-situ monitoring project calls, for example a 2020-21 rapid innovation call of about $185k on in-situ surface features. The amounts are small.

**Answer to "is anyone doing it?"** Many companies monitor. Nobody sells **certification credit**: the regulator-accepted claim that "this signature means this part meets the allowable, so skip CT and witness coupons." The one public pure-play (Sigma) failed because monitoring without credit is a cost, not a saving. (opinion)

## 3. Pathway today, and where each step is undone or broken

1. **Material and feedstock spec** (powder or wire lot spec: AMS7002/7015, recalled). Mostly done. Lot-to-lot variability is weakly linked to in-situ signals.
2. **Machine qualification (IQ/OQ/PQ)**: ISO/ASTM 52930, AMS7032, ASTM F3303 (recalled). This is per machine serial number. **Broken:** no cross-machine calibration artifact for sensor signals, so a fingerprint learned on machine A does not transfer to machine B.
3. **Frozen process (QMP)**: parameters locked. Any change means requalification costing months. **Broken:** the frozen-process idea conflicts with closed-loop AI control. A process that adapts layer by layer is by definition not frozen, and regulators have no framework for qualifying the *controller* instead of the setpoints.
4. **Allowables generation**: (recalled) MMPDS-style B-basis needs on the order of 100+ coupons over multiple lots and heats, and A-basis about 300. Fatigue at 10^7 cycles takes days per coupon on servo-hydraulic frames. **Physics clock.**
5. **Part qualification (QPP)**: first article, CT, sectioning, part-level tests. About $100k+ and months per part.
6. **Production acceptance per part**: witness coupons plus CT and NDE of every critical part. NDT and validation take 25-50% of part cost (vendor claim). **This is where in-situ data could replace cost, and today it earns zero credit.**
7. **Regulator acceptance of monitoring**: ARP7068 is still in draft. EASA-FAA WG3 is still listing "reliability requirements." **Missing entirely:** probability-of-detection (POD) curves for in-situ signals equivalent to MIL-HDBK-1823A for NDE. Without a POD (e.g., a90/95 flaw size), no regulator can give credit.
8. **Data-sharing and liability layer**: WG3 names "barriers to data sharing" explicitly. OEMs keep data proprietary, and no neutral clearinghouse or "Nadcap for monitoring" exists.
9. **Change management**: any change to machine, software or sensor firmware forces requalification. There is no tool-qualification standard for ML used in manufacturing acceptance, comparable to DO-330 for software tools.

## 4. What would need to be done (abundant compute and intelligence assumed)

1. **Build the POD corpus.** Run millions of layers with seeded defects (keyholes, lack-of-fusion, contamination, recoater streaks), pair every voxel of in-situ signal with CT ground truth, add destructive sectioning on a subsample, and fatigue-test a stratified sample. Then publish 1823A-style a90/95 POD curves for each sensor modality, alloy and machine family. This is the one dataset regulators say is missing. It costs capital and machine-hours, not genius. The physics clock is fatigue. Lever: **20 kHz ultrasonic fatigue** reaches 10^9 cycles in about 14 hours versus weeks, and robotic test cells running hundreds of frames in parallel.
2. **Write the statistical framework for "signature-basis" allowables**: a property allowable conditioned on the in-situ signature envelope instead of on a frozen recipe. This would be the AM equivalent of MMPDS B-basis, through ASTM F42, SAE AMS-AM and MMPDS. In practice, AI writes the ARP draft and the founder staffs the committees. Standards are written by whoever turns up with data.
3. **Qualify the controller, not the setpoints.** Propose a DO-330/DO-178-like assurance case for closed-loop AM controllers: bounded action envelope, runtime monitor, formal guarantees that the output stays inside the qualified signature envelope. Today nobody owns this.
4. **Cross-machine calibration artifacts and a neutral data schema**, so a fingerprint transfers between serial numbers. Today it is OEM-locked.
5. **A third-party "monitoring accreditation" body**, the Nadcap/PRI equivalent for in-situ systems, plus a DAR/DER-style delegation so a designee can sign signature-based acceptance.
6. **Extend the approach beyond AM.** Conventional processes already have process-fingerprint regimes that regulators accept: AMS2750 pyrometry for heat treat, Nadcap special processes, weld schedule monitoring, autoclave cure records. A "born-certified" machine shop for reverse-engineered spares (CNC plus heat treat plus NDT with full sensor capture) is closer to acceptance than AM. For example, spindle load and vibration fingerprints combined with on-machine probing could replace a separate CMM first-article inspection.
7. **Change the law or policy where needed.** DLA SAR and FAA PMA rules already allow "test and computation" equivalence. What is missing is guidance that names in-situ evidence as acceptable data. Likely routes: an FAA policy statement or AC, DLA engineering support activity (ESA) acceptance criteria per service (NAVAIR, AFLCMC, DEVCOM), and NAVAIR's AM Technical Data Package practice (recalled: NAVAIR has fielded hundreds of AM parts). Military ESAs can move faster than the FAA, so start there.

## 5. Verification-side AI plays (opinion)

- **A. "Evidence compiler" for the Approval Factory.** Every part the factory makes comes off an instrumented cell (AM or CNC) whose sensor log, CT and coupon data are compiled automatically into the SAR/PMA or first-article package. The KPI is engineering and reviewer hours per approval, and this play shrinks the reviewer side of it. The moat is the accepted evidence template with each ESA.
- **B. "UL for in-situ" (POD-as-a-service).** A neutral lab that runs the seeded-defect plus CT plus ultrasonic-fatigue corpus at scale, publishes POD and signature-basis allowables, and licenses certification credit to machine OEMs and part makers. Revenue comes from per-machine accreditation and per-part credit fees. This captures the "permission" layer the earlier review identified as where the money is. It is the most venture-scale and the least crowded: nobody does it today.
- **C. Qualified-controller house.** An AI closed-loop controller plus its assurance case, licensed into Nikon, EOS and Velo3D machines. Freeform is closest but keeps it captive.
- **D. Born-certified spares cell.** Combine A and B inside the Approval Factory: a reverse-engineered sole-source part is printed or machined on a fingerprint-accredited cell, so per-part acceptance cost falls from CT plus coupons (25-50% of part cost) to a signature check.

Recommended combination (opinion): the Approval Factory with an internal play B. Build the POD and signature corpus on its own parts, use the military ESAs as the first regulator to accept signature-based acceptance, then license credit externally. The data generated by making approved parts becomes the asset that makes the next approval cheaper.

## 6. Binding constraints and what changes them
- **Physics clock:** fatigue and allowables testing. Change it with ultrasonic fatigue, massive parallel test frames, and physics-informed models that cut coupon counts (regulators must accept reduced-sample statistics).
- **Law and regulator:** no POD-based credit framework yet (ARP7068 in draft). Change it by bringing the data and writing the draft. ESAs first, FAA second.
- **Liability:** whoever signs the acceptance owns the escape. Change it by accrediting the monitoring system and carrying insurance or warranty backed by the POD statistics.
- **Data access:** OEM-locked sensor formats. Change it with your own instrumented cells and retrofit sensors (Additive Assurance's "8+ brands" shows retrofit is feasible).
