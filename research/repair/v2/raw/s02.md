# s02: Step: data rights and IP clearance (DFARS 252.227, government purpose rights, trade secrets, design patents). How it is checked today, time, cost, failure modes.

Evidence is tagged [fetched] (from a primary or secondary source fetched this run) or (recalled) (from memory, so verify it). Opinion is tagged [opinion].

## 1. The legal layers a reverse-engineered or second-source part has to clear

| Layer | What it governs | Who checks it today | How it fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| DFARS 252.227-7013 / -7014 / -7015 (gov't data rights) | Whether the government can hand an OEM drawing or TDP to a second source | DLA engineering support activity (ESA) or service engineering, the contracting officer, and the program office's IP cadre | Restrictive legends asserted without basis; the government can't find its own TDP; legacy legends nobody challenges |
| 10 USC 3771-3775 (formerly 2320/2321) | Statute behind the clauses: who funded development decides the rights | Same | "Developed at private expense" decided "at the lowest practicable level" (part, subassembly) and almost never audited |
| DFARS 252.227-7037 (validating restrictive markings) | How the government challenges a legend | Contracting officer | Rarely used; a one-at-a-time, 60-day-plus process with no tooling |
| Trade secret (DTSA 18 USC 1836; state UTSA) | Whether a clean-room reverse engineering (RE) was "proper means" | Courts, after the fact | Ex-OEM staff, tainted data, an NDA or contract barring RE |
| Utility and design patents (35 USC 171) | Whether the part's function or shape is still protected | DLA Aviation and Land & Maritime check the part for patent markings; mostly nobody else | Unmarked patents, design patents on visible shapes, continuations |
| Copyright on drawings and software | Copying drawings or firmware | Rarely checked | Firmware or software pairing |
| Export control (ITAR/EAR) and DD 2345 JCP certification | Who may receive the data at all | DLA Joint Certification Program | Uncertified vendor; foreign-person access |
| FAA PMA (14 CFR 21.303, Order 8110.42) | The FAA approves design data, not IP ownership | FAA ACO/MIDO | The FAA expects test & computation data to be the applicant's own and does not referee IP (recalled) |

### Key mechanics [fetched: acquisition.gov DFARS 252.227-7013]
- **Unlimited rights:** use and disclose for any purpose. **Government purpose rights (GPR):** use within government, and release outside only for government purposes, which includes competitive reprocurement of spares. **Limited rights:** no release outside government for manufacture without the owner's written permission, with exceptions for emergency repair/overhaul and covered government support contractors.
- **GPR on mixed-funding data lasts 5 years** (or a negotiated period) from contract execution, **then converts to unlimited rights**. [opinion] On legacy fleets like the C-130 (1950s), KC-135 (1950s) and B-52, a large fraction of data marked GPR from mixed funding should by now be unlimited. Nobody systematically sweeps for these sunsets.
- **Unmarked data delivered to the government is presumed delivered with unlimited rights.** A contractor gets 6 months to fix inadvertent omissions. Nonconforming markings get 60 days' notice, after which the government may correct them at the contractor's expense.
- "Developed exclusively at private expense" means indirect cost pools or non-government-contract costs, determined "at the lowest practicable level."

### Statutory exceptions [fetched: 10 USC 3771, Cornell LII]
Even for data developed at private expense, the government keeps the right to release:
- form, fit and function (FFF) data;
- data necessary for operation, maintenance, installation or training (OMIT), excluding detailed manufacturing/process data;
- data that is public or was already released without restriction;
- data that corrects government-furnished data.

[opinion] FFF and OMIT data is often all a reverse engineering shop needs as the specification envelope: interfaces, envelope, loads, performance. That shrinks the "need the OEM drawing" problem to the internal details, which RE recovers.

### Challenge process [fetched: DFARS 252.227-7037]
- The contracting officer may challenge a restrictive marking **within 6 years of final payment or delivery, whichever is later**, or **at any time** if the data is publicly available, was furnished without restriction, or the restriction was fraudulently asserted.
- Sequence: written inquiry → challenge notice → contractor gets 60 days to justify with evidence (extendable) → contracting officer's final decision, generally within 60 days of the response → appeal to the Board of Contract Appeals or the Court of Federal Claims.
- [opinion] The minimum is about 4-6 months per challenge, realistically 1-2 years if appealed. It needs a contracting officer with the time and an engineer to show the funding history, which is why it is rarely used.
- Relevant cases (recalled): *Boeing v. Secretary of the Air Force* (Fed. Cir. 2020) held that a contractor cannot put non-standard restrictive legends on data delivered with unlimited rights or GPR. There are also Sikorsky/UH-60 data-rights disputes (Court of Federal Claims, circa 2018-2021).

## 2. How DLA checks it today [fetched: GAO-19-586, July 2019]
- FY2015-FY2018: DLA started **more than 1,600 reverse engineering projects**. DLA funded about 1,000 and contractors about 600.
- **124 contractors** did RE work; **103 (about 83%) were small businesses**.
- IP safeguards are the whole checklist:
  1. DLA does not release drawings carrying limited-rights legends.
  2. Aviation and Land & Maritime check the physical part for patent markings.
  3. Troop Support does not check patents, on the basis that its parts are "too old."
- No IP complaints were reported. GAO's sample was 19 projects covering 13 parts and is not generalizable.
- [opinion] This is a manual, legend-driven check. Nobody reconstructs the funding history, sweeps for GPR sunsets, searches design patents (unmarked design patents are invisible to a marking check), or checks continuations.

### Cost and time today
- (recalled) A source approval request (SAR) or RE package overall costs roughly $50k-$150k and takes 6-24 months, plus DLA ESA review queues of months to years.
- The IP portion inside that is small in dollars, maybe a few days of a contracts/IP attorney's time ($5-20k), but it is high variance:
  - a challenge to a marking adds 6-24 months;
  - a C&D letter or litigation risk (trade secret suits typically cost $2-10M through trial (recalled, AIPLA survey ranges)) kills the program outright.
- A freedom-to-operate (FTO) opinion from outside counsel typically costs $10-50k per product family (recalled).

## 3. Commercial / FAA side
- PMA via test & computation requires the applicant to produce its own design data. The FAA does not adjudicate IP. Order 8110.42 (recalled) says the applicant is responsible for IP issues, and identicality-by-license requires a licensing agreement with the TC/PMA holder. The FAA guidance page returned 403 this run, so this is unverified.
- Litigation history (all recalled unless marked):
  - Pratt & Whitney v. HEICO in the late 1990s, over PMA combustor/turbine parts. It settled, and P&W's resistance is often credited with Lufthansa Technik buying 20% of HEICO's flight support group in 1997.
  - Rolls-Royce/GE and other OEMs fight PMA mainly through warranty, contracts and lease-return conditions, not lawsuits.
  - *United States v. Lange* (7th Cir. 2002) [fetched summary]: an ex-employee of the PMA maker RAPCO sold its RE drawings, and was convicted under the Economic Espionage Act (EEA). The court held that RE-derived data can still be a trade secret of the reverse engineer, because doing the RE costs effort. [opinion] This cuts both ways: your RE output is protectable, but RE is lawful only if done cleanly.
  - *Alcoa v. Universal Alloy* (N.D. Ga. 2023) [fetched]: defense verdict; the jury found Alcoa owned no trade secrets in well-known aerospace extrusion technology.
  - *Aerosonic v. Joby* (M.D. Fla., reported 2026) [fetched headline]: $116.9M verdict for breach of NDA and trade secret misappropriation over air data probes. It shows the scale of risk when RE is entangled with a prior NDA or supplier relationship.
- Design patents:
  - *LKQ v. GM* (Fed. Cir. en banc, May 21, 2024) [fetched] dropped the rigid Rosen-Durling test, so design patents are now easier to invalidate as obvious.
  - [opinion] This matters for aftermarket replacement parts, where OEMs (auto first, increasingly aero interiors and fairings) used design patents on visible shapes to block copies. Design patents last 15 years from grant, so on legacy platforms they have mostly expired. The issue is newer platforms and fresh design patents on redesigned replacement parts.
- Software/firmware: DFARS 252.227-7014 covers software; DMCA 1201 covers circumvention of pairing keys. The Copyright Office exemptions for vehicle and medical device repair were renewed in 2024 (recalled). Aviation avionics is not explicitly covered.

## 4. Failure modes (ranked by how often they kill a program) [opinion]
1. **The government can't find or prove its own rights.** The TDP is lost, scanned without legends, or held only by the OEM, or the contract with the data delivery list (CDRL) can't be found. Contracting officers then default to treating the data as restricted.
2. **Over-assertion goes unchallenged.** OEMs mark broadly and the 7037 challenge needs funding history no one has.
3. **Clean-room taint.** Hiring ex-OEM engineers, or using OEM CMM/maintenance manuals beyond OMIT scope. This is a trade secret and contract risk.
4. **Patents nobody searched.** Especially design patents and continuations, since marking checks miss unmarked patents.
5. **Export control.** Foreign-national engineers or cloud tooling touching ITAR data. For an AI shop, this means LLM/cloud processing of ITAR TDPs needs a FedRAMP/ITAR-compliant environment.
6. **FAA side.** OEM warranty and lease-return terms do more than IP law to keep PMA out (commercial lock, not legal).

## 5. Who does pieces of this today
- **Law firms:** Wiley, Crowell & Moring, Covington, PilieroMazza and others for gov-con data rights. Hourly work, one dispute at a time.
- **DoD IP Cadre** (OUSD(A&S), created under the FY2018 NDAA §802; DoDI 5010.44, October 2019) (recalled). Advises program offices; a handful of staff.
- **Patent analytics tools** (PatSnap, Clarivate Derwent, LexisNexis PatentSight, IP.com) do FTO searching but not data-rights or funding-provenance work.
- **Gov-con data platforms** (GovTribe, HigherGov, Deltek GovWin) have contract and award data but no data-rights mapping.
- **DLA cFolders / JEDMICS / the SAR process** hold drawings but do not reason about rights. (recalled)
- **Defense IP startups** (recalled, verify): small LLM-for-contracts tools (e.g., Hazel, Rogo-like gov-con tools) do DFARS clause extraction, but nobody I know of does "rights provenance per part number."
- [opinion] No company productizes **rights clearance per NSN** at scale.

## 6. What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)
1. **A rights-provenance graph per NSN/part number.** Ingest:
   - FPDS/USAspending contract history, DD 1423 CDRLs and contract clauses;
   - JEDMICS/cFolders legend metadata (needs DLA partnership/CRADA);
   - OEM IR&D disclosures;
   - and the funding history.

   Infer the rights category for each component and flag GPR markings whose 5-year sunset has passed (now unlimited), nonconforming legends, and data delivered unmarked (presumed unlimited). Output a pre-packaged evidence file the contracting officer can use to issue a 7037 challenge, ideally batched across hundreds of parts.
2. **Automated FTO for physical parts.** Shape-match 3D scans against design patent drawings and utility claims, including continuations and pending applications, worldwide. Compute expiry dates, and add post-*LKQ* obviousness analysis plus prior-art packs.
3. **A provable clean room.** A hashed, timestamped log of every input the RE team or AI touched, showing no OEM proprietary data beyond FFF/OMIT, plus personnel-taint screening. This turns "trust us" into an auditable artifact for court or regulator, and dramatically lowers the trade-secret risk premium.
4. **An ITAR-compliant AI enclave** (GovCloud / IL5) so TDP ingestion is legal.
5. **Legal and policy changes.**
   - Get DoD to accept machine-generated 7037 challenge packages.
   - Push "deemed unlimited after GPR sunset" automation into DLA systems.
   - Lobby for statutory right-to-repair for DoD (Warren/Sheehy-type provisions in FY2025-26 NDAA debates (recalled; outcome uncertain, verify)).
   - Push the "data as a service"/IP cadre funding.
6. **Insurance.** IP-infringement insurance for RE parts, underwritten on the clean-room log and FTO output (a new product). This converts the liability constraint into a priced premium.

## 7. AI plays on this step [opinion]
- **A. Rights clearance as a service to DLA and the services ("RightsGraph").** Sell per-NSN rights determinations and challenge packages to DLA, the Air Force Life Cycle Management Center (AFLCMC), NAVSUP and the Army. It unlocks the competition that the 1,600-project RE program depends on. SBIR/STRATFI is a plausible entry, and it is an internal module of the Approval Factory first.
- **B. A clean-room provenance ledger** as the standard evidence format for RE parts, sold to the roughly 100+ small RE contractors and PMA houses (HEICO, Wencor, Jet Parts Engineering, Kapco and others), plus insurers.
- **C. Automated FTO for 3D parts:** a scan, a patent/design-patent match and an expiry check, in minutes not weeks.
- **D. A GPR-sunset sweep.** One-time "found money" analysis showing a program office which legacy data it already owns unlimited rights to.

[opinion] The bottleneck is **data access** (JEDMICS/cFolders and contract files are not public), not intelligence. So a play needs a DLA/AFLCMC partnership, a CRADA, or the user's own SAR flow as the wedge.
