# c18: Government organic capacity using AI for reverse engineering and qualification

Question: Air Force Rapid Sustainment Office, Tinker/Hill/Robins reverse-engineering cells, NAVAIR, Army DEVCOM, DLA R&D, Navy Maritime Industrial Base program. Budgets and tools.

Method: 3 WebSearch calls (quota), then WebFetch of linked pages. Several .mil pages return HTTP 403 to the fetcher (tinker.af.mil, dla.mil, metal-am.com, asdnews). Anything marked (recalled) is from model memory, not verified this session.

## Bottom line

- Government organic reverse-engineering (RE) capacity is real but small, artisanal, and hardware-centric: 3D scanning, CAD, additive manufacturing, circuit-card redesign. In every public source fetched, **none mentions AI** in the RE or qualification workflow. Search summaries said so explicitly for RSO/Tinker, DLA TDP work, and the Navy MIB.
- Government buys tools (scanners, printers), runs competitions (RSO Advanced Manufacturing Olympics, prize pot up to $100k), and outsources TDP creation through SBIR ($100k Phase I / $1M Phase II) and small IBAS grants ($1.8M over two years). It does not run an "approval throughput" machine. The bottleneck it never instruments is the Engineering Support Activity (ESA) / engineering authority review of the resulting package.
- Implication: the organic cells are **customers and validators, not competitors**, for an AI-native Approval Factory. The gap is software that turns scan + metrology + test data into a reviewer-ready TDP/SAR, and that lets the ESA reviewer approve faster.

## Evidence by organization

### Air Force Rapid Sustainment Office (RSO), Wright-Patterson
- Founded 2018 to address obsolete parts (airandspaceforces.com).
- Jan 2021: RSO put out a call for a "cutting-edge, automated 3D scanning system" to model and reproduce out-of-production parts. https://www.airandspaceforces.com/?p=95540 ; https://www.twz.com/39537/the-air-force-needs-to-reverse-engineer-parts-of-its-own-stealth-bomber.md
- Advanced Manufacturing Olympics (from summer 2020): 64 teams, up to $100,000 in prizes. Events included RE of a box of parts, making and certifying flight-ready F-16 parts, and printing from design data. https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-force-searching-for-savvier-sustainment/ (Oct 20, 2020)
- Will Roper (2020): sustainment is about 70% of USAF budget. The Air Force had printed nearly 10,000 parts. On the C-5M, over 6,000 phenolic wedges (118 per aircraft) were printed: 88% faster, 35% lighter, about $1M cheaper.
- RSO budget (recalled): tens of millions to low hundreds of millions per year, much of it CBM+ / predictive maintenance (e.g., the C3 AI PANDA predictive-maintenance platform) rather than RE. RSO's AI spending sits on the **predictive** side (when will a part fail), not on qualification (is this new part approvable).

### Oklahoma City ALC (Tinker): REACT lab (Reverse Engineering and Critical Tooling), 76th Commodities Maintenance Group
- Uses 3D scanning and printing to recreate legacy parts, low-volume tooling, and dimensional verification (Mar 2025 Air Force photo caption, via search). The "REACT: Engine for Innovation" article (tinker.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/4362517) returned 403.
- B-1 effort grew from 8 part numbers to 76 reverse-engineered interior panels (2020). Outreach to B-2 and B-21.
- Mar 31, 2026: Department of the Air Force SAM notice "For Reverse Engineering or Repair Development" (SASPO), white-paper RFI, place of performance Tinker. https://app.govly.com/public/opportunities/16305899 . So OC-ALC is also **outsourcing** RE/repair development to industry.

### Warner Robins ALC (Robins): 402nd EMXG REARM team; Ogden ALC (Hill)
- REARM teams (Reverse Engineering and Rapid Manufacturing) sit at all three AFSC ALCs: Robins, Tinker, Hill (TWZ 2021).
- WR-ALC redesigned a circuit card for a munitions loader fielded in the early 1980s (about 75% reliability gain), and a weapons control unit whose cards were out of stock (2020).
- Robins' Damon Brown: 3D CAD has to be flattened to 2D drawings for storage in legacy systems (JEDMICS-era), then emailed around. That is a **data-infrastructure gap**, and a direct opening for AI-generated model-based TDPs.
- B-2 example: Load Heat Exchanger (NSN 1660-01-350-8209FW) re-core RE contracted out (2021). The government supplies 2 units and gets 2 prototypes plus all technical data back.

### DLA (Aviation, Land & Maritime, R&D/SBIR)
- RPPOB / Bailment program: firms can inspect, borrow or buy government parts to RE them. Almost 200 items are in it. DoD owns data for about half; the other half "must be reverse engineered at vendors' expense" (dla.mil article 1771709, via search summary; 403 on fetch).
- SBIR DLA231-001 "Engaging the Manufacturing Industrial Base" (posted Jan 11, 2023): Phase I up to $100k / 12 months, with TDP + SAR due in the first 6 months. Phase II up to $1M / 24 months. Four paths: (a) 1G NSN with full TDP, (b) non-1G with TDP leading to a SAR, (c) RPPOB/surplus RE, (d) RE with GFM. Needs JCP certification and a CAGE code. ITAR. Keywords: NESO (nuclear), source approval, reverse engineering. https://www.highergov.com/contract-opportunity/engaging-the-manufacturing-industrial-base-in-supp-dla231-001-sbir-68991/
  - Read: DLA's main RE "program" is about $100k per NSN subsidy to small businesses. That is about the same as the prior estimate of roughly $100k per package. This is the **unit price an AI-native house must beat**, and the SBIR is a non-dilutive on-ramp.
- Value Management / Land & Maritime: parts examined to build complete TDPs. A separate solicitation sought contractors to make TDPs from physical items (CAD, drawings, MIL-STD config management). https://www.dla.mil/Land-and-Maritime/Offers/Technical-Support/Value-Management
- Data-rights wall, live example: Sep 27, 2026 sole-source award to Raytheon, up to $46.7M (SPRHA426C0032), DLA Weapons Support Hill, for F-16 Mission Modular Computer LRUs. DLA asked to buy data rights in Apr 2025 and Raytheon declined. A Dec 2025 RFI drew **zero** industry responses. https://www.highergov.com/news/raytheon-wins-47-million-dla-contract-for-f-16-mi-6490038/
  - Read: even with a $46.7M prize, nobody offered to RE the electronics. Avionics LRU RE plus qualification is an unserved slot.
- OSD IBAS: about $1.8M over two years to the Great Plains Innovation Network / K-State (awarded Aug 2025, announced Feb 2026). It makes TDPs (models, BOMs, quality docs) for at least 3 obsolete assemblies picked from the DLA catalog, where "technical data no longer exists." No AI. https://thedebrief.org/pentagon-unveils-new-reverse-engineering-effort-to-leverage-legacy-technologies-for-which-data-no-longer-exists/
- DLA AM tooling (recalled): JAMMEX (Joint Additive Manufacturing Model Exchange) is a repository of AM-ready models. DLA R&D funds AM and casting/forging source development (NDIA 2016 deck on SARs for castings and forgings: ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2016/DLA/SourceApprovalRequestsCastingandForging.pdf).

### Navy: Maritime Industrial Base (MIB) program, BlueForge Alliance, AM Center of Excellence, NAVAIR
- MIB funds GD Electric Boat to source parts from Lincoln Electric's large-scale metal AM facility (search summary).
- BlueForge Alliance (nonprofit SIB integrator) placed a US$1.84M order with AML3D for 5 non-safety-critical obsolete submarine components (Mar 31, 2026). These are WAM in NAB alloy, already qualified to Navy specs. Navy **hydrostatic testing** gated the production order. https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/aml3d-lands-us-navy-contract-for-obsolete-submarine-components-250387/
- ADDMAN/HARBEC is on a Navy project with Phillips, Austal USA and BlueForge to qualify AM CuNi submarine fittings (metal-am.com, 403 on fetch; via search).
- 2022 Navy position (Sermon): qualify **materials and processes**, not each part. https://portal.ct.gov/oma/in-the-news/2022-news/navy-looks-to-3d-printing-for-submarine-parts-to-ease-burden-on-strained-industrial-base
- Navy AM Center of Excellence (Danville, VA): more than 300 printed components delivered. RESTORE lab (NIWC Pacific, ex-SPAWAR) reverse-engineers legacy parts. Ohio-class parts were RE'd, and USS Michigan trim/drain valves replaced by AM (3dprintingindustry).
- MIB/SIB budget (recalled): several $B/yr in recent budgets, roughly $2-4B/yr in SIB investment, with multi-$B across the FYDP. Most goes to workforce, supplier capacity and AM capacity, not to RE software.
- NAVAIR (recalled): Fleet Readiness Centers (East/Southeast/Southwest) run RE and AM. NAVAIR AIR-4.0 airworthiness approves AM parts by part criticality class. No public AI qualification tooling found.
- UK parallel: QinetiQ plans to RE more complex Royal Navy submarine parts, made by an accredited SME network (June 2026, asdnews; 403). This is the closest government-side analogue to the "Approval Factory" pattern.

### Army DEVCOM / AMC
- Not verified this session (search quota). Recalled: Rock Island Arsenal JMTC is the AM Center of Excellence. DEVCOM AvMC holds airworthiness authority (AED) for Army aviation parts. DEVCOM GVSC/Ground Vehicle and AMCOM run RE for obsolete parts. The Army Organic Industrial Base 15-year modernization plan is about $16B+ (recalled), mostly facilities. No public AI-for-qualification program found.

## Pattern across all of them
1. Organic cells exist at every depot (REACT, REARM, FRCs, RESTORE, Rock Island). They scan, model, print and test in volumes of tens to hundreds of part numbers, not thousands.
2. Money flows to: equipment, prize challenges, SBIR at $100k per NSN, small IBAS grants, and AM capacity (MIB). It does not flow to approval-throughput software.
3. The approval step (ESA/engineering authority signs off on the SAR/TDP, or Navy tech authority accepts hydro/material data) is never measured or automated in public material.
4. Data infrastructure is legacy (2D drawings, JEDMICS, cFolders). Model-based TDP (MBD) is not standard.
5. Electronics/avionics RE is the hardest unserved slot. The F-16 MMC RFI got zero responses.
6. AI at RSO is spent on predictive maintenance (CBM+), not on RE or qualification.

## What would need to be done (assume abundant compute/intelligence)
- AI "TDP compiler": scan + CT + metallography + XRF + teardown photos go in; a MIL-STD-31000 model-based TDP comes out, with tolerances inferred from fleet populations, a material/process spec, inspection plan, and a SAR narrative mapped to each ESA's SAR checklist (AMSC codes, Category I/II/III).
- Reviewer-side copilot for ESAs (e.g., AFSC engineering, DLA ESA engineers, NAVSEA tech warrant holders): it pre-checks SARs against the checklist and similarity-to-approved precedent, and flags missing evidence. Here the KPI is reviewer hours per approval, and the depot cells and ESAs are the buyers.
- Process-qualified "approval lanes": follow the Navy's materials-and-process qualification idea, then use statistical/physics-informed models to argue part-family equivalence, so one physical test campaign covers N part numbers. The physics clock is paid once per family, not per part.
- Electronics RE: automated board delaminations/X-ray to netlist, firmware extraction and re-hosting, emulated-obsolete-IC (FPGA) substitution, plus an auto-generated qualification test plan (MIL-STD-810/461). The F-16 MMC case shows demand with zero supply.
- Fleet-data-to-tolerance: mine depot teardown and inspection records to set tolerances and wear allowances that OEM drawings never provided.
- Rights/permission work: a legal-AI pipeline that classifies each NSN's data-rights status (AMSC, DFARS 252.227-7013/7015 markings, government-purpose rights) and builds the documented clean-room RE record that defeats trade-secret claims.

## AI plays on the verification side
1. Sell "SAR autopilot" to the depot RE cells and DLA ESAs as a SaaS/OTA: the organic teams become distribution, not competition.
2. Run as the Approval Factory using DLA SBIR DLA231-type topics ($100k/NSN Phase I) as paid pilots. Show 10x lower engineering hours per approved SAR.
3. Family-qualification engine (materials/process allowables plus a similarity argument) for MIB/BlueForge submarine parts, and AF AM parts.
4. Avionics LRU RE plus emulation house targeting sole-source electronics awards like the $46.7M F-16 MMC buy.
5. Model-based TDP converter for legacy 2D/JEDMICS archives. This is a precondition that lets every other step go faster.
