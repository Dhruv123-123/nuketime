# s05: Design substantiation by analysis (FEA, fatigue, damage tolerance) for replacement parts. What regulators accept, how long it takes, how AI/compute could replace engineering hours.

Method: 3 web searches (quota), then direct fetches. Several faa.gov / dla.mil pages returned 403 to the fetcher, so some regulatory detail is from memory and marked (recalled). Evidence and opinion are labelled.

## 1. What regulators accept (evidence)

- **FAA PMA legal basis.** 14 CFR 21.303(a)(4): the applicant submits "test reports and computations necessary to show that the design of the article meets" the airworthiness requirements, unless it shows the design is identical to a type-certificated design. (a)(5) needs a statement certifying compliance. Source: https://www.ecfr.gov (fetched 21.303). So computation is written into the law as an accepted means. The question is how much test the FAA engineer or DER wants alongside it.
- **The FAA's own fatigue and damage-tolerance chief says where analysis is accepted.** Glaessgen (NASA LaRC) and Gorelik (FAA Chief Scientist for Fatigue & Damage Tolerance), CM4QC talk, NIST workshop, Apr 28 to May 1 2026 (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260003240/downloads/NIST%20041826.pdf):
  - Part 25 damage tolerance (AC 25.571-1D): "In general, 'analysis supported by test evidence' is accepted."
  - Part 33 engine LLPs (AC 33.70-1): analysis of stress, heat transfer and crack growth is accepted, but "the analysis approach should be validated against relevant test data". Probabilistic DT is "one of two elements necessary".
  - AC 20-146, dynamic seat certification by analysis, is "one of the few examples of 'certification by analysis'" and is still validated by test.
  - The slide on test-article representativeness says the test article "should be full-scale (component or sub-component)". That is the physics clock.
  - The CM4QC roadmap (NASA/TM-20260001729, DOT/FAA/TC-26/10) puts computational materials as augmenting testing for 1 to 5 years, partnerships for 5 to 10 years, and "dramatically" expanded capability at more than 10 years. "The naive expectation is that CM predictions may directly replace testing data." The listed gaps are trust, public validation data, material property data, UQ integration, and resources to run model-based and conventional Q&C side by side.
  - Steering group members: NASA, FAA, NIST, AFRL, NAVAIR, Army, Sandia, ORNL, SwRI, Boeing, LM, RTX/P&W, GE, Honeywell, Northrop, BAE, Spirit, Textron, UTSA, CMU and others. **No startups are listed.**
- **Precedent: the FAA accepted a damage-tolerance analysis in place of repair.** Abdi et al. (Alpha STAR Corp + Imperial College), "An analysis approach toward FAA certification for damage tolerance of aircraft components", Aeronautical Journal 118(1200), Feb 2014. Fuselage skin and doubler attachment cracking, using the FAA "Certification by Analysis-Supported-by-Test (CAST)" policy. The FAA accepted the analysis and the aircraft kept its certification without repair. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/aeronautical-journal/article/an-analysis-approach-toward-faa-certification-for-damage-tolerance-of-aircraft-components/96F965A0B37C25FA2128BD96ED8A3C04
- **FAA tooling exists.** The FAA-funded SMART|DT probabilistic damage-tolerance code from UTSA/Millwater wraps crack-growth codes (https://rrpress.utsa.edu/items/0cb3e2f4-0b93-4a1e-8dcc-4482d547327e). DARWIN (SwRI, FAA-funded) covers engine-disk probabilistic DT (recalled).
- **Military side (recalled).** MIL-STD-1530D (ASIP) and JSSG-2006 govern structural integrity. For a DLA Source Approval Request on a Critical Safety Item, the Engineering Support Activity does the engineering review: WR-ALC at Robins for the C-130 and OC-ALC at Tinker for the KC-135. A reverse-engineered CSI generally needs proof of equivalence: dimensional, material and process, often first-article and qualification tests. DLA/ESA SAR reviews commonly take 6 to 24+ months, and reviewer queue time is often longer than the applicant's engineering time (recalled; confirm in s-series DLA notes). The analysis tools are AFGROW (AFRL, free to US) and NASGRO (SwRI/NASA).
- **Means-of-compliance hierarchy for replacement parts (recalled, standard DER practice):**
  1. Identicality, licensed or shown identical: no new analysis.
  2. Test & Computation PMA by **comparative (equivalence) analysis**. Show the replacement is equal to or better than the OEM part at every critical section, in static strength, fatigue and DT, without knowing OEM loads. The reasoning: the OEM part was certified, so loads that produce its stresses are acceptable, and a replacement with lower stresses or better allowables everywhere is acceptable. This is the dominant method for PMA structural parts and the one most open to automation.
  3. Full substantiation from derived loads, needed when geometry or material changes or when no OEM part exists to compare against. Loads have to be derived, which needs the OEM loads model or a back-calculation.
  4. Plus tests: coupon tests for material equivalence; component static, fatigue or spectrum test when the DER, FAA ACO or ESA asks for it (always for engine LLPs and many CSIs).

## 2. How long it takes (mostly recalled; industry norms)

| Part class | Analysis effort | Test clock | Reviewer clock |
|---|---|---|---|
| Non-critical bracket or fitting, comparative static only | 20-80 eng-hrs | coupon chem/hardness/tensile: days | DER: days-weeks |
| Fatigue-critical structural part, comparative fatigue | 100-400 hrs | S-N coupons: 10^7 cycles at 20 Hz ≈ 6 days each; a curve takes weeks | DER + FAA ACO: weeks-months |
| PSE / damage-tolerance part (crack growth, inspection intervals) | 300-1,000+ hrs | spectrum fatigue component test: 1-6 months | ACO/ESA: months |
| Engine LLP (Part 33.70) PMA | thousands of hrs, $1M+ per part (recalled) | spin-pit, LCF: months-year | FAA engine directorate: 1-2+ yrs |
| DLA SAR on a CSI | 300-1,000 hrs (the prior review's ~$100k/package) | FAT/qual: months | ESA queue: 6-24+ months |

Where the hours go (opinion, from DER practice):
- About 30-40% goes to getting inputs: geometry by reverse engineering, material and temper identification, and finding allowables in MMPDS or proprietary data.
- About 20-30% goes to modelling and analysis: meshing, hand calcs (Bruhn/Niu), FEA, crack-growth runs.
- About 30-40% goes to report writing, traceability, margin tables, and answering reviewer comments over several rounds.

Most of the hours are paperwork and gathering inputs, not compute.

## 4. Binding constraints and what would change them

1. **Physics clock: validation tests.** Coupon S-N and crack-growth tests and component fatigue cannot be simulated away under current AC language ("validated against relevant test data", "full-scale ... sub-component").
   - Shorten: resonance and ultrasonic fatigue rigs (100 Hz to 20 kHz; 10^7 cycles in minutes to hours, though validity for aerospace alloys is debated); massively parallel robotic coupon farms; Bayesian optimal test design, so 3-5 well-chosen tests replace 15-30.
   - Shrink with reuse: a shared "legacy alloy fatigue/DT corpus" so each new package needs only confirmation tests.
2. **Data access.**
   - OEM loads: comparative analysis avoids needing them.
   - Material allowables: MMPDS is Battelle/FAA and licensed; there is little fatigue or crack-growth data for legacy tempers.
   - Fleet usage spectra: USAF ASIP Individual Aircraft Tracking data is government-held.
   - Change it: build the allowables yourself by testing, at a capex-bound physics clock. For military platforms, sign CRADAs with the ALCs for spectra.
3. **Regulator acceptance.** Analysis supported by test is already accepted (Part 25 AC, CAST precedent). What does not exist is acceptance of ML-surrogate results as the compliance number.
   - Change it: keep certified deterministic solvers (hp-FEM with error bounds, NASGRO/AFGROW) as the thing of record. Use AI only to generate geometry, meshes, critical locations and reports, so nothing new needs accepting.
   - Longer term: an FAA issue paper or accepted Means of Compliance for "automated comparative substantiation", with credibility evidence per ASME V&V 10 and NASA-STD-7009A. CM4QC is the venue; join it.
4. **Liability and signature.** A DER or ESA engineer signs. The PMA holder carries product liability.
   - Change it: get an **ODA** (Organization Designation Authorization) so the company's own unit makes findings under FAA oversight. That brings the reviewer clock in-house. On the military side, become an ESA-delegated source, or sell the reviewer tool to the ESA.
5. **Law.** 21.303 already allows computations, so no law change is needed. On the DoD side, CSI rules (10 USC 2319 / DFARS 209.270, recalled) require ESA approval, and the lever there is reviewer throughput, not law.

## 5. What would need to be done that nobody does today (opinion, with the founder's premise of plentiful compute)

1. **An equivalence engine.** CT or laser scan of the OEM part, then CAD with tolerances, then automatic identification of critical sections and load paths. Then batch comparative static, fatigue and DT across the whole envelope of plausible load cases (thousands of FEA runs; envelope bounding replaces knowing the loads). The output is a margin map showing the replacement is at or above the OEM part at every point. Nobody runs the full plausible-load envelope today because each run costs engineer time.
2. **A substantiation compiler.** A machine-readable argument graph: every margin linked to its load assumption, allowable source, method, solver version, mesh-convergence proof and test anchor. It also emits the DER/ESA report. A reviewer audits the graph, not 300 pages of PDF.
3. **Verified-solver backbone.** AI proposes, a deterministic verified solver disposes. Automatic convergence proofs come from hp-FEM error estimators; automatic hand-calc cross-checks run alongside. The point is that an AI hallucination cannot reach the report.
4. **An allowables-and-validation factory.** Robotic servo-hydraulic and resonance fatigue lanes, fractography and DIC, building A/B-basis fatigue and da/dN data for the roughly 50 legacy alloy/temper/process combinations in C-130/KC-135/legacy commercial fleets. This is the data moat, and also the physics-clock compressor.
5. **Bayesian test planner.** Chooses the minimum confirmation tests that drive posterior uncertainty below the margin needed, then pre-agrees that test matrix with the reviewer.
6. **Reviewer copilot for ESAs, ACOs and DERs.** It pre-checks incoming SARs and PMA packages against the substantiation-graph schema and flags missing evidence. The government reviewer queue (6-24 months) is the longest clock, and nobody is building for the reviewer.
7. **A regulatory path.** Join CM4QC; seek an FAA issue paper or Means of Compliance for automated comparative substantiation; pursue an ODA; run side-by-side model and test campaigns (which the CM4QC gap list asks for explicitly) and publish them as public validation data.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side

- A. AI-native PMA/SAR house with an in-house ODA and test lab. This is the Approval Factory, with verification as its core. KPI: engineer plus reviewer hours per approval, from about 300-1,000 down to 20-50.
- B. Substantiation-as-a-service sold to the roughly 100+ PMA holders and DER consultancies. A software and services wedge; data flywheel from every package.
- C. Government reviewer copilot (AFWERX/SBIR to the ALCs and DLA). It cuts the queue, which raises throughput for everyone, and positions you as the de facto schema owner for SAR evidence.
- D. Legacy-alloy allowables corpus with a robotic fatigue lab. Licensable data asset; a physics-bound moat that software competitors (PhysicsX, Neural Concept) cannot copy with compute.
