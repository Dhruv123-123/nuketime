# c17: Incumbent approval holders using AI (HEICO, TransDigm/JPE/Victor Sierra, Chromalloy, Wencor, StandardAero, AAR, Lufthansa Technik, GE/Safran repair development). Is there public evidence they use AI to speed PMA/DER/repair engineering?

Method: 3 WebSearch calls (used up), then WebFetch of SEC 10-Ks (HEICO FY2025, StandardAero FY2025, AAR FY2026, TransDigm FY2025), EDGAR full-text search, company newsrooms (GE Aerospace, Lufthansa Technik, Chromalloy) and trade news. Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not verified this session.

## Bottom line
- **No incumbent publicly says it uses AI to cut the engineering or approval hours per PMA, DER repair or DLA SAR package.** Every AI use I found sits on one of three sides: (a) shop-floor inspection (borescope and blade image analysis), (b) fleet and reliability analytics, (c) procurement, sales and back office. The design-substantiation-approval core (reverse engineering, analysis, test plans, compliance reports, DER/ODA sign-off, FAA interaction) has no disclosed AI.
- In filings, AI shows up only as a cyber or regulatory **risk factor** (HEICO, StandardAero, TransDigm). AAR is the exception: it markets AI, but for parts procurement.
- The economics in HEICO's own disclosures leave room for an AI-native entrant. FSG R&D was $43.7M in FY2025 against roughly 400-550 new PMAs a year plus "numerous" DER repairs. That is about $80-110k of R&D per new PMA (derived, assuming DER repairs share the budget), consistent with the ~$100k/package prior. HEICO's management also named the pace of FAA approvals as a concern (Dec 2025 call summary). The bottleneck incumbents name is the regulator's throughput, not engineering knowledge.

## Evidence by company

### HEICO (Flight Support Group; includes Wencor since 2023)
- 10-K FY2025 (filed 2025-12-22, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/46619/000004661925000082/hei-20251031.htm):
  - "adding new products to our line at a rate of approximately 400 to 550 Parts Manufacturer Approvals" per year; about 20,000 PMA parts approved in total.
  - "proprietary repairs approved by an FAA-qualified designated engineering representative"; "numerous new proprietary repairs per year" (no count given).
  - FSG R&D $43.7M (FY25), $36.7M (FY24), $26.4M (FY23). Spend grew 66% in two years. ETG R&D $77.2M.
  - PMA approval requires "the submission of sample parts, drawings and testing data"; throughput is limited by "the resources available to the FAA", the agency's confidence in the applicant, and filing volume.
  - The only AI mention is a cyber risk factor: "the use of emerging technologies, such as artificial intelligence". Nothing on AI in engineering.
  - About 11,100 employees; no count of R&D engineers.
- Q4 FY2025 call (2025-12-19; Motley Fool transcript https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/01/06/heico-hei-q4-2025-earnings-call-transcript): FSG sales $834.4M (+21%) at a 24.1% operating margin. PMA and DER repairs were credited for margin. Military PMA was described as a "medium-term opportunity dependent on government execution". No AI discussion found. A third-party summary (stocktaper) notes concern about the pace of FAA approvals for new PMAs.
- Wencor: acquired by HEICO for about $2.05B in 2023 (recalled). It is a PMA/DER house inside FSG, with no separate AI disclosure.
- **Evidence:** none of AI in PMA engineering. **Opinion:** HEICO's moat is its approval track record (FAA confidence), airline JVs (e.g. with Lufthansa Technik; recalled) and capital-light catalogue breadth. It would be a natural acquirer of any AI approval tool, not an early builder of one.

### TransDigm (and JPE / Victor Sierra)
- 10-K FY2025 (filed 2025-11-12, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1260221/000126022125000081/tdg-20250930.htm): about 90% of net sales from proprietary products and about 55% from aftermarket. EDGAR full-text search shows "artificial intelligence" in the FY24 and FY25 10-Ks; in the sections read it is not in operations, so it is presumably a risk factor.
- TransDigm agreed to buy Jet Parts Engineering and Victor Sierra Aviation (PMA and DER-repair houses) from Vance Street Capital for about $2.2B in 2025 (recalled, not verified). That would make TransDigm, the OEM-pricing archetype, a buyer of PMA capability as well. No AI disclosure for JPE or Victor Sierra.
- **Evidence:** none.

### Chromalloy (owned by Veritas Capital)
- Website innovation and solutions pages: Advanced Repair, casting and coating, DER repairs, PMA. No AI, ML or digital-engineering mention (https://www.chromalloy.com/innovation/).
- With Lufthansa Technik: six PMA turbine blade types for the V2500 SelectOne. The HPT stage-2 and HPC stage-7 blades are FAA-certified and installed in a double-digit number of engines, with about 1,800 FH and 1,100 FC (ePlane, 2025-10-15; LHT newsroom https://newsroom.lufthansa-technik.com/long-standing-partnership-with-chromalloy-expanded-lufthansa-technik-now-offering-parts-for-iae-v2500-engines/). The challenges cited are manufacturing yield and "underutilizing engineering resources". No AI.
- **Evidence:** none.

### StandardAero
- 10-K FY2025 (filed 2026-02-26, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2025410/000119312526072618/saro-20251231.htm): holds a "designation as an FAA Designated Engineering Representative" to "simplify the approvals of our component repairs"; works "closely with engine OEMs on selected component repairs to gain their formal approvals". It has a patent on a Reliability Centered Maintenance application and uses "dedicated repair cells". All AI mentions (16) are cyber, EU AI Act or ESG risk boilerplate. The full-text match on "machine learning" is a regulatory-risk line.
- **Evidence:** none in repair engineering. StandardAero sits mostly in OEM-licensed networks, so its repair development is often OEM-approved rather than independent DER.

### AAR
- 10-K FY2026 (filed 2026-07-22, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1750/000110465926085459/air-20260531x10k.htm):
  - Launched **Airvoyant**, "an artificial intelligence ('AI') platform that automates the procurement workflow from requisition to invoice ... using data science and agentic AI", with "automated ordering controlled by AI confidence thresholds".
  - Software: Trax (cloud MRO system of record), Aerostrat, Airinmar. Described as "AI-enabled aviation aftermarket software".
  - Acquired **ART**, an interiors engineering company with an FAA **ODA**, which "enables us to issue supplemental type certificates and Parts Manufacturer Approval ('PMA') without reliance on third parties". The Repair, Engineering & Software segment "develops PMA parts" and "designs proprietary DER repairs".
  - 8-K of 2026-09-28: AAR is buying 65% of MRO Holdings (Panama) for about $2.6B. No AI content.
- **Evidence:** AAR is the incumbent with the most AI, but the AI is in **procurement and records**, not approval engineering. AAR does own the approval stack (ODA, DER, PMA) and an MRO software stack (Trax), so it is the incumbent best placed to connect the two. Its strategy shows ODA ownership as the lever ("without reliance on third parties").

### Lufthansa Technik
- AVIATAR page (https://www.lufthansa-technik.com/en/aviatar): analytics, failure forecasting, condition monitoring, reliability benchmarking. No AI claim for repair development.
- Newsroom search for AI or artificial intelligence (through 2026-10-20): no AI article about repair engineering.
- Recalled (not verified): AI and automated borescope image analysis, plus automated or adaptive repair cells (scanning and adaptive machining) in engine parts repair. These are shop-floor automation. LHT runs a very large in-house DER/EASA Part-21J repair development operation, and a PMA JV with HEICO (HEICO Aerospace, 20% LHT; recalled).
- **Evidence:** none of AI cutting approval-engineering hours.

### GE Aerospace / Safran
- GE plans to invest more than $130M in European MRO and component repair by end-2026, including "AI-enabled inspection techniques" that GE expects to shorten turnaround times and widen component repair capability (Turbomachinery Mag, 2024-10-24, https://www.turbomachinerymag.com/view/ge-aerospace-invests-upwards-of-130m-to-expand-upgrade-european-facilities). This includes XEOS, the GE and LHT JV in Poland.
- GE uses robotics, automation and AI in shop visits, on-wing inspection and repair workflows (ePlane 2025-08-25, https://www.eplaneai.com/es/news/ge-expands-use-of-automation-and-ai-in-mro-operations). No metrics and no repair development or approval use.
- Recalled: GE's AI "Blade Inspection Tool" for borescope analysis of CFM56/LEAP HPT blades (2024-25); "Wingmate", a company-wide generative AI assistant on Microsoft Azure OpenAI (2024), used by employees including engineers for documents and code. GE is an OEM, so its repair development (via OEM manuals and Engine Manual repair approvals) does not need DER/PMA permission. Its AI is aimed at inspection and at deciding whether a part is serviceable, which also feeds the case for repairs over replacement.
- Safran: acquired Preligens (AI) for about €220M in 2024 and renamed it Safran.AI (recalled). Its focus is defense ISR and imagery plus industrial uses. No disclosure about repair development.
- GE news (2026-10-12): an MOU with Shield AI and WZL-2 for F110 MRO in Poland. The "AI" there is the AI-piloted X-BAT aircraft, not MRO engineering.

## Synthesis: where AI is used versus where it is not (by pathway step)
| Step | Incumbent AI today | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate selection (which parts to PMA) | Implicit data analytics (HEICO "analyzing industry information"); procurement AI (AAR Airvoyant) | 10-Ks |
| Reverse engineering / design | Not disclosed | none |
| Analysis and substantiation (stress, materials, equivalency) | Not disclosed | none |
| Test plan, test execution | Not disclosed | none |
| Compliance data package / report writing | Generic generative AI possible (GE Wingmate, recalled); nothing specific to approvals | none |
| DER/ODA sign-off, FAA/ACO review | None (FAA throughput named as the constraint by HEICO) | HEICO 10-K, Q4 call summary |
| Production conformity and inspection | Yes: AI borescope and blade inspection (GE, LHT) | GE $130M release |
| Fleet, reliability, maintenance records | Yes: AVIATAR, Trax, StandardAero RCM patent | sites, 10-Ks |

## What this implies (opinion)
1. There is open space: no incumbent claims AI on the engineering and approval spine. The incumbents' advantage is FAA confidence, DER/ODA staff and catalogues, which is permission, consistent with the prior review.
2. The most likely fast follower is AAR (ODA + DER + PMA + Trax data + an AI team already building agentic procurement). HEICO is the most likely acquirer, given its $43.7M FSG R&D and acquisition habit. Lufthansa Technik has the deepest repair-development bench and a HEICO JV.
3. The verification-side opening: the regulator's (FAA ACO and DLA engineering support activity) review hours are the named bottleneck. A tool that makes packages machine-checkable, and is trusted by the reviewer side (FAA, DLA, DERs, ODA administrators), would increase throughput for every applicant. That makes the incumbents customers rather than competitors.
4. Inspection AI from GE and LHT already produces traceable, image-based evidence of part condition. Extending this evidence chain to "equivalency evidence" for PMA (scan-to-model, material ID, comparing in-service behaviour of PMA and OEM parts) is not done by anyone publicly.

## What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)
- A structured, machine-readable PMA/DER/SAR compliance package format (requirements traced to evidence) that the FAA and DLA accept, ideally through a pilot with an ACO or a DLA ESA (engineering support activity), with DERs and ODAs as first users.
- An automated "pre-review" checker, trained on FAA Order 8110.42 (PMA), 8110.37 (DER handbook) and DLA SAR checklists, that flags package gaps before submission. This reduces rejection and resubmission cycles, which are the hidden cost.
- An "equivalency evidence engine": CT/3D scan, material chemistry and microstructure ID, and an automated comparison of the dimensional and material envelope against fleet in-service data. This produces test-and-computation (T&C) evidence with less physical testing where similarity holds.
- An in-service PMA reliability ledger: pooled removal and failure data for PMA and DER-repaired parts versus OEM parts across MROs. This addresses the regulator's and insurers' liability concern and builds "FAA confidence", which today accrues only to incumbents.
- An AI-assisted DER/ODA: a firm whose own designees sign off, with AI drafting and checking. AAR's ART acquisition shows that owning an ODA is the permission asset.
