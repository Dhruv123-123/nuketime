## 1. Evidence gathered

### Flow Engineering (flowengineering.com, fetched 2026-10-20)
- Requirements and systems-engineering platform for hardware teams. It is a linked "system of record" covering requirements, CAD, simulation, code and tests.
- **Flow Agents**: impact analysis, test-coverage checks, "verify requirements against standards on each change", agents for mass/cost/power budgets, and a "safety and regulatory compliance" agent. Custom agents are supported.
- Funding: **$50M Series B at a $750M valuation**, co-led by Antonio Gracias (Valor) and Gavin Baker (Atreides). This is from the site. A CB Insights snippet separately shows a "$23M on 10/14/2025" round, so there may have been two rounds.
- Customers shown: Rivian (R1/R2/R3), VW Group Technologies, Joby, Skydio, Astranis, Stoke, Anduril, Isar, Pacific Fusion, Firestorm, Mytra.
- Deployment: GovCloud and self-hosted, ITAR/EAR, NIST 800-171.
- Opinion: Flow is the venture-backed leader of "AI requirements for new-space and new-defense". Its buyer is the *designer* of a new system. It does not touch the regulator side or the "prove this replacement equals the original" problem.

### Trace.Space (trace.space, fetched)
- Requirements, tests, parameters and variants in one graph. AI layer "Trinity", plus a "Space Agent" that orchestrates sub-agents for change impact, quality, coverage and systems analysis.
- Its stated philosophy is "AI suggests, not dictates". It pairs AI with deterministic rule checks (requirement quality, broken links, coverage gaps, out-of-tolerance parameters). It exposes an MCP server so customers can bring their own models.
- Standards: the FAQ claims support for ISO 26262, ASPICE and DO-178C through templates, audit export and trace-coverage analytics. ARP4754 is not mentioned. Certifications: SOC 2 Type 2 and ISO 27001. Air-gapped deployment is available.
- Funding is not stated on the site. Latvia-based, seed stage, roughly EUR 4M seed in 2024 (recalled).

## 2. The verification pathway (for a replacement part: SAR/PMA/DER repair)
Where these tools sit, and the gaps.

| # | Step | Who does it today | Tools touching it | Gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify the requirement set: CFR 21.303/PMA, 14 CFR 25.xxx basis, MIL-HDBK-516C, DLA SAR category and "critical safety item" rules, the OEM drawing's notes and specs | Cert engineers, DERs, DLA/ESA engineers by hand | Requirements tools import documents; nothing ships the regulation as a machine-readable, versioned graph | **No canonical machine-readable CFR + AC + MIL-HDBK + DLA SAR-guide + OEM-spec graph** |
| 2 | Build the compliance matrix: requirement -> method of compliance (analysis/test/inspection/similarity) | Applicant cert engineer; agreed with ACO/DER (PMA) or ESA (SAR) | Jama/Polarion/DOORS/Codebeamer templates; Flow's compliance agent | Templates exist. **No precedent engine** says "for this part class, the reviewer accepted these MoCs last time" (SAR outcomes are not public; FAA issue papers are partly public) |
| 3 | Produce the technical data: reverse-engineered dimensions, materials, processes | Engineers with CMM, CT and metallurgy labs | CAD/scan tools | Covered by other questions (c01-c05) |
| 4 | Equivalence/identicality argument: the replacement equals or exceeds the original in form, fit, function and durability | Engineers writing narrative reports | **Nothing.** Requirements tools trace links; they do not argue | **The main gap: an automated, machine-checkable equivalence case (scan + material + FEA deltas -> GSN-style argument with quantified margins)** |
| 5 | Test planning, including choosing analysis instead of test | Cert engineer and reviewer negotiate | None AI-native | **No optimizer that minimizes physical tests subject to "reviewer will accept"** |
| 6 | Physical tests run (the physics clock: fatigue, environmental, qualification) | Test labs | Nominal, Sift, Epsilon3 capture data | Data is captured but **not auto-mapped to compliance findings with conformity provenance** |
| 7 | Conformity inspection and first-article inspection (AS9102), FAA conformity by DAR/ASI | Inspectors, physically | Inspection software (e.g., DISCUS, recalled) | Partially automated; **tamper-evident, sensor-attested conformity records do not exist as regulator-accepted evidence** |
| 8 | Package assembly (SAR package, PMA data package, DER 8110-3 form) | Applicant | Doc tools | Easy for AI; low value alone |
| 9 | **Regulator/designee review**: DLA ESA engineers (e.g., Tinker, Robins, Hill), FAA ACO/MIDO, DERs, ODA units | Scarce government engineers; DER hours | **No AI tooling on the reviewer side at all** | **Second main gap: the reviewer workbench.** SAR reviews often take 6-24+ months (recalled) |
| 10 | Approval, then continued airworthiness: field data, failures, SB/AD effects | OEM/PMA holder | Fleet maintenance systems | Nobody closes the loop from field data to the equivalence case |

## 3. What would need to be done (assuming intelligence and compute are plentiful)
1. **Regulation-as-code corpus.** Ingest 14 CFR, all FAA ACs/orders/policy statements, the FAA DRS, EASA CS/AMC, MIL-HDBK-516C, MIL-STDs, DLA SAR guides and ESA-specific requirements, and AMSC codes. Version them and link them so each requirement has a stable ID. No one maintains this publicly.
2. **A precedent database of approvals.** Every public PMA (the FAA PMA database lists holders and parts), every 8110-3 pattern, FOIA'd SAR decisions, and DLA "Source Approval" outcomes, linked to part class, material and MoC. This lets the system predict reviewer acceptance. It is data-access bound. FOIA plus a partnership with an ESA would be needed.
3. **An equivalence-case compiler.** Inputs: CT/CMM scan, material certs, process sheets, FEA/CFD on the replacement versus the OEM baseline, and test data. Output: a structured assurance case (GSN/SACM) with quantified margins and explicit residual uncertainties. Every claim links to signed raw evidence. Generation is AI; checking is deterministic, rule-based and replayable, which mirrors Trace.Space's "AI suggests, deterministic checks verify" split.
4. **Test-minimization planner.** Choose the smallest set of physical tests (by Bayesian value of information) that closes the equivalence case at reviewer-acceptable confidence. Run test articles in parallel, use accelerated or digital-twin tests where precedent allows, and schedule lab capacity. This attacks the physics clock directly.
5. **Attested evidence chain.** Lab instruments, CMM and CT sign their data cryptographically at capture (Nominal-style edge capture plus signing). Conformity then becomes remote-verifiable, and a DAR or ESA engineer can accept "remote conformity". FAA does allow some remote/virtual conformity post-COVID (recalled). This needs regulator acceptance: a pilot via an ODA, or a DLA/AFSC SBIR.
6. **A DO-330-qualifiable AI tool path.** No LLM-based tool is qualified. The workaround is to qualify the *deterministic checker* (TQL-4/5) and treat the AI as an unqualified generator whose output the checker fully verifies. That makes the AI legally invisible to the cert basis, which is the key move.
7. **Sell to the reviewer, the "approver workbench".** Give ESA/ACO/DER/ODA reviewers a tool that pre-screens incoming packages, flags gaps, cites precedent and writes the review record. Government routes: SBIR/STTR with AFLCMC, AF Rapid Sustainment Office, DLA R&D, Navy NAVAIR; FAA through the 2024 Reauthorization Act cert-streamlining provisions (recalled). DER/ODA firms are a commercial wedge because they bill hours and are capacity-constrained.
8. **Liability.** Reviewers cannot delegate their finding. The tool must keep the human finding and make it 10x faster. Product liability for a "missed gap" would need contractual caps or insurance (aviation product liability insurers). Over time, the ODA model (delegated authority to an organization) is the legal vehicle: an AI-native ODA, where the company itself holds delegated authority, is the endgame.

## 4. AI plays on the verification side
- **A. AI-native ODA/DER house (the "Approval Factory's own reviewer").** Hold delegation in-house: hire DERs, get an ODA for PMA/STC. Use AI to raise each DER's throughput 5-10x, and sell review capacity to other PMA and repair applicants. This is the strongest moat because it converts scarce *permission* capacity into a scalable service. Comparable precedent: HEICO and Wencor are large in-house DER/PMA operations, and independent DER firms exist.
- **B. Reviewer workbench for DLA ESAs and FAA ACOs.** A government SaaS/SBIR wedge. It earns data access to the precedent corpus as a byproduct, which no applicant-side tool can get.
- **C. Equivalence-case compiler.** Sold to PMA/SAR applicants (HEICO, Wencor, Jamco, small machine shops on the DLA QML) and used internally by the Approval Factory. The KPI is engineering hours per package.
- **D. Evidence-attestation layer** (signed lab, CMM and CT data to remote conformity) on top of Nominal/Sift-style test data. It could partner with or be acquired by those companies.
- **E. Regulation and precedent graph as an API** that Flow, Jama and Trace.Space plug into. Infrastructure, but probably not venture-scale alone. Valispace's roughly $16-20M exit is the warning.

## 5. Surprises
- No funded startup surfaced for AI-automated DO-178/ARP4754/CS-25/PMA compliance despite obvious demand. All the AI is in general systems-engineering tools (Flow, Trace.Space), sold to designers of *new* systems.
- Flow's $750M valuation shows investors will pay for "AI system of record for hardware". Customers like Anduril and Joby are certification-heavy, but Flow's compliance agent is generic.
- Collimator's domain is parked. AI-native model-based design startups are fragile.
- The standalone requirements tool Valispace exited small, at about $16-20M.
- Nobody builds for the *reviewer*, even though reviewer hours (DLA ESA, FAA ACO, DER) are the binding constraint the prior review identified.
