## 1. How the alternative pathway works (the analogue of PMA/DER/SAR)

### US (I-codes)
- IBC 104.11 (recalled text): the building official may approve an alternative material, design or method not prescribed by the code if it "complies with the intent of the provisions of this code" and is "at least the equivalent of that prescribed in this code in quality, strength, effectiveness, fire resistance, durability and safety."
  - 104.11.1 Research reports: the official may accept supporting data from "approved sources." In practice this means an ICC-ES ESR, an IAPMO UES ER, or a UL/Intertek listing.
  - 104.11.2 Tests: when the evidence is not enough, the official may require tests by an "approved agency" at no cost to the jurisdiction.
- IFC 104.10 (alternatives) has a parallel provision. IFC 104.7.2 lets the fire code official require a "technical opinion and report" prepared by a qualified engineer at the applicant's expense (recalled). This is the legal basis for third-party peer review of performance-based design (PBD), and the applicant pays for it.
- NFPA 101 Life Safety Code Ch. 5 (performance-based option), NFPA 5000 Ch. 5, and the SFPE Engineering Guide to Performance-Based Fire Protection (recalled; 2nd ed. 2007) define the steps: project scope, goals, objectives, performance criteria, design fire scenarios, trial designs, evaluation, documentation and O&M manual.
- The approval is discretionary and local. The decision belongs to one AHJ (Authority Having Jurisdiction) official, and the US has about 20,000+ AHJs (recalled). No national precedent register exists, so an alternative approved in Seattle carries no weight in Portland.
- What the official actually decides on: (a) an ESR (product-level, portable across jurisdictions); (b) a project-specific PBD report from a fire protection engineer (FPE), plus an applicant-paid third-party peer review; (c) a "modification" request (IBC 104.10) for practical difficulty.

### ICC-ES / product route (the closest thing to PMA)
- ICC-ES is a nonprofit subsidiary of ICC that publishes "thousands of reports" (ESRs). Each ESR evaluates a product against an Acceptance Criteria (AC) document approved by an Evaluation Committee in public hearings (fetched: https://icc-es.org/ and https://icc-es.org/evaluation-report-program/).
- Process (fetched): application package, draft report, test report submittal sheet, rules of procedure, and optional state supplements (for example, Florida).
- Gate: if no AC exists for a new product class, one has to be developed first. That takes months to more than a year of committee cycles (recalled). Tests must come from labs accredited under ISO/IEC 17025. ESRs carry annual renewal fees and plant inspections (recalled: from roughly $10k to well over $50k for a new ESR, and 6-18 months).
- Competitors: IAPMO Uniform Evaluation Service (UES), Intertek CCRR, DrJ Engineering (a "TER", a licensed-engineer route under state law; recalled), UL Evaluation Reports.

### Other jurisdictions
- UK: Approved Documents are guidance only, and compliance with the functional requirements can be shown through fire engineering (BS 7974, PD 7974). After Grenfell, the Building Safety Act 2022 created the Building Safety Regulator, which runs Gateway 2 (approval before construction) and Gateway 3 for higher-risk buildings. Gateway 2 has had a severe backlog (recalled: median decisions well above 40 weeks against a 12-week statutory target during 2024-25, and the BSR was moved out of HSE and restructured in 2025). The Act also mandates a digital "golden thread" of building information.
- Australia: the NCC allows "Performance Solutions" through a Fire Engineering Brief (FEB) and Fire Engineering Report under the International Fire Engineering Guidelines, with sign-off by a registered certifier and fire brigade consultation. The Shergold-Weir "Building Confidence" report (2018) found weak verification of performance solutions (recalled).
- New Zealand: Verification Method C/VM2 is a semi-standardized fire engineering method, which shows that a PBD can be made formulaic.

### Florida's statutory third-party path (an important precedent)
- Fla. Stat. 553.791 (recalled) lets an owner hire a licensed "private provider" for plan review and inspection instead of the AHJ. The AHJ must accept the work within statutory deadlines, and the private provider carries the liability. Later amendments (around 2023, recalled) tightened the AHJ's deadlines.
- Texas (recalled, 2023 HB 3699 / 2025 follow-ons) lets applicants use third-party review if a city misses its deadlines.
- Why it matters: a law already exists that moves the verification act to a licensed private party that a company can own. That party is the building-code equivalent of a DER or ODA.

## 2. Who profits (the incumbents)
- Fire protection engineering consultancies write and peer-review PBDs:
  - Jensen Hughes (formerly RJA plus Hughes Associates; PE-backed by Gryphon, recalled; about 1,500+ staff, recalled)
  - Arup Fire
  - WSP
  - Holmes (NZ/US)
  - Code Consultants Inc.
  - Telgian
  - Olsson Fire & Risk (Australia)
  - OFR
  - Affinity Fire
  - Fees run roughly $20k-$300k per PBD and $10k-$100k per peer review (recalled, order of magnitude).
- Testing and listing labs:
  - UL Solutions (NYSE: ULS, IPO April 2024, about $2.9B revenue in 2024, recalled)
  - Intertek
  - SGS
  - Element
  - FM Approvals
  - Southwest Research Institute
  - The scarce asset is furnace and facade test capacity: ASTM E119, NFPA 285, UL 263, full-scale burns. Full-scale tests cost from tens of thousands to more than $200k each, with queues of months (recalled).
- Evaluation services: ICC-ES (fees and annual renewals), IAPMO UES.
- Code publishers: ICC (sells the codes, owns ICC-ES, runs certification exams) and NFPA. They earn copyright and licensing revenue. Code text sits behind paywalls (see ASTM v. Public.Resource.Org litigation, recalled).
- Simulation tools: NIST FDS/Smokeview (free, public domain), Thunderhead PyroSim/Pathfinder (commercial), ANSYS, OpenFOAM. NIST publishes the FDS Verification and Validation Guides (recalled).
- Who loses: owners who pay twice (designer FPE plus peer-review FPE), jurisdictions without in-house FPEs, and innovators such as mass timber, novel facades and battery storage. Before the 2021 IBC Type IV-A/B/C provisions, every tall mass-timber building went through 104.11 one building at a time. BESS (battery energy storage) went the same way before NFPA 855 and UL 9540A became the de facto acceptance test.

## 3. Who is already applying AI (fetched evidence)
- Archistar AI PreCheck (Australia/US): automated code and zoning checks on PDF/CAD/BIM. "30-plus cities and local governments." Clients include LA County, Austin, Cape Coral, Yonkers and Surrey. Claims "up to a 55% reduction in permit review cycles." Funding not stated. Source: https://www.archistar.ai/
- CodeComply: AI checks against ICC, NFPA, ADA, FHA and local amendments, with design-time, pre-check and reviewer-side products. CivicPlus calls it its official plan review tool, and TXShare endorses it. Customers include Marriott, Mortenson, Suffolk, Princeton, Altamonte Springs and Danbury. Source: https://www.codecomply.ai/
- UpCodes (YC, code search and copilot), PermitFlow (YC, permit expediting; recalled to have raised a Series A of about $31M in 2024), Symbium, CivCheck, Clariti, Blitz and others (recalled) work on permitting workflow.
- What all of these check: prescriptive rules (egress widths, travel distances, occupancy tables). As far as visible, none verifies performance-based fire engineering, which covers fire and smoke modelling, egress simulation, tenability criteria and uncertainty. None performs ESR-grade product evaluation either. Where they sell to the AHJ, they sell software and do not take the licensed reviewer's sign-off or liability.

## 4. Full verification pathway: who does each step, and the gaps
| Step | Who does it | Time and cost | Broken or undone |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Identify that the design or product is non-prescriptive | Architect or manufacturer | Days | Done by experience. No tool maps design intent to code gaps |
| 2. Pre-application meeting with AHJ and fire marshal | FPE consultant | Weeks | Informal and unrecorded, and depends on the individual official |
| 3. Fire Engineering Brief: goals, objectives, criteria, design fires | FPE | 2-8 weeks | Design fire selection is judgement. There is no shared library of accepted scenarios across AHJs (NZ C/VM2 is the exception) |
| 4. Analysis: CFD (FDS), egress (Pathfinder), hand calculations | FPE | Weeks; compute is cheap | Uncertainty quantification is rarely done. Models are validated against limited data (NIST V&V) but are seldom sensitivity-tested |
| 5. Physical tests where required (E119, NFPA 285, UL 9540A, room-corner) | Accredited labs | Hours of burn, months of queue, $10k-$200k+ | Physics clock and scarce furnaces. No shared virtual-test equivalence is accepted |
| 6. Product evaluation (ESR) or AC development | ICC-ES or UES committee | 6-18+ months | AC development is the bottleneck. Each new product class needs new criteria |
| 7. Third-party peer review | Second FPE, applicant-paid | 4-12 weeks, $10k-$100k | Duplicates the analysis by hand. There is no checklist standard, and quality varies |
| 8. AHJ approval decision | Building official or fire marshal | Weeks to months | Discretionary and unpublished. Nothing carries over to the next AHJ |
| 9. Construction verification of PBD assumptions | Inspectors, special inspectors | During build | Inspectors check prescriptive items, while PBD-specific assumptions (smoke control capacity, fuel loads) are tested ad hoc |
| 10. Commissioning (smoke control acceptance testing, NFPA 92) | Commissioning agents | Days | Done, but rarely compared quantitatively with the model |
| 11. Lifetime maintenance of PBD assumptions (O&M manual, fire load limits, tenant fit-outs) | Owner and fire marshal | Decades | Largely undone. Nobody re-verifies a PBD when the tenant changes. The UK golden thread mandate exists, but tooling is weak |

## 5. What would need to be done (assuming abundant intelligence and compute)
1. Build a national and international precedent ledger of alternatives approved under 104.11, IFC 104.10, Gateway 2 and NCC Performance Solutions. Reconstruct it from public-records requests for permit files, which are mostly public records in US jurisdictions (scale through FOIA/PRA automation). This turns discretionary approvals into case law that a reviewer can cite.
2. Develop a machine-checkable PBD standard: encode SFPE guide, NFPA 101 Ch. 5 and C/VM2-style scenarios into a formal schema, so a submission can be verified rather than read.
3. Build an automated independent re-analysis engine. It would re-run the applicant's FDS and Pathfinder cases, plus thousands of perturbed scenarios, and output probabilistic tenability margins. Compute is cheap. What is missing is acceptance that this kind of re-analysis counts as a peer review.
4. Pursue virtual-test equivalence: surrogate models calibrated on the archive of decades of E119, NFPA 285 and 9540A results held by labs, then petition ICC-ES Evaluation Committees for ACs that accept analysis plus reduced testing for interpolated variants. ICC-ES ACs already allow engineering analysis for some variants (recalled). Changing the binding constraint needs the lab test archives (data access) and committee votes (regulator acceptance).
5. Set up a licensed private-provider firm that is AI-native: Florida 553.791 private provider, Texas third-party reviewer, UK Registered Building Control Approver, Australian registered certifier. It signs plan reviews and peer reviews and carries the professional-liability insurance. This is the building-code DER/ODA.
6. Run continuous post-occupancy verification: tie the approved PBD assumptions to building systems data (BMS, sprinkler supervision, occupancy sensors, fit-out permits), and flag the moment a tenant change breaks an assumption. This meets the UK golden thread requirement and NFPA 101 4.6.12 maintenance duties (recalled).

## 6. AI plays on the verification side
- **A. AI-native third-party peer-review and private-provider house.** Legal channels exist today (IFC 104.7.2 applicant-paid technical reports, Florida 553.791, Texas third-party review, UK RBCA, Australian certifiers). Licensed FPEs and PEs sign, and the AI does 90% of the review. The applicant pays, the AHJ accepts, and incumbents charge $10k-$100k per review.
- **B. Evaluation-report factory, the ESR counterpart of the "Approval Factory."** It would take on new building products (mass timber connectors, facade panels, BESS enclosures, heat pumps, prefab modules), draft ACs, ESR submissions and test plans, and minimise physical tests. Its KPI would be hours and dollars per ESR, which mirrors the PMA thesis exactly, and the product route is portable across about 20k AHJs.
- **C. Precedent graph plus probabilistic re-analysis sold to regulators.** Gateway 2's backlog and AHJs with no FPE staff need this, so the regulator is the buyer.
- **D. Lifetime PBD compliance twin.** This means continuous verification after approval, a part of the pathway nobody does.
- **Fit with the Approval Factory thesis.** It holds: the moat is the licensed sign-off and accepted criteria, not the analysis. Building codes have a weaker liability barrier than aviation, many more parallel regulators (more fragmentation, more AHJs to sell to), and a fast-growing demand driver in BESS, data centres, mass timber and UK higher-risk buildings. Unlike aviation, a private-provider law (Florida) already forces the regulator to accept a private verifier.
