### The mechanism that matters for the Approval Factory
Vanta did not automate the *auditor*. It automated the **applicant's evidence production** and then **co-opted the verifier** by bringing auditors onto its platform with an API, a shared evidence view, and audit times cut in half. The verdict stayed with a licensed human (a CPA firm for SOC 2). Three conditions made this possible:
1. **Evidence was machine-readable at its source.** AWS, Okta, GitHub and HR systems all have APIs, so "collect evidence" turned into connectors plus continuous tests.
2. **The standard was a control framework, not a physics question.** SOC 2 and ISO 27001 ask "do you have a control and does it operate", not "will this bracket survive 60,000 cycles".
3. **A demand shock created the buyer.** Enterprise procurement started requiring SOC 2, so every startup needed one at once. Vanta sold to the applicant, priced per framework (about $10-50k/yr, recalled), and became the default channel to auditors, who then referred clients back.

Business model: SaaS to applicants, a marketplace and network effect with verifiers, and multi-framework expansion (SOC 2 to ISO 27001, HIPAA, PCI, FedRAMP, ISO 42001, CMMC). Over time the platform becomes the "trust center" that buyers check. That is the real moat: the **evidence graph** sits between applicant, verifier and buyer.

## 4. Why hardware never got its Vanta (and what would change it)
1. **Evidence is not API-native.** It lives in CMM files, metallurgy lab PDFs, test-stand telemetry, OEM drawings and paper 8130-3 tags. *To change it:* own the instruments (in-house metrology, NDT and a test lab) so that evidence is born digital and signed at capture with a hash, operator, calibration record and sensor provenance. This is the "Nominal + lab" layer, operated rather than sold.
2. **The verifier is a government engineer, not a commercial auditor who wants throughput.** DLA engineering support activities and FAA ACOs have no revenue incentive to go faster. *To change it:* replicate CivCheck's model. Give reviewers a free, government-compliant (FedRAMP/IL5, recalled requirement) review workspace that pre-checks packages against the SAR checklist and the DLA/AMSC criteria, so a reviewer spends 2 hours instead of 40. Get it in via SBIR/OTA (DLA, AFLCMC and the FAA all run SBIR topics) or as part of the FAA's AI Safety Assurance roadmap (July 2024, recalled).
3. **The sign-off is personal liability under delegation** (DER, ODA unit member, DLA source approval). *To change it:* be the delegated entity, like Norm Law. That means an in-house ODA/DER bench, or partnering with DERs paid per package, where AI drafts and the DER signs. The KPI becomes DER-hours per 8110-3.
4. **Standards are physics, not controls.** Some tests must physically run (fatigue, qualification, environmental). *To change it:* pursue "analysis in lieu of test" acceptance, i.e. regulator-accepted simulation credibility frameworks (ASME V&V 40 is the medtech analog; FAA AC 20-115D and the Means of Compliance framework, recalled). Build a validated digital-twin library per material and process so similarity arguments replace repeat tests.
5. **No demand shock for any single framework**, whereas SOC 2 was required by every enterprise buyer. *Possible shocks:* the CMMC 2.0 rollout (2025-2028) is a cyber shock that already feeds the Vanta-style CMMC players. Other candidates: DoD sustainment and readiness pressure on aging fleets, the FY2025-26 NDAA right-to-repair and "data rights" fights, and DLA's push to increase competition on sole-source NSNs.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side (opinion, derived from the above)
1. **"Vanta for Source Approval"**: applicant-side SaaS that turns a reverse-engineering campaign into a SAR, TDP or PMA package, with continuous traceability from requirement to evidence and a shared reviewer portal. It sells to the ~100s of small DLA-qualified sources and PMA houses; HEICO and Wencor are the comparable buyers, though they would build it themselves.
2. **"CivCheck for DLA/FAA reviewers"**: a free or SBIR-funded government reviewer copilot that pre-screens packages against SAR checklists, similarity criteria and prior approvals. Applicants pay for "pre-screened" status. This sits closest to the actual bottleneck, which is reviewer hours.
3. **Reviewer-model corpus (the PermitFlow moat)**: a dataset of every reviewer comment, rejection reason and approval decision for SARs, PMAs and 8110-3s, used to predict and pre-empt rejections. You get the data by running many packages yourself, i.e. the Approval Factory generates it as exhaust.
4. **Signed evidence at capture**: cryptographically attested metrology, NDT and test data (instrument, calibration chain, operator), which lets a reviewer trust evidence without re-witnessing it. This removes the "witness test" step and travel.
5. **Own the sign-off (the Norm Law move)**: an AI-native DER/ODA house, where the platform is the internal tool and the business is approved parts and repairs. This is the most defensible play and the most consistent with "monopoly is held by permission".
6. **Credibility-of-simulation dossiers**: standard packages arguing that analysis meets compliance in place of test, pre-negotiated with the FAA/DLA per part family. This attacks the physics clock through regulator acceptance.

## 6. Surprises
- Norm Ai, the best-funded "AI compliance" company ($1.2B valuation), chose to **become a law firm**, i.e. to own the permission. That validates the Approval Factory over the SaaS framing.
- greenlite.ai redirects to Bretton AI. The "Greenlite for building permits" premise was wrong: Greenlite is AML/KYC.
- CivCheck's model, **free to the regulator and paid by the applicant**, already exists in building permits and has been acquired (by Clariti). It is the cleanest template for the verification side.
- The closest aerospace-certification AI company found (Xcert AI) has raised only CHF 1.1M, so the space is essentially empty at venture scale.
- Vanta's biggest asset is arguably the **auditor network** (100+ firms, 26k audits), not its software. The hardware analog would be a DER/DLA-reviewer network, and nobody has one.
