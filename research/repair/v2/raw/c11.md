# c11: Obsolescence and DMSMS intelligence and continuation (Rochester, SiliconExpert, Z2Data, Lytica, Sourceability, QP Semi, IC re-creation, FPGA emulation). Who does it, what the verification pathway is, what is undone, and the AI play on the verification side

Search budget: 3 WebSearch calls used. Everything else was fetched directly. Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not verified this session.

## 0. Corrections to the question's premises
- **Revasum** makes CMP and grinding equipment for SiC/GaN wafers (recalled). It does not re-create ICs. It is not relevant here.
- **Sarcos** made robotics/exoskeletons and was renamed Palladyne AI in 2024 (recalled). It does not do FPGA chip emulation. It is not relevant.
- The real IC re-creation and emulation players are SRI International (DLA GEM/AME), Rochester Electronics, Lansdale Semiconductor, Innovasic (now Analog Devices), QP Semiconductor, Micross and Teledyne e2v HiRel. DMEA (DoD's own microelectronics activity) does it in-house. See section 2.

## 1. Evidence gathered (with sources)

### DLA GEM / AME (SRI International, formerly Sarnoff). This is the government's own "approval factory" for chips.
- GEM started in 1987 and became a standalone production program in 1997. AME is the R&D sister program. Source: https://www.militaryaerospace.com/computers/article/55330677/
- **$73.7M AME Improvement contract to SRI**, awarded Oct 17, 2025, running to January 2031, with work in New Jersey. Scope covers logic, memory, ASICs, FPGAs, microprocessors, op-amps and linear/analog parts, plus additive manufacturing of obsolete ceramic/metal packages, radiation tolerance, and high-voltage analog. Source: same article.
- DVIDS figures: **560+ weapon systems** supported, **175,000+ parts** supplied, **~30,000 different microcircuit devices** made by SRI, one part (GEM34301QPA) covers ~30 systems, and 26,310 parts went to AEGIS. **"One emulation can take up to 48 weeks."** Batches run from 1 to 50 units. Redesign without GEM costs about **$1M per circuit card**. DoD's share of the microcircuit market fell from 60% to under 2%. Method: document review, electrical probing, delayering and high-resolution imaging, then a new device built on stocked base wafers by adding metal layers. Source: https://www.dvidshub.net/news/printable/488272
- From the SRI/Sarnoff paper: an AN/UYK-44 part was specified, designed and produced in 8 weeks and saved $1.2M (15:1). GEM NRE is about **20% of the NRE of one board redesign**. Specs are rebuilt by characterization testing. Equivalence is checked by FFF testing on ATE at component, PWB and system level across the mil-temperature range. Parts are screened to MIL-STD-883, and QML was planned. An estimated 40-60% of microcircuits in fielded systems have DMS problems. On the APG-63 radar, GEM could supply 86% of the ICs. Source: https://www.csl.sri.com/papers/dmc_paper1/dmc_paper1.html
- **Takeaway:** the method has been proven for about 35 years. The bottleneck is throughput (up to 48 weeks per part, one contractor), not feasibility.

### Rochester Electronics (Newburyport, MA, private)
- Has made **20,000+ device types** and claims it can make 70,000+. Holds **12B+ die in stock**. Process flows include MIL-STD-883, QML, and Class Q and V per MIL-PRF-38535. It builds from information transferred directly by the OCM. Source: https://www.rocelec.com/manufacturing
- Says it is authorized by 70+ OCMs. Revenue estimates from third parties are about $275M, or $200-500M. Neither is reliable. Source: https://bitscale.ai/directory/rochester-electronics-llc
- A recreation takes **about 18 months, with prototypes at about 12 months**. It works best for digital parts and is hard for RF and embedded-flash parts. Done with OCM authorization. Source: https://militaryembedded.com/comms/communications/talking-counterfeit-parts-semiconductor-obsolescence-component-recreation-a-conversation-with-daniel-deisz-of-rochester-electronics
- **Moat:** permission. It holds OCM licenses, the original die and test programs, and its parts are legally "authorized" (AS6496). This is exactly the "permission, not knowledge" pattern.

### Lansdale Semiconductor (Phoenix)
- Makes 3,000+ "classic design" ICs in their original packages, matching AMD, Fairchild, Intel and Motorola designs. Source: https://www.lansdale.com/
- Its model is buying masks and licenses from OCMs (recalled).

### Other continuation and re-creation players (recalled unless noted)
- **Innovasic** (Albuquerque): built clean-room re-creations of legacy microcontrollers and microprocessors (8051, 80C186, 68HC11 and others). Analog Devices acquired it around 2019 (recalled). Its site returned 503 this session.
- **QP Semiconductor** (Santa Clara area): re-creates and reproduces obsolete ICs and supplies hi-rel/QML parts (recalled). Its site returned 403.
- **Micross Components**: hi-rel die, packaging and continuation. **Teledyne e2v HiRel**: licensed continuation of Freescale/NXP PowerPC parts (recalled).
- **DMEA** (Defense Microelectronics Activity, Sacramento): runs DoD's own obsolete-microelectronics fab and design capability and administers the ATSP IDIQ (ATSP4, multi-billion ceiling; recalled). Its site returned 503.
- **TechInsights** (which absorbed Chipworks): does image-based IC reverse engineering and circuit extraction at commercial scale (recalled). This is the closest commercial asset to automated netlist extraction.
- **Retronix** (UK) and others: component reclamation and reballing (recalled).

### Obsolescence intelligence (data) players
- **SiliconExpert**: owned by Arrow Electronics since 2015 (recalled). Parts database of about 1B parts with lifecycle forecasting. Site returned 403.
- **Z2Data** (Herndon, VA): self-funded per Verdantix (https://atlas.verdantix.com/vendors/z2data). Acquired **AMSYS GmbH in July 2025** for lifecycle work in Europe (secondary source). Database of 1B+ components. Customers include Qualcomm, Teradyne, Dell, Airbus and Plexus. **EOL counts: 528,546 (2021), 756,087 (2022), 473,910 (2023)**. About 30% of 2023 EOLs came with no PCN warning, 78% were caused by low demand, and over 70% of 350 nm MCUs are EOL. Source: https://www.z2data.com/obsolescence-report
- **Accuris** (formerly IHS Markit parts management, sold to KKR in 2022-23, recalled): BOM intelligence. **Siemens Supplyframe** (acquired 2021, recalled). **Octopart** (Altium, now Renesas after the 2024 deal, recalled). **Lytica** (Ottawa): supply-risk and spend analytics for OEMs/EMS (recalled). **Sourceability/Sourcengine**: marketplace and distributor with lifecycle data (recalled; about page 404). **Total Parts Plus**, **QinetiQ/AVCOM**, and **Q-Star**: DoD-focused DMSMS case management (recalled).
- Government side: the DoD DMSMS program (DSPO), SD-22 DMSMS Guidebook, GIDEP notices, the DMSMS Shared Data Warehouse, and DLA Land & Maritime SMDs and QML (DSPO site returned 403).
- **Takeaway:** the "intelligence" layer is crowded and commoditized. It forecasts part EOL, but not the effect on a weapon system or what fix to apply.

## 2. The verification and approval pathway for an obsolete microcircuit, step by step

| # | Step | Who does it today | Time / cost | Undone or broken |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | EOL detection (PDN, GIDEP, forecasts) | SiliconExpert, Z2Data, Accuris; GIDEP | Subscription | ~30% of EOLs come with no notice. Mostly solved, and commoditized. |
| 2 | Where-used impact mapping: chip to board to LRU to NSN to platform to demand | Each program office/OEM separately, with incomplete indentured BOMs | Weeks to months per case | **Broken.** No cross-service graph exists. DoD often does not own lower-level BOMs, and one chip can sit in 30 systems while each program solves it separately. |
| 3 | Resolution choice per SD-22 (stock, reclamation, alternate, substitute, aftermarket, emulation, redesign) | Program DMSMS teams | Rules of thumb | No quantified per-case cost and risk model tied to the fleet. |
| 4 | Golden-sample acquisition and characterization (rebuilding the spec) | SRI GEM, Rochester (holds OCM data), DMEA | A large share of the up-to-48-week cycle | **Weakest step.** Datasheets omit timing corners and undocumented behaviour. Golden samples are scarce. Delayering and imaging are manual. |
| 5 | Replacement design (gate array/BiCMOS, new ASIC, FPGA, die re-use) | SRI, Rochester, Innovasic, DMEA | Months | Mostly a knowledge problem, which AI makes cheap. |
| 6 | Equivalence verification (ATE vectors, temperature, timing, board and system level) | Same, plus the OEM | Weeks to months | **No formal proof of equivalence.** It is test-vector based, coverage is unreported (the SRI paper gives no vector counts), and system-level retest is often required. |
| 7 | Qualification and screening (MIL-PRF-38535 QML, MIL-STD-883: burn-in ~160 h at 125C, 1,000 h life test, temperature cycling) | Manufacturer under a DLA L&M qualifying activity | **Physics clock: weeks to months** | Qualification by similarity and physics-of-failure methods are underused. |
| 8 | Drawing and part approval (DLA SMD, part number, QML listing) | DLA Land & Maritime | Months | A queue and reviewer-hours problem. |
| 9 | Platform engineering acceptance and configuration change (ECP, Class I/II); for airborne parts, airworthiness. **An FPGA replacement of an ASIC triggers DO-254/AC 20-152A DAL evidence** | Program office, OEM, airworthiness authority (MIL-HDBK-516 or FAA) | Months to years | **Most expensive permission step.** Each platform approves the same part separately. FPGA emulation turns a "part change" into a "complex electronic hardware" certification. |
| 10 | Cataloging and source approval (NSN, DLA SAR), then lifetime buy | DLA, the vendor | Months | Links to the Approval Factory's DLA SAR wedge. |

## 3. Where permission sits (consistent with the prior review)
- Rochester's moat is OCM licenses and original data, which is permission.
- SRI's moat is a sole contract since 1988 and DLA trust, which is permission plus incumbency.
- The program office's ECP and airworthiness sign-off is permission, given separately for every platform that uses the part.
- The knowledge steps (4-6) are where AI collapses cost. The permission steps (8-9) are where the product has to be **evidence that reviewers accept faster**.

## 4. What would need to be done (assuming abundant intelligence and compute)
1. **Fleet-wide where-used graph.** Ingest IPBs, tech orders, provisioning data (GEIA-STD-0007/LSAR), DLA FLIS/WebFLIS, GIDEP, SMDs and demand history. Map every microcircuit to every board, LRU, NSN and platform. Then rank emulation targets by the redesign cost avoided (about $1M per card). Nobody has this across services.
2. **Autonomous characterization cell.** A robotic ATE/bench plus an AI that actively probes a golden sample with adaptive test generation to recover a complete behavioural model, including undocumented modes and timing corners. Combine it with **SEM delayering and ML netlist extraction** to get a structural netlist. The aim is to shrink step 4 from months to days.
3. **Formal equivalence certificate.** Prove the replacement RTL/netlist equivalent to the extracted original netlist (formal tools), and add differential testing of the golden part and the new part on the same ATE, with coverage metrics. Package the result as a standard evidence dossier. This needs DLA L&M and program offices to accept "proof plus targeted test" in place of re-running system tests.
4. **Qualification-by-similarity / physics-of-failure engine.** Use the same process on the same fab node, with prior QML data, to argue for reduced qualification flows. The physics clock remains (burn-in and life test must run), but the number of separate qualification lots can shrink. This needs a change in qualifying-activity policy.
5. **Approve once, use everywhere.** A single equivalence and approval evidence package accepted by every platform that uses the part (30 systems for one GEM part). This needs a DoD/DLA policy instrument, such as an SMD-level "drop-in equivalence" designation that automatically clears Class II changes.
6. **DO-254-ready evidence generation** for FPGA or new-ASIC replacements in airborne LRUs: auto-generated requirements traceability, verification and DAL artifacts. Without it, FPGA emulation stays commercially unattractive for flight hardware.
7. **Pooled demand contracts.** Aggregate the 1-to-50-unit batch demand across services so that a single NRE is amortized.

## 5. The AI play on the verification side
- **"Equivalence-as-a-service" for obsolete electronics.** Take a golden sample (or OCM data), autonomously characterize and extract a netlist, then produce a formal equivalence proof, differential ATE results and a pre-filled evidence dossier for the SMD, ECP, DLA SAR and (where applicable) DO-254. The KPI is weeks and reviewer-hours per approved replacement, against GEM's up to 48 weeks and Rochester's 18 months.
- **Fleet obsolescence where-used graph** as the wedge or data moat. It is sold to program offices and DLA, and it also picks the highest-ROI targets for the factory.
- **Become the second source to SRI under AME/GEM, or a DMEA ATSP sub.** A $73.7M program through 2031 shows a budget line already exists, and an AI-throughput vendor is a natural competitor or partner.
- **Licensed-continuation accelerator.** Offer OCMs and Rochester-style continuation houses an AI pipeline to port legacy designs to modern fabs (the RF and embedded-flash parts Rochester says are hard).
- Risks: reliance on DLA and policy acceptance; ITAR; Rochester's licensing moat for commercial parts; liability if an emulated part fails in flight-critical hardware.

## 6. Surprises
- The DoD built an "approval factory for chips" in 1987 (GEM). The model is validated and the economics are documented (NRE is about 20% of a board redesign, and 15:1 savings in one case), but throughput is still up to 48 weeks per part, through one contractor.
- One GEM part solved obsolescence on about 30 systems. The value of a single approval multiplies across platforms, which argues for approval portability.
- The intelligence layer (SiliconExpert/Z2Data) is crowded, but it stops at the part level and does not reach the impact on a platform.
- Two names in the question (Revasum, Sarcos) are not in this market.
