## Short answer
1. **No public, labelled dataset of SAR or PMA rejection reasons exists.**
   - The FAA publishes approvals only, through the DRS PMA list and the PMA supplements. Reasons, issue papers and deficiency letters are not published.
   - DLA publishes neither SAR outcomes nor reasons. All that exists are aggregate counts in old briefing decks, plus a five-reason disapproval taxonomy.
   - Prediction is therefore bottlenecked by *data access*, not by model capability.
2. **The few numbers that exist say that adjudication is mostly an *approval* once a package gets decided.**
   - The RPPOB SARs went 40 approved, 4 rejected, 11 pending, which is about 91% of decided cases.
   - DLA Value Engineering's completeness gate accepted 90% in FY16.
   - The real losses are elsewhere:
     - (a) "pending" loops: in FY16 there were 184 pending against 55 approved, and the pending items held $28.5M of potential savings against $2.35M for the approved ones;
     - (b) "need more data" / "need qualification testing" round trips;
     - (c) packages never filed.
   - So the target for prediction should be **"how many review cycles and which questions will this ESA or ACO ask"**, not a binary accept/reject.
3. **Nobody does reviewer-outcome prediction for aerospace or defence parts today.**
   - The only reviewer-side AI award found is ELEVAN LLC with UTSA: USAF STTR Phase I, $109,997, Feb-May 2024, to "expedite the review and approval of supportability analysis requests" and save "thousands of unbudgeted hours". No Phase II was found.
   - The best analog proof that a reviewer-comment corpus is a moat comes from building permits. PermitFlow claims "12M+ data points on requirements, comments, and approval decisions" and raised $54M. GreenLite claims an average of 0.5 resubmission cycles and raised a $49.5M Series B (sibling c31/c32).
4. **The data has to be generated, not bought.**
   - An Approval Factory that files hundreds of packages a year creates the corpus as exhaust: every ESA/ACO question, every loop and every test the reviewer demanded.
   - Partner corpora that could be added to it:
     - DERs and ODAs, whose findings files they own;
     - GAO and Court of Federal Claims bid protests, which are public and contain agency reasons;
     - FOIA requests;
     - downstream failure data (PQDR, GIDEP, FAA SDR) as a label for "approved but should not have been".

## Evidence: what is known about rejection reasons

### DLA SAR (from 2016 DLA Land and Maritime NDIA deck, via sibling g03; deck URL https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2016/DLA/SourceApprovalRequestsCastingandForging.pdf)

**Government response types**
- Technical data available (the item is competitive).
- **Data missing.**
- **"Need to complete qualification testing": "good on paper, but need to prove will work in application".**
- Rejected, for one of five reasons:
  1. business case, or not in the Government's interest (low demand, years of stock on hand, multiple sources already on the TIR; DLA Aviation snippets);
  2. **incomplete**;
  3. **weapons criticality** (CSI);
  4. application upgraded or discontinued;
  5. **"Can't validate part (no government data to compare)"**.

**Clock**
- VE completeness review: 15 days, with 7 analysts.
- 30 days to cure an incomplete package.
- ESA engineering review: a 90-day nominal SLA.
- Nominal total: 5-6 months with no loops.

**FY16 throughput at DLA L&M**
- 211 received.
- 55 approved ($2.35M in annual savings).
- 184 pending ($28.5M).

**RPPOB, 2014-16**
- 742 bailment requests became 55 SARs: 40 approved, 4 rejected, 11 pending.
- "Typical timeline is 12 to 18 months for full approval."

**Interpretation (opinion)**
- Of the five reject reasons, two are *predictable from public metadata before any engineering is done*:
  - business case, which follows from demand history, stock and the TIR in FLIS/DIBBS;
  - application discontinued, which follows from platform status.
- One is predictable from the AMSC code and the CSI flag: weapons criticality.
- One is fully pre-emptable by a completeness checker: incomplete.
- One is an information-asymmetry reject that the applicant can fix by supplying a baseline: no government data to compare.
- That leaves the *engineering* loops (data missing, need qualification testing) as the true prediction problem.

### Case evidence: GAO B-409950, Rotair Industries (25 Sep 2014), fetched (https://www.gao.gov/products/b-409950)
- **The part:** Black Hawk drag brace overhaul, NSN 1620-01-523-0905, a CSI. Sources were limited to Sikorsky and Overhaul Support Services.
- **Timeline:**
  - Rotair requested SAR data in June 2013 and received it in July 2013.
  - It filed the SAR on 29 Jan 2014 (received 3 Feb).
  - **The Army did not begin its review until 28 Apr 2014**, nearly 3 months of queue.
  - The 5-year IDIQ was awarded to the incumbent on 2 May 2014.
  - After award, on 15 Aug 2014, the Army put the SAR "on hold": it "did not meet maintenance engineering and work requirements". It **requested revisions in six areas** and gave 60 days to respond.
- **Outcome:** GAO denied the protest. Agencies need not delay award for qualification.
- **Lessons (opinion):**
  - The reviewer's objections arrived as a **six-area deficiency list after about 6.5 months**. That list is exactly the kind of label a predictor needs.
  - The cost of a single loop was the loss of a 5-year contract.
- **GAO and COFC protest decisions are the only public, text-rich corpus of agency reasons for refusing source approval or alternate offers.** There are probably hundreds of decisions involving SARs, alternate offers, CSIs and AMSC C/D (estimate, opinion). Nobody has mined them.

### FAA PMA (mostly recalled; faa.gov blocked)
- **Routes** (recalled; 14 CFR 21.303 and FAA Order 8110.42D):
  - identicality by licence agreement (lightest);
  - identicality without licence, where the applicant must show the design is identical to the TC holder's;
  - test and computation (T&C), where the applicant shows compliance with the airworthiness standards. Reverse engineering lives here.
- **Common FAA findings and rework drivers** (recalled, practitioner knowledge; no FAA statistics exist publicly):
  1. criticality and "critical part" determination disputes, which escalate scope (engine life-limited parts, PSEs);
  2. inadequate comparative test or analysis sample size, especially for engine and APU parts;
  3. material and process equivalence (heat treatment, coatings, shot peen) without enough coupon data;
  4. eligibility (installation) evidence;
  5. ICA and design-change tracking against the OEM's revisions;
  6. a production quality system that is not ready (the PMA is a design *and* production approval);
  7. FAA "sequencing" and ACO workload, so queue time rather than findings.
- **New evidence:** MARPA (5 Oct 2026) reports that FAA **AC 33-13, "Sample Size Considerations for Comparative Test and Analysis"** for turbine engine and APU PMA and third-party repair parts was issued on **16 Sep 2026**. https://marpa.pmaparts.org/2026/10/05/faa-sample-size-ac-has-been-released/
  - An FAA AC on sample size suggests that sample-size adequacy has been a recurring point of reviewer disagreement and rework in reverse-engineered engine PMAs (inference).
  - Codified criteria are the raw material for a deterministic pre-check: does the package meet the AC 33-13 sample-size logic for this feature?
- **Eligibility:** MARPA, 2 Dec 2025 (https://marpa.pmaparts.org/2025/12/02/demonstrating-eligibility/).
  - Eligibility must be shown separately from airworthiness compliance, usually through the OEM IPC or ICA.
  - Some OEMs threaten carriers over use of ICAs, so carriers supply technical assistance letters instead.
  - A commenter reports that newer FAA staff read "product" as the carrier's own fleet. That is a *reviewer-variance* source: the same package gets a different outcome from a different reviewer.
- **Organisational change:** MARPA, 14 Oct 2026. FAA AIR-600 is being realigned into product-category branches AIR-660, 670, 680 and 690, with the stated aim of cutting redundant review layers. https://marpa.pmaparts.org/2026/10/14/faa-certification-standards-division-air-600-realigning/
  - Reviewer assignment and policy owners are shifting, so any reviewer model must be re-trained.
- **No public data:** the FAA publishes no PMA rejection or withdrawal statistics, no issue-paper corpus and no review-cycle counts. The DRS lists PMA supplements, so *approvals* are a public positive-only dataset: HEICO adds roughly 400-550 a year (prior run).

### Analogs where outcome prediction has worked (sibling notes; vendor claims)
- **Building permits:**
  - PermitFlow: a 12M+ data-point corpus on requirements, comments and decisions; claims 2.5x faster approvals.
  - GreenLite: 0.5 average resubmission cycles, against the typical 3+.
  - CivCheck: free to cities, with applicants paying a prescreen fee; reviewers take 20-30 min against 1-1.5 h.
  - Failure mode, from East Hampton (Jul 2026): the AI over-flags and invents requirements outside the town checklist.
- **FDA:**
  - eSTAR, the mandatory structured 510(k) template, replaced the refuse-to-accept checklist and cut early rejections (recalled).
  - Q-Sub pre-submissions settle the test plan before testing (recalled).
  - Several AI 510(k) builders exist (Complizen, Cruxi, Sentium Bio). None publishes clearance-rate data.
  - FDA built its own reviewer LLM, Elsa, in June 2025.
- **Lesson (opinion):** structured submission plus pre-agreement of the test plan removed more loops than prediction did. Prediction works where a large comment corpus exists (permits), and that corpus came from volume.

## What a predictor would look at (feature design, opinion)
Each feature group below lists the features, then where the data comes from.

- **Item metadata**
  - Features: AMSC/AMC code, CSI flag, FLIS criticality, demand history, stock on hand, number of sources on the TIR, platform status, ESA identity (WR-ALC, OC-ALC, OO-ALC, NAVAIR, AvMC).
  - Data source: public (PUB LOG/FLIS, DIBBS buy history).
- **Package structure**
  - Features: SAR category (I/II/III), the evidence present for each requirement, test-plan coverage, sample size against AC 33-13-style logic, material and process equivalence data, drawing completeness.
  - Data source: the applicant's own package.
- **Reviewer behaviour**
  - Features: past questions from this ESA or ACO, branch, individual engineer; cycle times; demanded tests.
  - Data source: **only from filing many packages**, or from DER/ODA partners.
- **Ground truth after approval**
  - Features: PQDRs, GIDEP alerts, FAA SDRs and ADs on PMA parts, counterfeit and nonconformance reports.
  - Data source: partly public (SDR, GIDEP for members, AD database).

Output: P(first-pass acceptance), the expected number of cycles, a ranked list of likely reviewer questions with the pre-emptive evidence for each, and a cost and time-to-cure estimate. This feeds the *portfolio choice* of which NSNs to attack. That is arguably worth more than per-package fixes, because the main loss is packages that are never filed or are filed against items DLA will not process.

## Who is doing anything like this
- **ELEVAN LLC / UTSA**: USAF STTR Phase I FA8649-24-P-0130, $109,997, Feb-May 2024, AI/ML plus computer vision to expedite review of requests that lead to source approvals. Reviewer-side; Phase I only. https://www.sbir.gov/awards/210672
- **SAR-prep services (applicant side, manual)**: the 284 sbir.gov "source approval" awards (sibling g03), and ad hoc subcontracts such as Hill AFB SPE4A7-26-T-351W (May 2026, KNOB ASSY, COURSE), which buys SAR package preparation as an engineering service. https://www.cleat.ai/government/contracts/technical-documentation-and-source-approval-j5kw
- **Novineer** (AI reverse engineering, CAD from photos) does knowledge, not approval prediction (sibling g03).
- **HEICO and Wencor**: by volume they hold the largest private store of PMA reviewer interaction, kept as tacit DER knowledge. There is no evidence it has been productised (opinion).
- **Permit and FDA AI firms** (PermitFlow, GreenLite, CivCheck, Complizen, Ketryx) are the analog templates. None works in aero or defence parts.
- **Haystack/Accuris, SERVA, Govini**: data lookups and supply-chain analytics. None predicts approval outcomes.

## What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence, time and compute)
1. **Build the outcome corpus.**
   - Run hundreds to thousands of SAR and PMA packages yourself, the Approval Factory.
   - Log every reviewer question, cycle and test demand in a structured form.
   - This is the only route to labels at scale.
2. **Mine public adjudications.**
   - Extract every GAO and COFC protest involving SARs, alternate offers, CSIs, AMSC B/C/D and qualification requirements into a reasons taxonomy.
   - Add FOIA requests for SAR disposition letters, which are agency records, and FAA issue papers.
   - 10 USC 3243 already requires agencies to tell offerors "promptly" why they failed qualification. Those letters exist and are FOIA-able.
3. **Codify reviewer criteria as machine-checkable rules.**
   - Encode DLA and service SAR guides, AFMC Form 260 first-article requirements, Order 8110.42D, AC 33-13 sample-size logic and CSI handbooks into a requirement-to-evidence schema.
   - Deterministic checks pre-empt the "incomplete" and "data missing" rejects entirely.
4. **Pre-empt the "no government data to compare" reject** by supplying the baseline: scan and CT a population of serviceable fleet parts, using RPPOB bailment, and attach a statistical as-built envelope.
5. **Turn prediction into pre-agreement.**
   - Push a DLA/ESA "test-plan concurrence" step, the analog of the FDA Q-Sub, so the predicted reviewer questions are settled before testing.
   - Legal hook: 10 USC 3243's duty to state written standards and give a "prompt opportunity".
6. **Close the loop with field data.**
   - Use PQDR, SDR, AD and GIDEP outcomes as labels for false accepts.
   - This makes the predictor a *risk* model the reviewer can trust, not just a rejection-avoidance tool.
7. **Give the model to the reviewer** (CivCheck pattern).
   - The same model, run on the ESA side as a triage and disposition-drafting copilot, cuts the 90-day-plus queue.
   - It needs an SBIR Phase II or OTA with one ALC, an IL4/5 ATO, and CUI/ITAR handling.
8. **Track reviewer variance and policy drift** (AIR-600 realignment, new ACs) and retrain, so the model predicts *this* reviewer, not an average one.

## Binding constraints and what it takes to change them
- **Data access** (the main one).
  - No labels are public.
  - To change it: generate them by volume, use FOIA and protest mining, and have DER/ODA partners contribute de-identified findings.
- **Law.**
  - Agencies need not wait for qualification before award (Rotair).
  - To change it: shot-clock or deemed-acceptance language (NDAA), or a pre-qualification ahead of each J&A expiry. The predictor tells you which items to qualify early.
- **Regulator acceptance.**
  - Prediction needs no acceptance on the applicant side. A reviewer-side tool needs an ATO and a pilot.
- **Physics.**
  - The prediction tells you *which* physical tests will be demanded, so you can run them in parallel before filing.
  - It cannot remove them. Example: JMS Southeast took about 14 weeks of FAI testing inside a 2.5-year SAR (sibling g03).
- **Liability.**
  - The predictor is advisory. The DER, ESA or DCA still signs. That is fine for an applicant-side factory.

## AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
- **A. A reviewer-twin inside the Approval Factory.** A per-ESA and per-ACO model of the questions each reviewer will ask. KPI: review cycles per approval, aiming at 1.0. This is the proprietary moat; competitors cannot copy it without the volume.
- **B. "CivCheck for ESAs".** A free or SBIR-funded government triage and disposition copilot trained on the same corpus. Applicants pay for "pre-screened" status.
- **C. A portfolio selector.** Predict P(approval), cycles and time for each NSN from FLIS/DIBBS metadata plus the reject taxonomy. Choose targets where approval lands before the next J&A or solicitation.
- **D. A protest and FOIA reasons corpus.** A one-time build that seeds the taxonomy. Possibly a product for bid-protest counsel and SBIR shops.
- **E. A risk model for false accepts.** Field-failure labels (PQDR, SDR) let the reviewer accept faster with evidence that risk is bounded. This is the bridge to model-credited approvals.
