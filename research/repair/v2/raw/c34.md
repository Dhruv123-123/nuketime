# c34: Pharmaceutical/biotech AI that shortens regulatory proof (in-silico trials, Certara, Unlearn.AI digital twins, FDA MIDD) as an analog for parts qualification

Search budget: 3 WebSearch used. FDA.gov pages returned HTTP 401 to WebFetch, so FDA guidance details marked (recalled) come from memory, not a fetch this session.

## 1. What pharma actually did (evidence)

### Certara / Simcyp (PBPK modelling replacing clinical studies)
- Simcyp product page (https://www.certara.com/software/simcyp-pbpk/), fetched 2026-10-20:
  - "120 FDA-approved novel drugs supported using the Simcyp PBPK Simulator in lieu of clinical studies"
  - "80 FDA-approved novel drugs leveraging PBPK in recent years used Simcyp"
  - "11 global regulatory agencies that use the Simcyp PBPK Simulator" (the regulators license the same tool the sponsors use)
  - Built through a 25-year consortium of 37 pharma companies; 29 population libraries; 100+ compound models; R&D team of about 50 scientists and 35 developers.
- What gets replaced: mostly *ancillary* studies, not the pivotal efficacy trial. Drug-drug-interaction (DDI) studies, organ-impairment studies and paediatric dose-finding are replaced or reduced (Certara MIDD blog: https://www.certara.com/blog/how-the-us-fda-midd-paired-pilot-program-helps-sponsors/).
- Business scale (stockanalysis.com, fetched): revenue $335.6M (2022), $354.3M (2023), $385.2M (2024), $418.8M (2025); TTM $408M; market cap about $1.49B. Growth is 4-9%/yr. This is a tool vendor to a $250B+ R&D market, and the value mostly goes to sponsors, not to the tool vendor.

### Unlearn.AI (digital twins / PROCOVA)
- EMA CHMP final favourable qualification opinion for PROCOVA, announced 28 Sep 2022 (Clinical Trials Arena, https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/ema-qualifies-unlearn-approach/). Scope: Phase II/III trials with continuous outcomes. Control arms reduced by up to 35%.
- Timeline (Unlearn blog, https://www.unlearn.ai/blog/success-is-never-a-straight-line--reflecting-on-our-ema-draft-qualification-opinion): founded 2017. In 2018 it tried synthetic/external control arms and abandoned them because regulators saw them as prone to bias. Mid-2019 patent on a three-step method: build a prognostic model, use it in trial design, adjust for it in analysis. An FDA Critical Path Innovation Meeting set the requirement that bias and type-I error control must hold even with unknown confounders. Late-2020 paper proved PROCOVA unbiased and type-I-error controlled. EMA application April 2021, draft opinion March 2022, final September 2022. That is about 17 months from application to qualification and about 5 years from founding.
- **Key design lesson (evidence, from Unlearn's own account):** "model quality affects efficiency but not validity". The AI never carries the safety claim. It only reduces how much physical evidence is needed, inside a statistical wrapper whose error rate is guaranteed whether the model is good or bad. That is why the regulator accepted it.
- Funding: $12M Series A (8VC, Apr 2020); $50M Series B (Insight Partners, Apr 2022); $50M Series C led by Altimeter (Feb 2024); about $130-135M total (revenuememo, Business Wire). A profile states the FDA concurred that the method does not deviate from current guidance (yespress profile; not independently verified).

### FDA MIDD and credibility frameworks
- MIDD Paired Meeting Program (FY2023-2027, CDER + CBER). It gives selected sponsors an initial and a follow-up meeting with agency modellers; packages are due 47 and 60 days before the meetings. These are *not* milestone (Type A/B) meetings (search result summary of the FDA page plus Leiden thesis). FDA's page says it announced the ICH M15 "General Principles for MIDD" guidance in June 2026 (search snippet of the FDA page; the page itself returned 401).
- An IQ Consortium survey found participants "were able to decrease development time and costs", but no counts were published (Certara blog).
- Credibility frameworks (recalled):
  - ASME V&V 40-2018: risk-informed credibility for medical-device models. Model risk = model influence x decision consequence, and the required verification and validation rigour scales with that risk for a stated **context of use (COU)**.
  - FDA CDRH final guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions", November 2023.
  - FDA draft guidance "Considerations for the Use of AI to Support Regulatory Decision-Making for Drug and Biological Products", January 2025, with a 7-step risk-based credibility framework (define the question, the COU, model risk, credibility plan, execute, document, adequacy).
  - FDA Modernization Act 2.0 (Dec 2022) removed the statutory animal-test mandate.
  - FDA roadmap (April 2025) to phase out animal testing for monoclonal antibodies in favour of NAMs and in-silico methods.
  - FDA VICTRE (2018) was an all-computational imaging trial.
  - FDA's medical-device Medical Device Development Tool (MDDT) program qualifies tools, including computational ones, for reuse across sponsors.
- PMC editorial (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12070237): FDA has accepted in-silico data as primary evidence "in select cases" (MIDD, virtual bioequivalence). It calls for a GCP-like standard for in-silico work, which does not exist yet. This is opinion, and the author has a conflict of interest.

### Other pharma/biotech in-silico players (mostly recalled)
- Simulations Plus (GastroPlus PBPK; about $70-80M revenue, recalled).
- Nova / novainsilico.ai (formerly Novadiscovery; jinko in-silico trial platform; France).

## 2. Mapping to parts qualification (opinion, grounded in the above)

| Pharma mechanism | What it replaced | Parts-qualification analog | Exists today? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Context-of-use + risk-tiered credibility (V&V 40, FDA 2023/2025, ICH M15) | Ad-hoc arguments about whether a model is trusted | A COU/risk-tiered credibility standard for using simulation/ML in a DLA SAR, an FAA PMA (test-and-computation or identicality) or a DER repair | Partial: 14 CFR 25.307 "analysis supported by test", AC 20-107B building block, CMH-17, MIL-HDBK-516C. **No ML-specific credibility guidance for reverse-engineered-part equivalence** (opinion; I found none) |
| Simcyp replaces DDI/organ-impairment *ancillary* studies (120 drugs) | Ancillary clinical studies | Replace ancillary tests in a part package: material equivalency coupons, dimensional/process conformity sub-tests, similarity analysis to an approved part, some environmental tests. Keep first-article and critical fatigue tests | Rarely; packages still lean on physical test plus a DER's judgement |
| PROCOVA: prognostic covariate adjustment, validity independent of model | 35% of control-arm patients | **Prognostic-covariate test-matrix reduction**: use a twin-predicted strength/fatigue life as a covariate so B-basis/A-basis allowables or equivalency need fewer specimens and batches, with the statistical error guarantee kept. Senvol ML (AM allowables with less data, US Army Research Laboratory (ARL) via NCMS/AMMP with Lockheed MFC, EWI) is the closest analog | Embryonic (Senvol). Not qualified by FAA, DLA or NCAMP as a reusable method |
| EMA qualification opinion / FDA MDDT: method qualified once, reused by every sponsor | Re-arguing the method in each dossier | A "qualified method" with DLA Engineering Support Activities, FAA (AC or Issue Paper precedent), or NCAMP/MMPDS shared databases. For example, "CT + microstructure inference → process-equivalence" accepted once, then cited in every SAR | No formal mechanism at DLA. FAA has ACs and NCAMP shared material databases (a good precedent) |
| Regulator licenses the sponsor's tool (11 agencies use Simcyp) | Reviewer re-derivation | A reviewer-side copilot or a shared verification environment for DLA ESAs (AFLCMC, NAVSUP, DLA Land and Maritime) and FAA ACOs/DERs. It replays the package's evidence chain and flags gaps, which reduces reviewer hours | Not seen |
| MIDD paired meetings (pre-submission modelling dialogue) | Late rejection | Structured pre-SAR model/plan agreement with the ESA (a "test-plan concurrence" step) | Informal at best (opinion) |

### Why the parts case is *stronger* than pharma (opinion)
- Metal and composite structural behaviour (static strength, much of fatigue, thermal) is far more physically modellable than human biology. The physics clock is the main reason pharma cannot simulate efficacy; for parts it binds mainly on fatigue, damage tolerance, novel processes and the first article.
- Yet the parts regulators lack the formal COU-credibility vocabulary that FDA, EMA and ICH built between 2018 and 2026. The opportunity is to import that vocabulary.

### Where the analog breaks
- Pharma sponsors own their molecule's data. A reverse-engineering house does *not* have the OEM design data. The hard uncertainty is **what the OEM part actually is** (alloy lot practice, heat-treat, residual stress, coatings, undisclosed process specifications), not how a known design behaves. So the verification AI that matters most is *inverse*: inferring process and design intent from the physical part (CT, EBSD, hardness maps, residual-stress measurements, chemistry). It then has to bound that uncertainty in a way that can be credited in a PROCOVA-style framework.
- Liability: FDA's reviewer accepts a drug label that the sponsor is liable for. In parts, the DER, the PMA holder or the DLA source carries liability. A credibility framework must be something a DER will sign.
- Market size: Certara at about $419M revenue and about $1.5B market cap shows that *selling* verification tools to regulated sponsors caps out modestly. Most of the value accrues to whoever holds the approvals. That supports the vertically integrated Approval Factory using its own verification stack, rather than a pure tool vendor.

## 3. What would need to be done (nobody does it today)
1. Write and publish a **V&V-40-style context-of-use credibility standard for reverse-engineered replacement parts** (SAR/PMA/DER repairs), and push it through ASTM F42/F07, SAE G-33/AMS, or as an FAA AC / DLA ESA memo. Regulators adopt a framework faster when industry brings it written.
2. Build a **PROCOVA-for-specimens** method: covariate-adjusted allowables and equivalency testing with a type-I error guarantee independent of model quality. Then seek a one-time method acceptance (NCAMP/CMH-17 for composites, MMPDS for metals, or AFLCMC/NAVAIR for military), modelled on the EMA qualification. Timeline benchmark: about 17 months from application at EMA.
3. Get a **qualified inverse-characterisation method**: part → process/material equivalence (CT + microstructure + chemistry + residual stress → equivalence claim with bounded uncertainty), accepted once and cited thereafter.
4. **Ancillary-test substitution library**: as Simcyp built 29 population libraries and 100+ compound models over 25 years with 37 pharma firms, build validated material/process "population libraries" (alloy x process x heat-treat) from historical DLA/AF test reports, NCAMP data and in-house testing. The libraries are the moat, the way Simcyp's consortium data is.
5. A **reviewer-side tool** given or licensed to DLA ESAs and FAA ACOs (the Simcyp-licensed-by-regulators pattern) that replays the evidence chain and shrinks reviewer hours. The reviewer queue is the throughput limit for the Approval Factory.
6. A **pre-submission "paired meeting"** analog: structured test-plan concurrence with the ESA before spending test money.
7. Use **accelerated test design** for the remaining physics clocks: Bayesian sequential testing, staircase fatigue with model priors, stop-early rules. These are borrowed from adaptive clinical-trial designs, which FDA accepts.

## 4. AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
- Approval Factory internal stack = "Certara + Unlearn for parts": a COU-risk-tiered credibility engine, covariate-adjusted test reduction, an inverse-characterisation twin, and auto-generated evidence dossiers. KPI: physical specimens and reviewer hours per approval.
- A method-qualification business: qualify statistical/ML methods with DLA/FAA/NCAMP once and then use them across hundreds of packages. This is a regulatory moat analogous to PROCOVA's EMA opinion.
- A reviewer copilot for DLA ESAs, FAA ACOs and DERs (the regulator-licensed-tool pattern).
- Shared material/process population libraries, funded through a consortium (Simcyp model) or via AFRL/RSO/DLA R&D SBIRs.

## Surprises
- Simcyp's 120 novel drugs is a large count of regulatory proof replaced by models. It replaced ancillary studies, not pivotal ones. That is the template: attack ancillary tests first.
- Regulators themselves license the sponsor tool (11 agencies). The reviewer side is a buyer.
- The pure verification-tool vendor (Certara) is only about a $1.5B market cap despite near-monopoly status. This argues for owning approvals rather than selling tools.
- Unlearn's acceptance came from making validity *independent of the AI*. It is a statistical-guarantee wrapper rather than a "trust our model" claim, and it is directly portable to specimen testing.
