## 1. Has anyone proposed an "ANDA for parts"?

**Evidence: no explicit proposal found.** I found no bill, FAA petition, GAO or DoD IG recommendation, or think-tank paper that proposes a standardized, abbreviated equivalence pathway for replacement parts, with a published evidence standard, a deadline for the regulator to decide, and a litigation safe harbor. Searching was not possible this run, so this is weak evidence of absence.

There are, however, partial versions in six regimes. Each has some ANDA features but none has all of them.

| Regime | ANDA-like feature | What is missing compared with ANDA |
|---|---|---|
| FAA PMA, 14 CFR 21 subpart K (fetched LII 21.303; current numbering 21.301-21.320 after the 2009 rewrite, recalled) | Two routes. **Identicality**: the design is identical to a TC'd design, either through a license or by showing identicality. **Test and computation (T&C)**: test reports and computations that show the design meets airworthiness requirements, plus a statement certifying compliance. Identicality without a license is the closest thing to an ANDA in any industry. | No published equivalence acceptance criteria per part class. No decision clock. EASA accepts US PMA only for non-critical parts under the BASA TIP (recalled). |
| DoD qualification, 10 USC 3243 (fetched) | The agency must justify any qualification requirement, specify standards that are "as unrestrictive as possible", estimate offeror test costs, give a prompt chance to demonstrate (for a fee), state specific reasons for any rejection, and revalidate requirements every 7 years. Subsection (e) requires an expedited qualification process for **advanced manufacturing** sources in wartime or when the Civil Reserve Manufacturing Network is activated. | No decision deadline. The Hatch-Waxman template (fetched, Wikipedia). The 1984 act was a bargain:
- Brands got patent-term restoration and 5-year data exclusivity.
- Generics got ANDA, where FDA may ask only about manufacturing, quality and bioequivalence, plus the research safe harbor, 35 USC 271(e)(1).
- It also created the Orange Book patent listing, Paragraph IV certification, and 180-day exclusivity for the first filer.
- Outcome: generics went from 13% of prescriptions in 1984 to 84% in 2012.
- **The lesson: the law worked because it gave the incumbents something** and because it **fixed the evidence standard by statute**, so the reviewer could not ask for more.

## 2. What a "Part Equivalence Act" would need, and the vehicles

### Seven design elements (opinion)
1. **Reference item.** Something like the Orange Book: a list of approved reference parts (NSN or TC part number) with their critical characteristics, published by DLA or the FAA.
2. **Statutory evidence standard.** Equivalence is shown by (a) dimensional and material identity within tolerance bands taken from N reference samples (statistical equivalence, e.g. a TOST-style test on key properties, like the 80-125% bioequivalence window), plus (b) functional or performance tests chosen by criticality class. A consensus body (SAE G-committee, ASTM or MMPDS-style) writes the class-specific test matrices, and the regulator incorporates them by reference.
3. **Decision clock.** 90-180 days, then deemed accepted unless rejected with specific reasons. 10 USC 3243 already requires specific reasons but has no clock.
4. **Accredited third-party reviewers.** Like ODA/DER for the FAA. For DoD, let accredited private "equivalence review organizations" sign for the ESA, using the model 10 USC 3243(b)(5) already allows (testing by a contractor not expected to benefit).
5. **Reverse-engineering safe harbor** like 271(e)(1). It would cover measuring, scanning and testing government-owned reference hardware and producing the TDP. It would also give a clear rule against trade-secret and design-patent claims for work on government-owned sustainment items, with a Paragraph-IV-style challenge path for asserted data rights.
6. **Incumbent sweetener** (the Hatch-Waxman bargain). Options: (a) OEMs keep N years of exclusivity on new platforms (e.g. 7-10 years after IOC) before equivalence filings open; (b) a royalty or "data access fee" schedule for OEM data that helps an applicant (like compulsory licensing); (c) the OEM gets first right to match price.
7. **Liability allocation.** For DoD, extend Public Law 85-804 indemnification or the government-contractor defense (Boyle, 1988) to equivalence-approved parts. For FAA, the PMA holder stays liable, as today. An insurance market (see g08) would price it.

### DoD route: the easiest of the three
- **NDAA amendment to 10 USC 3243.** Add a new subsection, "(f) Abbreviated qualification for replacement items", containing elements 2-5. The NDAA passes every year and has for 64 or more consecutive years. The vehicles are the FY2028 NDAA (marked up spring 2027) or a FY2027 floor amendment.
- Precedent language already in the section: 3243(e) on expediting advanced-manufacturing qualification, and 3243(d) on paying small business test costs. A new section could expand (d) to pay for **any** second-source qualification when there is one source.
- **Without new law.** The USD(A&S) can tell DLA and the services to publish class-wide equivalence criteria through DoDM 4140.01 and the DLA SAR guide. A DFARS case could add a "deemed qualified" clock to SAR handling. Either is faster (6-18 months) but can be reversed.
- **What blocks it:** each ESA's engineering authority (airworthiness under MIL-HDBK-516, and service airworthiness authorities such as NAVAIR 13034.1 and AFI 62-601, recalled) does not want to delegate. The law would need to say that a part accepted under the standard counts as airworthiness-acceptable unless the ESA rejects it with specific reasons.

### FAA route
1. **Policy route, no rulemaking (fastest, about 1-2 years).** T&C under 21.303 already allows any showing of compliance. The FAA can issue an Advisory Circular or Policy Statement that sets out an "equivalence evidence package" by part criticality class. It would follow the pattern of the AM and computational-model policy work (CM4QC; see sibling f06). Then delegate PMA findings to PMA ODAs (HEICO and others hold ODAs, recalled).
2. **Part 21 rulemaking (3-7 years: ARAC, then NPRM, then final rule).** Add a defined "equivalence" basis to subpart K with published criteria. The last big subpart K rewrite was the 2009 Part 21 production rule (recalled).
3. **Congressional directive.** The FAA Reauthorization Act of 2024 (P.L. 118-63, signed 16 May 2024) runs to 30 Sept 2028. The next reauthorization (2027-28) is the vehicle. It could direct the FAA to issue an equivalence standard within 18 months, set a PMA review clock, and require that FAA project sequencing not hold PMA work behind TC work.
4. **International.** EASA does not have PMA. Bilateral acceptance of critical PMA parts would need a BASA/TIP revision.

### What changes the binding constraints
- **Physics.** The statutory standard can *cap* tests, for example: fatigue only for criticality class 1, coupon-level otherwise. Accepted modeling (ICME with UQ) can substitute for part of the test matrix.
- **Law.** Elements 5 and 6.
- **Regulator acceptance.** Deemed approval plus accredited reviewers.
- **Liability.** Element 7.
- **Data access.** Government-owned reference hardware plus the safe harbor.

## 3. Who would lobby for and against (opinion, informed by recalled positions)

**For:**
- MARPA (the PMA trade association; fetched site: "uniform standards", "without imposing needless resource burdens").
- ASA (Aviation Suppliers Association), now focused on Part 145 and EASA issues (fetched: Aug 2026 items).
- Airlines: A4A, and IATA, which has publicly campaigned against OEM aftermarket restrictions (recalled).
- AEA.
- DLA and the Air Force Sustainment Center / Rapid Sustainment Office, as users.
- Senators Warren (D) and Sheehy (R). Bipartisan, with a "warfighter readiness" frame.
- Taxpayer and oversight groups: POGO, Taxpayers for Common Sense, U.S. PIRG and Repair.org.
- DoD IG reports on TransDigm excess spare-part pricing (2019 and 2021; recalled) are the political ammunition.

**Against:**
- AIA and NDIA. They opposed the FY2026 Senate repair language and backed "data-as-a-service" instead (recalled).
- GAMA, for civil OEMs.
- Engine and system OEMs whose margins depend on the aftermarket: GE Aerospace, RTX/Pratt, Honeywell, Safran, Boeing, Lockheed.
- Sole-source spare-part houses, especially **TransDigm**. Its proprietary-part model is directly threatened.
- Possibly **HEICO**. The pathway would lower its barrier to entry (it adds about 400-550 PMAs a year), but it could also lower its own cost per PMA. Ambiguous.
- Safety framing from some pilot unions (ALPA) and from FAA AIR certification staff, who are short of reviewers. The 2023 AOG Technics forged-parts scandal (sibling notes) will be cited against anything that looks like "looser parts approval".

**Likely compromise:** a DoD-only pilot for legacy, out-of-production platforms (C-130H, KC-135, B-52 and similar), limited to non-critical (class 2-3) parts, with deemed approval and paid testing. This gives the incumbents their protection (new platforms are excluded) and still opens the orphan-part market.

## 4. Implications for the Approval Factory (opinion)
- The evidence standard is the product. Whoever drafts the class-by-class equivalence evidence spec (the "eCTD for parts", see sibling notes) and has the largest corpus of side-by-side approvals becomes the reference implementation that the regulator writes into rule. That is the Hatch-Waxman role Teva and the generic association played. It was not just lobbying; they supplied the BE methodology.
- **AI plays on the verification side:**
  1. A dossier compiler that produces equivalence packages in a standard schema.
  2. Reviewer-side tooling sold to DLA ESAs and FAA ODAs (completeness and consistency checking, a precedent engine).
  3. Statistical-equivalence engines (TOST on CT, CMM and material data against reference lots).
  4. A "critical characteristics" extractor: given a part, its function and its failure history, propose the characteristics to verify (the EPRI NP-5652 approach, automated).
  5. A data-rights challenge assistant that tests OEM data-rights legends against 10 USC 3771 and DFARS 7013 records.
  6. Becoming an accredited third-party equivalence reviewer under a future 3243(f). That would be a permission asset in its own right.
- **Sequencing:**
  - 2027: DLA pilot via SAR guide policy, plus a USD(A&S) memo.
  - FY2028 NDAA: codify the 3243(f) amendment.
  - FAA: AC/policy statement on a T&C equivalence package; the 2028 FAA reauthorization sets a clock.
