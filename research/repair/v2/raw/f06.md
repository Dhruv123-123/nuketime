# f06: ICME and microstructure digital twins to predict properties: maturity, cases where they replaced tests (GE, Ford, QuesTek), what is missing

Researcher notes. Tags: [E] = evidence from a fetched source; [R] = recalled from memory, not re-verified this session; [O] = opinion or inference.

## 1. Bottom line

- [E] **Today ICME de-risks and shrinks qualification testing. It has not replaced the statistical test basis.** This comes from the regulators' own strategy document. The FAA/NASA CM4QC strategy (NASA/TM-20260001729, DOT/FAA/TC-26/10, March 2026) says "Current approaches for Q&C simply do not have procedures for making use of CM results." It calls "the naive expectation ... that CM predictions may directly replace testing data" and recommends that CM "augment rather than replace" testing. On the future split between test data and CM-informed qualification it says: "Balance between Test Data and CM-informed Q&C is Undetermined."
- [E] The best-documented "replaced tests" case is **QuesTek Ferrium M54 (2014)**. AIM analysis of **3 production heats** predicted the MMPDS A/B-basis design minima, which are normally derived from **10 heats**. That prediction gave the Navy T-45 program the confidence to fund hook-shank rig testing before MMPDS approval. The alloy went from clean-sheet design to flight qualification in under 6 years, against the usual 15-20. But QuesTek's own 2023 ICAF paper says: "MMPDS A- or B-basis testing allowables are still required to be met before a component can meet certification requirements to fly ... despite the need to ultimately complete the full testing dataset." The benefit was **parallelization and de-risking, not deletion** of tests.
- [O] For an Approval Factory this means ICME is not yet a way to skip tests. It **is** a lever on the KPI. It can (a) pick the 10-20% of tests that carry the information, (b) run the remaining work in parallel because pass/fail risk is known early, and (c) give the reviewer a quantified-equivalence argument. The model-friendly regulatory domains named in section 4 are where analysis can carry more of the burden.

## 2. Maturity: the FAA/NASA view (primary source)

Source: Glaessgen (NASA) and Gorelik (FAA Chief Scientist, Fatigue and Damage Tolerance), "CM4QC" talk at the NIST In-Situ Metrology for Metal AM workshop, Apr 28 - May 1, 2026. https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20260003240/downloads/NIST%20041826.pdf . Program page: https://www.nist.gov/programs-projects/cm4qc . Strategy doc: NASA/TM-20260001729 (https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20260001729).

- [E] Steering group:
  - Government: NASA, FAA, NIST, Sandia, AFRL, Army Aviation, NAVAIR, ORNL, SwRI
  - Industry: BAE, Boeing, Lockheed, RTX/P&W, GE Aviation, Spirit, Honeywell, Northrop, Textron
  - Academia: UTSA, CMU, Vanderbilt, Penn State, Northwestern
  - The group is "not to establish Federal policy" and gives "non-consensus" advice.
- [E] It uses a five-level CM maturity scale, adapted from Cowles, Backman and Dutton (2012), "V&V of ICME methods and models for aerospace applications," IMMI 1:3-18. Level 5 is "Model predictions are directly applicable to Q&C." Levels 3-4 are V&V'd for limited or industry cases, but results mainly "inform" qualification. [O] The talk implies most AM process-structure-property chains sit at levels 2-3 today.
- [E] Gaps it lists:
  - Prediction of process-to-microstructure, microstructure-to-property and property-to-performance. Performance covers fatigue, damage tolerance, surface finish and defects, complex load histories, environment.
  - Residual stress and distortion.
  - Build reproducibility.
  - NDE.
  - Quantitative uncertainty and reliability.
  - Regulator acceptance ("incremental with rigorous validation testing; requires trust in models and clear business cases").
  - Usability: laptop or workstation; non-proprietary I/O; public validation data such as AM Bench.
  - Material property data availability.
  - UQ integration.
  - **"Resources to conduct side-by-side CM-related and conventional Q&C activities, until CM tools are widely accepted."**
  - Moving national-lab codes into maintained, version-controlled commercial platforms.
  - Workforce.
- [E] Timeline:
  - Near-term (1-5 yr): use existing commercial software to design experiments and guide testing.
  - Mid-term (5-10 yr): partnerships that bring new CM into commercial tools and qualification practice.
  - Long-term (>10 yr): new methods, GPUs, much cheaper compute.
  - The next concrete step is a **NIST-led "pathfinder exercise" (what is possible today, biggest gaps)**.
- [E] Validation-metric requirements: metrics must be quantitative and objective, and kept separate from the acceptance criteria. They must account for model and experimental uncertainty, give a statistical confidence level tied to the amount of data, and assess predictive capability across an intended prediction region.
- [E] The FAA's test-article rule is quoted: "The test article should be full-scale (component or sub-component) and represent that portion of the production aircraft requiring test." [O] This **specimen-to-part similitude** problem is where microstructure twins could add the most. The deck lists "Better understanding of the similitude (or lack thereof) between test specimens vs. parts" as a key driver.

Analogy outside aero:
- [R] FDA final guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" (Nov 2023), built on ASME V&V 40-2018.
- [R] NASA-STD-7009 model credibility.
- [O] Medical devices have a working, regulator-adopted credibility framework for in-silico evidence. Aviation materials does not yet. CM4QC is trying to build one.

## 4. Where analysis is already accepted (model-friendly regulatory domains)

- [E] Part 25 damage tolerance, AC 25.571-1D: "analysis supported by test evidence."
- [E] Part 33 damage tolerance, AC 33.70-1: analysis accepted but validated against tests; probabilistic DT is required.
- [E] AC 20-146 seats; AC 25.562-1B "rational analysis."
- [E] **MMPDS has no A/B/S-basis for fatigue minimum life.** Its rule is that "methods used to define lower bounds of life must be accepted by the appropriate regulatory agency and may be negotiated by type certificate applicants and the regulators" (QuesTek ICAF 2023 quoting MMPDS). [O] This negotiable space is exactly where a model-based minimum-life argument can enter.
- [R] PMA "test and computation" route (FAA Order 8110.42 series): the applicant shows design equivalence. For reverse-engineered parts this often means material and process equivalence shown by testing. Engineering judgment and DER-approved analysis are allowed. DLA SARs for Critical Safety Items need first-article testing or qualification testing per the ESA (engineering support activity).

## 5. Players

  - ICMD cloud software (ICMD 2.0 launched Sept 14 2026) plus Materials-by-Design services.
  - Niobium suite with CBMM (Aug 2026); Daido Steel customer; Fast Company 2025.
  - Claims users save 20-80% of time per phase. No disclosed VC; SBIR- and contract-funded [E site, R funding].
  - Closest existing player to "ICME for qualification." Its focus is new alloys, not reverse-engineering legacy parts.
- **SwRI (DARWIN)**: FAA-accepted probabilistic rotor DT software. Nonprofit.
- **Senvol** (NYC): Senvol ML for AM data-driven qualification; America Makes, Navy, NIST, FAA work. Small, contract-funded [R].
- **Dyndrite** (Seattle): AM build software; leads AIM-4AM $2M (2026) [E].
- **Thermo-Calc / CompuTherm / Ansys-Synopsys Granta and Additive / Hexagon (Simufact, MSC) / Dassault (BIOVIA, Simulia)**: tool vendors that provide the ICME building blocks. They do not own approvals.
- **Citrine Informatics** (materials data / ML platform, about $60-80M raised [R]), **Intellegens**, **NobleAI**, **Phaseshift**: AI for materials development, not certification evidence.
- **AI materials-discovery labs**: Periodic Labs (2025, very large seed [R]), Lila Sciences [R], CuspAI, Orbital Materials, Radical AI. Discovery side; none target allowables, PMA or DLA approvals [O].
- **In-situ AM monitoring**: Sigma Additive (PrintRite3D), EOS/Nikon SLM tools, Phase3D, Additive Assurance [R]. These collect per-build process data that a twin would need.
- **NIST AM Bench; AFRL; ORNL MDF; Sandia**: public validation data and national-lab codes such as ExaAM / ExaCA / AdditiveFOAM.
- **OEM in-house**: GE Aerospace, P&W, Boeing, Lockheed, Honeywell all run ICME internally. [O] It strengthens their own certification, not third-party repairs.

[O] Threat to an Approval Factory:
- No one runs ICME as an engine for **third-party approvals** (PMA/DLA SAR/DER repair) on **legacy, already-certified alloys**.
- QuesTek is adjacent, since it could pivot to selling equivalence arguments.
- Senvol and Dyndrite are adjacent on AM allowables.
- The OEMs have the capability but no incentive to use it for third parties.

## 6. What is missing / what would need to be done (assume abundant compute and intelligence)

1. **Inverse ICME / forensic process inference.** From one or a few legacy parts, using CT, EBSD, SEM, chemistry, hardness and residual-stress maps, infer the original processing history: forging route, heat treatment, shot peen, plating. Then predict the property distribution. Nobody productizes this. It is the step that turns reverse engineering from guess-plus-test into measure-plus-predict-plus-confirm. [O]
2. **An equivalence calculus.** A statistically rigorous, reviewer-legible method to show that a substitute part's predicted property distribution (with UQ) is at least as good as the OEM part's, without OEM data. It would build on DARPA AIM, extended to fatigue as QuesTek began in 2023. It needs a written standard: an SAE AMS or ASTM practice, or an FAA policy statement or AC. CM4QC flags that FAA has no procedure to use CM results.
3. **Side-by-side evidence ledger.** CM4QC explicitly names "resources to conduct side-by-side CM and conventional Q&C" as a gap. A company that runs every one of its approvals both ways builds the validation dataset regulators need. Each package then becomes a calibration point. That is a data-moat flywheel. [O]
4. **Credibility framework adopted by the FAA, DLA and the ESAs (engineering support activities).** This would be the aviation equivalent of ASME V&V 40 / FDA 2023 guidance. The maturity levels already exist (Cowles 2012, CM4QC section 8). They need a regulator-accepted mapping from maturity level to allowed test reduction. To change this: fund and supply the NIST pathfinder, co-author the best-practice guide, and get a DER/ODA issue paper accepted on one PMA.
5. **Physics clocks.** HCF to 1e7 cycles at 20-50 Hz takes days per coupon. Creep and stress-rupture take 1,000-10,000 hours. Corrosion fatigue is slow too. Options:
   - Ultrasonic (20 kHz) gigacycle fatigue (exists [R]).
   - Massively parallel micro-specimen testing combined with microstructure twins to bridge specimen-to-part similitude.
   - Model-driven test selection so only the informative tests run.
   - [O] Without changing the law, the clock binds on the confirmation tests, not the exploratory ones.
6. **Open validation data for legacy alloys.** AM Bench covers AM. Nothing equivalent exists for 1960s-80s forged and cast aerospace alloys (4340, 300M, 7075-T73, 2024, IN718 forgings, cast A356/A357) at the microstructure-to-fatigue level. Building it is a moat. [O]
7. **Per-serial-number digital twins at production.** Witness coupons plus in-process data feed the twin, which issues a predicted-property certificate for each lot or part. Acceptance would shift from lot sampling to model-plus-sensor. [O]
8. **Usability and code transition.** National-lab codes need to become maintained, version-controlled, auditable tools whose outputs can go into a certification package. Today that is a gap (CM4QC). An AI-native firm can own this layer internally.

## 7. AI plays on the verification side (opinion)

- **"Allowables engine" inside the Approval Factory.** Inverse-ICME characterization of the legacy part, then predicted property distribution with UQ, then a minimal confirmatory test plan, then an auto-drafted equivalence substantiation for DER/DLA review. KPI: tests per approval and calendar days per approval.
- **Qualification-evidence-as-a-service for AM and legacy alloy suppliers.** This is a QuesTek/Senvol shape but outcome-priced per approved allowable. Risk: it is a services business unless tied to owning the approvals.
- **Regulator-side credibility tooling.** Software that scores a submitted computational argument against the CM4QC maturity scale and V&V metrics. It could be sold to FAA, DLA, NAVAIR ESA and ODAs to raise reviewer throughput. Lower moat, but it shapes the standard.
- **Validation data network.** Run both CM and conventional qualification on every package and accumulate the side-by-side corpus. After N packages, petition for a test-reduction factor. The accumulated corpus is the permission asset.
