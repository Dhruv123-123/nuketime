### EASA treatment of FAA PMA, TIP Rev 7 §3.3.4 (verbatim structure) [E]

EASA "shall directly accept all FAA PMA approvals, without further showing" in these cases:
- 3.3.4.1: the part is not a critical component and was approved (a) by identicality without a licence, or (b) by test and computation.
- 3.3.4.2: the part was made under a licence from the TC or STC holder, and EASA has validated that TC or STC.
- 3.3.4.3: the part is a critical component and either (a) EASA has validated the FAA STC, or (b)/(c) it was approved by identicality or test and computation and EASA has issued an equivalent STC.

Supporting provisions:
- Critical component (definition §1.13): a part identified as critical by the DAH or the SoD authority. Typically this means parts with a replacement time, inspection interval or related procedure in the ALS or CMR.
- §3.5.x(c): applications for critical-component PMA parts not made under licence "shall be classified as Non-Basic". That means EASA technical involvement in the validation.
- Application content for critical PMA (item (s)): the FAA PMA with all supplements and the means of approval, and a description of how part users are told of changes and of in-service issues.
- §5.7: rules for when a critical PMA is surrendered or terminated after EASA has issued an STC.
- §7.11: the 8130-3 must carry a Block 12 statement, one of "This PMA part is not a critical component.", "Produced under licensing agreement from the holder of [TC/STC]", or "Produced by the holder of the EASA STC number XXXXX."
- EASA FAQ 19218: forms with none of these statements "should be refused and the parts rejected." EASA also says it "has no authority over US manufacturers exporting to the EU". Enforcement runs MRO to NAA to FAA. EASA considers the burden "proportionate".
- Repairs, §3.3.5: FAA-approved repair data is broadly accepted by EASA except for alterations on critical components. The FAA accepts EASA Subpart M repair data if the FAA has validated the product and the repair is not in an AD area.

### CAAC, FAA-CAAC IPA 2017 (in force about 17 Oct 2017; replaced the 1995 SIP) [E MARPA, E FAQ]

- Accepted:
  - PMA for products whose SoD is the US (Boeing).
  - PMA for products with a non-US SoD if the basis is an STC (validated in China) or identicality with a licence.
  - PMA by test and computation on products with a non-US SoD (Airbus, Rolls-Royce) only where the failure condition is "no safety effect" or "minor".
- Friction:
  - Failure-condition classifications are not public. Typically only the PMA holder and the FAA office know them.
  - Older PMAs approved before the relevant ACs may have no documented failure condition, which makes them effectively unsellable into China.
  - Chinese buyers ask for written "minor" statements or refuse PMA on non-US-SoD aircraft outright.
- Validation paths: Acceptance, Streamlined Validation (35 working days, for TC/STC only, when none of the 15 Safety Elements applies), and Limited or Full Technical Validation. The VA can dispute how the CA classified a project. An item that needs a TSO (VDA/LODA) requires a matching CTSO.
- [O] The C919 and ARJ21 (Chinese SoD, no FAA TC for the C919) offer a Western approval house no bilateral path at all.

### Canada [E]

- The PDA is test-and-computation only and Canadian-applicant only. A US house's identicality-based PMA cannot be "converted" to a PDA. It enters Canada as an FAA PMA under the IPA.
- FAA STCs on grandfathered US Part 23/27 TCs are automatically accepted in Canada (IPA 3.3.3). Parts installed under them still need an 8130-3 or Statement of Conformity (CASA 2024-02).

### Brazil and the UK

- Brazil (ANAC): CPAA is the PMA-equivalent and COP is the production approval. Export depends on the destination's BASA [E SAS].
- UK CAA: since Brexit there is a separate US-UK TIP, and UK CAA acceptance of FAA PMA broadly mirrors EASA's [R].

## 3. Where a global approval house hits friction (ranked) [O on ranking]

1. **Approvals do not flow back to the US.** The FAA does not recognize non-OEM part approvals from any partner: EASA EPA (TIP 2.2.4.3(b) Reserved), CAAC PMA and, per the SAS note, the TCCA PDA for critical parts. The US is the only authority whose PMA travels, so a global house must anchor its approvals at the FAA and run US-located (or FAA-scrutinized) production. Being "born in Europe" is a handicap.
2. **Critical parts.** In the EU, critical parts need a separate EASA STC, classified Non-Basic, with technical review. For Airbus, ATR and Rolls-Royce in China, test-and-computation PMA is limited to no-safety-effect or minor failure conditions. Critical parts are also the highest-value PMAs (engine LLPs, hot section), so the value and the friction sit on the same parts.
3. **Criticality and failure-condition determination is opaque, and is done by humans each time.** The "critical" status depends on OEM ALS and CMR content that the PMA applicant may not hold. The FAA failure-condition classification is not public, so distributors and foreign MROs cannot verify eligibility.
4. **Paperwork failure at the installer.** A missing Block 12 phrase legally forces an EU MRO to reject a part that is technically fine. Enforcement is MRO to NAA to FAA, with no direct EASA authority.
5. **Methods do not translate.** TCCA does not accept identicality and requires design control. EASA requires a DOA holder. A dossier built for an FAA identicality PMA needs extra physical testing for Canada, and an EASA-approved organization for Europe.
6. **Military first stage.** DLA SAR approvals do not travel through civil BASAs at all. FMS, ITAR and EAR govern the export of C-130 and KC-135 parts [R]. Civil derivatives (L-100/LM-100J, the KC-135's civil cousins) need separate PMA work [O].
7. **Non-regulatory gates that carry the same weight.** Lessor return conditions, OEM warranty language and engine-shop policy for PMA on leased aircraft and engines matter as much as the rules. Airbus-fleet customers in the EU, Middle East and China are more conservative [R/O].

## 4. Who does this today

- **HEICO.** It has thousands of FAA PMAs and relies on TIP direct acceptance for EU sales. Lufthansa Technik has held 20% of HEICO Aerospace since 1997, which gives HEICO EU airline demand and engineering input [R]. HEICO bought Wencor in 2023 for about $2.05B [R].
- **Lufthansa Technik.** It develops its own parts and repairs under an EASA DOA, and uses them through HEICO and in house [R].
- Other PMA houses: Chromalloy, Jet Parts Engineering / Vance Street-backed, PMA of Aviation Parts, and others [R].
- **Consultancies** that handle bilateral validations by hand: SAS Sofia (source above), plus many ex-FAA and ex-EASA DER/CVE firms [E/R]. Trade associations: MARPA and ASA lobby on the bilateral rules [E].
- No company found that offers machine-readable bilateral eligibility, automated criticality determination, or a single dossier built for several authorities. Searching was limited (3 searches), so absence is not proven [O].

## 5. What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence and compute)

1. **A bilateral compiler.** Encode the TIPs and IPAs (FAA-EASA Rev 7/7.1, FAA-TCCA rev 3, FAA-CAAC 2017, US-UK, ANAC, CASA, JCAB) as executable rules. Input: part, design basis, criticality, failure condition, product SoD, target registry. Output: path, Block 12 text, gaps. This is pure knowledge work, and the binding constraint is regulator acceptance of the output [O].
2. **A one-campaign, multi-authority dossier.** Plan the physical test campaign once so it satisfies FAA identicality or test-and-computation, TCCA test-and-computation-only, EASA STC Non-Basic review, and CAAC minor-failure-condition evidence. The physics clock is paid once, not three or four times.
3. **Automated criticality and failure-condition classification.** Derive it from ALS, CMR, ICA, SB and AD corpora plus a 25.1309-style FHA. Publish a signed classification record per part, which fixes the China opacity problem. Data access to OEM manuals is the binding constraint.
4. **Installer-side verification.** Read the 8130-3, EASA Form 1 or TCCA Form One at receiving inspection, check eligibility against tail and engine serial number and the TIP condition, and flag reject or accept. This gives the MRO a defensible record and removes rejections that should never happen.
5. **A continued-airworthiness feed.** TIP item (s)(3)(4) and §5.7 require the PMA holder to tell users of changes and in-service issues. That can be automated monitoring across operators' reliability data, which is evidence that raises regulator trust.
6. **Policy work to change the law.** It would take three things:
   - fill TIP 2.2.4.3(b), so the FAA recognizes EASA EPA and TCCA PDA for non-critical parts, through the Bilateral Oversight Board (BOB decisions are the change mechanism, e.g. Decision 014 in Jan 2026)
   - get TCCA to accept identicality backed by high-fidelity evidence (scan, material and process equivalence)
   - get the FAA and CAAC to publish failure conditions.

   MARPA has lobbied these one at a time. An AI-generated evidence base showing equivalent safety outcomes is the lever [O].
