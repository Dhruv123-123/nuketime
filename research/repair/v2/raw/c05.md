# c05: Hardware test data and test automation platforms (Nominal, Sift, Epsilon3, Stell, NI, Dewesoft): do any target certification/qualification evidence?

Date: 2026-10-20. Search budget: 3 WebSearch used, the rest WebFetch. Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not verified this session.

## Short answer
No. None of them produces regulator-accepted qualification or certification evidence for a replacement part. Each covers one layer: the test data lake (Nominal, Sift, NI SystemLink), the procedure execution record (Epsilon3), requirements and compliance-matrix traceability (Stell), or acquisition hardware (NI, Dewesoft). Several now use "audit-ready" or "certify" language in their marketing. None of them does any of the following:
(a) derive *what must be shown* for a specific approval type (DLA SAR category, FAA PMA identicality vs test-and-computation, DER 8110-3 data);
(b) generate the qualification test plan and the conformity/test report in the format a reviewer accepts;
(c) hold a DO-330 qualified-tool status, or regulator acceptance of its output, so that machine analysis can replace a witnessed test or a reviewer's hours;
(d) act as the applicant or the approver.
Their customers are new-build programs (Anduril, Hermeus, K2, Firefly, ULA). None serves the reverse-engineering, alternate-source or MRO approval market.

## The gap these companies leave (what nobody does)
1. **Turning the requirement into an evidence plan for alternate-source approvals.** No tool reads a DLA SAR category / AMSC code, the original drawing or TDP, the FAA PMA path (identicality, licence or test-and-computation per 14 CFR 21.303 / Order 8110.42) and outputs the minimum test-and-analysis matrix. Stell holds matrices. It does not author them for this use.
2. **Producing the package in the reviewer's format.** Nobody writes the SAR package, the FAA 8110-3 or DER substantiation report, or first-article / qualification test reports from the raw data. Sift and Nominal produce engineering reports, not approval artifacts.
3. **Equivalence and comparison analytics.** Nobody statistically shows "the reverse-engineered part behaves like the OEM part" (material, dimensional and test-signature comparison) with a pre-agreed acceptance criterion.
4. **Regulator-side tooling.** None sells to the reviewer (DLA Engineering Support Activities, FAA ACO/MIDO, DERs) to cut review hours per package. Every customer is an applicant building new hardware.
5. **Tool qualification and data provenance accepted as evidence.** None holds DO-330 / AC 20-115D tool-qualification status or a regulator memo that its analysis output can substitute for a witnessed or repeated test. NI comes closest on provenance (calibration and configuration binding). No one has made a signed, tamper-evident chain from sensor to report into a reviewer-accepted artifact.
6. **A cross-package learning corpus.** Each platform's data sits in one customer's silo. Nobody aggregates outcomes across many approvals (which tests reviewers actually ask for, rejection reasons, cycle time), and that corpus is what would lower hours per approval.

## What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)
- Build the "evidence compiler": requirements in (TDP, spec, SAR category, PMA basis), minimum test-and-analysis plan out, test execution on standard benches, then a package auto-assembled in the DLA/FAA reviewer format. Version control, signed provenance, and calibration and config binding throughout.
- Get the tool accepted. Pursue DO-330 TQL qualification for the analysis and report generator under an FAA issue paper, or a DLA ESA (Engineering Support Activity) memo agreeing on a standard SAR evidence schema. A machine-generated package is only as good as a regulator's agreement to read it. This is the binding constraint (law and acceptance), and it is changed by negotiating with one DLA ESA (e.g., Warner Robins or Oklahoma City ALC for C-130 and KC-135) on a pilot.
- Shorten the physics clock with statistical design. Use sequential and Bayesian test plans, physics-informed models calibrated on a few coupons, and digital-twin comparison to the OEM part. This reduces the number of physical runs, though not to zero. Accelerated life testing still has to run.
- Offer reviewer-side copilots: completeness and consistency checks on incoming SARs and PMA packages, so a reviewer spends hours on judgement rather than on paperwork. The approval factory could give this to ESAs free, both to standardise the format it is best at and to lower the queue it waits in.
- Build the corpus. Every package the factory submits adds labelled data on reviewer behaviour that no single-program platform (Sift, Nominal) will ever see.

## Opinion: where the AI play sits on the verification side
- The test data platforms are infrastructure the Approval Factory would *buy* (Nominal or Sift for data, NI for benches, Epsilon3 for as-run records). They are not competitors. They are moving toward "certify" marketing for new aircraft and spacecraft programs, and in 3-5 years one of them may add a DO-330-qualified reporting module. That is the main adjacency risk.
- The unclaimed position is an evidence compiler and approval-format layer that is specific to *alternate-source and sustainment approvals*. It would be owned by whoever files the most packages, because the moat is the reviewer-outcome corpus and the regulator's acceptance of its format, not the software.
