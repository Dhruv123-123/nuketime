## 1. How qualification works in space, and why it matters for the Approval Factory

- **There is no FAA for spacecraft parts.** Acceptance is set by contract. The customer (NASA project, USSF/SSC, a prime, or the operator and its insurer) approves a parts list, a radiation hardness assurance (RHA) analysis, and the qualification report. That makes it the easiest verification market to enter: the "approver" is a parts engineer or mission assurance lead at the buyer, not a federal certifying office. The exception is crewed and launch-safety hardware (FAA AST Part 450 for public safety; NASA for crew).
- **Governing documents:**
  - NASA-STD-6016C with Change 1 (Standard Materials and Processes Requirements for Spacecraft): dated 2021-09-30, change dated 2023-11-15, next 5-year review 2026-09-30, owned by the NASA Office of the Chief Engineer. It covers "all flight hardware ... including vendor-designed, off-the-shelf, and vendor-furnished items" (fetched from standards.nasa.gov). Deviations go through Materials Usage Agreements (MUAs), and materials ratings live in MAPTIS (recalled).
  - SMC-S-016 / MIL-STD-1540 test requirements allow "qualification by similarity" with a documented delta analysis against a qualified heritage unit (recalled).
  - ECSS-E-ST-10-02/-03 uses heritage categories A to D. A means off-the-shelf with no modification, so no qualification testing is needed. D means a new design, so full qualification is needed (recalled).
  - For radiation: ESCC 22900 (TID) and ESCC 25100 (SEE) in Europe; MIL-STD-883 TM1019 and JESD57 in the US (recalled).
- **The similarity argument is the bottleneck.** Most "qualification" of COTS-heavy, high-rate buses rests on claims like these: same die and same process as a tested part; same design with minor deltas; or a heritage unit flown before. Today those claims are hand-written by scarce RHA and parts engineers, and they are fragile. Part numbers hide die revisions, lot-to-lot variation for COTS is large, and die-level provenance is often unknown.

**Conclusion on (1):** Nobody commercial sells "AI-generated qualification-by-similarity dossiers that buyers accept." The only data-driven attempts are agency research (NEPP 2018, which stalled at prototype; CORHA-2, 22 parts, ending 2027).

## 3. The pathway: what is undone or broken

1. **Part selection / BOM triage:** which COTS parts are risky. Done by hand by RHA engineers. Databases are fragmented PDFs (radhome, ESCIES, IEEE NSREC/REDW papers, company-private data).
2. **Identity and provenance:** is this the same die as the tested one? Mostly not done. Part numbers hide die shrinks, fab moves and second sources. NEPP's own future work flagged that flavor consolidation was naive. Decap, X-ray and SEM die imaging are done ad hoc.
3. **Environment definition:** mission TID/LET spectra via CREME96, SPENVIS, OMERE. This step is solved.
4. **Prediction / similarity argument:** the core gap. It is an expert-judgment memo today. There is no calibrated model with uncertainty that buyers accept.
5. **Test:** this is the physics clock. Heavy-ion beam time is scarce: TAMU, LBNL 88-inch, NSRL, MSU FRIB SEE area, UCL, RADEF, GANIL, and now HEARTS. Costs run roughly $1-2k+/hr, with months-long queues in the US (recalled; National Academies 2018 "Testing at the Speed of Light" report on SEE test infrastructure shortfall, recalled). Laser and two-photon SEE plus Co-60 are cheap surrogates but are not accepted as replacements for heavy-ion testing.
6. **Lot acceptance:** COTS lot-to-lot variation forces re-test per lot. A sample-size-by-similarity rule does not exist.
7. **Qualification report / delta analysis (SMC-S-016 similarity, ECSS heritage category):** written by hand, typically 100-500 engineer-hours per unit (opinion/recalled).
8. **Acceptance by buyer / mission assurance / insurer:** per-program judgment, with no reusable "certificate." The same part is re-argued on every program.
9. **Materials (NASA-STD-6016):** MUAs are rewritten per program even when MAPTIS ratings exist.

## 4. What would need to be done (assuming abundant compute and intelligence)

- **Universal radiation-results corpus.** Ingest every public test report (NASA, ESA, IEEE TNS/REDW over 40 years, CERN, Sandia, JPL) into a normalized schema: part, die revision, lot, facility, ion, LET, fluence, bias, temperature, outcome. Add a data co-op so commercial buyers (Apex, K2, Anduril, Stoke, constellation operators) contribute private results in exchange for access. The binding constraint is data access, which can be changed by give-to-get economics.
- **Die-identity service.** Decap plus X-ray plus SEM imaging, with AI matching of die layouts to prove "same die and same process" across part numbers and lots. This turns a similarity claim from an opinion into evidence. It is cheap physics (hours, not beam queues).
- **Physics-informed, calibrated predictors with conformal uncertainty for SEL, SEU, SEFI and TID** across process node, foundry and architecture. Outputs should be "probability SEL below LET 37 at 85C," not a yes/no. Validate prospectively by predicting before tests and publishing hit rates. A public prospective scorecard is what buys acceptance.
- **Surrogate-test calibration.** Use pulsed laser / TPA, X-ray microbeam and high-energy protons, calibrated per technology to heavy-ion results, so that beam hours are spent only where the model is uncertain. This is active learning over physical tests, and it attacks the physics clock directly.
- **Own or reserve capacity.** Pooled beam-time purchasing, where one run tests a part for many buyers. A compact in-house accelerator or laser SEE lab is also possible. Scarce capacity is permission in this market.
- **Auto-generated similarity dossiers.** Produce the SMC-S-016 delta analysis, ECSS heritage justification, RHA report and NASA-STD-6016 MUA draft, with traceability back to raw test data (integrate with Nominal-style test data platforms).
- **A reusable "part passport."** Once a part, lot and die is accepted by one buyer, the acceptance and evidence travel. The law and acceptance change needed is persuading NASA NEPP, Aerospace Corp (TOR standards) and SSC mission assurance to endorse a model-plus-sample-test RHA method. Aerospace Corp TORs are the de facto standard-setter for USSF.
- **Liability.** Offer the dossier with warranty or insurance backing (partner with space insurers). Insurers price on-orbit failure risk and would value calibrated per-part risk.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side

- **"Similarity Engine":** a radiation and qualification evidence graph plus die-identity matching plus calibrated prediction that outputs buyer-acceptable qualification-by-similarity dossiers. The KPI is engineer-hours and beam-hours per qualified part or lot. Space is the beachhead because acceptance is contractual, not regulatory, and the buyers (high-rate bus makers) are venture-backed and time-starved.
- **Active-learning test house:** a lab that uses the model to decide the minimum physical tests (laser and Co-60 in-house, heavy-ion only where uncertain), selling "qualified part" outcomes rather than beam hours.
- **Part passport / data co-op:** the network-effect moat. Every accepted dossier makes the next one cheaper. It is the space analogue of the Approval Factory's PMA library.
- **Bridge to the Approval Factory:** the same engine (identity proof + similarity argument + minimum test + auto-dossier) maps onto DLA Source Approval Requests ("same as OEM part" is literally a similarity argument), FAA PMA identicality and test/computation, and DO-254/COTS avionics. Space builds the corpus and acceptance track record, and aviation and defense monetize it.
- **Threats:** Istari (digital certification narrative with the USAF); test-data platforms (Nominal) moving up-stack; vertically integrated primes building it internally (K2 at 80% in-house; Apex's Octopus); ESA CORHA-2 open database commoditizing public data.

## Surprises
- NASA built a predictive SEL model in 2018 (NEPP/JPL) and it apparently never left prototype. The gap is acceptance and productization, not the idea.
- ESA is explicitly funding "AI-supported RHA" (CORHA-2, 2024-2027), but on only 22 parts. Agencies validate the thesis but lack scale.
- Even NASA GSFC's similarity reasoning rests on "thought to be 16/12 nm (unconfirmed)." Die identity is the weakest link, and it is cheaply solvable.
- Space qualification has no federal certifier for most hardware. Permission sits with buyers and insurers, so it can be won with data and track record faster than FAA approval.
