# s04: Material and process identification of an existing part

Question: Step in the reverse-engineering / approval pathway: identify the material and process of an existing part (alloy, heat treat, coatings, grain structure, residual stress; XRF, OES, LIBS, EBSD, hardness, metallography). What can be found without destroying the part, what needs destruction, and where AI could help.

Method note: I could run only 3 web searches (the quota is shared). Most standards numbers, instrument specs and costs below are from memory and are marked (recalled). Figures with a source URL are evidence. Everything else is opinion or inference.

## 1. Why this step matters to the Approval Factory

- A Source Approval Request (SAR) to the Defense Logistics Agency (DLA) for a part with no technical data package (TDP), or an FAA Parts Manufacturer Approval (PMA) by "test and computation", has to show the new part is identical or equivalent in material and process. Geometry is now cheap: photos, CT or a scan turn into CAD. One example is Novineer NoviVision, which makes CAD from 1 to 4 phone photos in about 2 minutes (https://runwaygirlnetwork.com/2026/04/press-release-novineer-launches-ai-reverse-engineering-with-am-craft/). Material and process identity is not cheap. Most of the hidden, permission-relevant risk sits there: temper, melt practice, peening, hydrogen bake-out and grain flow.
- The binding constraint is the number of samples, not lab capability. Sole-source and orphaned parts are often available as only 1 to 3 physical examples. Every destructive test uses up evidence. Reverse-engineering practice usually budgets at least one sample for sectioning (recalled). MIL-HDBK-115A, the US Army reverse engineering handbook, sets out the classic workflow (https://www.wbdg.org/FFC/ARMYCOE/COEMILHDBK/hdbk115a.pdf; content recalled).
- Cost and time today (recalled): basic chemistry and hardness cost $300 to $1,500 per part and take 1 to 5 days. A full metallurgical characterization (chemistry, gas content, metallography, grain flow, coating section, residual stress profile, tensile coupons) costs $10k to $40k and takes 2 to 6 weeks at commercial labs (Element, Applus, NTS, Lambda Technologies, IMR). This step is a modest share of the roughly $100k cost per package. It is a much larger share of the risk of rejection or rework.

## 2. What each property needs: non-destructive (NDE), semi-destructive, or destructive

| Property | Non-destructive / field methods | Limits | Destructive confirmation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bulk chemistry (major and minor elements) | Handheld XRF (Thermo Niton, Evident Vanta, Bruker S1 Titan, SciAps X-series, Hitachi) for Ti, Cr, Ni, Fe, Cu, Zn, Mo, Nb, W and others | Surface only (tens of µm). Weak below Mg. Cannot see C, B, Li or Be. Struggles to separate close grades such as 2024 vs 2124 and 7075 vs 7050 vs 7475 (impurity limits on Fe and Si) (recalled). Coatings get in the way. | Spark OES on a lab coupon (ASTM E415 for steel, E1251 for Al, E2994 for Ti) (recalled); ICP-OES/MS |
| Carbon and light elements | Handheld LIBS: SciAps Z-series claims carbon testing in steel by LIBS only, and the Z-903 claims "full elemental coverage H to U" (https://www.sciaps.com/). Mobile arc/spark OES (Hitachi PMI-MASTER, SPECTRO) | LIBS leaves a micro-crater, so it is "quasi-NDE". Carbon is good enough to separate L from H stainless or 4130 from 4340 (recalled, about ±0.01 to 0.02 wt% C). Spark OES leaves a burn mark several mm across. Some parts and drawings do not allow either. | Combustion analysis for C and S (ASTM E1019) |
| Interstitial gases (O, N, H) | None reliable on a finished part | These are critical for Ti (alpha case, H embrittlement) and for steel melt practice | Inert gas fusion (ASTM E1409 and E1447 for Ti, E1019) (recalled). Destructive, gram-sized samples. |
| Melt practice (VAR, ESR vs air melt), e.g. AMS 6414 (4340 VAR) vs AMS 6415 (4340 air-melt) | Ultrasonic cleanliness hints only | Separating VAR from air melt needs inclusion ratings | Inclusion rating (ASTM E45), magnetic particle cleanliness (AMS 2300 vs 2301) (recalled). Section needed. |
| Temper / heat treat condition, aluminium | Eddy current conductivity in %IACS (ASTM E1004) plus hardness. AMS 2658 sets these as an accepted heat-treat verification method (recalled). It separates 7075-T6 from T73, and 2024-T3 from O. | Over-aging vs mis-quench is ambiguous. Clad layers bias the reading. Thin sections cause errors. | Tensile coupons (ASTM B557), DSC, TEM of precipitates |
| Temper / heat treat, steels and Ti | Portable hardness (Leeb ASTM A956, UCI ASTM A1038, portable Rockwell). Barkhausen noise (Stresstech) for case depth, grinding burn and decarb. Hardness converts to UTS for steel only (ASTM A370 / E140). | Gives strength level, not the cycle that produced it (e.g. austenitize temperature or temper sequence). Ti STA vs annealed is partly visible in hardness. | Microhardness traverse and metallography on a section, case depth (SAE J423) |
| Mechanical properties (yield, UTS) | Automated ball indentation (Frontics AIS, ABI). NDT&E Int. 2023 used ML on ultrasonic plus eddy current data to estimate YS, UTS and elongation of Al alloys within about 8% of tensile tests (search summary; paper on SSU ScholarWorks, handle 2018.sw.ssu/49441). Small-punch test (EN 10371:2021) (recalled) is minimally invasive. | Regulators and DLA do not yet accept these in place of coupon allowables (opinion / recalled) | Tensile, fatigue and fracture toughness coupons. Physics clocks: fatigue at 10^6 to 10^7 cycles takes days to weeks per specimen. |
| Grain size, texture and grain flow | Ultrasonic backscatter/attenuation for mean grain size. Spatially resolved acoustic spectroscopy (SRAS, Univ. Nottingham) for surface orientation maps (recalled). Field metallography by surface replica (ASTM E1351) after polishing a small spot. Laboratory diffraction-contrast tomography (Zeiss/Xnovo LabDCT) for small samples (recalled). | Forging grain flow (the main forging vs machined-plate question) cannot really be seen without sectioning | Macroetch (ASTM E381 / E340), EBSD on a section, optical metallography (ASTM E112 grain size) |
| Porosity, castings, AM internals | X-ray CT, digital radiography, UT | Resolution scales with part size | Section metallography |
| Residual stress, surface (shot peen, machining) | Portable XRD (Proto iXRD, Pulstec µ-X360; ASTM E915, E2860) (recalled). Barkhausen. Eddy current: an AFRL ASM RSC 2026 abstract describes down-selecting eddy current and ultrasonic NDE techniques to verify cold-expansion hole residual stress in Al, extended to Ti and steel (search summary; confex link now 404). | XRD sees only about 5 to 30 µm. A depth profile needs electropolish layer removal, which is semi-destructive. | Hole drilling (ASTM E837), slitting, contour method |
| Residual stress, through-thickness | Neutron diffraction (ORNL HFIR/SNS, NIST NCNR, ISIS) and synchrotron (APS, CHESS, NSLS-II). NDE, but needs beam time. | Weeks to months of queue, limited access, a physics clock on access | Contour method |
| Shot peen intensity and coverage | Inferred from the XRD/Barkhausen profile. Coverage by visual check or a fluorescent tracer only at process time (AMS 2430 / 2432) (recalled). | Almen intensity is a process measurement and cannot be measured directly on a finished part | Profile by layer removal |
| Coatings and plating | XRF thickness and composition (ASTM B568). Magnetic/eddy thickness (ASTM B499, B244, D7091). Identifies Cd vs Zn-Ni vs IVD Al, chromate conversion, anodize thickness. | Seal type, Type II vs III anodize partly; underlayers; diffusion aluminide depth on turbine parts | Cross-section metallography (ASTM B487), SEM-EDS |
| Process steps that leave no or ambiguous signature | Hydrogen embrittlement relief bake after plating (AMS 2759/9), stress relief, passivation, cold work and straightening, weld procedure, NDT done at manufacture | Largely invisible after the fact | Sustained-load H-embrittlement tests (ASTM F519) apply to the new process, not the old part |

Summary: about 70 to 80% of what a reviewer needs (alloy family, grade, temper class, coating type and thickness, surface hardness, surface residual stress) can be found today without destruction, or with micro-marks only (opinion, based on the table). The rest is grain flow, melt practice, gas content, through-thickness properties, coating microstructure and invisible process steps. That remainder drives the need to destroy samples, and it is where approvals stall.

## 3. Who is doing what (players)

- Instrument OEMs: Thermo Fisher (Niton XRF), Evident/Olympus (Vanta), Bruker, SciAps (LIBS and XRF, Woburn MA), Hitachi High-Tech (XRF, mobile OES), SPECTRO/AMETEK, Proto Manufacturing and Pulstec (XRD residual stress), Stresstech (Barkhausen), Frontics (ABI). Their software matches spectra to grade libraries. No vendor fuses data across instruments (recalled).
- Commercial labs: Element Materials Technology, Applus+ Laboratories, NTS, IMR Test Labs, Lambda Technologies (residual stress services), Mistras. They do the work by hand and bill by the hour.
- Microstructure image AI: MIPAR (Ohio, ML metallography and grain analysis), Clemex, ZEISS ZEN Intellesis, Thermo Fisher Avizo, BlueQuartz DREAM3D (open source, AFRL-funded) (recalled). All are adjacent to this step. None does reverse engineering.
- Materials data and ML: Citrine Informatics, Ansys Granta (holds MMPDS data), Senvol (AM qualification data and ML) (recalled). These are adjacent.
- Reverse engineering, TDP and AI: DISCUS Software was selected by AFWERX in October 2020 (one of 3 from about 150 teams, amount undisclosed, with Renaissance Services) for AI in reverse engineering, TDP generation and first article inspection (FAI). Its focus is extracting requirements from drawings by OCR, not identifying material (https://www.discussoftware.com/news/afwerx-selects-discus-software-company/). Novineer NoviVision does geometry only. Composite Vision (Berlin) does AI NDE for composites (https://gust.com/companies/composite-vision). Government shops (AF Rapid Sustainment Office, Oklahoma City ALC reverse engineering, NAVAIR FRCs, Army CCDC/AvMC) do reverse engineering in house (recalled).
- Search found no company doing AI fusion of multimodal NDE to infer spec, temper and process for reverse engineering or approvals. This is evidence of absence from limited search, so the confidence is moderate.

## 4. Gaps that nobody fills, and what it would take

1. **Multimodal fusion into a posterior over specifications.** Today each instrument gives a separate readout and a metallurgist combines them by hand. What is needed is an inference engine. It would take XRF, LIBS, eddy conductivity, hardness, Barkhausen, XRD, UT backscatter, CT, geometry, the part's era, OEM and platform, plus drawing callouts. It would output a calibrated probability over (material spec, temper, product form, process route). An example output: "7075-T7351 plate per QQ-A-250/12, now AMS 4078, p=0.93; 7075-T7651 p=0.05". Nobody has the calibration data (see 3).
2. **Optimal experimental design to minimise destruction.** When only 1 to 3 samples exist, the engine should choose the next test with the most information per destroyed gram. It should also say where to section: one strategic slice can give grain flow, a microhardness traverse, coating cross-section and gas-fusion chips together. A related gap is cross-fleet pooling, so that the same part number pulled from AMARG or scrap gives extra samples.
3. **A paired NDE-to-destructive ground-truth dataset.** This does not exist publicly. It would mean taking a few thousand retired parts of known spec from the 309th AMARG boneyard at Davis-Monthan, DLA Disposition Services scrap and DEMIL streams, and airline teardown (AerSale, AAR, GA Telesis teardowns), each with records. Each part would be scanned with every NDE mode and then fully destroyed. Cost estimate: about $5k to $20k per part, so $10M to $60M for about 3,000 parts (opinion). This dataset is the moat. Data access, via DLA disposition agreements and teardown partners, is the real constraint, not compute.
4. **Decoding the historical spec genealogy.** Legacy drawings cite superseded specs (QQ-, MIL-, old OEM process specs such as Lockheed or Boeing BAC, cancelled AMS revisions). A knowledge graph is needed that maps each spec and revision to its chemistry window, heat-treat cycle and property minimums over time, plus its modern equivalents. This is mostly text and LLM work and is very feasible. Part of the data sits in SAE AMS, which is paywalled and licensed.
5. **Inference of invisible process steps.** Some steps cannot be measured (H-bake, peening intensity, stress relief). The fix is conservative envelope design: specify a modern, superset process (e.g. peen to AMS 2432 at the highest plausible intensity, bake per AMS 2759/9) and show by analysis and test that it bounds the original. AI can draft the bounding argument and the substantiation test plan. That turns a forensic question into an engineering justification, which DERs and DLA engineers already accept in principle (opinion).
6. **Regulator acceptance of NDE-inferred equivalence.** There is precedent: AMS 2658 (conductivity plus hardness) is already an accepted temper verification (recalled). To change the rules, one would sponsor an SAE AMS or ASTM E07 standard for "multimodal NDE material identification for reverse engineering", with a validated probability-of-correct-ID like MIL-HDBK-1823A POD curves (recalled). The route would be a DLA or AFRL pilot and then FAA acceptance through DER data packages. The constraints here are law and regulator acceptance. A credible path is to publish POD-like validation on the dataset from item 3.
7. **Faster through-thickness residual stress.** Neutron and synchrotron beam time is the physics and access clock. Possible answers are compact accelerator neutron sources or lab high-energy XRD, or ML that infers a full stress field from surface XRD plus a process model, validated against contour-method ground truth.
8. **Robotic autonomous lab cell.** One cell would combine CT, XRF, LIBS, eddy current, hardness, XRD, a sectioning saw, auto-polish, optical microscopy, EBSD and gas fusion, scheduled by the inference engine. Cycle time would drop from 2 to 6 weeks to about 24 to 72 hours, and labor from 40 to 100 hours to about 5 to 10 reviewer hours (opinion). The physics clocks that remain are coupon fatigue and tensile tests on the new part.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side

- **"Material forensics as a service" / an engine inside the Approval Factory:** the fusion, design-of-experiments and spec-graph stack, run in house. It is the KPI lever: it cuts engineering hours per approval and the number of samples destroyed.
- **Selling the engine or standard to DLA, AFRL and OC-ALC reverse engineering shops and FAA DERs.** This could be a wedge through SBIR, for example the AFWERX / RSO reverse engineering topics that DISCUS won in 2020.
- **The ground-truth dataset plus a consensus standard:** owning the calibration data and the approved method is permission-like rather than knowledge-like. That fits the thesis that monopolies come from permission.
- **Counterfeit and suspect-unapproved-part detection:** the same engine identifies material mismatch in incoming parts. This sells to DLA counterfeit programs and to MROs as a second market.
