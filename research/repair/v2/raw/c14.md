# c14: Simulation credibility and certification-by-analysis vendors (Ansys, Synopsys, Hexagon MSC, Altair, NASA CbA roadmap, ASME V&V 10/20/40, Vanderplaats): what is the state of "simulation as evidence" in aerospace and medical?

## 1. Bottom line
- **Regulators accept simulation as evidence, but they never accept a tool.** They accept a *data package*: a model plus validation against tests, within a stated domain, with margins. FAA's Patrick Safarian said in 2018: "The regulations are written for parts, not tools. So we don't certify software ... [parts are certified] either by tests, or by analysis supported by tests." In Jan 2026 Synopsys/Ansys, Siemens and Dassault all said the same. There is "no preapproved list of FEA or CFD software." What the regulator wants is "sufficient proof that your calculations and methods produce results that match reality." (Digital Engineering 247, 2018 and 29 Jan 2026.)
- **In aerospace, acceptance is case by case and negotiated.** It is mature for derivatives and minor changes (antennas, radomes, structural repairs, fatigue and damage tolerance, crash, bird strike, blade-out containment support). For new designs it is immature: "For new designs, we are not yet at the point where physical testing can be eliminated" (Brice Segaud, Synopsys, 2026). Siemens' Thierry Olbrechts called it "a continuous negotiation, involving simulation and tests."
- **Medical has a written framework and aerospace mostly does not.** FDA final guidance "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions" was issued 16 Nov 2023. It is a nine-step, risk-informed framework built on ASME V&V 40-2018 (context of use, then model influence x decision consequence = model risk, then credibility goals). It covers physics and mechanistic models only, and explicitly excludes ML/AI models (Exponent, Mondaq, HPN summaries; the FDA site returned 401).
- **No regulator has rules for ML surrogates or ROMs.** Segaud says ROMs are acceptable if they correlate with test or historical data and are used within their training range. Olbrechts: "the reliability of these ROMs are not yet fully understood." The 2026 Ansys/Synopsys CbA webinar, with Safarian (FAA) and Wim Doeland (EASA), refers only to "emerging guidance" on AI/ML surrogates.
- **The vendor layer has consolidated into EDA giants.** Synopsys bought Ansys (~$35B, closed July 2025, recalled). Siemens bought Altair (~$10.6B, closed March 2025, recalled). Cadence agreed to buy Hexagon's Design & Engineering business, which includes MSC Nastran/Adams (~EUR 2.7B, announced Sept 2025, recalled; the Cadence press page returned 403). Dassault (SIMULIA/Abaqus, PowerFLOW, CST, MODSIM) is the remaining independent large player. Vanderplaats R&D (GENESIS structural optimization) is a small private optimizer, not a credibility player; I could not verify any status change.
- **What the vendors sell is solvers and traceability**, with marketing pages full of claims that are not backed up. Dassault's CbA page names one example (an Airbus "smarter testing" wing demonstrator) and gives no numbers. None of them sells *the credibility argument itself*: the VVUQ dossier mapped to the specific regulation and means of compliance, with the validation tests chosen and the uncertainty margins computed. That work is done by OEM methods groups, DERs/ODA units and consultancies (Exponent; Boeing and Airbus internal teams).

## 2. Aerospace evidence base
- **Rules that allow analysis already.** For transport airframes, most structural rules (14 CFR 25.305/307, 25.571) say "by analysis supported by test" (recalled). AC 20-107B (composites) sets out the building-block approach: coupons, then elements, then subcomponents, then the full article. Analysis goes up the pyramid and tests anchor each level (recalled). AC 25-7D §3.1.2.6 (2018) sets general principles for proposing flight simulation in place of flight test (cited in the Polimi/RoCS paper).
- **EASA.** EASA notified a proposed Certification Memorandum, "Modelling & Simulation – CS-25 Structural Certification Specifications" (2020, referred to as CM-S-014), cited in the RoCS paper. The EASA URL I guessed returned 404, so final status is unverified. Doeland (EASA): EASA and FAA are "very much aligned"; decisions are case by case; virtual tests have begun replacing some physical tests for derivatives where the model has been validated with flight and ground tests; EASA is pushing tool developers toward QA standards because "it lacks the resources to vet tools on its own" (Aerospace America, June 2022).
- **Precedents:**
  - The FAA accepted RANS CFD in place of flight tests in 2018 for internet antennas and radomes on A350-derived installations and on the A330-200 and 737-800 (Aerospace America caption).
  - Boeing (Rob Gregg) estimates CbA could replace up to 50% of physical flights by thinning flight-test conditions.
  - Type-certificate flight-test campaigns run 12-18 months.
  - Gulfstream G650 crash (April 2011): the NTSB found takeoff speeds had not been validated with simulation or physics-based analysis, which is the counter-lesson. Certification slipped about a year, to Sept 2012.
- **NASA.**
  - "A Guide for Aircraft Certification by Analysis," NASA/CR-20210015404, May 2021. Authors are Mauery, Cary, Lee, Schaefer and Slotnick (Boeing), Malecki and Medic (P&W), Alonso (Stanford) and Mavriplis (Wyoming). It gives a long-term CbA vision and a computational-technology roadmap covering CFD, propulsion, aeroelastics and stall.
  - Alonso's targets: four to five nines of reliability ("reasonable doubt"). RANS vs LES CLmax differences are 10-15%. A RANS cruise run takes 2-4 hours on 500 cores with 150M cells. An edge-of-envelope LES maneuver takes 10k-100k cores. Running 100,000 simulations could become routine in 10-15 years. "Quantifying uncertainties ... is absolutely necessary" and is "kind of the holy grail."
  - NASA-STD-7009A (2016) is NASA's M&S credibility standard. A 7009B revision exists (2024, recalled). It has a credibility assessment scale.
  - AIAA R-154-2021 is a recommended practice for using flight modelling to reduce flight testing.
- **EU RoCS (Rotorcraft Certification by Simulation).**
  - A Clean Sky 2 / Horizon 2020 project. Guidelines "Update No 1, Jan 2023" are at rocs-project.org/guidelines.
  - Its framework grades each applicable certification requirement on an Influence x Predictability (P1 interpolation up to P4 extrapolation) matrix, and sets confidence ratios for the margins.
  - It claims the UQ methods and tools are "currently available in the public domain" and that "What is now required is for Industry to take the process forward." In other words, the methodology exists but is not productized (Quaranta et al., ERF 2023, Polimi PDF).
  - eVTOL: EASA SC-VTOL and FAA AC 21.17-4 (powered-lift; reported by AIN in Aug 2025) open new means-of-compliance negotiations, which is where RoCS proposes CbS as the preferred approach.
- **Applications vendors list (Segaud, 2026):**
  - Structures: ingestion, composite fan blades, containment, fatigue and damage tolerance, crashworthiness.
  - CFD: icing and external flow.
  - Electromagnetics: EMI/EMC and lightning.
  - Optical.
  - Model-based verification of software, safety and cyber.
  - Tests that motivate simulation: fan blade-out (destroys a multimillion-dollar engine), crash tests, and natural icing (teams "basically watching the weather map").

## 3. Medical evidence base
- **Framework.**
  - FDA CM&S credibility guidance, final 16 Nov 2023. Its nine steps start with: question of interest, context of use, model risk.
  - It accepts evidence beyond the "traditional" bench validation of V&V 40: code verification, calculation verification, bench, in vivo, population-based evidence, and emergent model behavior (categories recalled from the guidance).
  - It recommends pre-submission (Q-Sub) meetings.
  - It applies to in silico trials, digital twins and patient-specific planning, but not to ML.
- **Standards.**
  - ASME V&V 40-2018 is FDA-recognized; a 2023/2024 revision is in progress (recalled).
  - ASME V&V 10 covers computational solid mechanics (2006, updated 2019) (recalled).
  - V&V 20 covers CFD and heat transfer (2009).
  - V&V 50 covers advanced manufacturing; there is also an ASME V&V 60 effort (recalled).
- **Precedents (all recalled):**
  - FDA's VICTRE in silico imaging trial (2018) replicated a breast-imaging (DBT) clinical comparison with about 3,000 virtual patients.
  - Medtronic used a virtual-patient Bayesian prior to reduce clinical trial enrollment.
  - MRI RF-heating safety of implants is routinely shown by simulation on Virtual Population (IT'IS) anatomies under ISO/TS 10974.
  - The FDA MDDT program has qualified a few computational tools.
  - EU: the Avicenna Alliance and the VPH Institute are pushing in silico trials.
  - Commercial: InSilicoTrials, Novadiscovery, the Dassault Living Heart, Exponent (consultancy).
- **Acceptance.** FDA uses simulation extensively for bench-type claims (stents, MRI conditional labeling, orthopedics). It rarely replaces a pivotal clinical trial.

## 5. Gaps nobody fills
1. **No productized "credibility dossier compiler."** Nothing maps a regulation and means of compliance to the required credibility level, validation tests, UQ and margins, and then writes the compliance report. RoCS, NASA-STD-7009 and V&V 40 are all frameworks on paper.
2. **No shared, regulator-visible validation-data library for legacy parts.** Material allowables exist in MMPDS and CMH-17. But validation cases for specific failure modes on legacy parts (fatigue of 7075-T6 fittings, KC-135-era alloys) are locked in OEM files.
3. **No regulator-side tooling.** EASA explicitly lacks resources to vet tools. Nobody gives FAA ACOs or DLA engineering support activities a way to review a simulation package quickly. Reviewer hours are the binding KPI.
4. **No accepted ML-surrogate credibility method.** FDA excludes ML from the CM&S guidance, and FAA/EASA have "emerging" guidance only.
5. **No test-minimization planner.** Nothing designs the smallest physical test set (coupons and elements) that anchors the model enough to meet credibility goals, using Bayesian optimal experimental design. The physics clock is set by how many tests you run, so this directly attacks the clock.
6. **No closed loop from fleet data into model credibility.** Service-history data, which DLA and the USAF hold for legacy platforms, is not used as validation evidence.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side
- **Credibility Engine:** an AI that, given a part, its regulatory basis and failure modes, generates the V&V 40 / NASA-7009 / RoCS-style credibility plan, runs the solvers (through existing vendor tools), runs UQ, picks the minimal anchoring tests, and writes the 8110-3 / SAR-ready substantiation. Its KPI is reviewer hours per approval.
- **Reviewer copilot sold or given to regulators and DLA ESAs:** it checks submitted analyses against the credibility framework, reruns them independently, and flags extrapolation beyond the validation domain. This attacks the regulator-acceptance constraint directly.
- **Bayesian optimal test design plus a lab:** an autonomous coupon/element test lab driven by the model's uncertainty, which shortens the physics clock.
- **A validation-data consortium or registry for orphaned parts:** a data network effect, with possible government funding via DLA, AFRL or NASA.
- **ML-surrogate credibility standard work:** lead the ASME V&V / SAE G-34 / EUROCAE WG-114 committees on ML-surrogate credibility to shape the rules.

## 7. What it would take to change each binding constraint
- **Regulator acceptance:** precedent by precedent. Start where analysis is already allowed (DER repairs, PMA "test and computation", DLA SAR reverse engineering with similarity analysis). Publish credibility dossiers. Get FAA/EASA reviewers involved early through issue papers and certification memoranda.
- **Physics clocks:** fatigue tests and environmental exposure cannot be simulated away. They can be shrunk by optimal design, run in parallel in an autonomous lab, and supplemented with fleet history.
- **Liability:** the signatory (DER/ODA or the PMA holder) carries it. The AI house must hold the approval itself or employ the signers.
- **Data:** validation data is the moat. Generate it in-house through testing and reverse engineering of physical parts, or get it through government data rights.
