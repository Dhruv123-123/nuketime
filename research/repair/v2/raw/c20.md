- **DNV RuleAgent (27 Mar 2026)**: an LLM search over more than 30,000 pages of DNV rules. It returns paragraphs you can trace back, with links, and filters by ship type and notation. - **ClassNK Survey Compass (beta, Aug 2026)**: a retrieval-based assistant over Part B rules and survey guidance. The user stays responsible for the technical assessment. Flag-state rules and vessel-specific data are planned. Nothing on drawing review. https://hansa.news/classnk-is-rolling-out-ai-support-for-shipping-companies/
- **Lloyd's Register + Microsoft (6 Mar 2025)**: Azure OpenAI generative tools that draft nuclear-maritime *permitting documents* from historic licensing data and search regulatory precedent. No time savings are quantified. This sits on the *applicant* side (drafting), not the *reviewer* side. https://wnn.world-nuclear.org/articles/lloyds-register-to-use-ai-to-aid-maritime-nuclear-licensing

### Rail (US)
- **FRA Automated Track Inspection (ATI) waiver**: AAR requested it in April 2025, and FRA's Railroad Safety Board granted a 5-year waiver on 8 Dec 2025 with 12 conditions. Railroads give 30 days' notice and their subdivision list is fixed for 1 year. They must run automated inspection of all main tracks and sidings at least monthly. Visual inspections drop from twice weekly to once weekly. They file monthly and annual reports and report any derailment within 24 h. Democratic senators led by Cantwell opposed it (Gillibrand letter, Jul 2025). FRA had earlier denied BNSF, UP and NS program expansions. https://www.freightwaves.com/news/feds-ok-automation-say-railroads-can-make-fewer-visual-track-safety-checks , https://www.gillibrand.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/FINAL-Letter-to-FRA-on-ATI-Waiver.pdf
  - Key pattern: **automation entered the regime by waiver plus data-reporting conditions**, not by rule change. The machine output is accepted as *evidence* that cuts the frequency of human inspection. It does not replace human sign-off.

## Recalled (not verified this session)
- **AAR M-1003 (Manual of Standards and Recommended Practices, Section J)** is the quality-assurance spec. Builders, rebuilders and repair shops for freight cars and many components (wheels, bearings, couplers, brake valves, tank cars) must hold M-1003 certification to sell into interchange. AAR's QA staff audit on a roughly 3-year cycle with surveillance audits. Separately, the **AAR Equipment Engineering / Coupling & Draft and Brake committees approve new component designs** (conditional, then full approval after service trials, often 2 or more years and a set number of units in service). Railinc runs **Umler** and the **Component Registry** (serialized tracking of wheelsets, bearings, couplers, side frames and bolsters, introduced after the 2015-17 side-frame and bolster problems). AAR is a private standards body with interchange rules. FRA defers to it for many items (49 CFR 215, 232 incorporate AAR standards). (recalled)
- **FRA**: 49 CFR 238 for passenger equipment, and 49 CFR 179 / AAR M-1002 tank car approvals (AAR Tank Car Committee plus FRA). Approvals go through AAR form 4-2 and similar, with no AI review. (recalled)
- **EN 15085 (EU railway welding)**: certification of welding manufacturers at levels CL1 to CL4 by recognized certification bodies, the dominant one being DVS ZERT in Germany, with others such as TUV and Lloyd's Register. Certificates are valid 3 years with audits. Several thousand certificates exist worldwide, many held by Chinese and Indian suppliers. Approval is largely a paper and audit process with no AI. (recalled)
- **ECM, Regulation (EU) 2019/779**: every vehicle has an Entity in Charge of Maintenance, certified by accredited or recognised certification bodies and listed in ERA's ERADIS/ECM database. The maintenance functions (management, development, fleet management, delivery) are audited. This is the rail analogue of a Part-145/CAMO split. (recalled)
- **IACS**: UR Z17 covers approval of service suppliers (thickness measurement, drone/RIT firms). IACS Rec. 42 covers remote inspection techniques. Remote survey guidance was issued after COVID (around 2020-22), and there is IACS work on electronic certificates (IMO FAL.5/Circ.39 accepts e-certificates). (recalled)
- **Class-accepted robotic and AI inspection vendors**: Flyability (Elios drones, class-accepted RIT), Scout Drone Inspection (ScoutDI), Gecko Robotics (robotic UT; ABS and LR acceptances announced around 2022-23), Abyss Solutions, Square Robot (tank inspection, API-oriented), Greensea IQ / SeaRobotics hull crawlers. Rail: Duos Technologies (Railcar Inspection Portal, AI machine vision, Amtrak), ENSCO, Loram, Herzog/Tetra Tech TRAIL, Wabtec, Rail Vision. (recalled)
- **Digital-class products**: ABS Nautical Systems / "ABS Wavesight" (spun out 2021-22 as a SaaS fleet-management business), ABS MyFreedom portal, LR "Class Direct", BV "Veristar Hull" / "Veristar AIM3D", DNV "Nauticus Hull" / Sesam for design-side rule checks. These rule-check tools let the *designer* pre-check scantlings against class rules, and they belong to the class society itself (recalled).

## Analysis (opinion)

### The pathway (marine example; rail is analogous)
1. **Rules**: published by class (DNV and others), IACS URs, IMO/SOLAS, flag. Machine-readable only as PDF or HTML. No society publishes executable rules.
2. **Design submission**: hundreds to 1,000+ drawings per newbuild (recalled), uploaded through eApproval, MyFreedom and similar portals. Designers can pre-check with class-owned tools (Nauticus Hull, Veristar).
3. **Plan approval**: a human class engineer reviews each drawing and issues comments. Iterations take weeks. **No public evidence of AI reviewers at any IACS society as of Oct 2026.** AI so far is rule *search* (RuleAgent, Survey Compass) and applicant-side *drafting* (LR nuclear).
4. **Material and component certification**: works approval of mills and makers, type approval of equipment (DNV TA certificates), and witnessed tests by surveyors.
5. **Construction survey**: surveyors at the yard, hold points and witnessed tests. This is a physics clock plus a human presence requirement.
6. **In-service survey**: annual, intermediate and special (5-yearly) surveys, thickness gauging by UR Z17-approved firms, drone/RIT and remote surveys now accepted for subsets.
7. **Repair and alteration approval**: damage survey, repair procedure approval, welding procedure qualification (WPQR), and approved repair yards.
8. **Certificates**: class certificate and statutory certificates on behalf of flag (RO status). E-certificates are accepted.

Rail: rules (AAR MSRP, FRA CFR, TSIs, EN standards) feed design approval (AAR committees, tank car committee, NoBo/DeBo/AsBo in the EU). Then come shop certification (M-1003, EN 15085, ECM), component tracking (Component Registry, Umler), and in-service inspection (FRA, with ATI waiver evidence).

### What nobody does or does badly
- **Executable rules**: no class society or AAR publishes rules as code, so every checker re-encodes PDFs. DNV RuleAgent is retrieval, not computation.
- **Reviewer-side automated plan review**: the reviewer side is where the hours are. Applicant-side pre-check tools exist but are class-owned and siloed. No third party offers a "pre-approved submission" that class trusts enough to fast-track.
- **Cross-society equivalence**: the same component is type-approved separately by DNV, ABS, LR, BV, KR, CCS and others. That is N redundant reviews of the same evidence. IACS mutual recognition (recalled: an IACS MR scheme exists for some type approvals) is limited.
- **Evidence-grade data pipelines**: drones and crawlers produce data, but acceptance is per-vendor (UR Z17) and the surveyor still attends or reviews manually. No continuous-evidence regime ("survey by data") exists, although class has "condition-based" and "machinery PMS" schemes that partially allow this.
- **Rail component re-approval / reverse engineering**: AAR committee approval for a new-source component needs service trials, which is a physics plus calendar clock. Nobody runs a model-based equivalence argument that could shorten conditional approval.
- **Repair procedure approval**: each repair is reviewed by a surveyor case by case. There is no library of pre-approved, parameterized repair procedures with AI-generated variants.

### What would need to be done (assume abundant intelligence and compute)
1. Compile class rules, IACS URs, AAR MSRP and EN 15085 into **executable, versioned rule code**, with a test-case corpus that reproduces past approvals. Sell or license it to class societies as their *reviewer* engine, or offer it as a neutral "pre-review" whose report class accepts. What has to change: class has to accept a third-party verifier report, much as UR Z17 approves thickness-measurement firms. That makes an IACS "approved digital review supplier" category the regulatory unlock.
2. **AI plan-review copilot for class engineers**: ingest the drawing (PDF/DWG/3D model), extract scantlings and systems, check them against rule code, and draft the comment sheet. KPI is reviewer hours per drawing. The liability stays with the class society, which keeps the stamp. That is why it is sellable: no law needs to change.
3. **Multi-society type-approval packager**: generate one evidence pack and auto-map it to DNV, ABS, LR, BV, CCS and KR requirements. Equivalent maps for AAR and EN suppliers.
4. **"Survey by evidence" stack**: robotic and drone data plus provenance (signed sensor data, chain of custody), giving a machine-generated survey report that a surveyor countersigns remotely. The ATI waiver template (conditions plus monthly reporting plus a 5-year horizon) is the precedent to copy in marine and in rail mechanical (wayside detectors for car inspection under 49 CFR 215/232, which already moving that way via Duos portals; recalled).
5. **Pre-approved repair procedure library**: parametric repair methods (doubler plates, crop-and-renew, welding procedures) with FE checks auto-generated per case, submitted for "approval by reference".
6. **Rail model-based conditional approval**: digital-twin and accelerated-fatigue evidence arguing that a reverse-engineered component is equivalent, shortening AAR service trials. Needs AAR committee acceptance, which you would get by co-running parallel trials to build a track record.

### Fit with "Approval Factory"
- Marine class and AAR are **private** approvers. They are more commercially motivated to adopt reviewer-side AI than FAA or DLA, and they compete with each other (DNV vs ABS vs LR), so a vendor can play one against another. The class society keeps the liability and the stamp, which removes the liability blocker.
- The likely wedge is to sell AI review and executable rules to the approver, or to the applicant as pre-review, rather than being the applicant. The risk is that class societies build it in-house: DNV with Veracity/RuleAgent, ABS with ex-Wavesight teams, LR with Microsoft.
- The rail aftermarket (freight car components, the AAR Component Registry) is a near-copy of the DLA sole-source problem: few approved makers and long committee approvals. It is a second vertical for the Approval Factory with private, faster approvers.
