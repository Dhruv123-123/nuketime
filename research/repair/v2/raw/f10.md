## Recalled landscape (not re-verified this session; flag as recalled)
### Legacy chip re-creation (permission or emulation houses)
- DLA GEM (Generalized Emulation of Microcircuits): DLA Land and Maritime program since the late 1980s. Originally Sarnoff, later SRI, with foundry work at BAE/"GEM" lines. It builds form-fit-function (F3) emulations of obsolete digital ICs on a gate-array/ASIC process, then qualifies them to MIL-PRF-38535 / SMD. Equivalence is shown by testing against the original part's datasheet and test vectors, often derived by characterising a "golden" original. It is not formal proof. (recalled)
- Lansdale Semiconductor, Rochester, Teledyne e2v (licensed PowerPC/legacy), Micross and Solitron: these re-manufacture from transferred masks/data, so permission is the model. (recalled)
- Innovasic (Albuquerque): "clean-room" re-creation of obsolete MCUs (80C186, 8051, Z80 families) by black-box behavioural characterisation and new RTL. Acquired by Analog Devices around 2019. This is the closest historical precedent to AI behavioural reconstruction. (recalled)
- Army/AF depots (Warner Robins 402nd SWEG, Hill, Tobyhanna, Ogden, NAVAIR Jacksonville/Cherry Point) do in-house circuit-card RE and FPGA re-hosting of obsolete logic, plus test-program-set (TPS) re-hosting. Typical industry lore puts a single circuit-card reverse engineering effort at months and roughly $50k-$500k. (recalled, opinion)
- PCB reverse-engineering shops (for example, various small "board cloning" services) and board-test vendors (ABI Electronics, Huntron signature analysis, DiagnoSYS, Teradyne/Astronics/Marvin legacy ATE) all exist. Netlist extraction from X-ray CT plus layer-by-layer delamination is done semi-manually with tools like Zeiss/Nikon CT, then hand-traced into CAD. (recalled)

### Chip imaging and netlist extraction
- TechInsights (Circuit Vision, recalled), Texplained (France), Battelle (hardware assurance), Riverside Research, Sandia, MITRE, and the DMEA "trusted" RE lab. Academic work includes automated SEM-image-to-netlist (Degate, open source), PSI X-ray ptychography for 3D nanoscale chip imaging (Holler et al., Nature 2017 and 2019), and DARPA programs IRIS (2010s, chip integrity via RE), TRUST and SHIELD. (recalled)
- Gate-level netlist to RTL "reverse synthesis" and "netlist abstraction" are academic topics (for example, functional identification of word-level blocks, as in Subramanyan et al. 2013 "Reverse engineering digital circuits using functional analysis"). (recalled)

### Equivalence and formal verification tooling
- Commercial logic equivalence checkers (LEC) include Cadence Conformal, Synopsys Formality and Siemens Questa Equivalence (Calypto SLEC for sequential/C-to-RTL). These prove RTL-to-gate equivalence between two designs that both exist. They cannot prove equivalence against a physical obsolete chip that has no netlist. (recalled)
- Formal methods and hardware-security companies: Galois, Cycuity (Radix information-flow), Caspia Technologies (UF FICS spinout, AI-plus-formal hardware security; caspiatech.com domain now parked, so status unknown), Axiomise, and ChipAgents / Cognichip / Normal Computing (AI for RTL verification, 2024-26). None of these targets legacy F3 replacement. (recalled)
- AI binary-lifting and firmware tools: GrammaTech, DARPA AMP (Assured Micropatching, 2020-) and V-SPELLS. (recalled)

## The verification pathway for a re-created electronic part (as it works today)
1. Obtain specimens: buy or pull golden originals, ideally several date codes. Cost runs from $ to $$$ and lead time can be months. Today this is ad hoc, and specimens are scarce or counterfeit-prone.
2. Non-destructive imaging: X-ray CT of the board or package. This is available (Lumafield, Zeiss, Nikon), but CT-to-netlist is semi-manual.
3. Destructive delayering: PCB lamination grind and imaging; IC decap, SEM delayer and image (RAVEN-style). This is done by a handful of labs. Image-to-netlist is partly automated and error-prone, and nobody publishes error-rate guarantees.
4. Electrical characterisation and signal capture: in-circuit logic analysers, boundary scan, power/timing/IV curves, analogue behaviour over temperature. This is done per job. No reusable "behavioural fingerprint" dataset exists.
5. Firmware extraction: reading PROM/EPROM/MCU flash. Fuse and protection bits block it, which leaves glitching or decap probing. There is a legal grey zone (DMCA 1201 and ITAR).
6. Model reconstruction: netlist to RTL, firmware disassembly to a behavioural spec. Today this is manual, takes weeks to months, and has a high-skill bottleneck.
7. Re-implementation: FPGA (radiation and temperature grade), ASIC or GEM gate array, or board redesign with modern parts. Timing quirks, undocumented behaviour, analogue edges, tri-state and bus contention all have to match.
8. Equivalence demonstration: today this is mostly vector-based comparison against the golden unit plus datasheet tests, i.e. testing-based. Formal LEC is applied only between the reconstructed RTL and the new implementation, never to the original silicon. Coverage claims are weak, and nobody quantifies the residual risk that the original has behaviours outside the tested vectors.
9. Qualification: MIL-PRF-38535 / 38534 / SMD, MIL-STD-883 screening, environmental (DO-160 for aviation, MIL-STD-810), and radiation for space. These are physics clocks (burn-in, temperature cycling, life test), typically 3-12 months.
10. Platform acceptance: engineering support activity (ESA) approval. This is the DLA SAR for the NSN, or an OEM/PCO engineering change (ECP) and form-fit-function determination. In aviation, DO-254 Design Assurance (DAL A-E) for complex electronic hardware (FPGA counts as complex) plus DO-178C for firmware, with FAA DER or EASA sign-off. DO-254 DAL A demands elemental analysis and requirements-based verification, so "equivalent to the obsolete part" is not an accepted DO-254 argument by itself.
11. Sustainment and configuration control: TDP delivered with rights, so the next obsolescence cycle is not another RE.

## What nobody does, or does well
- A quantified equivalence certificate against physical silicon or boards, i.e. "probability that the replacement differs from the original on any reachable input/state < epsilon", built by combining formal proof (reconstruction RTL to new design) with statistical/active-learning conformance testing (original silicon to reconstruction model). Today the link from the physical original to the model is just testing with ad-hoc coverage.
- Automated, error-bounded image-to-netlist for boards and legacy (0.35-5 micron) ICs with self-consistency checks (redundant modalities: CT plus delayer plus electrical probing, cross-checked).
- Active-learning hardware model inference: automata learning (L*-style) on a live golden part in a hardware-in-the-loop rig, where the AI chooses the next test vector to maximise disagreement between candidate models. This exists in academic "model learning" but not as a service.
- A reusable corpus of legacy part behaviour (fingerprints, errata, undocumented modes) across date codes. Each RE job starts from zero.
- A regulator-accepted methodology. No DLA, DMEA, MIL-PRF or DO-254 guidance accepts AI-reconstruction-plus-formal equivalence as a qualification credit. Acceptance is done case by case by an ESA engineer, and that engineer is the bottleneck.
- Independent T&E for AI reconstruction in hardware, i.e. a TRACTOR/Lincoln-Lab-style benchmark: a set of known chips and boards with hidden ground truth to measure reconstruction error rates. This does not exist publicly.

## Binding constraints and what would change them
- Physics clocks: burn-in, temperature cycling and life test cannot be skipped. They can be parallelised, and qualification-by-similarity can be argued if the replacement uses an already-qualified FPGA/ASIC platform. Change lever: a pre-qualified "universal emulation substrate" (one qualified FPGA/ASIC carrier per package family) so each new part inherits package and reliability qualification, and only functional equivalence is new. This is what GEM did with gate arrays (recalled). An AI-native GEM would do it at software speed.
- Law: DMCA 1201 (firmware protection circumvention), copyright in firmware/mask works (Semiconductor Chip Protection Act, 17 USC 902, 10-year term, so most legacy masks are expired), ITAR/EAR for military boards, and government data rights (DFARS 252.227-7013). Change lever: DFARS "deferred ordering", or government purpose rights asserted by the program office, which authorises RE for its own sustainment. The Air Force has publicly pushed RE of sole-source parts (Rapid Sustainment Office; Roper-era 2020 statements) (recalled).
- Regulator acceptance: the gap is a methodology document. Lever: get one ESA (for example, the AFLCMC C-130 or KC-135 SPO, or a DLA Land and Maritime engineering activity) to accept "formal + statistical equivalence certificate" as the F3 basis on a pilot NSN, then push it into MIL-STD/SAE (SAE G-19 / G-12 committees, or a DMSMS SD-22 guidebook update). For aviation, propose a DO-254 Appendix B-style alternate method or an FAA issue paper.
- Liability: today the product warrants F3. A quantified equivalence certificate makes the residual risk insurable and priceable.
- Data access: golden specimens and test data. Lever: depots' scrap and teardown streams, and DLA demilitarised returns. Build the corpus by RE-ing whatever crosses the bench.

## Who is the competition (summary)
- Direct (same F3-replacement-of-obsolete-electronics job): DLA GEM/SRI-BAE line, depot RE shops, Innovasic (ADI), small board-cloning houses, and defence primes' obsolescence groups. None is AI-native, and none sells quantified equivalence. (recalled)
- Adjacent: Rochester/Lansdale/Micross (licensed re-manufacture; permission model, which competes for the same budget); TechInsights/Texplained/Battelle (RE imaging); Lumafield/Zeiss (CT); Cybord (authenticity AI); Cadence/Synopsys/Siemens (LEC); and AI-RTL verification startups such as ChipAgents and Cognichip (recalled).

## The AI play on the verification side (opinion)
"Equivalence-as-a-service for legacy electronics": an AI-native lab and software stack that takes golden originals and outputs (a) a reconstructed model (netlist/RTL/firmware/behavioural spec), (b) a replacement implementation on a pre-qualified substrate, and (c) a machine-checkable equivalence dossier. The dossier combines formal LEC (model to replacement), active-learning conformance testing with quantified coverage (original to model), and multi-modal reconstruction cross-checks, formatted as the evidence an ESA / DLA SAR / DO-254 reviewer needs. The KPI is reviewer-hours per approval, not just engineering hours. The moat is the corpus of legacy part behaviour plus the first regulator acceptance of the methodology. A second product: sell the equivalence engine to depots and primes, and to DLA as an independent verifier of others' replacements (the TRACTOR T&E analogue).
