## 1. The framework (what exists)
- **ASME V&V 40-2018**, "Assessing Credibility of Computational Modeling through Verification and Validation: Application to Medical Devices". Fetched from asme.org. It is currently on stabilized maintenance and remains in effect.
  - Required credibility is set by **model influence** (how much the decision leans on the model) multiplied by **decision consequence** (the harm if the decision is wrong).
  - The standard relies on engineering judgment, not a fixed formula.
  - It builds on V&V 10 (solid mechanics) and V&V 20 (CFD and heat transfer).
  - Sibling standards (recalled): V&V 50 for advanced manufacturing, V&V 60 for energy systems, and a newer ML/AI and V&V 70 effort.
  - FDA lists V&V 40 as a recognized consensus standard (recalled).
- **FDA guidance**, "Assessing the Credibility of Computational Modeling and Simulation in Medical Device Submissions". Draft Dec 2021; final 17 Nov 2023 (recalled; the Federal Register document 2023-25390 URL exists but could not be fetched).
  - The steps are: question of interest -> context of use (COU) -> model risk -> credibility goals -> credibility evidence -> adequacy assessment.
  - It defines **8 categories of credibility evidence** (recalled):
    1. code verification
    2. model calibration
    3. bench validation
    4. in vivo validation
    5. population-based evidence
    6. emergent model behavior
    7. model plausibility
    8. calculation verification / UQ
  - It is used alongside the 2016 guidance "Reporting of Computational Modeling Studies in Medical Device Submissions" (recalled).
- **MDDT program (CDRH Medical Device Development Tools)**. Final guidance Aug 2017 (recalled).
  - There are three tool categories: Clinical Outcome Assessment, Biomarker Test, and Nonclinical Assessment Model (NAM). The NAM category is where in-silico tools sit.
  - A tool is qualified *for a context of use*. After qualification, any sponsor can cite it without FDA re-reviewing the tool's validity for that COU.
  - Roughly 15-20 tools have been qualified since 2017 (recalled). Very few are computational.
  - The headline computational one (recalled): **IMAnalytics** (ZMT Zurich MedTech / IT'IS Foundation), qualified around 2021. It assesses MRI RF-induced heating and tissue exposure for implants using Virtual Population anatomical models.
  - Others are mostly phantoms, questionnaires and lab methods.
  - Literature search shows groups (e.g., Hart et al. 2023, J Pathol, PMID 37794720) still at the *initial interaction* stage for a validation-dataset MDDT. Qualification is slow and rare.
- **Related drug-side frameworks** (recalled):
  - FDA's PBPK guidances.
  - Model-Informed Drug Development (MIDD) paired meeting program, 2018-.
  - The FDA Modernization Act 2.0 (Dec 2022) removed the statutory animal-testing mandate for drugs.
  - In Apr 2025, FDA announced a roadmap to phase out animal testing for monoclonal antibodies in favor of NAMs, including in silico.
  - EMA has qualification opinions for model-based tools.

## 2. Cases where simulation replaced or reduced physical or clinical tests
1. **UVA/Padova Type 1 Diabetes Simulator** (recalled). FDA accepted it in 2008 as a *substitute for preclinical animal trials* of closed-loop insulin control algorithms. This is the clearest "replaced a test" precedent, and it has been used in many artificial-pancreas IDEs.
2. **MRI RF heating of active implants** (recalled). ISO/TS 10974 tiered approach.
   - Labeling for MR-Conditional pacemakers, ICDs and neurostimulators (e.g., Medtronic SureScan) rests on simulation across virtual human populations (ViP), plus bench transfer-function measurements, in place of in vivo heating measurement.
   - This is routine practice and the source of the IMAnalytics MDDT.
3. **VICTRE** (fetched). Badano et al., JAMA Netw Open 2018;1(7):e185474, doi 10.1001/jamanetworkopen.2018.5474.
   - 2,986 virtual patients, Monte Carlo x-ray imaging and a computational reader.
   - It reproduced the clinical-trial finding that DBT beats digital mammography (delta AUC 0.0587, P<.001; larger for masses than calcifications).
   - FDA authors concluded in-silico trials are a "viable source of regulatory evidence". It was positioned as replacing a reader study that cost millions and took years (recalled).
4. **Bayesian augmentation with engineering models** (recalled). Haddad et al. 2017 (J Biopharm Stat) is an FDA/Medtronic paper. It used virtual-patient lead-fracture simulations as an informative prior in a Bayesian clinical trial, reducing the required enrolled patients (reported reduction in trial size on the order of tens of percent).
5. **Stents and orthopedics** (recalled). FEA is accepted for worst-case size and configuration selection, which reduces the number of bench fatigue tests:
   - ASTM F2514 / F2477 stent fatigue
   - FDA non-clinical stent guidance
   - spinal and knee implant worst-case selection
   It rarely eliminates the final bench test. Simulation *selects* what gets physically tested.
6. **Drugs: PBPK in place of clinical DDI studies** (recalled). FDA reviews hundreds of PBPK analyses. Simcyp (Certara) models routinely *waive* dedicated clinical drug-drug-interaction trials and pediatric dose-finding studies, and some labels are written from simulation alone.
7. **Digital-twin control arms** (recalled). Unlearn.AI's PROCOVA received an EMA qualification opinion in Sept 2022, with FDA concurrence. It uses AI-generated prognostic digital twins to shrink control arms (around 20-35% smaller trials). Unlearn has raised about $130M+.
8. **CiPA** (recalled). This in-silico ventricular myocyte model (O'Hara-Rudy) is proposed to reduce reliance on the thorough-QT clinical study. ICH E14/S7B Q&As (2020-2022) allow nonclinical plus in-silico data to substitute in some cases.
9. **Living Heart Project** (recalled). Dassault SIMULIA launched it in 2014. In 2019 FDA signed a 5-year collaborative research agreement on an in-silico clinical trial for mitral valve repair devices (a virtual cohort intended to supplement or replace part of a clinical trial). No public approval wholly based on it is known.

Pattern: simulation earns acceptance in three steps:
- (a) it **selects** worst cases (common)
- (b) it **augments** a physical or clinical dataset as a prior or covariate (growing)
- (c) it **replaces** a test entirely (rare: T1D simulator, MRI heating, PBPK DDI)

Replacement happens where the physics is well bounded, a validation comparator exists, and the regulator itself co-built or qualified the model.

## 3. Players (verification side, medtech/pharma)
- **Certara (Simcyp)**. Public (NASDAQ: CERT), roughly $350-400M revenue (recalled). Its biosimulation is the most accepted.
- **Dassault Systemes SIMULIA** (Living Heart) and **Ansys** (now part of Synopsys, deal closed 2025, recalled). These sell solvers. Credibility work is left to the user.
- **ZMT Zurich MedTech / IT'IS** (Sim4Life, IMAnalytics). Owns the only well-known computational MDDT.
- **InSilicoTrials** (Italy; fetched). Pharma-focused trial-simulation platform with an "IRIS" multi-agent layer. It claims FDA/EMA alignment and co-authorship of "Toward Good Simulation Practice" with FDA. The site shows no regulatory acceptances.
- **Novadiscovery** (France) and **VeriSIM Life** (US). Drug-side in-silico trials (recalled).
- **FEops** (Belgium, HEARTguide; FDA-cleared AI and simulation for TAVR/LAAO planning, recalled; acquired by Materialise 2024, recalled). **Virtonomy** (Germany; virtual-patient device testing, recalled). **Unlearn.AI** (digital twins, EMA-qualified).
- **Exponent and consultancies**. Do V&V 40 credibility dossiers by hand.
- **Avicenna Alliance / VPH Institute / In Silico World (EU H2020)**. Policy and standards groups, including ISO work on in-silico standards (recalled).

Nobody visible sells an automated **credibility-dossier generator**: a tool that takes a model and a COU and produces the V&V 40 risk assessment, verification and UQ evidence, validation-comparator design, and an applicability argument in regulator-ready form. Today this is bespoke, hand-assembled engineering paperwork.

## 4. Transfer to the Approval Factory (aviation and defense parts)
- **Structural analogy.** FAA PMA "test and computation" and DER substantiation, and DLA SAR first-article and qualification testing, are the same problem as V&V 40. In each case the regulator must decide whether the model plus limited tests is credible enough for this part in this use.
- **What aviation lacks.**
  - There is no MDDT-like *qualify-once, cite-many* program for computational tools. Each DER or applicant argues credibility per project.
  - The closest analogs are shared materials allowables: MMPDS, CMH-17 and NCAMP (Wichita State), which let one qualification dataset serve many applicants (recalled).
  - NASA's 2021 "Certification by Analysis 2040" vision study (recalled) names the gaps: model credibility, UQ, and an agreed process. None of these is institutionalized at FAA or DLA.
- **Physics clocks.** Fatigue, creep and environmental tests still run in real time. The medtech precedent shows simulation shrinks the *number* of physical tests (worst-case selection, Bayesian priors) long before it removes the last one.
- **Liability and acceptance.** FDA accepted replacement only where FDA co-developed or qualified the model (UVA/Padova, VICTRE, ViP/IMAnalytics). That implies the play must include co-development with the regulator (an FAA/DLA CRADA, like the FDA-Dassault 5-year agreement), not just a better solver.

## 5. What would need to be done (nobody does it today)
1. **A V&V 40-style credibility standard for sustainment parts.** It would map model influence x decision consequence to required evidence for PMA/SAR part classes (e.g., Class 1 rotating parts vs non-critical brackets). It could be pushed through ASME (V&V committee), SAE or RTCA, with FAA AC recognition.
2. **An "MDDT for aviation/DLA": qualified computational tools per context of use.** Examples are castings porosity-to-fatigue models, bearing L10 models, and corrosion-allowance models. One qualification would let every SAR or PMA cite the tool. This needs a CRADA or OTA with FAA Tech Center / AFRL / DLA R&D.
3. **An automated credibility-dossier engine.** AI would generate the COU and risk assessment, run code and calculation verification, propagate uncertainty, design the minimal validation test matrix (Bayesian optimal design), and draft the regulator-formatted report and traceability. The KPI is reviewer hours per approval.
4. **Bayesian test-reduction protocols.** Use fleet and teardown data plus simulation as a prior so that first-article and qualification tests need fewer coupons, mirroring Haddad 2017 and PROCOVA. This requires the regulator to agree the statistical method in advance, which is precedent-able.
5. **A shared validation-comparator library.** This is a materials, process and failure database (like NCAMP) owned by the factory and licensed to DERs. Data access is the moat.
6. **Regulator-side tooling.** Give FAA ACO and DLA engineering-support-activity reviewers AI tools to check dossiers. FDA itself built VICTRE in-house, so the regulator is a customer.

## 6. AI plays on the verification side
- **A. Credibility-as-a-service / dossier compiler.** Sell to DERs, PMA houses and DLA SAR applicants, then to medtech, which has a proven framework and is a bigger near-term market. Lowest regulatory risk because it produces evidence humans sign.
- **B. Qualified-tool owner.** Build one model and qualify it via an FAA/DLA CRADA for a COU that hits hundreds of SARs. That makes a toll road: every applicant cites your tool.
- **C. Test-matrix minimizer.** Use Bayesian optimal experimental design to cut physical coupons and first articles. It pays per test avoided.
- **D. Reviewer copilot.** Sell to regulators and primes (DLA ESA, Air Force engineering authority) to clear SAR backlogs.
- **E. Vertical integration (Approval Factory).** Do A to C in-house and keep the approvals. Highest value, and it uses the credibility engine as the internal KPI lever.
