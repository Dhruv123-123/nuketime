# s11: Continued airworthiness and in-service feedback for non-OEM parts (failure reporting, SDRs, PMA part reliability data). Is there any evidence PMA/non-OEM parts fail more or less? Who tracks it?

## Bottom line

Nobody can answer "do PMA parts fail more or less" with a population-level rate. Not the FAA, not MARPA, not the OEMs. The reason is structural: the US system collects numerators (failure reports) but not denominators (installed base and hours per part number and per approval holder). It also does not reliably record whether a failed part was OEM or PMA. The best evidence is (a) one FAA qualitative review (2008/09 RAF Team) that found "no substantial evidence" of systemic PMA problems, and (b) individual ADs where FAA did compute a rough comparative rate. The clearest is ECi PMA cylinders, which separated at roughly 32x the OEM rate. Industry "equal or better" claims (IATA, MARPA, HEICO, trade press) are opinion, not measured data.

For the Approval Factory, this is a verification-side gap: a continuous, part-number-level, approval-holder-tagged reliability ledger with denominators. If it existed, it would turn "prove equivalence before approval" into "prove it in service and keep proving it". That is the evidence regulators and DLA engineering support activities (ESAs) would need to accept faster or lighter-weight approvals.

## Evidence (sourced)

### 1. What the FAA's own review found (qualitative, no rates)
- FAA AVS Repair, Alteration and Fabrication (RAF) Team Study. Published for comment in the Federal Register on 2008-09-05 (E8-20460), final issue 2009-01-30, Rev 1 2009-05-22. https://www.pmaparts.org/gvt/raftreport.pdf
  - Quote: "the team did not find any substantial evidence of failures or unsafe conditions arising from non-TC/PC holder developed data that would indicate a systemic lack of compliance or capability ... yet the occurrence of service difficulties and airworthiness directives on such parts ... have not increased proportionally."
  - Quote: "there were occasions where a PMA part or non-TC/PC holder repairs or alterations were associated with failures in service and occasionally with accidents." ADs have been written against PMA parts. In some cases PMA holders "unknowingly replicated design flaws that existed in the TC holder's type design", and the AD then covered both OEM and PMA parts.
  - On tracking: "aftermarket companies do not have tracking systems that are as comprehensive as theirs [OEMs']. The team concluded this is likely true in most cases ... there is no requirement for manufacturers and fabricators of parts to track them or to have a tracking system."
  - Honeywell comment (Appendix): "OEMs can only be responsible for tracking reliability data for parts manufactured by the OEM. Much of this data is considered proprietary and cannot be shared with competitor companies." Honeywell also says aftermarket parties are "routinely" missing from investigations.
  - This is the source of the widely repeated trade-press claim that "FAA, Transport Canada and EASA found no statistical evidence" (AMU Magazine, https://amumagazine.com/?p=2631). The primary documents I found are FAA-only and qualitative. The "statistical" wording is a paraphrase I could not verify.
- FAA SAIB NE-08-40 (2008-08-08), https://www.pmaparts.org/pdf/2008-08-08SAIBonTCPCSupport.pdf. It contains no data. It states PMA/STC/OEM parts are interchangeable and that life limits on OEM parts are unchanged by adjacent PMA parts. Key admission: "the TC/PC holder has no knowledge or data about the PMA and STC parts installed in the product." Each approval holder owns continued operational safety (COS) for its own parts, so COS data is split by design.

### 2. The one hard comparative number: ECi / Airmotive Engineering PMA cylinders
- AD 2016-16-12 (FR 2016-08-11, Docket FAA-2012-0002), https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2016-08-11/html/2016-18708.htm
  - "The rate of separation for the affected ECi cylinder assemblies is at least 32 times greater than that of OEM cylinder assemblies over the same period." About 4x as many OEM cylinders are in service, yet ECi had more head separations since 2004. Elsewhere in the same AD: "eight times the number of OEM separations, even though only one-quarter as many ECI cylinders were in service".
  - Data used: ECi and RAM Aircraft warranty reports, SDRs, and other field reports. FAA cites "under-reporting of cylinder assembly cracks in the SDR system".
  - FAA on hours-based rates: "Without knowing the total number of hours flown on all affected cylinders, it is not possible to accurately calculate an hours-based failure rate. This data is not available for general aviation aircraft." This is the denominator problem in the regulator's own words.
  - Scale: about 6,200 US engines affected, estimated cost $88.6M (industry estimated $169M–$320M). Two fatal accidents cited. The rulemaking ran from the 2013 NPRM through two SNPRMs and drew over 500 comments before the 2016 final rule.
- Other PMA-specific ADs found via the Federal Register API:
  - Superior Air Parts investment-cast cylinders, AD 2009-16-03, superseded 2014-03-21.
  - SAP crankshaft, AD issued 2020-12-11.
  - SAP intake valves, AD issued 2023-02-02, after 3 valve failures.
  - AVStar fuel servo diaphragm, 2012.
  - A 2015 SAP piston pin NPRM was withdrawn.
  - Pattern: PMA ADs cluster in piston-GA engine parts. I found none in a quick scan that singles out a transport-category PMA part, though some ADs cover OEM and PMA parts together.

### 3. Why the SDR system cannot answer the question
- Current 14 CFR 121.703(e) required contents: aircraft, operator, phase, nature of failure, "affected part and system", apparent cause, disposition, grounded or not. **Part manufacturer and part number are not required fields.** (eCFR, current.)
- The FAA tried to fix this. The 2000 final rule (65 FR 56192, 2000-09-15) would have required "the manufacturer, manufacturer part number, part name, serial number, and location of the part". The FAA said it was prompted by an internal FAA review and "air carrier industry concern over the quality of the data being reported". It was delayed five times, then proposed for withdrawal on 2005-09-14 (70 FR, doc 05-18176). Reasons given: industry cost objections, plus "populating data collection systems with inappropriate data could have a negative impact". The 2001 Commercial Airplane Certification Process Study had identified "underlying deficiencies in the SDR program". The effect of withdrawal is "the retention of the regulation currently in effect."
- The SDRS front end (https://sdrs.faa.gov) does have Section 5 "Specific Part ... Causing Difficulty" and Section 6 "Component/Assembly" fields. Part number and manufacturer can be entered and are looked up, but they are free and optional. GA reporting (Form 8010-4) is voluntary. Mandatory reporting covers Parts 91K/121/125/135/145.
- 14 CFR 21.3: PMA holders must report failures in a listed set of serious occurrences within 24 hours. They are exempt if they judge the cause to be improper maintenance or the failure was already reported by someone else. This captures severe events only, gives no reliability rates, and depends on the holder's own judgement.
- MARPA COS guidance (MA-07-0316 Rev 4, 2022-03-21, https://pmaparts.org/government/pdf/COSGuidance.pdf) is voluntary. It recommends "Article Specific Performance Data Trend Analysis" and a closed loop for field inquiries. It also tells PMA applicants to mine SDRs, ADs and operator surveys for OEM field problems *before* designing. So SDRs are used today mainly as a design-input source (finding OEM weak points), not as a PMA-vs-OEM scoreboard.
- IATA "Guidance Material and Best Practices for Alternate Parts (PMA) and Approved (non-OEM) Repairs (DER)", 2nd ed., March 2015, https://www.iata.org/contentassets/d1ca23709ff94de6a30e8837952a57bf/pma-der-2nd-edition.pdf
  - Recommends: "The reliability system should distinguish between OEM parts and related PMA parts." So segregation is left to each airline's reliability program. Results are not pooled or published.
  - Claims "Experience shows that PMA parts ... are just as reliable", citing only the RAF report. This is advocacy (IATA represents airlines that want PMA competition).

### 4. Who tracks what, by segment
| Tracker | Scope | Sees PMA vs OEM? | Has denominator? | Public? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FAA SDRS (AFS-620, Oklahoma City) | All US reports, mandatory for 121/135/145 | Only if part number or manufacturer is entered (optional) | No | Yes (query) |
| FAA 21.3 reports | Serious failures, by the design or production approval holder | Yes (holder reports own parts) | No | No |
| FAA ASIAS (with MITRE) | Airline FOQA/ASAP plus SDR fusion | Not part-level (recalled) | Partial (flight hours) | No |
| Airline reliability programs (CAMP / Part 121 Subpart L; ATA Spec 2000 Ch. 11 reliability data) | Own fleet, MTBUR/MTBF per part number | Yes, if they segregate (IATA recommends it) | Yes | No, kept proprietary |
| OEMs (GE, P&W, Honeywell, Collins) | Their own parts; shop findings from their MRO networks | Only their own parts | Yes (for own parts) | No ("proprietary, cannot be shared") |
| PMA holders (HEICO, Wencor, Jet Parts Engineering, ECi...) | Warranty returns and customer reports | Own parts only | Sometimes (sales volume) | No |
| EASA occurrence reporting (Reg. 376/2014, ECCAIRS) | EU | Not systematically (recalled) | No | Partial |
| DoD JDRS (Joint Deficiency Reporting System, NAVAIR-hosted) / PQDRs (joint regulation DLAR 4155.24 / AR 702-7 / SECNAVINST 4855.5 / AFI 21-115) (recalled) | Military product quality deficiencies | Yes, by CAGE/source | No | No |
| PDREP (NAVSEA Portsmouth) -> SPRS supplier risk scores (recalled) | Supplier quality history across DoD | Yes, by CAGE | No | Controlled |
| GIDEP | Failure experience, nonconformance, counterfeit alerts | Yes | No | Members only |

Military note (recalled): aviation critical safety items (CSIs) from alternate sources must be approved by the design control activity (FY2004 NDAA §802, formerly 10 USC 2319(g), now recodified). This followed early-2000s GAO findings of defective alternate-source parts. The military has supplier-tagged deficiency data (PQDR/JDRS by CAGE code). This is closer to what is needed than the FAA SDR, but it still lacks installed-base and flight-hour denominators by part number and source, and it is fragmented across services.

## Evidence vs opinion summary
- EVIDENCE that PMA can fail more: ECi cylinders (32x OEM separation rate, FAA-computed, 2016). There are also several SAP PMA ADs. All are piston GA.
- EVIDENCE of no systemic problem: the FAA RAF Team (2008/09). It is qualitative: AD and SDR counts did not rise in proportion to the PMA population. It offered no rates, and no denominator was available to compute them.
- OPINION that PMA is "equal or better": MARPA, IATA, HEICO, and trade press (AviationPros 2017, AMU 2014). No published MTBUR comparisons.
- OPINION that PMA is worse: OEM manuals and OEM statements (which the FAA rebuked in SAIB NE-08-40). Also lessor "OEM-only" lease clauses, which are commercial terms and reflect no measured reliability difference.
- Plausible but unverified: airlines such as Delta TechOps, United and Lufthansa Technik (HEICO JV) hold internal PMA-vs-OEM MTBUR data. That data has never been published in aggregate.

## What nobody does today
1. Part-number-level, source-tagged, denominator-bearing reliability across operators. There is no "PMA vs OEM MTBUR registry".
2. Mandatory part number and manufacturer in SDRs. The FAA tried in 2000 and abandoned it in 2005 over cost.
3. Linking SDRs, warranty returns, shop teardown findings, 8130-3 tags and installed-base records into one graph. The 8130-3 and 21.3 data exist but sit in silos and on paper.
4. Statistical equivalence monitoring after approval, for example sequential tests showing PMA failure rate is no worse than OEM rate with X% confidence after N fleet hours. No regulator requires it, and no one offers it as a service.
5. Military: fusing JDRS/PQDR/SPRS with flight hours (REMIS/G081/IMDS for the Air Force, recalled) for reverse-engineered parts, so the ESA can accept alternate sources based on field evidence.

## What it would take (assume abundant compute and intelligence)
- **Data access, the binding constraint.** Airline reliability data (Spec 2000 Ch. 11 is a standard format), MRO teardown reports, and OEM shop findings are all proprietary. Possible levers:
  - Give operators free reliability benchmarking in exchange for contributing data (the CAMP/ASIAS model).
  - A trusted third party or federated learning, so raw data never leaves the operator.
  - Buy MRO shops and component repair stations to get teardown data first-hand.
  - Military: Government Purpose Rights on the data, through a CRADA or OTA with the Rapid Sustainment Office or DLA.
- **Regulator acceptance.** FAA Order 8110.42 (PMA) already allows "comparative analysis". A service-history-based means of compliance, or reduced-test approvals justified by strong field equivalence, would need FAA AIR policy or an issue paper. EASA would require bilateral acceptance.
  - Path: first use the ledger as a COS system (voluntary under MARPA guidance, low regulatory risk).
  - Then petition to use it to justify lighter re-approvals, part-number family extensions, or DLA Source Approval Request (SAR) waivers where "similar item" approval is allowed.
- **Physics clocks.** Field failures accumulate slowly. A rare-failure part may need millions of fleet hours to show equivalence statistically. AI cannot speed up the arrival of failures, but it can do three things:
  - Pool hours across every operator.
  - Use leading indicators (wear measurements at teardown, borescope images, oil debris, vibration) instead of waiting for failures.
  - Use Bayesian priors from physics models, with a digital twin per part number, to cut the hours needed.
- **Law and liability.** Reporting data that implicates your own part creates discovery exposure. Both OEMs and PMA holders have reason not to look too closely. Fixes include protected-data status, as with ASIAS and 49 USC 44735 protection of voluntarily submitted safety data (recalled), and owning the analysis yourself as the approval holder.

## Players (adjacent; none does a PMA-vs-OEM reliability ledger) — mostly recalled
- Airbus Skywise (Palantir-based; includes reliability modules), Boeing AnalytX / AHM, GE Digital / GE Aerospace. These are OEM platforms that see only their own parts and favour OEM parts.
- Lufthansa Technik AVIATAR, AFI KLM E&M Prognos. MRO and airline-owned predictive maintenance.
- MRO software (Swiss-AS AMOS, IFS Maintenix, Ramco, TRAX, Veryon). Hold the raw removal records, but each operator's data stays in its own system.
- C3 AI PANDA (USAF Rapid Sustainment Office predictive maintenance), Uptake (DoD). Military predictive maintenance by aircraft tail, not by source.
- MITRE / FAA ASIAS. Fleet safety data fusion, not part-source reliability.
- HEICO, Wencor (Arcline), Jet Parts Engineering, Aero Precision. PMA holders tracking only their own warranty data.
- MARPA. Voluntary COS guidance; no data pool.
