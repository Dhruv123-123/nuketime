## 1. The pathway this layer sits on
A part's "permission to fly" today is a **paper claim from an issuer**, not proof attached to the part. The chain runs like this:
1. **Design approval.** TC/STC/PMA/DER repair, or a DLA SAR/TDP, defines the approved design.
2. **Production approval and conformity.** A PAH quality system, FAI (AS9102), and DAR/ODA/DCMA conformity inspection show a given part matches that design.
3. **Release certificate.** FAA 8130-3, EASA Form 1, TCCA Form One, or for DoD a DD250/WAWF receiving report plus a CoC. The certificate refers to a part number and serial or batch.
4. **Identity marking.** Data plate or serial, Spec 2000 Ch.9 2D/RFID marking (recalled), or DoD IUID under MIL-STD-130 / DFARS 252.211-7003 for items of $5k or more (recalled).
5. **Life history.** Installation, removal, shop visits and new 8130-3s. Life-limited parts (LLPs) need "back-to-birth" trace (recalled).
6. **Receiving inspection and counterfeit screening.** Rules include AS5553/AS6174 and DFARS 252.246-7007/7008 (recalled). DLA added DNA marking for FSC 5962 microcircuits in 2012 using Applied DNA Sciences' SigNature (recalled).

**The structural gap: no step physically binds the certificate to the object.** A serial number is a printed claim that anyone can copy. The certificate states that a part conforms; it carries no evidence (inspection data, scans, material chemistry). The digital thread, where it exists, stops at the OEM's factory door.

Motivating event: **AOG Technics (UK, 2023)** sold CFM56/CF6 parts with forged EASA Form 1s to major airlines. The forged parts were found on ~100+ engines and its director was prosecuted (recalled). Paper certificates failed, and a forged certificate is indistinguishable from a real one until someone phones the issuer.

## 3. Gaps (what nobody does or does well)
1. **Binding gap.** No one cryptographically binds certificate to object. Signing a certificate proves who issued it, not which physical piece of metal it describes.
2. **Unserialised parts.** Bushings, fasteners, seals and most of the C-130/KC-135 consumables that an Approval Factory would make have no unique identity. Only a batch CoC exists.
3. **Evidence-free certificates.** An 8130-3 asserts conformity but carries no CMM/CT/chemistry/hardness data or hashes of it, so every buyer re-trusts the issuer and re-inspects.
4. **No neutral registry or trust root.** There is no equivalent of TLS "Certificate Transparency" for parts: no public append-only log where anyone can check that a certificate exists, was not revoked, and was not issued twice for the same serial.
5. **Regulator acceptance of physical identity.** There is no FAA AC, EASA AMC or SAE standard for mechanical-fingerprint false-accept/false-reject rates, durability through overhaul (stripping, peening, recoating, wear), or who holds the reference database.
6. **Legacy fleets.** The DoD digital thread is forward-looking. Nobody back-fills proof onto the existing inventory at depots and in DLA stock.
7. **Records reconstruction.** "Paperless" or incomplete-trace LLPs and USM lose most of their value. Rebuilding their history is manual detective work.
8. **Evidence-to-approval loop.** Conformity evidence from production does not flow back as statistical evidence to justify risk-based oversight, such as reduced first-article testing or skipped source inspection for reverse-engineered parts.

## 4. Binding constraints, and what it would take to change each
- **Physics clocks.** A fingerprint's survival across life must be physically tested: thermal cycles, corrosion, overhaul processes. The work can be parallelised with accelerated-ageing coupons, but it cannot be skipped. One-time cost per material and process family.
- **Law and regulator acceptance.** Electronic certificates are already legal (FAA 8130.21H, AC 120-78A). Recognising fingerprints needs an SAE G-19 (counterfeit) or G-21 committee standard plus an FAA AC. The route is to run a pilot with DLA first, since DLA already mandated DNA marking for microcircuits, so the precedent exists.
- **Liability.** A false accept passes an unapproved part. The registry operator needs E&O insurance and a protocol in which the fingerprint is an extra check, never a replacement for the certificate, until the data supports more.
- **Data access.** OEMs hold birth records and resist a neutral registry. Entry points that avoid OEMs are DLA, USM brokers after AOG Technics, and lessors who need back-to-birth trace at lease return.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side
1. **Proof-carrying output from the Approval Factory itself.** Every reverse-engineered part ships with a signed evidence bundle: hashes of CT, CMM, chemistry and process logs; the equivalence argument; and the part's surface fingerprint. This bundle is what lets DLA or FAA move the factory to risk-based oversight. It is the strongest fit because it lowers reviewer-hours per approval, which is the stated KPI.
2. **Automated conformity oracle.** Robotic CT/CMM/XRF/hardness cells plus AI produce machine-checkable conformity claims against the TDP. The pitch is a replacement for human DAR/DCMA conformity inspection and first article testing at the regulator's desk.
3. **Certificate forensics and registry for USM.** AI ingests scanned 8130-3s and Form 1s, cross-checks issuer, signature, serial uniqueness and duplicate use, and flags forgeries. The registry grows into a "Certificate Transparency log" for parts. Adjacent players: Aerotrax, ePlaneAI, flydocs.
4. **Back-to-birth reconstruction.** Agents rebuild LLP and USM history from fragmentary records, logbooks and shop-visit reports. Value comes from recovering the price gap between traced and untraced material.
5. **Legacy fleet fingerprint sweep.** Depot and DLA inventory is scanned, fingerprinted and given a retroactive digital twin. Each item becomes a verified reference for reverse engineering, which helps feedstock for play 1.

## Opinion
- PUFs alone are a feature, not a company.
- The venture-scale wedge is the evidence bundle plus the registry, owned by whoever produces lots of approvals. That makes it a moat for the Approval Factory rather than a separate startup, unless it is sold to DLA as counterfeit infrastructure.
