## 1. What "hardware provenance verification" is today: the actual pathway

The rules and standards that create the obligation:
- **DFARS 252.246-7008 (Sources of Electronic Parts, Jan 2023)**, fetched: buy from the OCM or an authorized source first. Otherwise use a "contractor-approved supplier", keep risk-based **traceability from the OCM to Government acceptance**, and if traceability can't be shown, **inspect, test and authenticate under industry standards**, notify the CO in writing, and keep the records (FAR 4.7). The contractor is liable for authenticity. The clause flows down to subcontracts, including commercial items. https://www.acquisition.gov/dfars/252.246-7008-sources-electronic-parts.
- DFARS 252.246-7007 (counterfeit detection and avoidance system), from NDAA FY2012 s.818 (recalled). Counterfeit parts are an unallowable cost, and GIDEP reporting is mandatory (recalled).
- Industry standards (recalled): SAE AS5553 (counterfeit avoidance, OEM side), AS6081 (independent distributors), AS6171 (test methods: external visual, XRF, X-ray, C-SAM, decap and die verification, SEM, electrical, burn-in, with test levels chosen by risk), AS6174 (materiel other than electronics), and IDEA-STD-1010 visual inspection.
- NIST SP 800-161r1 (May 2022, updated Nov 2024) (recalled): C-SCRM *practices*. It is guidance and does not define a test.
- CMMC 2.0: the 32 CFR 170 program rule took effect Dec 16, 2024, and the 48 CFR DFARS acquisition rule took effect Nov 10, 2025, with a phased rollout (recalled). It covers CUI protection (NIST 800-171) and says almost nothing about the provenance of hardware delivered to DoD.
- NERC CIP-013-2 (effective Oct 2022) (recalled): a utility must have a supply chain risk-management *plan* (vendor notification, verifying software integrity and authenticity, and so on). It does not require anyone to physically verify hardware. Fortress's A2V and NAESAD exist because utilities pool vendor questionnaires to meet CIP-013.
- CISA HBOM Framework (Sep 25, 2023), fetched: voluntary naming and format guidance. It is not mandatory and has no verification method. https://www.cisa.gov/resources-tools/resources/hardware-bill-materials-hbom-framework-supply-chain-risk-management
- **New hard-provenance mandates (the real demand driver):**
  - BIS Connected Vehicles ICTS rule, fetched: issued Jan 14 2025, effective Mar 17 2025. Software prohibitions start MY2027. **VCS hardware import prohibition starts MY2030 / Jan 1 2029.** Importers and OEMs must file annual **Declarations of Conformity**, and the BIS page names no evidence standard. https://www.bis.gov/connected-vehicles
  - NDAA FY2023 s.5949 (recalled): from about **Dec 23 2027**, federal agencies may not buy products or services that contain semiconductors from SMIC, YMTC or CXMT (or their affiliates). Proving that a commercial board has no die from these firms is a die-level provenance problem that paperwork cannot settle.
  - Similar rules (recalled): FCC Covered List and equipment authorization limits, the drone rules (American Security Drone Act, NDAA FY2025 DJI language), the battery FEOC rules (NDAA FY2024 s.154 CATL/BYD ban from Oct 2027), and the inverter and grid-equipment Chinese-component concerns behind the 2025 "rogue communication devices" reports.

How it is actually done (recalled, typical):
1. **Paper trail**: CoC from the OCM or distributor, lot and date codes, distributor AS6081 or AS9120 cert, plus ERAI/GIDEP database checks.
2. **Lab test for anything off the authorized chain**: an independent AS6171 lab runs visual inspection plus XRF plus X-ray, decap for die marking, and electrical tests. This costs roughly $500 to $5,000+ per lot depending on the test level and takes about 1 to 3 weeks (recalled, order of magnitude). It is destructive and done on a sample.
3. **On the production line**: AOI and AXI check placement quality, not authenticity. Cybord is the exception (see below).
4. **Compliance attestation**: SPRS/CMMC for cyber, CIP-013 plans, and BIS Declarations of Conformity. All are **self-attested or questionnaire-based**. Nothing ties an attestation to physical evidence about the delivered serial number.

Conclusion: **nobody connects physical evidence about a specific serial or lot to a legal attestation that a regulator accepts.** Risk-graph firms (Exiger, Altana, Fortress) handle the paper and supplier side. Inspection firms (Cybord, Lumafield) produce physical evidence but sell it as quality data, not as a compliance artifact accepted under DFARS 7008, s.5949 or BIS. Test labs are slow, manual, destructive and sample-based.

## 3. What's broken or missing (what would need to be done)

1. **No accepted AI/NDE test method in AS6171.** The method list predates AI and is built around a human examiner. Needed: an SAE G-19 sub-method (or a DLA/DMEA-recognized alternative) that accepts **non-destructive, 100%, AI-scored evidence** (multi-angle optical plus XRF plus CT plus electrical fingerprint) as equal to or better than destructive sampling. Getting there takes a validation corpus with known-counterfeit ground truth and published ROC curves. The validation is the moat.
2. **No die-origin reference library.** s.5949 and the FEOC battery and drone rules need answers to "which fab made this die / cell / module". Needed: a large reference corpus of die photos, CT volumes and electrical/side-channel fingerprints keyed to fab and process node (SMIC 28nm vs. TSMC 28nm and so on), built by buying and decapping parts at scale. Physics clock: decapping is destructive and costs about $100 to $1k per sample, so a 100k-sample library is roughly $10M to $100M. This becomes cheap if AI learns to classify from non-destructive X-ray/CT once trained on the destructive ground truth.
3. **No lot-level shared evidence registry.** Every buyer re-tests the same lot. Needed: "test once, trust many", a signed evidence record per lot/reel/serial (C2PA-style or Sigstore-like attestations) that distributors, primes and DLA can query. This turns a cost into a network. GIDEP and ERAI record *failures*, not *passes*.
4. **HBOMs are not machine-verifiable.** The CISA HBOM framework and CycloneDX HBOM are voluntary formats with no binding to physical evidence. Needed: an HBOM where each line item carries an evidence pointer (an image hash, XRF spectrum or CT scan) and a confidence score, generated automatically from teardown and CT of a golden sample plus in-line inspection data.
5. **Attestations are self-certified.** BIS Declarations of Conformity, SPRS scores, CIP-013 plans and s.5949 reps rest on supplier paperwork. Needed: third-party assessment backed by physical evidence (for hardware, the equivalent of a C3PAO). Law: no regulator requires it yet. What would change that: one bad prosecution or recall (for example the 2025 inverter "rogue radios" reports, recalled), or DoD/BIS naming a recognized test method in guidance. Becoming an accredited 3rd-party body (ISO/IEC 17025 lab plus 17065 certifier) is the permission asset.
6. **Reverse-engineering and provenance are the same instrument.** CT, decap, XRF and electrical characterization of a legacy part are exactly what is needed (a) to reverse-engineer it for a DLA SAR/TDP and (b) to authenticate it. The Approval Factory's characterization lab is a provenance lab, and the shared fixed cost lowers the cost of both.
7. **Firmware/hardware pairing.** SBOM tools (Finite State, NetRise, Fortress, recalled) read firmware. Nobody pairs the firmware image with the physical board revision and die origins into one attested record per serial number.

## 4. Binding constraints and what it would take to change each
- **Physics clocks**: destructive decap and burn-in take real time. The fix is to train non-destructive models on destructive ground truth so that most lots skip decap. CT scan time is minutes per board (Lumafield class).
- **Law**: DFARS 7008 already allows "industry standards", so the route is through SAE G-19 / AS6171 and DLA Land & Maritime QPL/QML practice (recalled). s.5949 FAR implementation (the proposed rule, recalled) has not named a verification method. Commenting and helping shape it is a 2026-27 window.
- **Regulator acceptance**: DLA, DMEA, BIS and NERC each need to recognize the method. The fastest is probably DLA Land & Maritime, through its counterfeit program, plus prime contractor flowdowns (Lockheed and RTX supplier quality clauses).
- **Liability**: the contractor already carries authenticity liability under 7008. A verifier that sells "authenticity insurance" (backed by an insurer, using its own false-negative data) moves liability onto a priced product.
- **Data access**: reference parts are purchasable. Image data from SMT lines belongs to the CM (Cybord's wedge is the pick-and-place cameras).

## 5. AI plays on the verification side
1. **Physical-evidence provenance attestation house ("Provenance Lab as a service")**: an ISO 17025 lab with CT, XRF, optical and electrical rigs plus AI, delivering signed per-lot/per-serial evidence that satisfies DFARS 7008 testing, s.5949 die-origin reps and BIS hardware Declarations of Conformity. Wedge: s.5949 (Dec 2027) and BIS VCS hardware (Jan 2029) create forced demand with no accepted verification method today.
2. **Die-origin fingerprint library**: a proprietary corpus mapping die images and CT data to fab and node, sold as an API to labs, primes and CBP.
3. **Test-once-trust-many evidence registry**: signed pass records per lot, a network-effect layer over distributors (AS6081) and DLA.
4. **Shared instrument with the Approval Factory**: the same CT, decap and characterization pipeline produces DLA SAR technical data *and* authenticity evidence, so one lab serves two revenue lines. Provenance testing is a recurring-revenue on-ramp (every lot of every non-authorized-source buy) that funds RE capacity.
5. **Auto-generated, evidence-linked HBOM per serial**: CT plus AI teardown of a golden unit, then in-line image matching, giving a CycloneDX HBOM where each component line carries an evidence hash.

Opinion: the strongest venture shape is #1 plus #2, aimed at s.5949 and FEOC compliance. It is distinct from Cybord, which works in-line at CMs on commercial quality, and from Exiger and Altana, which work on paper and supplier graphs. Its main risk is that the rules get delayed or watered down, or that regulators keep accepting self-attestation, in which case demand stays small and comes from lab testing alone.
