# c12: Nuclear: AI for licensing, commercial-grade dedication (CGD) and obsolescence. Who automates CGD packages?

Date: 2026-10-20. Search budget used: 3 WebSearch calls, then WebFetch. Items marked (recalled) are from memory and were not verified this session.

## Bottom line
Nobody sells an AI product that produces a complete, signable CGD package (technical evaluation, critical characteristics, acceptance plan, test or inspection results, Part 21 acceptance). The funded nuclear-AI startups work on documents and search: Atomic Canyon (licensing search and review), Nuclearn (CAP and condition-report screening, routine documentation, plus an undescribed "Parts AI"), and Everstar (a "Gordian" platform whose website claims qualification will "clear materials, parts and suppliers at scale", with no detail behind it). The only public demonstration of agentic AI applied to reverse engineering and CGD steps is an academic or small-firm pilot: MIRAE-EN's REDiX (Korea, May 2026). The CGD work itself is done by services firms: Paragon (now Mirion), Curtiss-Wright, Westinghouse/Framatome, Park Nuclear, utilities' in-house procurement engineering, and others. Paragon's own 2018 talk to the NRC described the bottleneck as access to design information plus test and documentation hours, and its proposed fix was generic, product-line dedications. That is the "approval factory" idea in nuclear form, done by hand.

## Evidence

### What the CGD pathway is (evidence: Paragon CEO Doug VanTassell, NRC CGD workshop, June 14, 2018, https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML1815/ML18150A373.pdf)
- Program basis: 10 CFR Part 21; ASME NQA-1-2008/1a-2009; EPRI 3002002982 (Rev 1 of NP-5652 and TR-102260). NRC endorses this via RG 1.164 (recalled).
- Technical evaluation: determine the safety function, identify performance requirements, confirm the commercial-grade criteria, run an FMEA, identify critical characteristics.
- Acceptance: Method 1 (special tests and inspections), Method 2 (commercial-grade survey of the supplier), Method 3 (source verification), Method 4 (acceptable supplier or item performance record).
- The EPRI 3002002982 revision asked for more documented basis for sampling plans, tolerancing, FMEA, active vs passive safety function, and each critical characteristic. That means more paperwork per package.
- Equipment qualification (EQ) adds seismic, EMI/RFI, temperature and humidity, mechanical and thermal aging, and radiation testing. These are physics clocks.
- Software dedication covers platform and application firmware, compilers, design and analysis codes (for example TORMIS), and cybersecurity.
- Challenges Paragon listed: (a) access to design information, since the utility lacks design attributes, safety function, qualification requirements and host-component info, and its POs carry dated specs; (b) OEMs are unwilling to share, treat the data as proprietary, or are obsolete and gone; (c) more engineering and testing hours, more test requirements and more documentation; (d) utilities doing CGD in-house rather than outsourcing.
- Paragon's efficiency idea: move from "PO-specific" dedication (for example, an Eaton breaker CGD for LaSalle) to "product-line specific" generic dedication procedures (GDP-210 for molded case circuit breakers), where "Tech Eval lives in the GDP, does not get re-invented." This is the reuse lever.
- Paragon at the time: NUPIC approved, about $100M of stock, plus inventory management.

### Paragon Energy Solutions (now Mirion)
- Site (https://www.paragones.com): more than 12 million nuclear parts; more than 22,000 components supplied with dedication plans or qualification reports; 175 nuclear facilities served. Offerings include the PeAks Marketplace ("changing the way the power generation industry buys parts") and the HIPS I&C platform. The site does not mention AI.
- Mirion IR press-release list: "Mirion Completes Acquisition of Paragon Energy Solutions", Dec 1, 2025 (https://ir.mirion.com/news-events/press-releases?page=2). Price about $585M (recalled, not verified).
- Read: the largest independent CGD and reverse-engineering house was sold to a strategic buyer at a high multiple (recalled). There is a scaled, valuable market, and it is run by services firms with no AI.

### MIRAE-EN REDiX (Korean Nuclear Society Spring Meeting, Jeju, May 7-8, 2026; https://www.kns.org/files/pre_paper/55/26S-563-손권우.pdf)
- Authors Sohn, Yoon and Yang of MIRAE-EN Co. (Daejeon). This is a pilot of "REDiX", a reverse-engineering-based digital transformation platform built on MBSE (ISO/IEC/IEEE 24641).
- Agentic AI prototypes:
  - (1) Procurement engineering: turns PDF datasheets into images for a multimodal LLM, uses an expert-persona prompt to produce JSON, then auto-populates HWP technical specification templates.
  - (2) Quality-class reclassification of RE parts: a Bayesian network with exponential failure rates and CPDs computes PFD and suggests a downgrade or a design change.
  - (3) CFSI (counterfeit, fraudulent and suspect item) discrimination.
- The paper stresses "early engagement of regulation." It is a pilot, not a product. It is the closest public analog to "AI-automated CGD."

## Where the hours go (opinion grounded in the Paragon deck and the REDiX paper)
1. Find the design basis: safety function, host-component requirements, original specification. The data is scattered across utility records, ADAMS, OEM manuals and dead OEMs. LLM and RAG can do this; Atomic Canyon and Nuclearn are positioned here.
2. Technical evaluation: FMEA and critical characteristics, written to EPRI 3002002982. This is template-heavy and repeated across the fleet, and "PO-specific" work is reinvented every time. Generic CGDs are the lever, and AI could generate and maintain a fleet-wide generic CGD library.
3. Acceptance: test plans, sampling plans, inspection, lab tests, seismic and EQ. These are physics clocks. Sample sizes per EPRI sampling plans, aging and seismic tests, and lab capacity (KEMA, Wyle/NTS, Curtiss-Wright labs) are binding.
4. Supplier verification (Method 2/3) via NUPIC audits on 33-month cycles.
5. Part 21 liability: the dedicating entity takes on Part 21 reporting obligations. This is the permission and liability bottleneck. Only a dedicating entity with an Appendix B/NQA-1 QA program that is NUPIC-audited can sign.

## What nobody does (and what it would take, assuming plenty of compute)
- **Fleet-shared, machine-readable CGD and critical-characteristic library.** Every utility and dedicator holds its own PDFs. A neutral library keyed by part number, with safety function, critical characteristics, acceptance methods and evidence, would let a "generic dedication" be cloned rather than re-authored. To get there: data-sharing agreements (EPRI or NUPIC could host), or a dedicator that builds the corpus from its own volume.
- **Evidence-to-claim traceability graph.** Link every critical characteristic to its acceptance criterion, the test record, the instrument calibration and the person who signed. Today this is Word, PDF and paper. NRC vendor inspections and NUPIC audits check exactly this, so a machine-checkable package shortens audits.
- **AI-generated test-plan optimization.** Use physics and statistical models to minimize Method 1 tests: choose the critical characteristics with the most discriminating power, and use Bayesian sampling plans (REDiX shows a Bayesian network for quality class). Changing this needs EPRI guideline acceptance of model-based sampling justifications, which is a regulator-acceptance question. RG 1.164 is already performance-based.
- **Non-destructive "digital twin of the part" for reverse engineering.** CT scanning plus metallurgy (XRF/OES, hardness) plus electrical characterization feed an AI that generates the RE technical data package and the CGD technical evaluation together. Today these are separate firms and separate weeks.
- **Automated CFSI and counterfeit screening** from imagery, X-ray and electrical signatures, tied to the GIDEP and NRC counterfeit databases. REDiX pilots this; nobody in the US sells it as a CGD acceptance input.
- **Digital I&C software dedication automation.** Static analysis, formal methods and test generation for firmware in commercial digital items (EPRI's digital CGD guidance, recalled TR-106439 / 3002011816). This is a massive bottleneck for I&C obsolescence. AI can do code review and test generation at scale, but it needs NRC acceptance of tool-qualified evidence.
- **Reviewer-side tooling.** A "co-reviewer" for utility procurement engineers and NUPIC auditors that checks a vendor's CGD package against EPRI 3002002982 and flags missing basis. This sells to whoever bears Part 21 liability, and is the safest first product.
- **The dedication entity itself.** The real moat is being the Appendix B/NQA-1, NUPIC-audited dedicator that takes Part 21 responsibility, with AI driving down hours per package. That is the nuclear version of the Approval Factory. The incumbents (Paragon/Mirion, Curtiss-Wright, Westinghouse, Framatome, Park, GE-H) do this manually. Getting there: a QA program, a NUPIC audit (5 sponsoring utilities, then the 33-month cycle), lab access, and Part 21 insurance and reserves. Roughly 12-24 months to first approved-supplier status (opinion).

## AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
1. **"CGD co-pilot to signable package":** sell first to utility procurement engineering departments (Nuclearn-like distribution), then become a dedicating entity.
2. **Verification-evidence platform:** a traceability graph, plus audit-ready export for NUPIC and NRC vendor inspection.
3. **Model-based acceptance:** physics-informed sampling and test reduction, with the regulatory acceptance path run through EPRI and NEI.
4. **Digital I&C firmware dedication via AI static analysis and formal verification.** This is the most technically hard piece and is where the obsolescence pain sits in I&C.
5. **Cross-domain:** the same engine serves DLA SARs, FAA PMA and nuclear CGD. All three map a design basis to critical characteristics to acceptance evidence to a liable signer. Nuclear has the highest price per approval and an aging fleet of 94 US reactors (recalled), plus restarts and uprates and new builds (Palisades, TMI/Crane restarts; recalled) driving parts demand.
