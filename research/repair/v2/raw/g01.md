## The rule (eCFR, evidence)
- **21.303(a):** the application is made in a form and manner the FAA prescribes. It names the product the article fits and the manufacturing facility.
- **21.303(b):** the design must include drawings and specifications for configuration, dimensions, materials and processes. Test reports and computations must show the design meets the airworthiness requirements of the product it is installed on, **unless the applicant shows the design is identical to a design covered under a type certificate.** If the design was obtained through a licensing agreement, the applicant must provide evidence of that agreement.
- **21.303:** an applicant relying on test reports and computations must certify compliance.
- **21.305:** organization document and an accountable manager. **21.307:** quality system per 21.137.
- **21.316:** the holder must ensure each article conforms to its approved design and must mark it per Part 45.
- **21.319:** minor changes go by a method acceptable to the FAA. A major change needs FAA approval first.
- **21.314:** a PMA is not transferable. **21.309:** relocating the facility needs FAA approval.
- History (recalled): the Subpart K rewrite was published October 2009 and took effect April 2011.

## The three routes (recalled, consistent with eCFR)
1. **Licensing agreement with the TC holder.** The cheapest engineering by far, but the OEM decides, and OEMs rarely license to competitors. This route is a permission problem, not a knowledge problem.
2. **Identicality without a licence.** The applicant must show the design is identical in every respect to the TC holder's approved design. In practice the FAA does **not** accept reverse engineering as proof of identicality (recalled; AC 21.303-4 / Order 8110.42D). You need the actual type design data, for example as a former OEM supplier holding the drawings, or through a released data set. For a newcomer this route is mostly closed unless they acquire data rights, for instance by buying an orphaned or discontinued OEM product line or a supplier with build-to-print data.
3. **Test and computation (T&C).** This is the main route for reverse engineering. The applicant shows the part meets the airworthiness standards of the certification basis: the 14 CFR 23/25/27/29/33/35 amendment levels listed on the TCDS, plus ICA and special conditions. It is shown by analysis and test, usually as comparative equivalence to the OEM part.
   - Criticality drives effort. Non-safety-significant parts can use the Order 8110.119 streamlined path. Safety-significant and critical parts, including life-limited engine parts under 33.70 and parts covered by 33.14/33.15, need deep substantiation.

## Identicality path (the no-licence version)
- Steps 4-5 and 8-11 collapse into **evidence of data provenance plus a drawing comparison**. The ACO checks the drawings against type design data (sometimes the FAA holds the data, or the TC holder's data is requested).
- Elapsed time is weeks to a few months. The binding constraint is **lawful possession of the OEM data** (data rights, trade secrets, export control), not engineering.
- So identicality is a **data-access play**. Acquire the data rights of orphaned or abandoned type designs, defunct OEM suppliers or discontinued product lines. Bankruptcy estates and TC transfers are where cheap identicality approvals come from.

## Where the queue is (recalled, flag for verification)
- **FAA AIR structure.** Since the 2017 reorganization, ACOs sit in the Compliance & Airworthiness Division (AIR-700), and MIDOs under the Manufacturing Inspection branches.
- **Staffing.** AIR engineer headcount is roughly flat to down, and the FAA leans heavily on delegation: DERs, DARs and ODAs.
  - 2025: federal hiring freeze, deferred-resignation buyouts and 2025 shutdown effects (recalled, unverified).
  - Program priorities such as 737 MAX and eVTOL powered-lift soak up ACO bandwidth. PMA projects for low-risk parts rank low in "sequencing."
- **Delegation.** Large PMA houses hold **PMA ODAs** (recalled: HEICO units, Wencor, Chromalloy). An ODA finds compliance internally and can issue approvals for many parts with limited FAA involvement, a large structural advantage. This is also why HEICO can add roughly 400-550 PMAs a year.
- **Policy.** Order 8110.119 created a streamlined path for non-safety-significant articles using a uniform data package (search snippet, evidence). The 2024 FAA Reauthorization Act pushed more delegation and ODA oversight (recalled).
- **Transparency.** The FAA publishes no queue metrics for PMA. Applicants can't see their place in line.

## What nobody does well (opinion, from the pathway)
1. A machine-readable certification basis per part: TCDS amendment level, ADs, special conditions, ICA.
2. Process-parameter recovery from forensics (heat treat, peening, coatings): the main cause of failed fatigue tests.
3. Tolerance and variability inference from small sample sets.
4. Predicting FAA/DER comments before submission. Each cycle costs 4-8 weeks.
5. Queue visibility and scheduling of conformity inspectors and test witnesses.
6. Test-count minimisation: statistically defensible arguments for fewer specimens and shorter tests.
7. Eligibility expansion across models and dash numbers as a cheap delta.
8. Choosing which parts to go after, by joining market value with approval difficulty.

## What would need to be done (assume compute and intelligence are plentiful)
- **Cert-basis compiler.** Ingest every TCDS, AD, SB, CMM and Order, and output the requirement list plus means of compliance for each part number. Data access: TCDS and ADs are public; CMMs and IPCs are OEM-licensed.
- **Inverse materials-process model.** Map microstructure, residual stress (XRD), hardness profile and chemistry to process history, validated on a built library of known-process coupons. This is physics: you have to make and test the coupons once. After that it is reusable across every part.
- **Small-sample tolerance and equivalence statistics** that the FAA accepts. This needs one regulator-accepted method paper or AC-like precedent.
- **Comment-letter predictor and pre-submission red team.** Train it on your own submissions, DER feedback and public FAA guidance. Getting more data takes partnering with DERs or ODAs.
- **Digital conformity:** signed metrology data from CMM/CT, tamper-evident, accepted as conformity evidence in place of an inspector's visit. Needs FAA policy acceptance; remote conformity precedent exists (recalled).
- **Become an ODA**, specifically a PMA ODA. This is the real permission moat: own the reviewer seat. It takes a track record, roughly 3-5+ years (recalled), plus FAA acceptance.
- **Tool qualification for AI in compliance findings,** analogous to DO-330 tool qualification. The FAA has no policy on AI-generated compliance data. Its 2024 "Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance" (recalled) covers AI *in aircraft*, not AI as a certification tool. Someone has to write and win that precedent.
- **Data-rights acquisition** for the identicality route: buy orphaned TCs or supplier drawing sets. Law and contracts, not physics.
- **Physics clocks stay:** fatigue and endurance tests must run. To shorten them, use more parallel rigs, accelerated test methods with validated correlation, and analysis-in-lieu-of-test arguments for non-critical parts. Each needs regulator acceptance.

## AI plays on the verification side (opinion)
1. **Reviewer-side copilot sold to DERs and ODA units,** and eventually to the FAA. It checks submissions against the cert basis and flags gaps before the comment letter. Monetise per package. It raises reviewer throughput, which is the binding queue.
2. **AI-native ODA / DER firm (the "approval factory" as verifier).** It finds compliance for third parties' PMA packages at 10x throughput. This captures the scarce permission (delegated authority) rather than the cheap knowledge.
3. **Process-forensics-as-a-service:** process parameters inferred from sample parts, with a validated confidence statement that a DER can sign against.
4. **Digital-thread conformity:** sensor and metrology evidence replaces witness and conformity visits.
5. **Queue and eligibility intelligence:** pick parts by approval cost against market value, and auto-generate eligibility-expansion deltas.
