# g09: Space and defence electronics: qualification by similarity, MIL-PRF-38535/883 screening, DMSMS form-fit-function replacement approval: pathway and bottlenecks

Evidence quality: the web-search quota ran out after 3 searches, and most .mil and vendor pages returned 403 or 503. Figures marked (recalled) come from memory and need checking before anyone relies on them.

## 1. Sources actually retrieved
- Wikipedia, MIL-PRF-38535 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIL-PRF-38535). QML requires a manufacturer whose QM program is certified and qualified by the government Qualifying Activity (DLA Land and Maritime). The Qualifying Activity must approve all changes. With enough quality and reliability data, manufacturers may "modify, substitute, or delete tests" through their QM program and review board. Appendix C covers RHA, with total-dose designators from 1 krad to 1 Mrad. The QML approach dates from 1995.
- DLA 2024 DSP award briefing, "New RH PEM Class N/P MIL-PRF-38535". It was found by search; the fetch returned 403. https://www.dsp.dla.mil/Portals/26/Documents/Conference/2024%20Briefings/ It shows DLA is actively adding plastic and space PEM classes, and the briefing lists "cost avoidance" as an outcome.
- Kipo AI (Mobilio Co. DBA Kipo AI), https://www.kipo.ai. It sells AI agents for parts discovery, supplier risk, RFQs and procurement. A TechConnect DTC poster (https://events.techconnect.org/DTCFall/partner/abstract/poster.php?i=220) titled "Autonomous System for Managing Part Obsolescence in Long-Lifetime Assets" claims LLM-standardised data on millions of COTS parts and form-fit-function matching. Customers shown on the site: US Army Letterkenny Army Depot (Patriot sustainment; "60% faster procurement", no baseline given) and LLNL. The site makes no qualification or approval claims.
- Z2Data (https://www.z2data.com). Founded 2016, about 50 employees. It forecasts EOL up to 36 months out and offers "compliant alternates in hours", with 7 AI agents. Customers include Qualcomm, Teradyne, Dell and Airbus. It makes no FFF qualification claim.

More than 15B devices across more than 200k part numbers, more than 12B die in stock, authorised by more than 70 OEMs. It has built more than 20,000 device types as a licensed manufacturer and can make more than 70,000. ## 2. The pathway (who signs, which clock runs)
Steps A to D are component level; E and F are board/box and program level; G is logistics.

A. Detect obsolescence. Inputs: PDN/EOL notices, GIDEP, SiliconExpert (Arrow), Z2Data, Accuris (ex-IHS), Siemens Supplyframe, and the DoD tool AVCOM. This step is mostly done, is commoditised, and is now getting AI layers (Kipo, Z2, BAE). It is not a moat.

B. Choose a resolution, following the DoD SD-22 DMSMS Guidebook hierarchy (recalled): existing stock, then reclamation, then alternate part (FFF, already approved to the same spec or SMD), then substitute (FFF but a different spec, which needs engineering approval), then aftermarket (Rochester, Teledyne HiRel, Micross, Lansdale), then emulation (DLA's GEM program; the DMEA ARMS foundry at Sacramento runs legacy 0.5 um+ processes), then minor or major redesign. Cost metrics (recalled; DoD DMSMS cost-avoidance factors, order of magnitude only): alternate roughly $5k-20k, substitute roughly $20k-100k, emulation roughly $50k-500k, redesign roughly $250k to several $M per board, plus requalification.

C. Component qualification and screening:
- Hermetic QML Class Q (military) or V (space). Screening per MIL-STD-883 includes TM1010 temperature cycling, TM2001 constant acceleration, TM2020 PIND, TM1014 seal, TM2012 radiography (V) and TM1015 burn-in. Burn-in is 160 h at 125C for Q and 240 h for V (recalled). Then QCI groups A through E; Group C includes a 1000 h TM1005 life test and Group E is radiation, with TM1019 TID per wafer lot.
- Plastic: Class N (military PEM) and Class P (space PEM), added in recent revisions. Class Y covers non-hermetic ceramic flip-chip for space and Class T covers user-defined flows. Other plastic routes: SAE AS6294 (PEMs for space), NASA EEE-INST-002 and PEM-INST-001 upscreening, and ECSS-Q-ST-60-13C for COTS in Europe (recalled).
- Each new device needs an SMD (5962-xxxxx) issued by DLA Land and Maritime, or a program SCD or VID. Drafting and coordinating a new SMD takes months (recalled: 3-12 months).
- Physics clocks that cannot be compressed by analysis today: burn-in of 160-240 h, a life test of 1000 h (about 6 weeks) at minimum, HAST/THB at roughly 96-1000 h for PEMs, temperature cycling of 100-1000 cycles taking days to weeks, and radiation beam time. Beam-time slots run weeks to months out, and US facilities number roughly 5 (TAMU, LBNL 88", MSU FRIB SEE line, BNL NSRL, MGH/Northwestern Medicine protons). Shortage concerns date from the 2014 closure of Indiana University's cyclotron (recalled).

D. Qualification by similarity (QbS). Where it is allowed:
- MIL-PRF-38535 qualification families and generic data within a certified QM plan; JEDEC JESD47 and AEC-Q100 family rules.
- At box level, DO-160 and MIL-STD-810 similarity analysis.
- In space programs, heritage and "delta qual" approved by the program's Parts Control Board (PCB) and Material Review Board (MRB).

Where it breaks:
- Radiation, because lot-to-lot and fab-to-fab variability means TID needs lot acceptance testing and SEE depends on design and process.
- Die shrinks or a fab change that is hidden behind the same part number (the classic COTS problem).
- Package or material changes affecting moisture, CTE or tin whiskers.
- The similarity argument itself. Today it is a hand-written engineering memo judged by a human board, with no standard schema, no shared evidence base and no reuse across programs.

E. Program approval of the change:
- The Parts Management Plan under MIL-STD-3018 and the Program Parts Selection List (PPSL).
- A Nonstandard Parts Approval Request (NSPAR) to the PCB for space and missile programs.
- An ECP (Class I or II per EIA-649 / MIL-HDBK-61). A Class II change (no interchangeability impact) is quicker; a Class I change needs a government CCB.
- The design-authority OEM or prime usually has to sign. For legacy weapon systems with orphaned TDPs, a depot engineering support activity (ESA) signs, e.g. NAVAIR FST, AFLCMC or AMCOM.
- If FFF is not exact, board-level requalification follows: thermal, vibration, EMI, software or FPGA timing.

F. Counterfeit-avoidance testing for any part from non-franchised sources, under SAE AS6171 / AS5553 and DFARS 252.246-7007/7008. Steps include XRF, X-ray, decap, DPA and electrical tests. It typically takes 2-6 weeks and costs about $2k-15k per lot (recalled).

G. Logistics. NSN cataloguing; if a new source supplies a spared item, the DLA Source Approval Request (SAR) path, which is the "Approval Factory" step from the prior review.

## 3. What nobody does, or does well
1. No machine-readable qualification evidence. Similarity cases, test reports, DPA images and radiation data live in PDFs held by primes, test labs, NASA GSFC Radhome/NEPP, ESA ESCIES and DTIC. Nobody holds a cross-program, queryable "evidence graph" that says part X has evidence E under conditions C, and E covers claim K for application A.
2. No quantified similarity. QbS is accepted or rejected on engineering judgement. There is no accepted metric for how much prior data transfers when only the package, die revision, fab or lot changes.
3. Test minimisation. MIL-PRF-38535 already allows a certified manufacturer to delete or modify tests with data, but primes and test labs run the full flow by default because no one presents the statistical case.
4. Radiation prediction. SEE and TID results are not predicted credibly from process and layout data before beam time. Beam time is the scarcest clock, and pulsed-laser SEE is under-used as an accepted substitute or pre-screen.
5. FFF equivalence beyond the datasheet. Today's matching (Z2, SiliconExpert, Kipo) compares parametric datasheets. Nobody verifies behavioural equivalence in the specific application: timing margins, power-up sequencing, undocumented behaviours, or FPGA bitstream behaviour on a replacement FPGA.
6. Die-level truth. Non-destructive verification that a replacement or aftermarket part really contains the claimed die (X-ray CT, delayering, recovered netlist) is done ad hoc by counterfeit labs and DMEA. It is not a productised "certificate of sameness".
7. Package assembly for the approver. NSPAR, SCD, SMD drafts, ECPs and the similarity memo are written by hand. Each package takes weeks of engineering time (recalled order: 40-300 engineering hours for a substitute part; several thousand for a board redesign).
8. Cross-program reuse. The same obsolete part is resolved independently by multiple primes and programs. GIDEP shares problems, but not resolutions or qualification evidence.

## 4. Who is doing what
- Obsolescence data and alternates: SiliconExpert (Arrow), Z2Data, Accuris/IHS, Siemens Supplyframe, Octopart (Renesas/Altium), Total Parts Plus, Lytica; DoD AVCOM and PDREP. Adjacent to the Approval Factory: they find candidates but do not produce approvals.
- AI procurement and obsolescence startups: Kipo AI (Army depot, LLNL) and Cofactr (YC; defense electronics supply chain, raised roughly $17M Series A in 2024, recalled). Adjacent.
- Aftermarket and licensed manufacture: Rochester Electronics, Teledyne HiRel, Micross, Lansdale, and Spirit Electronics (AZ, value-add upscreening). These hold the permission today through OEM licences and QML lines. Spirit and Micross are direct analogues to a parts approval house.
- Rad-hard and space-EP OEMs: Frontgrade (ex-CAES/Cobham), Microchip, Renesas/Intersil, TI Space EP, ADI, BAE, Apogee Semiconductor, Zero-Error Systems (Singapore). These are where the space parts get made and qualified.
- Test labs: Alter Technology (TÜV Nord), Serma, TRAD (France), Radiation Test Solutions, VPT Rad, Hi-Rel Labs, and the counterfeit labs (e.g. Integra, recalled). They sell hours and have no AI incentive.
- Government: DLA Land and Maritime (QML/QPL Qualifying Activity, SMDs), DMEA (ARMS foundry, Trusted accreditation), NASA NEPP/GSFC REAG, ESA ESCIES, and DLA GEM emulation (Sarnoff/SRI legacy).
- Primes: BAE is building a cloud DMSMS tool with AI; Lockheed, Raytheon and Northrop run in-house PCBs.
- Silicon-lifecycle analytics (adjacent technology): proteanTecs, Synopsys SLM and PDF Solutions Exensio apply ML to test data, but for yield, not military qualification.
- AI PCB redesign (adjacent; this is the redesign branch): Quilter, CircuitMind, Celus and Flux.
- AI counterfeit inspection: Cybord (component imaging on SMT lines) and Nokomis (RF emissions).
- No company I found offers AI-generated similarity cases or delta-qualification packages accepted by a PCB or DLA.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side
1. Evidence Graph plus Similarity Engine. Ingest every reachable test report, DPA, radiation database (GSFC Radhome, ESCIES, NEPP) and datasheet, plus physics-of-failure models. For a candidate replacement, output (a) a quantified similarity score per qualification claim, (b) a minimal delta test plan and (c) an auto-drafted NSPAR, SCD, SMD or ECP package. The KPI is engineering and PCB hours per approved substitution.
2. Become the certified entity: a QML (Class T/P/N) or AS6081/AS6171 value-add qualifier and upscreen house. Its AI argues test deletions and modifications through the QM plan that MIL-PRF-38535 already permits. In other words, own the permission and use AI to cut cost per approval.
3. Radiation prediction plus capacity. Use TCAD+ML SEE and TID prediction from process and layout, plus pulsed-laser pre-screening, to cut heavy-ion beam hours. Pair it with owned or contracted beam capacity, which is the scarce physical permission.
4. Behavioural FFF verification. Build an in-circuit digital twin of the original part (from captured signals, a recovered netlist or the original TDP) and run formal and differential equivalence of the replacement or emulation under the actual board's stimulus. This could become a "certificate of FFF equivalence" usable for a Class II ECP instead of Class I.
5. Die-sameness certificates. Automated X-ray CT, die imaging and electrical fingerprint matching against a reference library, sold as counterfeit and "same die" proof for the aftermarket and for QbS.
6. Accelerated-life inference. Bayesian or physics-based models that combine early-time burn-in and life data with family data to stop tests early or to justify burn-in deletion. MIL-PRF-38535 provides the hook; JEDEC JEP148 (reliability by physics of failure) is a precedent (recalled).

## 6. Binding constraints and what would change them
- Physics clocks (burn-in, life test, HAST, beam time). Change: statistically prove equivalence of shorter or accelerated flows and get the Qualifying Activity to accept them under a QM plan. Own beam capacity; a compact accelerator or laser SEE lab is a capex play.
- Law and regulator acceptance. DLA Land and Maritime certifies QML; program PCBs and CCBs approve parts; DCMA oversees. Change: a DSP/DLA pilot in which AI-generated similarity packages are reviewed side by side with traditional ones; a published SAE G-19/G-12 or JEDEC standard for a similarity-evidence schema; NASA NEPP endorsement.
- Liability. A part failure on a missile or satellite falls on the prime. Change: the house warrants its approvals, holds insurance, and builds a track record on low-criticality (Class B/military ground) parts before space.
- Data access. Test data is held by OEMs, primes and labs, under ITAR, proprietary terms and CUI. Change: start with public NASA/ESA radiation databases and the GIDEP/DTIC corpus; offer primes a "contribute data, get cheaper approvals" consortium; generate your own data by running a test lab.

## 7. Surprises
- MIL-PRF-38535 already allows test deletion with data. The permission exists, but nobody industrialises the evidence case.
- Rochester holds more than 12B die and licences from more than 70 OEMs. In legacy chips the "permission" is mostly OEM licences plus die bank, not regulator approval.
- Primes such as BAE are building their own AI DMSMS tools, so the detection and alternate-finding layer will get crowded fast.
- DLA is still adding new classes (N/P PEMs for space), so the rules are moving towards COTS plastic and analysis-heavy qualification. That creates a window.
