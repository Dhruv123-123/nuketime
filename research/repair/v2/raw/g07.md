# g07: Rail: AAR M-1003 quality approval, FRA, EN 15085, European ECM and TSI conformity for spare parts; pathway and bottlenecks for a non-OEM spare.

Evidence status: 3 web searches used (quota). Fetches of aar.org/aar.com redirected (aar.com now redirects to mxvrail.com); ERA and eCFR pages 404/blocked. Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this run. Items marked (fetched) or (search) were seen this run.

## 1. Bottom line

Rail is the least "permission-locked" of the repair verticals in one way and the most fragmented in another.
- **North America freight (AAR interchange):** FRA does not type-certify spare parts. The gate is the AAR: (a) facility quality certification to **M-1003** (MSRP Section J), and (b) for "AAR-approved components" (couplers, knuckles, yokes, side frames, bolsters, wheels, axles, bearings, brake valves, draft gears and so on), a **component approval** from the relevant AAR committee, often first *conditional* with a service trial, then *unconditional*. Non-OEM aftermarket is already large and normal here (Strato, Progress Rail, PowerRail, Longwood Elastomers, Alco Spring all advertise M-1003 (search)). The moat is held by the incumbent OEM/foundry oligopoly sitting on the committees, plus multi-year service-trial clocks, not by drawings.
- **US passenger/transit:** FRA 49 CFR 238 (passenger equipment safety, fire safety 238.103), 229 (locomotives), 232 (brakes, incorporating AAR standards by reference) (recalled). There is no FAA-PMA analogue. The operator's own engineering and configuration control plus Buy America (FTA 49 CFR 661) decide. Legacy fleets (Amtrak Amfleet I, about 1975; NYCT R46/R62; SEPTA Silverliners) have severe obsolescence, and agencies reverse-engineer in-house or through small job shops (recalled).
- **EU:** The binding gate is the **ECM (Entity in Charge of Maintenance, Reg. (EU) 2019/779)**. Its "maintenance development" function must approve any component substitution under change management. If the spare is "replacement in the framework of maintenance" (identical function, performance and interfaces, Dir. 2016/797 and Reg. 2018/545), **no new vehicle authorisation is needed**, but the ECM carries the liability and must hold the evidence (recalled). Parts that are TSI *interoperability constituents* (wheelsets, some brake parts, couplers and so on) need an EC declaration of conformity with a Notified Body module (CB/CD/CF/CH1...) (recalled). Manufacturing of welded parts needs **EN 15085-2 certification** (CL1 to CL4) by a recognised body (DVS ZERT, TÜV, etc.). Polymer parts need **EN 45545-2 fire testing** in an accredited lab. Large operators add their own supplier/product qualification on top, for example DB's **HPQ (Herstellerbezogene Produktqualifizierung)** for safety-relevant parts (recalled).
- **Who is doing an "approval factory" in rail?** Nobody at venture scale. The closest are operator-internal programmes (DB's additive spare-parts unit, about 1,000 digital models and a target of 10,000 by 2030 (fetched)), consortia (Mobility goes Additive "Approval" WG: first approved AM brake suspension link, Hamburger Hochbahn, Aug 2019, tested by TÜV SÜD (search)), software for AM part libraries (3YOURMIND at SNCF (search); Replique, a BASF spin-off, with Alstom/DB (recalled)), and OEM service arms (Siemens Mobility Rail Service Centres, Alstom, Knorr-Bremse RailServices, Wabtec). None of them sells "evidence/approval per part" as a product.

## 2. Pathway for a non-OEM spare (step-by-step)

### A. North America freight interchange (AAR)
1. **Identify part and governing spec.** AAR MSRP section (e.g. Sec. S for couplers/castings, Sec. G wheels/axles, Sec. E brakes, Sec. H bearings) (recalled). The Field Manual of Interchange Rules determines what can be applied in interchange. Many specs are purchasable (~$hundreds to thousands per section) (recalled). Data is not secret, but **design drawings for OEM-proprietary parts are**.
2. **Facility M-1003 certification.** QMS audit by AAR QA auditors (historically AAR, now through AAR Technical Services / MxV Rail). Recert audits every ~3 years with surveillance in between (recalled). Cost roughly $20k to $60k and 6 to 12 months for a first-time shop (opinion/recalled). Many hundreds of facilities already hold it.
3. **Component approval (if the part is on the "AAR-approved" list).** Submit the application, drawings and test reports to AAR Technical Services. Committee review (Coupling System & Truck Castings, Wheels/Axles/Bearings, Brake Equipment, etc.). Lab testing to spec (fatigue, static, metallurgy, X-ray of castings). Then usually **conditional approval with a service trial**: a defined number of units (e.g. 100s of parts or 10s of cars) for a defined period (often 1 to 3 years or N miles) with periodic inspection reports. Then unconditional approval (recalled). **Physics clock: the service trial.**
4. **Casting-specific:** foundry approval per M-201 and related specs, plus first-article and ongoing radiography and sampling. After the 2010s import coupler/knuckle and side frame quality problems, AAR tightened foundry audits and added marking/traceability rules (recalled). Expect hostility to new foundries.
5. **Marking and traceability.** AAR approval number, manufacturer code and date marked on the part. Umler/EMIS records (Railinc) for some components (recalled).
6. **Sale.** To car owners, lessors (GATX, Trinity/TrinityRail, Union Tank Car, Greenbrier), railroads and repair shops. Price discovery is via AAR Office Manual billing rates (Rule 101 etc.), so aftermarket margins are bounded by AAR price tables (recalled).
Bottlenecks: committee seats are occupied by incumbent suppliers and railroads; service-trial duration; foundry qualification; AAR price tables cap upside.

### B. US passenger / transit
1. Operator (Amtrak, a commuter rail or transit agency) owns configuration control. There is no federal part approval.
2. FRA compliance only where the part touches regulated systems: brakes (232/238), fire safety (238.103: NFPA 130 / ASTM E162/E662 flammability and smoke), crashworthiness, glazing (49 CFR 223: FRA-certified glazing, a real federal approval for windows (recalled)), and safety appliances (231).
3. Agency engineering review: drawing equivalence, first article inspection, often a "trial installation" on 1 to 4 cars for 3 to 12 months.
4. Procurement: FTA Buy America, DBE, low-bid rules, and the APTA PRESS standards.
Bottlenecks: tiny, fragmented engineering staffs at agencies (the reviewer is the bottleneck, not the regulator); procurement rules; no portable approval, so each agency re-approves the same part.

### C. EU / UK
1. **Classify the change:** like-for-like replacement in the framework of maintenance (ECM change management only), versus a change to an authorised vehicle requiring the proposer's assessment under the CSM-RA (Reg. 402/2013), possibly an AsBo, and possibly re-authorisation (Reg. 2018/545 Art. 15/16) (recalled).
2. **If an interoperability constituent:** EC declaration of conformity with a NoBo (TSI WAG / LOC&PAS). Months, €20k to €150k+ (opinion/recalled).
3. **Manufacturer certs:** EN 15085-2 (welding), ISO/TS 22163 (rail QMS, the IRIS successor; often demanded by OEMs and operators), and EN 45545-2 material fire tests (HL1 to HL3; weeks per test, €2k to €10k per material/test set (recalled)).
4. **Product standards:** EN 13260/13261/13262 wheelsets, axles and wheels; EN 15827 bogies; EN 16452 brake blocks (composite brake blocks need UIC/TSI approval, with dynamometer and in-service tests (recalled)).
5. **ECM acceptance:** the ECM's maintenance development function signs off. In freight wagons the ECM is often a keeper (VTG, GATX Rail Europe, Ermewa, Wascosa) or a workshop. In Germany the VPI-EMG maintenance guide sets norms (recalled).
6. **Operator supplier qualification:** DB HPQ, SNCF qualification, UK RISQS plus Network Rail PADS (infrastructure). Each is a separate audit.
Bottlenecks: the ECM bears liability, so it defaults to the OEM; no central register of approved non-OEM equivalents; NoBo capacity; repeated audits per operator; EN 45545 lab slots.

### D. Other large markets (adjacent opportunity)
India **RDSO** vendor approval (Part II to Part I vendor progression, years-long) and Indian Railways' huge indigenisation drive. Australia RISSB/ONRSR. China CRRC is self-contained. These matter for the "one dossier, many regulators" play (recalled).

## 4. What nobody does today (would need to be done)
1. **A portable equivalence dossier.** One machine-generated evidence package per part that satisfies ECM change management (2019/779 Annex II), CSM-RA hazard log, NoBo IC module, AAR committee submission, an agency FAI and RDSO, each as a "view" of the same underlying evidence graph. Today each is re-done by hand.
2. **A shared register of approved non-OEM equivalents** across ECMs and agencies (a "PMA list for rail"). It does not exist, so every ECM and agency re-approves the same part. A company that holds the register controls distribution.
3. **Service-trial compression.** Instrument the conditional-approval fleet (strain, accelerometers, wayside detectors, plus Railinc/Umler and wayside data such as WILD/ABD/HBD) and run physics models so committees can accept statistical life evidence earlier. This needs an AAR committee rule change; the lever is a 2 to 3 year pilot with one Class I or lessor and MxV Rail.
4. **Virtual fire/smoke qualification for EN 45545 / NFPA 130** from material formulation, so physical tests become confirmations and not iterations.
5. **Weld-quality evidence automation for EN 15085-2/-4/-5.** Auto-generated WPS/WPQR, sensor-logged welds, AI-read NDT (RT/UT). This cuts the cost of certification and per-part documentation.
6. **Agency-side reviewer capacity.** Transit agencies have 1 to 5 engineers reviewing obsolete-part substitutions. An "outsourced ECM maintenance-development function" (EU) or outsourced configuration-control engineering (US), with professional-engineer sign-off, sells the permission and not just the drawing.
7. **Legal: Buy America plus domestic manufacturing.** Pair AI-RE with US contract manufacturing so the part clears FTA 661 and Amtrak Buy America, which incumbent offshore castings cannot.

## 5. AI plays on the verification side (ranked, opinion)
1. **"Outsourced ECM / configuration-control engineering" with an AI back end.** Become a certified ECM (2019/779 certification for the maintenance-development function alone is possible, since functions can be outsourced (recalled)) or a US PE-stamped engineering service for transit. AI generates the equivalence case and the shop builds the part. This sells permission, which matches the prior finding.
2. **Equivalence-evidence compiler (multi-regime).** Input: a scanned or legacy part plus fleet data. Output: an AAR / NoBo / ECM / CSM-RA / RDSO-ready dossier. SaaS to ECMs, NoBos and agencies, or used internally by the factory.
3. **Service-trial statistical acceptance engine** with MxV Rail and a lessor (GATX/Trinity). Turns a 2 to 3 year conditional approval into months plus monitored deployment.
4. **Cross-recognition registry:** the "approved non-OEM equivalents" ledger that ECMs and agencies query. Network effects.
5. **EN 15085 / M-1003 audit automation:** QMS-as-code, continuous evidence, so a new micro-factory gets certified in weeks.

## 6. Binding constraints, and what would change them
- **Physics clocks:** AAR service trials (1 to 3 years), fatigue testing (weeks), EN 45545 tests (weeks), brake dynamometer and in-track trials for brake blocks (months). To change them: instrumented-fleet statistical acceptance plus validated digital twins accepted by committees, starting with a pilot.
- **Law:** EU 2016/797, 2018/545, 2019/779 already permit like-for-like replacement without re-authorisation. The law is *not* the constraint; ECM risk appetite is. US: there is no FRA parts approval, so the constraint is AAR private governance plus agency procurement.
- **Regulator acceptance:** AAR committees are industry-run and slow to admit newcomers. To change this: bring a Class I railroad or a big lessor as sponsor; MxV Rail validation.
- **Liability:** the ECM, operator or agency holds it. To change it: the factory takes the liability (insurance-backed warranty plus ECM certification), which converts the "default to OEM" incentive.
- **Data access:** OEM drawings are proprietary. But fleet/condition data (Railinc, wayside, ECM maintenance records) is held by operators and keepers, who are the customers, so data access is easier than in aerospace.

## 7. Surprises
- In freight interchange, non-OEM aftermarket with M-1003 is already routine. The approval itself is not a monopoly; the committee-held component approval plus AAR price tables are what limit the upside.
- The EU legal framework already allows like-for-like replacement without re-authorisation; the binding constraint is the ECM's risk appetite and the missing evidence tooling, which is directly an AI/evidence opportunity.
- DB is building a 10,000-part digital spares warehouse in-house. The biggest EU customer is also the most advanced competitor, but it lacks the capacity to do the approvals (opinion).
- No equivalent of the FAA PMA parts list exists in rail, so there is no portable approval asset. Creating one is greenfield.
