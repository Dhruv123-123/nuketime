# c03: Scan-to-CAD and AI reverse engineering tools (Backflip AI, Physna/Thangs, Geomagic, Creaform, Hexagon, Revopoint and others): what they do, who buys, how close to a qualified design package.

Method: 3 WebSearch calls plus WebFetch of company and press pages (several 403/404). Items marked (recalled) come from memory and were not re-verified this session.

## 1. Landscape by layer

### A. Capture hardware (geometry in)
- **Creaform (AMETEK)**: HandySCAN / MetraSCAN handheld laser scanners, metrology-grade (~0.025 mm accuracy class, recalled). Site footer now credits "FARO Technologies, Inc. and Creaform Inc.", consistent with AMETEK's 2025 acquisition of FARO (~$920M, recalled). Software: Creaform Metrology Suite with a Scan-to-CAD module (Scan-to-CAD and Scan-to-CAD Pro): extract primitives and build "accurate parametric solid models" with extrude/revolve/cut, export IGES/STEP/DXF, live transfer to SOLIDWORKS, Inventor, Fusion, Solid Edge. FAQ names legacy components without CAD, aftermarket parts, tooling. No GD&T or deviation reporting mentioned on that page. https://www.creaform3d.com/en/products/software/creaform-metrology-suite/scan-to-cad-software-module
- **Hexagon** (Absolute Arm, AT960 laser trackers, CMMs, PC-DMIS) and **ZEISS (GOM ATOS)**: metrology-grade structured light / CMM. Buyers: OEM quality labs, MRO shops, defense depots. (recalled)
- **Revopoint, Shining3D (EinScan), Artec 3D**: $300-$20k scanners; Revopoint is consumer/prosumer (0.02-0.05 mm claimed, recalled). Buyers: hobbyists, small shops, education. Not metrology-traceable for aerospace.
- **Industrial CT**: Lumafield ($75M Series C, 13 Mar 2025, IVP lead; Spark, DCVC, Kleiner, Lux, Matter, G2, Wellington; plus $50M SVB credit line Nov 2025 per CB Insights). 85% of its customers are first-time CT users; customers named are EVs, medical devices, athletic, packaging, i.e. not aerospace sustainment so far. "Voyager" cloud AI analysis. Also Waygate (Baker Hughes), Nikon, ZEISS CT (recalled). CT is the only route to internal geometry (cored passages, porosity) without destroying the part.

### B. Mesh-to-CAD software (geometry to model)
- **Geomagic Design X** (3D Systems sold its Geomagic software line to Hexagon, ~$123M, deal 2024-25, recalled): industry-standard RE software; auto-surfacing, feature extraction, parametric transfer to SOLIDWORKS/NX/Creo, "Accuracy Analyzer" color deviation map. Seat ~$10-25k (recalled). Human-driven, hours to days per part. Hexagon page 403'd.
- **Siemens NX / Creo / SOLIDWORKS** native RE tools, **QuickSurface**, **Polyga FlexScan**, **Artec Studio** (recalled).
- **Backflip AI** (founded 2022 by Markforged creators; CEO Greg Mark; ~$30M venture funding per Extruct/Modelwire aggregators, round/investors unverified; 1-10 employees listed). Second-generation "mesh-to-CAD foundation model" generally available 4 Aug 2026 as Autodesk Fusion add-in and web app (earlier Onshape extension in 2025; Onshape/others "coming"). Output: full parametric feature tree (extrude, revolve, pattern, chamfer, fillet, sketch constraints), STEP export. Also drawing-to-CAD and photo-to-CAD (one known dimension). Modes: Fast (minutes), Thinking (agentic self-check iterations for "dimensional accuracy"). Scope self-declared: "moderate-complexity 3-axis CNC milled and turned parts". Pricing: 4 free reconstructions, then from $20/month. Claim: digitizing a part from ~$1,500 to ~$10, 1-5 minutes (company claim, unbenchmarked). Customer: unnamed major automaker in production (unverified). Training data: in-house CAD made by staff (MMS, Sept 2025). Independent test (Engineering.com): all five candidates for a simple knob missed some geometry; an adaptor ring looked right visually. No published accuracy numbers, no GD&T, no tolerance or material inference. Working toward SOC 2 Type II; no mention of ITAR/CMMC.

## 2. Who buys what
| Layer | Buyer | Price point |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer scanners (Revopoint) | hobbyist, small shop | $300-2k |
| Metrology scanners/CMM/CT (Creaform, Hexagon, ZEISS, Lumafield) | OEM quality, depots, MRO | $50k-$1M+ / CT subscription |
| RE software (Geomagic, Creaform module) | RE engineers, tooling | $10-25k/seat (recalled) |
| AI mesh-to-CAD (Backflip) | product designers, makers, auto plant maintenance | $20+/mo |
| TDP/RE services | DLA, AFSC, NAVSUP, FMS, PMA houses | $50k-$250k+ per part package; 6-12 months |

The money is overwhelmingly in the last row; tools are a small market sold per seat.

## 3. How close to a qualified design package?
A DLA SAR / Air Force RE TDP or FAA PMA package needs (MIL-STD-31000 TDP, ASME Y14.5/Y14.100, MIL-HDBK-115, FAA Order 8110.42):
1. Nominal geometry: **largely solved** by scanning + Geomagic; Backflip automates it for prismatic/turned parts. ~5-15% of package hours (opinion).
2. Design intent and tolerances (GD&T) inferred from several samples plus mating interfaces and wear: **not done by any tool**. Scan of one worn part gives one sample, not a tolerance band.
3. Material, temper, heat treatment, coatings, surface finish, grain/forging direction: needs destructive lab (OES/XRF, hardness, metallography, SEM). **No tool links this to the model.**
4. Process spec and critical characteristics / key characteristics (what must be controlled, special processes, NDT acceptance): engineering judgment. **Not done.**
5. Engineering substantiation: stress/fatigue/damage tolerance vs original loads (often unknown), test plan, similarity/identicality argument (PMA test-and-computation). **Not done** by RE tools.
6. Drawings to Y14.100 and DoD format, configuration management, CDRL formatting, data rights markings (DFARS 252.227-7013/7014). **Not done** (drafting is manual).
7. First article, qualification test, regulator/ESA review: physical and institutional. Untouched.

Conclusion: today's AI scan-to-CAD tools reach step 1 (and partly the drafting in 6). They deliver perhaps a tenth of the hours of a qualified package. The remaining 85-90% (tolerancing, materials, process, substantiation, evidence assembly, reviewer acceptance) is where cost sits and where nobody has a product.

## 4. What would need to be done (assume compute and intelligence abundant)
- **Multi-sample statistical tolerance inference**: scan N fielded/surplus parts (CT + optical), separate wear from manufacturing variation, infer the original tolerance stack and datum scheme from mating parts; output Y14.5 GD&T with confidence.
- **Material/process identification fused to the model**: automated small-sample lab pipeline (OES, XRF, microhardness, EBSD, residual stress) whose results become spec callouts (AMS/MIL specs) inside the TDP.
- **Equivalency evidence engine**: produce the "identicality / test-and-computation" argument the reviewer reads: deviation maps, statistics, material equivalence, FEA vs inferred loads, all traceable to raw scan and lab data with metrology uncertainty budgets (ISO 17025-style).
- **Reviewer-side tooling**: give DLA/ESA engineers, DERs and FAA ACO reviewers a viewer that checks a package automatically (completeness vs MIL-STD-31000 / 8110.42 checklists, scan-vs-drawing conformance), cutting reviewer hours, the bottleneck nobody sells to.
- **Accuracy benchmarks**: no public benchmark exists for mesh-to-CAD fidelity on aerospace parts; whoever defines one (with a metrology lab) shapes what regulators accept.
- **ITAR/CUI-compliant deployment** (CMMC L2, on-prem): Backflip mentions SOC 2 only; defense data cannot go to its cloud.
- **Regulator acceptance change**: what it would take is a DLA/AFSC pilot accepting AI-generated TDPs with human sign-off (like the RSO's AM qualification pilots), then FAA policy on computational substantiation. Law does not forbid it; acceptance is precedent-driven.

## 5. Players that threaten an "Approval Factory"
- Direct: none of the tool vendors produce approvals. Closest are services primes (Amentum PROS VI) and small 8(a) RE houses, plus HEICO/PMA houses (in-house RE), who could buy Backflip-like tools.
- Adjacent: Backflip (could move up to TDP), Lumafield (internal geometry + AI analysis, well funded, could add RE-to-TDP), Hexagon (owns Geomagic + metrology + Nexus, could bundle), AMETEK (Creaform + FARO), Physna (part matching).

## 6. Surprises
- Backflip, the flagship AI RE startup, scopes itself to "moderate-complexity 3-axis" parts, sells at $20/month, and the first model "wasn't good" (CEO). It's a designer tool, not an aerospace one.
- Lumafield's customers are mostly first-time CT users in consumer/EV/med, not aerospace sustainment, despite CT being the key RE sensor for castings.
- The Air Force explicitly blames "incomplete data packages" for readiness problems and runs 6-12 month NRE per RE effort, with 8(a)-only vehicles. The channel is small-business set-aside services, not software.
- The geometry consolidators (Hexagon bought Geomagic; AMETEK bought FARO) are metrology hardware companies, so the software layer is a hardware add-on.
