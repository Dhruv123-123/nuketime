# c27: Legal/IP side: AI tools for freedom-to-operate, clean-room reverse engineering audit trails, trade-secret risk in RE (patent search AI like PatSnap, IP firms). Is anyone combining clean-room RE with AI?

Search budget: I used 3 WebSearch calls and then fetched pages directly. Anything marked (recalled) comes from memory and has not been verified.

## Short answer
- AI FTO / patent analytics is crowded and well funded. These tools are built for attorneys and patent landscapes: PatSnap Eureka, Solve Intelligence, Patlytics, IPRally, Amplified, XLSCOUT, plus incumbent Clarivate/Derwent and LexisNexis PatentSight. None of them reads a physical part, a CMM scan or a TDP and asks "does this design infringe or contaminate?"
- I found no company doing **clean-room reverse engineering of hardware with AI plus an evidentiary audit trail**. My searches found nothing for aerospace PMA or DLA SAR. The only "AI clean room" products I found are in **software**, and the most visible one is a provocation: MALUS "Clean Room as a Service" (malus.sh), which uses AI to re-implement open-source packages from docs only so customers can drop the license. Its audit-log and indemnity claims are unverified, it routes indemnity through an offshore subsidiary, and the site currently shows "temporarily unavailable". The chardet 7.0.0 release is described as a "ground-up, MIT-licensed rewrite" of a formerly LGPL library. Public dispute over whether an AI-assisted rewrite counts as a clean room (recalled: maintainer used Claude; original author objected, Mar 2026) shows the legal question is live and unsettled.
- For an Approval Factory, IP and legal clearance is a real but secondary gate. In the US, the FAA and DLA do not adjudicate IP. Risk falls on the applicant through trade-secret suits, patents (including design patents) and ITAR. The opening is a **provenance-and-clearance layer** that turns each reverse-engineered TDP into a defensible, litigation-grade record. That lowers liability cost and could become part of what reviewers accept.

## Evidence
1. **Trade-secret law on RE (US).** Under the UTSA and DTSA, reverse engineering a lawfully obtained product is a proper means, not misappropriation. Robinson & Cole (Rattigan and Lass, 24 Dec 2025) argue that AI makes secrets more "readily ascertainable", which could erode OEM trade-secret status. They also cite the 11th Cir. holding that bot-scraping of publicly accessible quotes was "improper means". https://www.dataprivacyandsecurityinsider.com/2025/12/reverse-engineering-in-the-age-of-ai-are-your-trade-secrets-still-safe/
2. **United States v. Lange, 312 F.3d 263 (7th Cir. 2002).** A former RAPCO employee (RAPCO makes aftermarket aircraft brakes and parts by RE) offered RAPCO's drawings and certification data for $100,000. RAPCO's development and testing took 1-2 years. RAPCO could skip some certification tests if its parts were identical to already-certified parts. Lange argued RE-derived data can't be a trade secret. He was convicted, and (recalled) Easterbrook affirmed: data that is costly to reverse engineer is not "readily ascertainable". Two lessons: (a) RE-derived TDPs are themselves protectable assets, the moat; (b) the dominant failure mode is an employee or contamination case, not the RE itself. https://www.quimbee.com/cases/united-states-v-lange
3. **Classic contamination cases.** Rockwell Graphic v. DEV (7th Cir. 1991): ex-employees joined a replacement-parts competitor and were accused of using piece-part drawings; the defendant could not show which drawings came lawfully from vendors. Colt v. Springfield Armory: the OEM argued that RE-ing every M-16 part was too large a task, so the drawings must have been copied. Pattern: **the defendant loses when it cannot prove where each drawing came from.** That is exactly what a provenance log solves. https://caselaw.findlaw.com/us-7th-circuit/1262973.html
4. **Solve Intelligence (FTO).** Offers FTO templates for US, EPC Art. 69, UK and DE; claim-element charts with present/not-present per element; doctrine of equivalents and prosecution-history estoppel; search across 170M+ publications in 107 jurisdictions; outputs clearance memos and non-infringement opinion drafts for attorney sign-off. Reports 700+ IP teams. Its own claim is 60-90% efficiency gains (vendor figure). https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/what-is-a-freedom-to-operate-analysis-and-how-ai-speeds-it-up
5. **PatSnap Eureka FTO.** Semantic search across USPTO, EPO and WIPO, claim parsing, feature-to-claim mapping, and risk scoring by claim breadth and assignee enforcement history. Reports 18,000+ customers, 2B+ data points and 120+ countries. Claims "75% faster" (no methodology). Says traditional FTO takes "weeks" and costs "tens of thousands of dollars". https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/rd-blog/ai-freedom-to-operate-analysis-patsnap-eureka/
6. **Other AI-IP players (recalled, funding unverified):** Patlytics (Series A of about $14M, late 2024), Solve Intelligence (Series B of about $40M, 2025), IPRally (Finland), Amplified (Japan), XLSCOUT, DeepIP, Ankar, Tradespace (IP management and commercialization). All of them start from text: claims and specs.
7. **Aerospace PMA RE firms** (Av8 PMA: 500+ FAA/EASA parts; HEICO; Wencor; Jet Parts Engineering) publish nothing on AI-assisted clean-room or IP audit tooling. https://www.aviationpros.com/aircraft-maintenance-technology/engines-parts/aircraft-airframe-accessories/press-release/12407362/av8-pma-av8-pma-expands-operations-with-new-dedicated-engineering-facility
8. **Regulatory posture (recalled):** FAA PMA (14 CFR Part 21 Subpart K; Order 8110.42) accepts a design shown by "test and computation" or by licence. The FAA does not certify IP clearance, but in practice it asks applicants about their right to use the data. DLA SARs require a TDP and do not indemnify IP. Data developed at private expense keeps the contractor's rights under DFARS 252.227-7013/7015. Design patents on replacement parts matter more after **LKQ v. GM (Fed. Cir. en banc, May 2024)** replaced Rosen-Durling with a more flexible obviousness test, which makes design patents somewhat easier to invalidate and is relevant for FTO on spare parts. RE of a defense article creates ITAR technical data.

## Pathway: IP and legal sub-steps of an RE approval package
| Step | Who does it today | Cost / time (est.) | What is undone or broken |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part selection: FTO and patent screen on the part (utility and design patents, coatings, processes) | outside patent counsel, often skipped for out-of-patent legacy parts | $10-50k per opinion, weeks (PatSnap: "tens of thousands") | Done in text against claims, never against the actual scanned geometry or material. No portfolio-level FTO across 1,000s of NSNs. |
| Clean-room setup: segregating staff who saw OEM drawings (ex-OEM hires) from RE engineers | ad hoc HR policy, NDAs | low cash, high risk | No tamper-evident record of who saw what. Contamination is the main way RE shops lose cases (Lange, Rockwell, Colt). |
| RE data capture: scan, CMM, metallurgy, coupons | RE engineers | the bulk of the 300-1,000 hrs | Raw data is not cryptographically tied to the specific physical specimens (serial, source, chain of custody). |
| Design synthesis into the TDP | engineers / DER | | No record that each dimension or tolerance came from measurement or engineering analysis rather than an OEM drawing. AI-assisted design adds a new contamination vector: models trained on or prompted with OEM data. |
| Data-rights marking (DFARS legends, export classification / ITAR jurisdiction) | contracts staff | | Manual; errors either forfeit rights or create ITAR exposure. |
| Regulator submission | FAA ACO / DLA engineering support | | Regulators get no structured IP-provenance evidence and do not ask for it. |
| Post-approval defence (OEM cease-and-desist, trade-secret suit, ITC 337) | litigators | $1-5M+ per case (recalled AIPLA ranges) | Provenance is rebuilt after the fact from email and notebooks. |

## What would need to be done (assuming plentiful compute and intelligence)
1. **Provenance ledger for physical RE.** Every specimen (serial number, purchase receipt, chain of custody) is hashed. Every scan and test file is signed at the instrument. Every TDP feature carries a derivation graph back to specific measurements and analyses. This is an evidence-grade "lab notebook" that can show independent derivation feature by feature.
2. **Automated contamination screening.** Compare the generated TDP against any OEM data the firm can lawfully see: government-furnished drawings, public IPCs and CMMs, DLA cFolders with restricted legends, leaked-data watchlists. Flag "too-identical" features: tolerances, notes and datum schemes that measurement alone would not produce. That pattern is the Colt argument, now testable.
3. **AI-model clean room.** Certify that the design-AI stack was never trained on or prompted with proprietary OEM data. That means model and data bills of materials and air-gapped inference for each program. This is the hardware equivalent of the chardet and MALUS dispute, and nobody offers it.
4. **Geometry-to-claim FTO.** Map scanned 3D geometry, material chemistry and process (coatings, heat treatment) directly onto utility and design patent claims and figures. Today's tools map text features. Run it portfolio-wide across thousands of NSNs to rank which parts are legally clear first.
5. **Automated data-rights and export tagging.** Generate DFARS 7013/7015 legends and an ITAR/EAR jurisdiction pre-classification for each TDP so the company keeps private-expense rights in data it creates.
6. **Regulator and insurer acceptance.** Get an IP-provenance package accepted by an insurer (IP defence/infringement cover with lower premiums), and ideally referenced by DLA or the FAA as a standard attachment, turning the ledger into a soft requirement. What would change this: a pilot with DLA's Value Engineering or Source Approval office, plus one won or deterred case that shows the ledger working as evidence.
7. **Physics clocks are untouched.** Coupon tests, fatigue tests and so on still run in real time. The legal layer mostly takes out litigation-tail risk and lawyer hours, not test time.

## Opinion: AI plays on the verification side
- **"Independent-derivation ledger" as a feature of the Approval Factory**, not a standalone product at first. It makes the factory's TDPs litigation-proof and insurable, and it turns Lange-style protection of the firm's own TDPs into a durable asset.
- **Spin-out SaaS later:** "provenance and contamination checker" for the roughly 200+ US PMA holders, DLA SAR applicants, MRO shops and right-to-repair players (ag, medical, defense). Comparable to Black Duck/FOSSA for software provenance, but for physical parts.
- **Portfolio FTO triage over orphan NSNs** to pick targets: legal clearance score × demand × test burden.
- Risk: OEMs could argue AI-assisted RE is "improper means" if it uses scraped manuals or prompt-extracted OEM data. Using only measurement as input is the defence.
