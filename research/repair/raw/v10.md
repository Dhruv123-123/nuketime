# v10: Military right to repair: FY2024-FY2026 NDAA proposals, Army right-to-repair policy (2025), GAO reports on technical data rights and contractor-locked sustainment (F-35 ALIS/ODIN, ship maintenance). Quantify cost and readiness impacts.

Researched 2026-10-20. Labels: [HARD] = GAO/IG audit data; [CLAIM] = politician or advocate anecdote, not independently verified; [OPINION] = industry or advocate position.

## Findings

1. [HARD] GAO-23-105341 (Sep 2023), F-35 sustainment. The fleet mission-capable rate was about 55% (Mar 2023). The program has a $1.7T life-cycle cost, $1.3T of it for operations and sustainment. More than 10,000 components were awaiting repair. Depots could repair only 44 of 68 core components, with full capability not expected until 2027. DOD had "neither determined the desired mix of government and contractor roles nor identified and obtained the technical data needed". Field maintenance is hampered by a "lack of technical data and training". All 7 recommendations were still open as of Mar 2026. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-23-105341
2. [HARD] GAO-26-108113 (Jun 2026). The F-35 mission-capable rate fell from 67% in FY21 to 44% in FY25. The full-mission-capable rate fell from 38% to 25%. Over 800 US jets are in service, with lifetime sustainment estimated at $1.6T. The "Global Support Solution Reset" needs $13.7B more than planned through FY31: about $2.2B of new spending plus about $11.5B of unfunded requirements. The contractor's incentive fees were paid for performance that was not aligned with readiness. Drivers named: software delays (jets accepted while non-mission-capable), scarce parts and corrosion. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-26-108113 ; https://www.militarytimes.com/industry/techwatch/2026/06/12/only-1-in-4-f-35s-is-fully-mission-capable-gao-finds/
3. [HARD] ALIS, the F-35 logistics information system. GAO (2019) found one Air Force unit spent the equivalent of more than 45,000 hours a year on manual workarounds because ALIS did not work as needed. A DoD-commissioned plan, cited by GAO in 2016, warned that ALIS problems could add $20-100B in costs. GAO-22-105128 reported that the planned ODIN replacement had been reduced to incremental modernisation; ALIS has inaccurate or missing data and poor usability. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-22-105128 ; https://aircosmosinternational.com/article/new-report-highlights-100bn-f-35-logistics-risk-2415
4. [HARD] GAO-25-107468 (Sep 2025), weapon system data rights.
   - None of the 5 programs reviewed fully met DOD's IP-strategy policy.
   - Stryker: the Army failed to get unlimited data rights and in 2024 signed a sole-source technical-support deal worth about $534M over 5 years.
   - F/A-18: for over a decade the program could not get data rights for RF cables, so maintainers cannibalised grounded jets.
   - LCS: a crane hydraulic motor repair waited about 2.5 weeks for the OEM to show up. The OEM holds the propulsion and diesel spares, tooling and data.
   - Virginia-class: OEM schedules set repair timelines, and crews cannibalise parts from other submarines.
   - F-35: corrosion repairs need contractor support because the government lacks the technical data.
   - Delivered data was often unusable. F/A-18 rejected 80% of data deliveries over improper markings. Stryker had at least 1,274 data items under one contract that nobody was sure had been reviewed.
   - In a DOD data call, nearly half of respondents said proposed prices for data or licences were not fair and reasonable.
   - Officials said reverse engineering, additive manufacturing (AM) and deferred ordering are "generally less effective and efficient" than buying rights early.
   - The key legal gap: statute gives DOD unlimited rights to operation, maintenance, installation and training (OMIT) data, EXCEPT "detailed manufacturing or process data". GAO asks Congress to clarify this.
   https://files.gao.gov/reports/GAO-25-107468/index.html
5. [HARD/history] GAO-06-839 (2006). The C-17, F-22, C-130J and other programs were already limited in their sustainment plans by missing technical data rights. This has been a known, structural problem for 20 years. https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-06-839.pdf
6. [HARD] DoD Inspector General reports on TransDigm.
   - 2019: $16.1M excess profit on 46 of 47 parts, with margins of 17% to 4,451%. TransDigm repaid the money.
   - 2021: $20.8M excess profit on $38.3M of contracts, with one margin of 3,850%. More than 95% of $268M in contracts sat below the Truth in Negotiations Act (TINA) threshold for certified cost data.
   - The business model: acquire sole-source part makers, then raise prices.
   This is a pricing monopoly built on sole-source position, not on knowledge. https://oversightdemocrats.house.gov/news/press-releases/after-oversight-committee-inquiry-inspector-general-finds-rampant-price-gouging
7. [HARD] Fleet-wide aircraft readiness, GAO-23-106217 (Nov 2022). Only 4 of 49 aircraft types met their mission-capable goal in most years from FY11 to FY21, and 26 never met it. Reasons given: aging aircraft, maintenance challenges, parts and supply. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-23-106217
8. [HARD] Navy shipyards, GAO-20-588. 38 of 51 carrier and submarine maintenance periods (75%) finished late, on average 113 days late for carriers and 225 days for subs. The causes were unplanned work (more than 4,100 delay-days) and workforce capacity (more than 4,000 delay-days), with shop overtime at 25-32%. These are capacity and planning problems, not IP. https://www.gao.gov/products/GAO-20-588
9. [HARD] GAO-26-109256 (Aug 2026), attack submarines. Maintenance delays over 10 years caused more than 15,000 lost operational days and cost about $3.4B. Idle time peaked at 1,518 days in 2022 and fell to 166 in FY25. Inactive idle time (subs waiting for a decommissioning dry dock) cost $722M from 2016 to 2025. GAO projects more than 14,000 days and $3.1B more for FY26-30. The binding constraint is dry dock and shipyard capacity. https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-26-109256
10. [POLICY] Army policy. On 30 Apr 2025 Hegseth sent Army Secretary Driscoll a memo directing the Army to put right-to-repair provisions into all new contracts and to seek modifications to existing ones where IP limits maintenance, "while preserving the intellectual capital of American industry". On 4 Jun 2025 Driscoll told the House Armed Services Committee the Army "would not sign contracts that don't give us a right to repair". I found no public 2026 count of contracts actually modified. https://www.theregister.com/2025/05/02/us_secretary_of_the_army/ ; https://www.techradar.com/pro/security/us-dod-wants-right-to-repair-provisions-in-army-contracts-to-access-tools-software-and-technical-data-without-ip-constraints
11. [CLAIM] Anecdotes on cost and readiness.
   - Army safety clip: the supplier quoted $20 per clip and months of lead time; the Army printed one in under an hour for 16 cents (Warren).
   - Black Hawk cockpit knob: a $15 part, but the contractor charges $47,000 to replace the whole screen, about 4 times a month (Warren, May 2026).
   - The Air Force pays $900 per page for maintenance-manual updates.
   - The Avenger air-defence system cannot be repaired without contractor software.
   - Army Materiel Command (Lt Gen Mohan): a vehicle was down over a tire valve whose data sat with a distant subcontractor.
   - USS Ford: 6 of 8 galley ovens were down while sailors were not permitted to fix them, and elevator repairs need the manufacturer (Navy Secretary Phelan).
   - KC-135 rudder trim wheel: printed for about $55 versus more than $700 the traditional way (Air Force Secretary Wilson).
   https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/at-hearing-warren-army-secretary-calls-for-congress-to-authorize-the-military-right-to-repair-debunks-defense-contractors-claims-undermining-reforms ; https://www.bairdmaritime.com/security/naval/naval-ships/us-secretary-of-the-navy-advocates-for-sailors-right-to-repair-equipment ; https://www.executivegov.com/articles/army-3d-printing-parts-ip-issues ; https://www.airandspaceforces.com/air-force-searching-for-savvier-sustainment/
12. [POLICY/OPINION] FY26 NDAA fight.
   - The Senate bill had §836 (Warren-Sheehy), which would have made "instructions for continued operational readiness" a mandatory deliverable in all future contracts.
   - The House bill had §863, plus §1832, a "data-as-a-service" model under which contractors deliver data electronically or by machine-to-machine link and charge per access. Industry backs this model.
   - The White House (OMB statement of administration policy, 9 Sep 2025) supported the reforms.
   - Industry opposed them. AIA said they would "cripple innovation"; PSC called contractor IP "crown jewels"; NDIA issued "Policy Points" against them.
   - All of the right-to-repair language was stripped in conference in December 2025.
   - In May 2026 Driscoll called contractors' IP-theft claims "preposterous" and said the Army wants to buy data, not take it. The services committed to send Congress data on programs that lack repair technical data.
   - The Project on Government Oversight (POGO) warns that data-as-a-service leaves forces exposed in wartime or if a vendor fails.
   https://rollcall.com/2025/11/17/defense-contractors-fight-back-against-ndaa-repair-language/ ; https://www.warren.senate.gov/newsroom/press-releases/warren-sheehy-blast-removal-of-right-to-repair-from-ndaa ; https://pirg.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/IP-professors-letter-NDAA.pdf ; https://www.foxnews.com/politics/trump-backed-military-right-repair-plan-stripped-from-congress-final-defense-bill
13. [HARD/history] AM qualification. NAVAIR flew a V-22 with a flight-critical 3D-printed titanium nacelle link in 2016, but moving from a demonstration flight to fleet configuration changes takes years. Navy guidance lets ships print only "low criticality" parts, with commanding-officer approval. In June 2025 Velo3D signed a 4-year cooperative research agreement (CRADA) with NAVAIR to qualify metal AM for flight-critical parts, which is still in progress a decade after the V-22 flight. https://www.additivemanufacturing.media/news/-navair-demonstrates-3d-printed-safety-critical-aircraft-parts ; https://3dprintingindustry.com/news/velo3d-enters-crada-with-u-s-navy-to-advance-metal-additive-manufacturing-for-defense-241192/ ; https://breakingdefense.com/?p=21043
14. [HARD/news] Obsolescence. The Air Force has to reverse engineer parts of its own B-2 bomber because the original suppliers are gone. The Rapid Sustainment Office (founded 2018) claims "tens of millions" of dollars in savings, which is small against the roughly $1.6T F-35 sustainment bill. https://www.twz.com/39537/the-air-force-needs-to-reverse-engineer-parts-of-its-own-stealth-bomber.md

## Reasoning
- The military is the cleanest real-world case for C2. The knowledge exists; the owner (DOD) paid for the system and still cannot legally access the knowledge. The monopoly is CONTRACTUAL and LEGAL (data rights, software licences, the manufacturing-data carve-out in the OMIT rule), not cognitive. AI does not change who owns the drawings and source code. In many cases the gap is not a lack of knowledge but a right to use it. Reverse engineering, whether done by AI or by printers, is what GAO calls the less efficient fallback.
- Quantified impacts are mostly programme-level: about 45k workaround hours a year at one unit, a $534M Stryker sole-source deal, $13.7B of F-35 reset, $3.4B in sub delays, F-35 mission-capable rate down to 44%. No source attributes a total dollar figure to data rights alone. The cost of missing rights shows up as cannibalisation, OEM wait times (weeks), sole-source premiums (TransDigm margins up to 4,451%) and lost depot capability.
- Big counter-evidence: the largest readiness losses in ship maintenance (thousands of delay-days, $3.4B) come from shipyard capacity, workforce, dry docks and unplanned work. That is the physical repair stage, not diagnosis or knowledge. F-35 problems include parts scarcity and software that is not ready, as well as data rights.
- C5 is weak. Qualification and airworthiness gate printed parts, and only low-criticality parts are routinely printed. Flight-critical AM qualification is still under R&D in 2025-26.
- C7 is partly supported. Contractor incentive fees were not tied to readiness, and sole-source pricing is structural. But the outcome of the conference shows the lock-in is political: policy beats technology.
