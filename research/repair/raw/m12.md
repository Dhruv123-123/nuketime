## Findings

### 1. GB passenger fleet is long-lived and large [VERIFIED THIS SESSION]
ORR data portal, Rail infrastructure and assets, figures as of 31 Mar 2025:
- 15,348 passenger railway vehicles in operation, average age 16.7 years.
- Traction mix: 70% electric, 19% diesel, 8% bi-mode, 3% locomotive hauled.
- Source: https://dataportal.orr.gov.uk/statistics/infrastructure-and-environment/rail-infrastructure-and-assets/
- Implication: assets run for 30-45+ years. Obsolescence management is a core cost, not an edge case.

### 2. InterCity 125 / HST: a 50-year fleet kept alive by re-engining and refurbishment [VERIFIED THIS SESSION]
Wikipedia, InterCity 125:
- Built 1975-82, 95 sets. First withdrawals came in 2017, 41 years after introduction, and ScotRail still runs them in Dec 2025, with replacement expected in 2027-28.
- In 2005, Angel Trains (a ROSCO, i.e. the lessor, not the OEM or the operator) led the replacement of the ~30-year-old Paxman Valenta engines with MTU 16V4000s on 54 power cars, as part of a £110m investment.
- Refurbishments were done by Wabtec at Doncaster, a third party and not the original builder BREL.
- By late 2022, GWR's "Castle" sets were "proving expensive to operate and increasingly difficult to maintain due to their age."
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity_125
- Implication: the "repair" answer to obsolescence is often a system-level re-engineering (new engine, new interfaces, re-approval), not a one-off part copy. The asset owner (ROSCO), not the OEM, ran the programme. This undercuts a pure OEM knowledge-monopoly story (C2) for UK rail.

### 3. The independent / aftermarket layer is consolidated into big players [VERIFIED THIS SESSION]
- Wabtec merged with GE Transportation in a deal announced at $11bn in May 2018 and closed 25 Feb 2019. Revenue was $11.17bn in 2025, operating income $1.79bn. "Locomotive servicing, overhaul and repair" is a listed service line. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabtec
- Caterpillar bought Progress Rail in 2006 for $1bn. Progress Rail bought EMD in 2010 for $820m. It sells remanufactured locomotives and railcars, repowers, and repair and refurbishment services, from 110 US locations. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_Rail
- Implication: in North American freight the two locomotive OEMs (Wabtec/GE, Progress/EMD) also own most of the overhaul, modernisation and parts aftermarket. This is OEM capture, but it is capture of the installed base through engineering, parts and service agreements, not just through secret knowledge.

### 4. UK structure separates ownership, operation and maintenance [VERIFIED THIS SESSION: list; BACKGROUND: roles]
- ROSCOs listed include Angel, Eversholt, Porterbrook, Beacon and Rock Rail, a structure that dates from the 1994 privatisation. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_stock_company
- Background: newer fleets (e.g. Hitachi IEP Class 800, Siemens Thameslink Class 700) come with long OEM Technical Support and Spares Supply Agreements (TSSSAs) or full maintenance contracts lasting 25-30 years. Older fleets are maintained by the train operators in their own depots, with ROSCOs managing heavy overhauls through third parties (Wabtec, Alstom, Arlington, Gemini, etc.).
- Implication: who holds the knowledge depends on the contract era. New-build "train-as-a-service" deals lock maintenance to the OEM for decades, by contract rather than by an information gap.

### 5. OEM service businesses are large, high-margin and backlog-heavy [BACKGROUND, NOT RE-VERIFIED]
- Alstom: Services are roughly 20% of sales and run at higher margins than rolling stock. The services backlog has been reported in the €30bn+ range, out of a total backlog of about €90bn+ (Alstom annual results FY2023/24-FY2024/25).
- Siemens Mobility: describes Rail Services/Customer Services as its most profitable business, with long-term maintenance contracts (e.g. Railigent X predictive maintenance).
- Wabtec Freight Services (aftermarket, modernisations, parts, digital) is about 40%+ of Freight segment revenue. Modernisation (MOD) kits for AC4400/Dash-9 locomotives are a key growth line (Wabtec 10-K / earnings calls 2023-25).
- Implication: OEMs earn more on the long service tail than on the sale. This supports C7's claim that the incentive structure is "built" this way, but it is a commercial structure, not a knowledge gap.

### 6. Obsolescence is dominated by electronics, not castings [BACKGROUND, NOT RE-VERIFIED]
- The rail industry standard for obsolescence management is IEC 62402 (Obsolescence management, 2019), used by RSSB/UK and EN 50126-based processes.
- Practitioner reports (UK Rail Industry Association, Railway Gazette, DB Systemtechnik) consistently name the main problems as traction converters (GTO/IGBT generations), train control and TMS computers, and signalling on-board units (e.g. older AWS/TPWS, then ETCS baseline changes). Rolling stock lasts 30-40 years, but semiconductor product life is about 5-10 years.
- Fixing these means redesign plus re-approval (vehicle authorisation, EMC, safety case), not printing a part.
- Implication: contradicts C5's "AI parts factory solves it" for the highest-cost obsolescence class.

### 7. Deutsche Bahn additive manufacturing: real, but modest in scale [BACKGROUND, NOT RE-VERIFIED; DB page fetched but had no text]
- DB began 3D printing spare parts in about 2015-16 and co-founded the "Mobility goes Additive" network in 2016. DB has reported more than 100,000 printed parts in total and several hundred to over a thousand distinct part types, with goals in the 10,000s of parts per year. Examples include headrest covers, coat hooks, ventilation grilles, cable housings, brake/bogie components and metal parts for old ICE/regional fleets. DB claims lead-time cuts from months to days for obsolete items.
- DB also has to get certification for safety-critical printed parts, and only a minority of printed parts are safety-relevant.
- Other examples: Siemens Mobility RRX Rail Service Center in Dortmund-Eving printing spares; Alstom and Stadler use AM for low-volume spares.
- Implication: this is partial support for C5 for non-critical and low-criticality obsolete parts, where the binding constraint is reverse-engineering data plus approval. It is not a general solution, and it is a decade old, predating LLMs.

### 8. Availability and cost data [MIXED]
- [VERIFIED] GWR said the HSTs were costly and hard to maintain from age (finding 2).
- [BACKGROUND] UK and European OEM maintenance contracts typically guarantee fleet availability of about 90-95% at peak, with penalties.
- [BACKGROUND] For US Class I railroads, locomotive maintenance (labour plus materials) is a few billion dollars a year industry-wide (STB R-1 reports, Schedule 410). Big US railroads cut active fleets by 20-30% under Precision Scheduled Railroading (2017-2022). That reduced spending on maintenance by parking and cannibalising locomotives, not by better diagnosis.
- [BACKGROUND] Studies cited by UIC/EU Shift2Rail estimate that maintenance makes up about 30-50% of a train's lifecycle cost, and that condition-based/predictive maintenance can cut maintenance cost by 10-30%. That is a meaningful saving but not an order of magnitude.

### 9. Diagnostics is already heavily digitised by OEMs [BACKGROUND, NOT RE-VERIFIED]
- Siemens Railigent and Alstom HealthHub (with remote condition monitoring, e.g. pantograph, wheelset and brake monitoring at depot entry), Wabtec Trip Optimizer / RailConnect, and Hitachi remote monitoring all exist. Wheel-profile and underframe automatic inspection portals are used at UK and EU depots.
- The hard part is depot capacity, wheel-lathe slots, skilled fitters, and getting heavy-maintenance shop slots. Diagnosis is not the bottleneck.

### 10. Regulation: ECM certification [BACKGROUND, NOT RE-VERIFIED]
- In the EU, every vehicle must have an Entity in Charge of Maintenance (ECM), certified under Regulation (EU) 2019/779. ECM functions are management, maintenance development, fleet maintenance management and maintenance delivery. Independents can and do act as ECMs, so the maintenance role is legally open to non-OEMs, provided they hold the engineering knowledge and records.
- The ECM regime was strengthened after the Viareggio 2009 derailment (a freight wagon axle failure killed 32 people). This is a direct example of inspection and certification becoming more important, not less (supports C6).
- In the US, FRA rules under 49 CFR Part 229 require locomotive periodic inspections every 92 days and annual tests. Part 238 covers passenger equipment.

### 11. Repair process shape (relevant to C1) [reasoning]
Rail maintenance is mostly scheduled and preventive (exams by mileage/time: A/B/C exams, heavy overhaul every 8-12 years, mid-life refurbishment), not a notice-diagnose-repair chain triggered by failure. Most work is planned, and fleet-level programmes (re-engining, re-tractioning, ETCS retrofit) are capital projects. The C1 chain fits corrective repairs but misses (a) scheduled maintenance, (b) modification/upgrade programmes driven by obsolescence or regulation, and (c) cannibalisation and fleet-pool decisions.

## Reasoning / synthesis
- **Where the money goes:** mainly labour and depot capacity for scheduled exams, plus heavy overhaul and mid-life programmes, and obsolescence redesign of electronics with re-approval. Diagnosis and parts-ordering are smaller pieces.
- **Knowledge monopoly:** real in places (OEM software, design data, TSSSAs, North American OEM aftermarket duopoly). But it is enforced by contracts, IP, data rights and approval regimes, not by owners simply lacking know-how. AI does not transfer IP rights or design authority.
- **Bottleneck:** design authority plus approval for modified or replacement parts. That means the safety case, vehicle authorisation and ECM sign-off, plus depot and shop capacity and skilled labour.

## Company shapes suggested
1. Obsolescence-as-a-service for rail electronics: reverse-engineer and form-fit-function replace legacy control and traction boards, with the approval dossier included. This is a known niche (e.g. small UK firms doing this for ROSCOs), and AI could speed up documentation and test evidence.
2. A tool to build and maintain ECM / approval dossiers: AI that drafts the safety-case and conformity evidence for replacement parts. It targets the real bottleneck.
3. An AM spare-parts qualification broker for non-safety-critical rail parts, for ROSCOs and operators that have no OEM support for 30-year fleets.
4. Depot automation (inspection portals, robotic underframe inspection) to tackle the labour and capacity bottleneck.
