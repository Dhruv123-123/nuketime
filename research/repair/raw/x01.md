## Findings

### F1. Aquant (technician copilot / "service intelligence"): customers and claims [fetched, vendor-reported]
- Source: https://www.aquant.ai/ (fetched 2026-10-20)
- Sells "Conversational AI for physical product and equipment support" plus an agentic platform. Verticals: industrial equipment, telecom, home appliances, food equipment, medical devices, energy and data centers.
- Named customers: Beckman Coulter, Makino, Comfort Systems, Powerscreen, Terex MP. Logos: JLG, Siemens, Stryker, Cytiva, Waters.
- Main quantified claim: Powerscreen saves "up to 1 hour per tech daily". The homepage gives no first-time-fix or cost-reduction percentages.
- Case-study framing: "bridge its skills gap" (Makino), "reduce repeat service visits and improve diagnostics accuracy" (Beckman Coulter).
- Funding [UNVERIFIED-memory]: Series B of about $70M in 2021 (Lightspeed is believed to have led), with total raised around $100M+. Founded about 2017 in Israel and New York.
- Reading: this supports C2 framed as a skills or tribal-knowledge gap at the OEM's own service org. Aquant's buyer is the OEM, not the independent repairer. The AI makes the OEM's knowledge monopoly more efficient, not dissolved. The size of the claimed gain (about 1 hr per tech per day, or roughly 12% of an 8-hr day) is incremental, not 10x.

### F2. Augmentir (connected worker / AI work instructions) [fetched]
- Sources: https://www.augmentir.ai/about and https://www.augmentir.ai/news
- Founded 2018 by the founders of Wonderware (1987), Lighthammer (1997) and ThingWorx (2008): repeat industrial-software founders.
- Traction milestone (Nov 5 2025): "True Productivity surpasses 5 million AI-optimized time & motion studies".
- Customers announced: Armstrong World Industries, Müller Group.
- Recognition: Gartner Cool Vendor 2022, CB Insights Advanced Manufacturing 50 (2022), Frost & Sullivan Leader in Augmented Connected Worker radar (Jan 2026).
- Funding is not disclosed on its site. [UNVERIFIED-memory]: modest venture rounds, in the single to low double-digit $M.
- Reading: the value is in measuring and up-skilling a workforce with uneven skill, i.e. the knowledge gap sits *inside* the operator's workforce. Recent headlines are about productivity, not repair-cost collapse.

### F3. Veryon (aviation MRO software, PE-backed roll-up) [fetched, vendor-reported]
- Source: https://www.veryon.com/ (fetched 2026-10-20)
- 7,600 customers, 75,000 users, 142,000 aircraft. Claims to serve 25% of the worldwide commercial airline fleet and 100+ OEMs, with "over 50 years" in business.
- AI layer "Veryon AIRE": a proprietary LLM, gen-AI in-app assistance and agentic workflows, trained on "100m+ real-world maintenance events".
- "Veryon Diagnostics": defect analysis, reliability and guided troubleshooting.
- Ownership [UNVERIFIED-memory]: formed as a rebrand and roll-up (Flightdocs plus aviation tech-pubs and diagnostics businesses) under private equity (believed to be Arcline).
- Reading: the scarce asset in aviation diagnostics AI is proprietary maintenance-event data (100M+ events) plus the installed base, not model capability. An incumbent consolidator, not a seed startup, owns that data. This argues against C3: the knowledge is being re-enclosed in proprietary data moats.

### F4. Fieldcode (European FSM for IT and telecom break/fix) [fetched, vendor-reported]
- Source: https://www.fieldcode.com/
- Claims: "up to 100% reduction in manual dispatching", average 40% higher engineer efficiency, 35% less time per work order. HP saves $4M a year with 30% less drive time. Hemmersbach handles 7M tickets a year. Braeriach cut admin workload 30% and travel/idle time 20%.
- AI features: zero-touch auto-dispatch, route optimisation, voice agents for appointments, and ticket summarisation and translation.
- Reading: the measured wins are in *logistics and dispatch* (drive time, admin, scheduling): 20-40% gains, not 10x. They are not in diagnosis. This is evidence that money and time in field service sit in travel, scheduling and admin as much as in knowing what to fix.

### F5. ServiceTitan (trades / home-service operating system), the biggest public outcome [fetched, Wikipedia]
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ServiceTitan
- IPO Dec 12 2024 on Nasdaq (TTAN). Raised about $625M at $71 a share and popped 42% to about $8.9B. 2025 revenue was $771.9M. Net loss about $195M in 2024.
- AI product "Atlas" (2025). Partnerships with Ford Pro, ABC Supply and Home Depot Pro.
- Reading: the largest venture outcome in repair-adjacent software is a *workflow, CRM and payments* system for contractors, not a diagnostic-knowledge product. Value has accrued to the business-operations layer, which counts against C3/C4's framing that knowledge is the bottleneck.

### F6. Samsara (connected operations / fleet telematics) [fetched, Wikipedia]
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsara_(company)
- NYSE IPO Dec 2021 raised $805M at an $11.5B valuation. ARR passed $1B by Dec 2023. 2024 revenue about $937M with a net loss of about $286M.
- Reading: a sensor-data and "noticing" layer (fault codes, vehicle health) reached $1B ARR. It shows the *notice* stage (C1 step 1) can be productised at scale. Repair itself still happens in shops.

### F7. Uptake: an AI predictive-maintenance cautionary tale [UNVERIFIED-memory; the Wikipedia fetch 404'd]
- Chicago, founded 2014. Raised about $250-300M and was valued at about $2.3B in 2019. It ran several rounds of layoffs from 2019 onward and narrowed from a horizontal "industrial AI" pitch to fleet, rail and heavy-equipment maintenance analytics. It is reported to have struggled to turn pilots into broad deployments.
- Reading: "AI will fix maintenance" was pitched and funded heavily in 2015-2019. The bottlenecks were data integration, customer change management and the cost of acting on predictions, not the algorithms. This is a direct counter-example to C3/C4 as stated.

### F8. Augury (machine health / vibration AI) [UNVERIFIED-memory]
- Valued at $1B+ around 2021 (Series E). Reported layoffs in 2023. Raised again in 2025 (believed about $75M).
- Reading: machine-health "noticing and diagnosis" is valuable but capital-intensive (hardware sensors plus vibration analysts in the loop). Growth slowed after the 2021 peak.

### F9. Gecko Robotics (robotic inspection plus data platform) [UNVERIFIED-memory]
- Unicorn round of about $125M in 2025 (about $1.25B valuation). Robots crawl boilers, tanks, ship hulls and Navy assets, and the data feeds an AI integrity platform.
- Reading: supports C6. Inspection is a large, fundable, AI-transformable industry in its own right, and the robotic data-capture layer is the moat.

### F10. Tractian (low-cost sensors plus AI maintenance for mid-market plants) [UNVERIFIED-memory]
- Brazil and Atlanta. Large Series C (believed about $120M, late 2024).
- Reading: it went for the mid-market with cheap sensors bundled with CMMS. That is evidence that making the "notice plus diagnose" pair cheap is investable outside the Fortune 500.

### F11. Incumbent absorption (exits are acquisitions, not IPOs) [UNVERIFIED-memory]
- Siemens acquired Senseye (predictive maintenance, 2022). IFS acquired Poka (connected worker, 2023), Falkonry (industrial AI anomaly detection, 2023) and Copperleaf (asset investment, 2024). PTC owns Servigistics (service parts optimisation). Microsoft shipped Copilot in Dynamics 365 Field Service (2023). Salesforce pushed Agentforce for field service (2024-25).
- Reading: platform incumbents (ERP, EAM, FSM, CRM) bundle AI copilots as a feature. A standalone "technician copilot" startup faces commoditisation risk. Exit path: tuck-in acquisition.

### F12. Aviation MRO: the data and regulation stack, not the knowledge, gates speed [inference from F3 plus general knowledge]
- In aviation the fixes are already documented: AMM, IPC, troubleshooting manuals, ADs, SBs. Delays come from parts availability (AOG), certified-labour scarcity and approved-data constraints (repairs outside the SRM need OEM or DER approval). AI software vendors attack records, planning and troubleshooting time. None can grant airworthiness approval.
- Reading: this contradicts C5 for regulated assets (a new part needs PMA/DER/OEM approval) and supports C6.

## Synthesis for the founder
- **Funding pattern:** the dollars went to (1) workflow and operations OSes (ServiceTitan IPO; Samsara IPO), (2) sensing and inspection hardware plus AI (Samsara, Augury, Tractian, Gecko), and (3) data-moated incumbents and PE roll-ups (Veryon). Pure "technician copilot" startups (Aquant, Augmentir) raised modest to mid amounts and sell to OEMs and large enterprises. The ROI they claim is about 1 hr per tech per day or 20-40% efficiency, not 10x.
- **Failures and struggles:** horizontal "industrial AI" pitches (Uptake) burned large rounds. Machine-health firms (Augury) cut staff after 2021. The recurring failure mode is pilot purgatory: integration with legacy EAM/CMMS, scarce clean labelled failure data, and buyers unable to act on predictions.
- **On the knowledge monopoly (C2/C3):** AI copilots are sold *to* OEMs to scale the OEM's own tribal knowledge across a retiring workforce. They tend to entrench the OEM data advantage (Veryon's "100m+ events" moat) rather than dissolve it. Generic LLMs without proprietary service history give weak diagnostic answers. That is why every vendor trains on the customer's tickets and manuals.
- **On where time goes:** vendor metrics point to dispatch, travel, admin and parts logistics (Fieldcode: 30% drive time, 35% time per work order) as much as diagnosis.
