# n03: Condition monitoring sensors and industrial IoT (vibration, oil analysis, thermography; Augury, Samsara, Tractian): cost per asset, evidence of avoided failures, who captures value

Research date: 2026-10-20.

All findings below therefore come from WebFetch of primary URLs I already knew or could build (Siemens report PDF, NIST reports, SEC EDGAR, AWS pricing and docs, vendor pages, Bain, Crunchbase, AJC). ## Findings

### 1. The size of the problem: Siemens "True Cost of Downtime 2024" (vendor-funded survey, Senseye PdM marketing)
- Source: https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:1b43afb5-2d07-47f7-9eb7-893fe7d0bc59/TCOD-2024_original.pdf
- Fortune Global 500 lose about **$1.4 trillion a year** to unplanned downtime, which is **11% of revenue** ("the GDP of Spain"). That is down 6% since 2022.
- One hour of downtime in a large automotive plant costs **$2.3M** ("more than $600 a second"). Since 2019, the cost per hour has risen **113% in automotive** and **319% in heavy industry**, against 19% US inflation.
- The average large plant loses **27 hours a month** to unplanned downtime (39 in 2019), which is 326 hours a year. Incidents fell 41% to 25 a month. Mean time to recover rose from 49 to 81 minutes (cited in Tractian's Series C post).
- 87% of manufacturers gather PdM-relevant data, and almost half have dedicated PdM teams. Siemens credits PdM "going mainstream" for the fall in hours lost.
- Claimed Senseye outcomes from deployments: 85% better downtime forecasting, **50% less unplanned downtime**, 55% more maintenance staff productivity, **40% lower maintenance costs**, and up to 40% fewer replacement parts.
- Reading: this is a hard survey number for the scale of the problem, but the PdM benefit figures are vendor marketing. Note that hours lost fell while the cost per hour exploded, so the value of each avoided hour keeps rising. Either way, the economics favour monitoring high-cost-per-hour assets.

### 2. NIST AMS 100-34 (2020, Thomas & Weiss), a survey of US manufacturers and the best independent evidence here
- Source: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ams/NIST.AMS.100-34.pdf (doi 10.6028/NIST.AMS.100-34)
- 2016 machinery maintenance spend (NAICS 321-339, excluding 324 and 325): **$57.3B**. Adding failure costs ($16.3B) and buffer inventory ($0.9B) gives a total of $74.5B.
- Preventable maintenance losses: **$119.1B**. Of that, $18.1B is downtime, $0.8B is defects and **$100.2B is lost sales from delays and defects**. So most of the loss is downstream commercial loss, not the repair bill.
- On average **45.7% of maintenance is still reactive**.
- The top quartile by reactive share had **3.3x more downtime, 16x more defects**, 2.8x more defect-driven lost sales and 4.9x more inventory build-up than the bottom quartile.
- Among plants under 50% reactive, the top half in predictive maintenance had **15% less downtime**, an 87% lower defect rate and 66% less inventory build-up. Heavy investors in preventive or predictive maintenance had 44% less downtime.
- The perceived benefit of more PdM is $73.8B a year ($6.5B from downtime and $67.3B from sales).
- Reading: these are correlational, not causal. The marginal downtime gain of predictive over preventive is a modest **15%**. The big gap is reactive versus anything planned. That supports "move off run-to-failure", not "AI fixes repair by an order of magnitude".

### 3. NIST AMS 100-18 (2018), a literature review on maintenance economics
- Source: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ams/NIST.AMS.100-18.pdf
- US manufacturers spent $50B on maintenance and repair in 2016. Maintenance runs **15%-70% of COGS** in the literature, and 0.5-25% of turnover (Komonen).
- In Sweden, **37% of the maintenance budget is salaries and 32% is spare parts**. This is useful for "where the money goes": labour and parts dominate, and diagnosis is a small part.
- Preventive maintenance is applied unnecessarily **up to 50% of the time** (Mobley).
- Published PdM impacts on maintenance cost range from **15% to 98%**. NIST notes the metrics vary wildly between sources.
- Piotrowski's pump cost per horsepower per year: reactive **$18**, preventive $13, predictive **$9**, RCM **$6**. That is roughly 2-3x, not 10x.

### 4. The widely cited DOE/FEMP figures (old, recycled)
- Source: the DOE FEMP O&M Best Practices Guide, as cited in Reliable Plant at https://www.reliableplant.com/Read/12495/Oil-analysis-cost
- Claims for PdM: up to **10x ROI**, 25-30% lower maintenance cost, **70-75% fewer breakdowns**, 35-45% less downtime. PdM saves 30-40% over reactive and 8-12% over preventive.
- Reading: these numbers trace back to 1990s guidance and are endlessly re-quoted in vendor content. Treat them as folklore, not measurement. "8-12% over preventive" again says that most of the gain comes from leaving reactive maintenance, not from better prediction.

### 5. Cost per asset: hardware is now cheap (AWS Monitron price list)
- Source: https://aws.amazon.com/monitron/pricing/
- Sensor about **$115** (5-pack for $575), gateway $140-180, service fee **$50 per sensor per year**. An ATEX/Ex-rated 5-pack costs $800.
- A typical motor-pump train needs 2-4 sensors, so hardware is about $230-460 plus $100-200 a year. **At hyperscaler pricing the sensing-plus-analytics layer is a few hundred dollars per asset per year.**
- Commercial vendors (Augury, Tractian) do not publish prices. Tractian's pricing page has only an estimator and claims a "3-month payback": https://tractian.com/en/solutions/condition-monitoring/vibration-sensor/pricing. Augury pricing is quote-only and claims payback "under 6 months": https://www.augury.com/pricing/. [memory, not re-verified] Analyst and press reports put full-service machine-health subscriptions in the low hundreds to over $1,000 per asset per year, because they bundle human analyst review.

### 6. A contradiction: Amazon exited the space
- **Amazon Monitron closed to new customers on 31 Oct 2024** (https://aws.amazon.com/monitron/pricing/).
- **Amazon Lookout for Equipment** closed to new customers on 7 Oct 2025 and was **discontinued on 7 Oct 2026** (https://docs.aws.amazon.com/lookout-for-equipment/latest/ug/what-is.html; https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/preserve-access-and-explore-alternatives-for-amazon-lookout-for-equipment/). AWS points customers to IoT SiteWise anomaly detection and to partners.
- Reading: cheap commodity sensors plus generic ML did not build a durable business, even for AWS. The value is not in the sensor or in the anomaly model. It sits in domain-specific diagnosis, workflow integration and analyst service. That points against "knowledge is free now" (C3): generic AI at $50 per sensor per year lost to vertical vendors with human experts.

### 7. Who captures value: Samsara (public, audited numbers)
- Source: Samsara Q2 FY2027 8-K, Ex. 99.1, filed 2026-09-03: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1642896/000162828026060438/samsaraepr-q22027.htm
- Revenue **$508.4M**, up 30% year on year. **Ending ARR $2.125B**, up 30%. Net new ARR $134.1M. Customers above $1M ARR account for more than $500M of ARR and grew over 50% year on year. GAAP net income $16.2M, FCF $64.7M (13% margin).
- Samsara's equipment-monitoring page (https://www.samsara.com/products/equipment-monitoring) shows what customers actually pay for. Most published ROI is about **theft recovery, location, utilization and fuel**, not failure prediction: $1.3M recovering a stolen excavator, $2.5M asset recovery, 60% better trailer utilization. Maintenance value comes through **OEM telematics fault codes and usage-based PM**. The standout PdM claim is Maxim Crane Works saving $13M with a predictive maintenance program, and MAVIN reports 25% fewer breakdowns.
- Samsara's AI agents release (https://www.samsara.com/company/news/press-releases/agent-studio) says agent adoption grew 7x. The maintenance example is paperwork: Grand Isle Shipyard cut inspection-report generation time by 99%, to about 5 minutes per division.
- Reading: the scaled winner in "industrial IoT" earns its money as an operations and compliance platform, and failure prediction is a minor feature. Its maintenance diagnostics depend on **OEM telematics feeds**, so the OEM still controls the data spigot.

### 8. Tractian (private, fast growing)
- Sources: https://news.crunchbase.com/venture/sapphire-led-raise-manufacturing-ai-startup-tractian/; https://www.ajc.com/business/2026/02/one-of-the-countrys-fastest-growing-ai-startups-moves-hq-to-midtown/; https://tractian.com/en/press
- $120M Series C in Dec 2024 (Sapphire lead, with General Catalyst, Next47 and NGP). About $200M raised in total. **Valuation $720M** (AJC, Feb 2026). About 500 staff. Customers include Kraft Heinz, Hyundai, John Deere, Bosch and Georgia Aquarium. Ranked #24 on the Deloitte Fast 500 (2025). FedRAMP High via Knox.
- Product strategy: sensors **plus CMMS (work orders, parts inventory)**. In other words, they move up from "notice" to owning the work-order and parts workflow. Their marketing frames it as combining "machine, OEM and operating data".

### 9. Augury (private)
- Sources: https://www.augury.com/media-center/, https://www.augury.com/media-center/press/, https://www.augury.com/pricing/
- Claims 150-170+ global companies, **5-20x ROI**, "310% ROI in six months or less", and a DuPont quote of "7x ROI" within a year of the proof of concept. These are self-reported marketing figures.
- Machine Health is "AI powered by reliability experts", meaning human vibration analysts remain in the loop. 2026 moves: a Rockwell Automation partnership on agentic AI (Jul 2026), inclusion in Gartner's APM Market Guide, and a Bengaluru engineering hub.
- [memory, not re-verified] Unicorn round in 2021, and a $75M Series F in early 2025.

### 10. History of overpromise: Bain IoT survey and GE Predix
- Bain (https://www.bain.com/insights/how-providers-can-succeed-in-the-internet-of-things/): about 90% of respondents were still at the planning or proof-of-concept stage, only about 20% expected to run at scale by 2020, and only 10% had "the right people in the right roles". Barriers were integration, security and ROI.
- GE Digital / Predix (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Digital): launched in 2016 as the industrial IoT platform. In 2018 GE planned a $1.2B standalone IIoT company. It was then folded into GE Vernova (Apr 2024). [memory, not re-verified] It is widely reported to have missed its revenue targets badly, with layoffs.
- Reading: horizontal "platform" plays failed. The vertical, service-heavy ones (Augury, Tractian) and the operations-platform ones (Samsara) survived.

### 11. Physics limits on prediction: RCM and failure patterns
- Wikipedia on RCM (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliability-centered_maintenance): the 1960s United Airlines and FAA studies found that the fixed-lifetime belief "was wrong in nearly every case examined". The result was Nowlan & Heap (1978) and later MSG-3. [memory, not re-verified] Nowlan & Heap found only about 11% of aircraft component failure modes are age-related and about 89% are random or infant-mortality patterns. That is why on-condition monitoring beats time-based overhaul.
- The Wikipedia condition-monitoring article cites Kaboli & Oraee (2016): **over 80% of complex mechanical equipment fails randomly**, regardless of age. That is a warning against relying on sensors alone. Vibration, oil, thermography and ultrasound detect bearing wear "weeks or even months" before failure. So detection lead time exists for degradation modes, not for sudden failures.
- Reading: condition monitoring works for a subset of failure modes with a detectable P-F interval, mainly rotating equipment, lubrication and electrical connections. It cannot "notice" everything.

### 12. Oil analysis and thermography
- [memory, not re-verified] Commercial oil-analysis labs typically charge about **$20-60 per sample** for a routine panel. IR thermography surveys are typically priced per day, at about $1,000-2,000 per day for a contractor survey. These are mature, cheap techniques. The gap is interpretation and follow-through, not data acquisition.
- Reliable Plant cites the WSJ: unplanned downtime costs industrial manufacturers about **$50B a year**, and **equipment failure causes 42%** of it. So about 58% of downtime is not equipment failure (changeovers, materials, people), which a sensor cannot prevent.

## Where the money and time go
- NIST shows the dominant loss is **lost sales from downtime and defects ($100B of $119B)**, not the repair itself. The labour (37%) and spare parts (32%) shares of maintenance budgets dominate direct spend. Diagnostics and monitoring are a small slice.
- The value of condition monitoring comes from **turning an unplanned stop into a planned one**. Parts are staged, crew is scheduled and the asset comes down at a convenient time. That means the time saved is mainly in *waiting for parts and crew*, because notice comes before failure. It does not come from making the diagnosis itself cheaper.
- Cost per asset for sensing plus analytics is now about $100-$500 a year (AWS Monitron pricing), and premium managed service costs more. Against $2.3M an hour in automotive, the hardware is not the bottleneck.

## Stances on hypotheses
- **C1 (universal chain): mixed.** CM/PdM moves "notice" before failure and lets diagnosis, parts sourcing and repair be scheduled. That breaks the strictly serial chain, because steps run in parallel during the P-F interval. And 58% of downtime is not equipment failure at all.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly): mixed, leaning supports for diagnosis-interpretation skills.** The bottleneck cited is people: Bain found 10% have the right people, Augury found workforce constraints a top challenge for 43%, and Augury still needs human analysts. But the "monopoly" here is a scarcity of vibration analysts and reliability engineers, not OEM gatekeeping, and the data is not hidden. The exception is Samsara, which relies on OEM telematics, so the OEM controls the data feed.
- **C3 (AI closes the gap): contradicts, partially.** AWS's generic ML monitoring (Monitron, Lookout) was discontinued in 2024-2026. Survivors pair AI with human experts and workflow. Data quality (47%) and process (43%) are the top barriers. AI does lower the cost of interpretation, and Samsara's agents cut report writing by 99%.
- **C4 (10x drop in four middle steps): contradicts as stated.** The independent evidence shows predictive over preventive gives about 8-15% less downtime (NIST, FEMP), with 2-3x cost differences per horsepower between strategies. The big gains come from moving off reactive maintenance, which needs no AI. Vendor claims of 5-20x ROI are self-reported.
- **C5 (bespoke part factory): silent.** Nothing in this evidence covers part manufacture.
- **C6 (inspection remains essential): supports.** RCM and MSG-3 history shows on-condition inspection is the core of aviation maintenance. Sensors are "continuous inspection", and Samsara's maintenance AI value is in inspection paperwork. Inspection is evolving into sensor plus human.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient): mixed.** 45.7% of maintenance is reactive, and up to 50% of preventive maintenance is unnecessary, so there is real inefficiency. But that is a capability and incentive problem rather than deliberate design, and the industry has improved hours lost by about 30% since 2019 (Siemens).

## Real bottleneck
Interpretation and action capacity, not sensing. Sensors cost about $115 and $50 a year. The scarce layers are: (a) trusted diagnosis with low false alarms, which still uses human analysts; (b) integration into work orders, parts and planning, which is why Tractian bundles CMMS; and (c) plant workforce to execute. Plus organisational change: 45.7% of maintenance is still reactive despite cheap tools.

## Company shapes implied
- A vertical "monitor + diagnose + work-order + parts" stack for one asset class with a high cost per hour, which is the Tractian direction. Value is captured by owning the workflow after the alert.
- An AI analyst layer that replaces human vibration and oil analysts across vendors' sensors, making the expert-in-the-loop model cheaper. The risk is that AWS shows generic ML commoditises. It needs a labelled failure corpus as the moat.
- An outcome-priced service (an uptime guarantee or insurance-linked monitoring), because ROI claims are unverified. Pricing on avoided downtime aligns incentives.
- Energy and grid assets (transformers, wind and solar inverters, data-center power): high cost per hour, "45% of data center outages are power", and fewer incumbents than discrete manufacturing.

## Surprises
- Amazon killed both Monitron (2024) and Lookout for Equipment (Oct 2026). Cheap sensors plus generic ML is not a business.
- Samsara's published equipment ROI is mostly theft recovery and utilization, not failure prediction.
- The cost per downtime hour rose 113-319% from 2019 to 2023 while hours fell, so the value of each avoided hour keeps rising.
- NIST: lost sales ($100B) dwarf direct repair and downtime costs.
- In Augury's own survey, workforce constraints (43%) now outrank unplanned downtime (40%).
