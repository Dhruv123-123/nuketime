# o01: OEM aftermarket economics: share of revenue and profit from services/spares in industrial and aerospace OEMs (GE Aerospace, Rolls-Royce, Caterpillar, Siemens Energy); what do OEM executives say is the strategy (razor-blade, lifecycle lock)?

Researched 2026-10-20. Note: the shared WebSearch budget for this workflow ran out partway through. A few items are marked [from memory, not re-verified this session] and should be checked before anyone quotes them.

## Findings (hard data first)

### GE Aerospace
1. **FY2025 CES services revenue was $25,010m out of $33,314m (about 75%); equipment was $8,304m.** On the restated segment basis it is $24,250m services out of $31,947m. The 10-K says CES was about 73% of GE Aerospace revenue and services were 75% of CES. CES operating profit was $8.9bn (about 26.7% margin). Services grew 26% in FY25 and 31% in Q4. Spare parts revenue grew more than 25%. Installed base is about 50,000 commercial and 30,000 military engines.
   - GE 2025 segment supplemental: https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/2025-ge-aerospace-segment-adjustment-supplemental-financials.pdf
   - 10-K FY2025: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40545/000004054526000008/ge-20251231.htm
   - Q4 2025 release: https://www.geaerospace.com/news/press-releases/ge-aerospace-announces-fourth-quarter-2025-results
2. **Q4 2025 CES margin contracted 420 bps because volume shifted toward new engines with a lower spare-engine ratio.** This is the razor-blade pattern showing up in reported numbers: more new equipment means lower margin.
3. **Culp, Aviation Week, 13 June 2023:** "A lot of people don't appreciate the reality that 70% of the business is in the aftermarket... It's those decades-long relationships with customers in the aftermarket that define what we do and the economics of the business." Also: "We are who we are, and that is the business model that has been built up over time."
   - https://aviationweek.com/shownews/paris-air-show/why-ges-larry-culp-all-aerospace
4. **Culp, Q4 2025 call (via Leeham):** "It's really all about our ability to move spare parts out to third parties to complete our own internal shop visits." Third-party shops do more than 15% of LEAP shop visits. GE has about $1bn of MRO investment planned, about $500m of it for LEAP. The LEAP fleet is expected to roughly triple from 2024 to 2030. In other words, the binding constraint is physical parts output and shop capacity, not knowledge.
   - https://leehamnews.com/2026/01/22/ge-aerospace-fy-and-q4-2025-earnings-thrust-higher-propelled-by-services-growth-leap-volume-and-expanding-margins/
5. **The "sold at or near cost" framing is secondary.** Fortune and analyst guides say GE/CFM engines are sold at cost or at a loss to win platforms. Safran disclosed negative LEAP OE margins in its early years (2016 investor material) and guided to per-unit gross breakeven by about 2025 at its 2021 CMD (via AirInsight). Safran investor doc: https://www.safran-group.com/download/media/396613 ; https://airinsight.com/leap-production-up-to-2-000-engines-from-2023/

### Rolls-Royce
6. **FY2025 Civil Aerospace underlying operating margin was 20.5% (2024: 16.6%).** The company credits "stronger large engine aftermarket performance, contractual margin improvements and higher spare engine profitability." H1 2025 margin was 24.9%. Group underlying operating profit was £3.5bn and free cash flow £3.3bn, the latter driven partly by LTSA balance growth (£0.6bn net of RRSAs). The LTSA (TotalCare, power-by-the-hour) model ties revenue to engine flying hours.
   - https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/press-releases/2026/26-02-2026-rr-holdings-plc-2025-full-year-results.aspx
   - https://www.rolls-royce.com/media/press-releases/2025/31-07-2025-rr-holdings-plc-2025-half-year-results.aspx
7. **The strategy is changing at the margin.** Under Erginbilgic (CEO from 2023, who called the company a "burning platform"), Rolls-Royce stopped signing loss-making OE engine deals "hoping to recoup the money through maintenance work later." It also pushes durability: more than a 100% time-on-wing improvement for in-production Trents by end-2027. Durability work reduces shop visits per flying hour, which matters because the company earns per flying hour under LTSAs, not per repair. This is evidence that a fixed-price-per-hour OEM is motivated to make engines more reliable, which contradicts a simple "built to be inefficient" reading.
   - https://fortune.com/europe/2023/12/20/rolls-royce-ceo-tufan-erginbilic-burning-platform-best-stock-performance-30-years
8. **2026 guidance:** 550-600 OE deliveries and 1,480-1,550 shop visits. Shop visits are up more than 50% in three years, and network capacity is planned to grow another 20%. New JV shops are opening (BAESL Beijing, up to 250 overhauls a year by the mid-2030s; Turkish Technic, up to 200 a year by end-2027). Capacity, not know-how, is what is being added.

### Caterpillar
9. **2019 goal: double ME&T services from $14bn (2016) to $28bn by 2026.** Actuals were $22bn in 2023, $24bn in 2024 and $24bn in 2025 (flat). At the 2025 Investor Day the target was reset to $30bn by 2030. Services are defined as "aftermarket parts and other service-related revenues." The connected fleet is more than 1.6 million assets. Total 2025 sales were $67.6bn, so services are about 35% of revenue. Caterpillar does not disclose a services margin, but management has historically said services/parts carry higher margins [qualitative].
   - 2023 annual report strategy: https://www.caterpillar.com/en/investors/reports/ar-archived-reports/annual-report-2023/executing-our-strategy.html
   - Q4 2025 call transcript: https://s25.q4cdn.com/358376879/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/4Q-2025-Caterpillar-Inc-Earnings-Conference-Call_Transcript.pdf
   - Q4 2025 slides: https://s25.q4cdn.com/358376879/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/4Q-2025-Analyst-Slide-Deck_Final.pdf
   - Interpretation: even a dominant OEM with dealers, telematics and 1.6m connected machines could not grow aftermarket capture on schedule. Parts and service share is contested by independents, will-fit parts and rebuilders. Lock-in is leakier in heavy equipment than in aero.

### Siemens Energy
10. **CMD November 2025, Gas Services (Karim Amin):** FY25 revenue €12.2bn, of which service is 66% and new units 34%. Order backlog is 77% service. Service backlog grew from €34bn (FY22) to €41bn (FY25) and is targeted above €50bn by FY28. Group service backlog is above €60bn. A gas turbine's service lifecycle is about 20 years, with about €30bn of service revenue potential on the ~78 GW new-unit backlog (about €400m per GW). Siemens Energy holds more than 90% share of service on its own fleet, with a more than 90% renewal rate for long-term programs. The margin target is 18-20% by FY28 (FY25: 13%). The slides cite "productivity gains via AI" from service scale. Transformation of Industry segment: service is more than 50% of revenue, and service revenue is about twice asset revenue.
    - https://assets.siemens-energy.com/dam/5fc093ca-4fd8-482c-8390-b39b004f66f3/251120_CMD-2025_Gas_Services_FINAL-pdf_Original%20file.pdf
    - https://assets.siemens-energy.com/dam/d5779232-c603-491a-942e-b39b004f7da7/251120_CMD-2025_Transformation_of_Industry_FINAL-pdf_Original%20file.pdf

### Cross-industry and customer view (aerospace)
11. **AlixPartners 2025 aerospace profit pool:** engine OEMs (GE, Safran, P&W, RR, MTU) took 47% of the profit pool at a record 19.5% margin. Lessors took 38%. Airframers earned about 1.5% EBIT, aerostructures -24%, MRO about 7%. AlixPartners says value sits with whoever controls design authority, IP and data rights, and that "in most subsystems the component designer keeps nearly all" of the aftermarket margin.
    - https://www.alixpartners.com/insights/102nrqd/engine-makers-gain-from-the-aerospace-earnings-divide/
12. **IATA/Oliver Wyman, "Reviving the Commercial Aircraft Supply Chain" (Oct 2025), customer/campaigner view:** The report's root cause #1 is that the economic model "has resulted in an unbalanced situation where OEMs aim to generate a larger portion of their profitability in the aftermarket (repairs and spare parts) versus new equipment sales." On the mechanism, OEMs hold "design details, technical publications, repair instructions, test equipment, and spare parts." Licensed MROs are "typically restricted by OEM commercial license terms from using other approved repairs (EASA Part 21/FAA DER), PMAs, and even USM." The report notes that embedded license and royalty costs are often invisible to airline procurement. **The cost structure is the key point.** Materials are about 60% of the direct cost of a narrowbody engine overhaul and repairs another 20-30%; labour is only about 5-10%. With life-limited parts the shop visit costs 2x or more and materials reach 65-80%. Licenses are most restrictive early in a platform's life; over time unlicensed providers develop cheaper repairs and USM. Supply-chain costs to airlines exceeded $11bn in 2025.
    - https://www.iata.org/contentassets/85b59d951fc04c1c83fa2aab47824300/reviving-the-commercial-aircraft-supply-chain.pdf
    - https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2025-releases/2025-10-13-01/
13. **IATA-CFM agreement renewed 20 Jan 2026 to Feb 2033 (first signed 2019).** CFM commits that manuals and repair instructions remain usable when engines contain non-CFM parts or repairs, and that warranties are judged on cause rather than sourcing. For CFM56, about 40 shops compete and CFM does about one third of overhauls. For LEAP, there are six Premier MROs plus a dozen-plus licensed shops. Walsh: "Airlines have long struggled with the aftermarket business practices of manufacturers." Aftermarket problems added $5.7bn to airline engine leasing and maintenance costs in 2025. There is a parallel IATA/Rolls-Royce best-practices statement on competition.
    - https://www.iata.org/en/pressroom/2026-releases/2026-01-20-01/
14. **[From memory, not re-verified this session] DoD IG DODIG-2019-060 on TransDigm:** 46 of 47 sole-source spare parts were sold at excess profit (about $16m excess on about $26m), with some markups above 4,000%. A follow-up report (2021) had similar findings. This is the extreme form of aftermarket lock: sole-source, proprietary parts where cost data is withheld. https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/1773201/review-of-parts-purchased-from-transdigm-group-inc-dodig-2019-060/

## Reasoning and synthesis

- **Share:** At heavy-asset OEMs, services and spares are 60-77% of segment revenue (GE CES 75%, Siemens Energy Gas Services 66%, Rolls-Royce Civil mostly aftermarket) and a larger share of profit, because OE is sold near breakeven or at a loss early in a program. Caterpillar is about 35% services, with weaker lock-in.
- **Strategy in executives' own words:** Executives rarely say "razor-blade." They say "installed base," "lifecycle," "decades-long relationships," "LTSA" and "service engine." Siemens Energy frames each new unit as a "~20-year service lifecycle" worth about €400m/GW, which is a lifecycle lock in all but name. Rolls-Royce has moved partly away from loss-leading OE pricing.
- **Where the money in a repair goes (aero engines):** It goes to materials (60-80%) and OEM-controlled repairs (20-30%). Diagnosis and labour are small. The value an OEM captures is enforced through **IP, design authority, certification and licensing, plus physical production of parts** (castings, forgings, HPT blades, LLPs). The epistemic "how do I fix this" gap is not where the cost sits.
- **What this means for C3/C4:** AI could make troubleshooting and manual navigation cheaper, but that is about 5-10% of the cost of the dominant event. Even a large drop there barely moves the total. The levers that matter are (a) legal and regulatory access (PMA, DER repairs, USM, ICAs under FAA Order 8110.54A) and (b) physical capacity. The IATA report notes that independents already erode OEM share over a platform's life through PMA, DER and USM. That is the existing analog of "AI parts factory" (C5), and it takes years per part because each needs approval.
- **Counter-evidence to C7 ("built to be inefficient"):** Under fixed-price power-by-the-hour contracts, the OEM's incentive is fewer shop visits, so it invests in durability (Rolls-Royce's 100% time-on-wing target). The system is structured to capture rent, which is not the same as being structured to be inefficient. Customers also buy risk transfer from OEMs, especially early in a platform's life, when only the OEM can price risk.
