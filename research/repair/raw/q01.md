# q01: Global maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) spend by sector (aviation, industrial, auto aftermarket, consumer, defence sustainment, energy): best available estimates with sources

## Method note and limitations (read first)
- The shared WebSearch budget for this workflow turn (200 calls across all agents) was used up before this agent ran a single search. Every search attempt came back "budget used up". I did not get around this. Instead I fetched primary-source URLs I already knew of directly with WebFetch.
- Many guessed URLs returned 404/403/503: the McKinsey aftermarket piece, the Auto Care Factbook, Census NAICS 811, BEA, Grainger IR, the Wood Mackenzie wind O&M page and the IATA MRO page.
- Findings marked **[VERIFIED-FETCH]** were read from the source during this session.
- Findings marked **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** are widely cited figures I recall from before my training cutoff. They were NOT re-checked in this session. Treat them as order-of-magnitude anchors, and re-check them in a follow-up run that has search budget.

## Findings

### Aviation (commercial)
1. **[VERIFIED-FETCH] Oliver Wyman, Global Fleet & MRO Market Forecast 2025-2035**
   - Commercial air transport MRO demand is **$119B in 2025**, a record 12% above the 2019 peak.
   - It is forecast to reach **$156B by 2035** (2.7% CAGR). North America goes from $28B to $34B.
   - Aircraft production fell from more than 1,800 aircraft (2018) to fewer than 1,300 (end of 2024), because of "supply chain delays and labor and raw material shortages".
   - Regulatory inspections of GTF and LEAP engines will run into 2027, grounding hundreds of aircraft at various times.
   - URL: https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2025/feb/global-fleet-and-mro-market-forecast-2025-2035.html
2. **[VERIFIED-FETCH] Oliver Wyman 2024-2034 edition**
   - 2024 MRO is about **$104B**, up from $101B in 2023. It is forecast at $124B by 2034.
   - Engine MRO is the main growth driver, because new engine platforms have problems.
   - The $104B (2024) to $119B (2025) jump comes partly from a change in scope or definition between editions, so the numbers are not strictly comparable.
   - URL: https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2024/feb/global-fleet-and-mro-market-forecast-2024-2034.html
3. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED] Segment mix**
   - Engines are typically about 40-45% of commercial MRO spend. Components are about 20-25%, line maintenance about 15-20%, and airframe heavy checks plus mods about 15%.
   - OEMs (engine makers especially, through power-by-the-hour / LTSA contracts) capture a large and growing share of engine and component aftermarket profit.
   - Sources are Oliver Wyman and AeroDynamic Advisory market commentary over many years.

### Defence sustainment
4. **[VERIFIED-FETCH] GAO-23-106217 (Military aircraft mission-capable rates and O&S costs)**
   - O&S costs for 49 reviewed aircraft fleets were about **$54B in FY2020**.
   - Maintenance costs rose about $1.2B since FY2011, even though total O&S fell $2.9B.
   - Only 4 of 49 fleets met mission-capable goals in a majority of years FY2011-21, and 26 never did.
   - Officials cited aging aircraft, maintenance challenges, and parts and supply problems.
   - URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106217
5. **[VERIFIED-FETCH] GAO-24-106703 (F-35 sustainment)**
   - Projected F-35 sustainment costs rose 44%, from **$1.1T (2018) to $1.58T (2023)**.
   - The Air Force cost per aircraft per year is $6.6M, against a $4.1M target.
   - 30 of 43 GAO F-35 recommendations remain unimplemented.
   - URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-24-106703
6. **[VERIFIED-FETCH] GAO-23-105341 (F-35 sustainment: DOD needs to reassess contractor role)**
   - Of an estimated **$1.7T** life-cycle cost, about **$1.3T is operating and sustainment** (about 76%).
   - DOD "relies heavily on its contractor to lead and manage F-35 sustainment." It has **not identified or obtained the technical data** it needs.
   - The fleet mission-capable rate was about 55% (March 2023). **More than 10,000 components were awaiting repair**, and depot stand-up was behind schedule.
   - As of March 2026, the recommendations are still mostly "Open - Partially Addressed".
   - URL: https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-105341
   - This is the strongest hard evidence in my set for a *data/IP rights* monopoly. It is a contractual/legal lock-in, not a pure knowledge gap.
7. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED] DoD-wide sustainment**
   - The commonly cited GAO/DoD figure is that O&S is about **70%** of a weapon system's life-cycle cost.
   - GAO has repeatedly said DOD spends roughly **$90B per year on weapon-system depot maintenance**.
   - The total DoD O&M appropriation is about $330-340B per year (FY2024-25), but that is broader than repair.
   - Global military MRO (aircraft only) is often estimated at about $40-45B per year.

### Auto aftermarket
8. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** The Auto Care Association / MEMA *Joint Outlook* puts the US light-vehicle aftermarket at roughly **$390-420B (2023-2025)**.
9. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** Global automotive aftermarket estimates (parts + service labour) are roughly **$0.8-1.2 trillion**.
   - McKinsey (around 2017-2023) put it at about EUR 760B, with about $1.2T by 2030. Independent aftermarket (IAM) channels hold the majority of out-of-warranty work in mature markets.
   - This is the single largest repair vertical, and it has the most mature independent / right-to-repair ecosystem (the 2014 Massachusetts / MOU data-access agreement).

### Industrial MRO
10. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** "Industrial MRO" as a *supplies-distribution* market (Grainger, Fastenal, MSC, Würth etc.) is commonly estimated at about **$650-750B globally**, and about $150-200B in the US and Canada (Grainger investor materials).
    - This counts parts/consumables only. It excludes in-house maintenance labour, which in process industries is often comparable or larger.
    - Rule-of-thumb maintenance budgets are 2-5% of replacement asset value per year.
11. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** Siemens *True Cost of Downtime* (2024) puts unplanned downtime at about **$1.4T per year for the Fortune Global 500**, about 11% of revenue.
    - This suggests that the cost of *not repairing in time* (downtime) can dwarf direct MRO spend in heavy industry.

### Energy
12. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** Global wind O&M spend is estimated at roughly **$15-30B per year** in the mid-2020s, rising toward $30-40B+ by 2030 (Wood Mackenzie-type estimates).
    - O&M is about 20-30% of wind LCOE.
    - Turbine OEM service agreements dominate the post-warranty market, but ISPs gain share as fleets age.
    - Power-plant/grid T&D maintenance and oil & gas maintenance add further tens to hundreds of billions. I did not obtain a reliable consolidated global figure.

### Consumer
13. **[BACKGROUND-UNVERIFIED]** US Census NAICS 811 (Repair & Maintenance) receipts are on the order of **$200-250B per year**. Automotive repair is the majority, about $150B+. Electronic and precision equipment, commercial machinery, and personal and household goods repair are tens of billions each.
    - Appliance and consumer-electronics repair is the *smallest* slice by dollars, and is fragmented and shrinking in many categories, because replacement is often cheaper than repair.
14. **[VERIFIED-FETCH, page metadata only] FTC "Nixing the Fix" (May 2021)**
    - The FTC report to Congress on repair restrictions exists and covers OEM control of parts, tools, diagnostics and information.
    - The page itself gave no figures, and I could not read the PDF in this session.
    - URL: https://www.ftc.gov/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions

## Reasoning re: hypotheses
- **Where the money goes.** The verified defence data and the aviation data both point the same way.
  - Cost and time are dominated by **parts availability, depot/shop capacity, and the physical repair/overhaul itself**, especially engines and components: "10,000 components awaiting repair"; GTF/LEAP groundings into 2027; parts and supply problems cited for low mission-capable rates.
  - Diagnosis is not where most of the dollars go.
  - This partly *contradicts* C4. Diagnosis, understanding and sourcing info are a minority of spend. The big-ticket items are parts (often OEM-controlled), labour hours of skilled certified technicians, and downtime.
- **C2.** The F-35 case strongly supports a lock-in, but the lock-in is **legal/contractual (technical data rights, IP)**. It is not merely a knowledge gap that AI dissolves.
  - AI cannot grant data rights, type certificates, or approved repair designs. This contradicts **C3** as stated.
- **C6.** Aviation and defence spend sit inside regulated certification regimes (FAA/EASA Part 145, PMA/DER approvals, military airworthiness). Certification is a gating layer, which supports C6.
- **C1.** The market data are organised by asset class and by MRO category (line/base/engine/component), not by a single chain. The chain is broadly consistent with practice, but my evidence says little about it beyond that.
- **C5.** No evidence here. The aviation backlog suggests parts are scarce, but making them is constrained by qualification/certification, not only by manufacturing.
- **C7.** Mixed. MRO spend keeps growing and readiness keeps falling despite huge budgets (supports C7). But that inefficiency is largely the result of incentives (OEM aftermarket economics, power-by-the-hour) and regulation, not stupidity. Those incentives are rational for incumbents.
