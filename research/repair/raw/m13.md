## Findings

### F1. Caterpillar dealer structure: independent, territorial, exclusive [VERIFIED]
- Source: Wikipedia, "Caterpillar Inc." https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_Inc.
- About 220 dealers serve nearly 200 countries. Dealers are independently owned and each has an **exclusive geographic territory**. They handle sales, maintenance and repair, rental and parts distribution, and most run on Caterpillar's Dealer Business System (DBS). Finning is the largest dealer, and the mining-heavy dealers WesTrac, Hastings Deering and others are listed.
- Relevance: the "dealer lock" is structural. Each territory has one authorised channel for OEM parts, OEM diagnostics (Cat ET / SIS) and warranty. This is a distribution and service monopoly per territory, not mainly a knowledge monopoly.

### F2. Replacement parts are a core profit pool for Caterpillar [VERIFIED that the issue exists; numbers BACKGROUND]
- Source: US Senate Permanent Subcommittee on Investigations, hearing "Caterpillar's Offshore Tax Strategy", 1 April 2014. https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/subcommittees/investigations/hearings/caterpillars-offshore-tax-strategy/ . The Wikipedia tax section (ref. 187) says Caterpillar SARL (Switzerland) earned profits from "sales of replacement parts" in 2007-2016, which was the subject of the IRS dispute and settlement.
- [BACKGROUND] The PSI report described the replacement-parts business as Caterpillar's most profitable line. The Swiss structure shifted roughly $8bn of parts profits offshore and deferred roughly $2.4bn of US tax (2000-2012). Commentators have long noted that parts and service carry well above whole-goods margins.
- Relevance: the OEM economic model is built around aftermarket parts and service. This supports a weak form of C7: the system is optimised for OEM and dealer capture, not for the owner's lowest total cost.

### F3. Caterpillar is steering the company toward services [BACKGROUND]
- Source: Caterpillar Investor Day 2022 and 2025, plus annual reports (caterpillar.com, investors.caterpillar.com; not fetchable this session).
- Figures: services revenue was ~$14bn in 2016, ~$22bn in 2022 and ~$24bn in 2023-2024. The 2022 target was $28bn by 2026. A later target extends further, to roughly $30bn by 2030 (verify). Caterpillar reports ~1.4-1.5 million connected assets and uses them to drive "Customer Value Agreements" (CVAs) and parts sales.
- Relevance: the OEM is already monetising data and diagnostics. Condition monitoring telemetry (VIMS/Product Link on mining trucks since the 1990s) and S·O·S fluid analysis are mature. The diagnostic knowledge is not missing; it is owned by the OEM and dealer, then packaged and sold back.

### F4. Mining maintenance is a large share of cost; availability is the KPI [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: widely cited industry and consultancy figures (McKinsey Metals & Mining; Deloitte "Tracking the Trends"; SMRP/mining maintenance literature).
- Maintenance is commonly quoted at **30-50% of mine operating costs** (some sources say 20-35% for open pit; the higher figures include parts and labour together).
- Typical targets for large haul trucks are **85-92% mechanical availability**, with best-in-class around 90%+. Loading tools usually run lower (80-88%). Planned-to-unplanned maintenance ratio targets are often 80:20.
- The binding economic variable is availability and utilisation (tonnes moved per hour), not the cost of a single repair event. One idle ultra-class truck can forgo several thousand USD per hour in production.
- McKinsey (2015-2017) found mining productivity fell roughly 28% from its 2004 peak to 2014, with equipment effectiveness and operational discipline cited as the main gaps. Industry OEE for large mobile fleets is often reported in the 40-60% range.
- Relevance: if the four intermediate steps fell 10x in cost, the result would be bounded by how much downtime they actually cause. In mining, unplanned downtime is driven by **parts and component availability, labour shortage and planning discipline**.

### F5. Contracted availability guarantees (MARC/CVA) are the dealer lock in practice [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: dealer annual reports (Finning, WesTrac/Seven Group, Hastings Deering/Sime Darby, Komatsu distributors); Caterpillar MARC (Maintenance and Repair Contract) literature.
- Dealers sell **MARCs priced per operating hour, with guaranteed availability** (for example 85-90% on trucks). The dealer bears component life risk, keeps component exchange pools and runs on-site workshops.
- Finning: product support (parts and service) is ~60%+ of revenue, and mining customers are product-support heavy. [BACKGROUND, verify in Finning annual report.]
- Relevance: many miners willingly buy the lock in exchange for availability risk transfer. This contradicts a pure "knowledge monopoly" reading (C2): the customer is buying **risk-bearing capacity and rotable capital**, not just knowledge.

### F6. Rebuild and reman economics: OEMs already capture the "second life" [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: Cat Reman and Cat Certified Rebuild marketing; Komatsu Reman (e.g., Komatsu Reman Center in Chile and Australia); USITC "Remanufactured Goods" study, Pub. 4356 (2012), https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/pub4356.pdf (403 this session).
- Cat Reman components are typically priced at **~40-70% of new**, with an "as new" warranty and **core return** required. Cat Certified Rebuild of whole machines (trucks, dozers) costs roughly **~50-60% of a new machine**. Caterpillar claims these programmes take back tens of thousands of tonnes of cores a year (~2 million+ units a year historically).
- The USITC (2012) identified heavy-duty and off-road (HDOR) equipment as the **largest US remanufacturing sector** (~$7-8bn output, about a third of US reman). Reman goods sold at substantial discounts to new.
- Typical component lives on 200-400 t haul trucks: engines ~18,000-25,000 hours to overhaul, final drives and wheel motors ~15,000-30,000 hours. These are planned "component change-outs" from exchange pools.
- Relevance (against C5): rebuild of high-value components is a mature, industrialised, OEM-controlled business. The value comes from cores, metallurgy, salvage specs and test benches, with OEM salvage and reuse guidelines as proprietary knowledge. It is not a gap that an AI parts factory obviously fills.

### F7. Independent aftermarket competes where parts are simple and high-wear [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: industry knowledge of aftermarket suppliers such as ITR/USCO and Berco (undercarriage), ESCO (now Weir) and Hensley (Komatsu) ground-engaging tools (GET), Bradken, and independent rebuilders (e.g., large independent component rebuild shops in Australia, Chile and the US).
- High-volume wear parts (GET, undercarriage, filters, hoses) have a large non-OEM share. Mining houses routinely use non-OEM GET (Weir ESCO is a top supplier). Independent component rebuilders win on price and turnaround.
- Relevance (mixed on C2 and C7): where knowledge and geometry are simple, the market is already contestable. The lock is strongest on electronics and calibrated diagnostics, emissions software, warranty, and large, metallurgically critical parts.

### F8. Right-to-repair legal pressure on equipment OEMs: ag yes, mining and construction mostly exempt [VERIFIED for ag; BACKGROUND for construction exclusions]
- FTC and the Illinois and Minnesota AGs v. Deere & Company, filed 15 January 2025, N.D. Ill. (FTC vote 3-2). Source: https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-states-sue-deere-company-protect-farmers-unfair-corporate-tactics-high-repair-costs
  - The complaint alleges Deere maintains a **"100% market share"** in repairs requiring its fully functional tool (Service ADVISOR, dealer-only). Customer Service ADVISOR is "inferior". Deere allegedly does not provide generic tool makers the information needed to build a full tool, "unlike automotive and trucking manufacturers". Dealers "almost always" use Deere-branded parts. [VERIFIED]
- Colorado's Consumer Right to Repair Agricultural Equipment Act (2023) was the first US farm-equipment law. Its digital electronic equipment law (HB24-1121) takes effect 1 January 2026. The AEM/EDA "Statement of Principles" (2018) was criticised in 2021 for non-delivery. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_repair [VERIFIED]
- [BACKGROUND] State digital fair repair laws (e.g., New York 2022, Minnesota 2023) generally **exclude** off-road, construction and mining equipment. In mining, the Deere-style fight has largely not happened, because big miners hold enough bargaining power and in-house capability.
- Relevance: supports C2 as an **access-control** problem (software tools, parts channel), not an "unknowable knowledge" problem. AI cannot unlock a dealer-only, authenticated diagnostic tool or an encrypted ECU. The barrier is legal and technical access, which C3 skips over.

### F9. Skilled labour shortage, not knowledge, is a recurring bottleneck [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: Australian Jobs and Skills Council and Mining Skills Organisation reports; Minerals Council of Australia; dealer earnings commentary (Finning, Seven Group/WesTrac) 2022-2025 on technician shortages; Caterpillar dealer technician-programme messaging (e.g., "ThinkBIG").
- Heavy-diesel fitters, auto-electricians and boilermakers sit on skills-shortage lists in Australia and Canada, and dealers cite technician capacity as a constraint on product-support growth.
- Relevance: AI-assisted diagnosis can raise the productivity of each fitter (an analogy for C3). But hands-on change-outs, line boring, welding, crane work and isolation and safety (permit-to-work) on 400 t machines stay labour-bound, so the "repair" stage stays expensive.

### F10. Parts and tyre supply shocks dominated downtime episodes [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: 2005-2008 and 2011-2013 OTR (ultra-class) tyre shortages, with mines parking trucks; 2021-2023 supply-chain lead-time spikes for components and engines, cited in Caterpillar, Komatsu and dealer earnings calls as "supply constraints"; and dealers' practice of carrying large rotable component pools.
- Relevance: **sourcing and delivery** of physical items is a real bottleneck, supporting part of C4. But the cost is in capacity, cores and capital tied up in inventory, not in information. AI can improve forecasting and pooling, but it cannot conjure a 63-inch OTR tyre or a final drive.

### F11. Autonomy and condition monitoring are OEM-controlled ecosystems, adding lock [BACKGROUND, partly VERIFIED]
- Komatsu FrontRunner and Cat MineStar Command each run on the order of **700+ autonomous haul trucks** worldwide (2024-2025), together with billions of tonnes hauled. Autonomy yields productivity gains commonly cited at 15-30%, and reported lower maintenance and tyre costs from consistent driving.
- [VERIFIED] Caterpillar agreed in October 2025 to acquire mining software provider RPMGlobal (Wikipedia ref. 101).
- Relevance: AI and data are being used by **incumbents** to deepen lock-in through a proprietary fleet-management, autonomy and health-monitoring stack. This works against C3's implication that AI dissolves the monopoly. AI may concentrate the knowledge rather than diffuse it.

### F12. Additive and bespoke parts in mining: real, but niche and qualification-bound [BACKGROUND]
- Sources: BHP and other miners' on-demand and 3D-printed spare parts pilots (around 2018-2022, with partners such as Wabtec and other AM firms); Sandvik and Weir additive programmes; Caterpillar's own AM work for obsolete and low-volume parts.
- Published cases are mostly non-critical parts (covers, brackets, impellers, liners, some wear parts) or legacy parts for out-of-production machines. Critical structural and drivetrain parts need material certification, heat treatment and OEM-equivalent specs.
- Relevance (against C5 as "solved"): bespoke manufacturing helps with **obsolescence and long-tail lead time**. Mining is a good beachhead (remote sites, long lead times, old fleets), but qualification and liability, not CAD knowledge, set the pace.

## Stances on the claims

- **C1 (universal chain): mixed.** Mining follows the chain for **unplanned** failures. But most value runs on a **planned component-life model**: change-out at hours or condition from rotable exchange pools, with rebuild off-site in parallel. This splits the chain into two loops (a fast swap on the machine, a slow rebuild in the shop). "Diagnose, then source" is largely pre-empted by pre-positioned rotables. Safety inspection is mostly isolation, commissioning and statutory checks, not certification as in aviation.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly): mixed, leaning to "access and channel monopoly".** Exclusive dealer territories, dealer-only diagnostic and calibration tools and proprietary salvage and reman specs are real lock (F1, F2, F8). But large miners have deep in-house knowledge and still buy MARCs for risk transfer and capital (F5), and simple wear parts are already contestable (F7).
- **C3 (AI removes the gap): contradicts.** The barriers are authenticated software, encrypted ECUs, warranty terms, parts channel, labour and capital. OEMs are using AI and telemetry to deepen lock (F3, F11). AI can help independents and miners with diagnosis and documentation, but it does not grant access.
- **C4 (10x cost drop in four middle steps): mixed, mostly contradicts on magnitude.** Downtime cost is dominated by component and parts availability and labour. Diagnosis is already largely telemetry-driven for big fleets. Sourcing and delivery costs are physical (pools, freight to remote sites, tyres). A 10x cut in information costs would move total repair cost far less than 10x.
- **C5 (bespoke parts solved): contradicts "solved"; supports it as an opportunity.** AM for obsolete and long-tail parts at remote mines is real but niche. Material qualification and liability bind (F12). Core rebuilding is a mature, OEM-led industry (F6).
- **C6 (inspection remains essential): mostly supports, weakly.** Safety (isolation, brakes and steering checks, statutory inspections under mine safety regulators such as MSHA and state regulators) remains central. Evidence on inspection "evolving for AI" is thin in this domain. Autonomy brings new functional-safety assurance (ISO 17757), which partly supports C6.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient): mixed.** It is built for **OEM and dealer margin capture** (parts profit pool, F2) and territorial exclusivity. But for mining customers, the system is a sensible response to extreme downtime costs (availability guarantees, pools, on-site dealer crews). It is inefficient on price, but efficient at what large customers buy: uptime.

## Where money and time go
In mining, the dominant cost is **lost production from unavailability**, not the repair labour bill. Within direct maintenance spend, **parts and components (including rebuilds and exchange units) are typically the largest share, often around half or more**, ahead of labour (roughly a third), then contractors and overhead [BACKGROUND, verify]. Time is lost waiting on parts and components, technicians and planned-shutdown windows. Diagnosis is a minor share for big telemetry-equipped fleets, but larger for mid-tier and older fleets and for electrical and electronic faults.

## Real bottleneck
The binding constraints are rotable component capital and core supply, skilled technicians at remote sites, and OEM control of diagnostic and calibration software, warranty and the parts channel through exclusive dealers. Knowledge itself is not the bottleneck.

## Company shapes suggested
1. **An independent, OEM-agnostic diagnostic and maintenance copilot** for mid-tier miners, quarries and contractors with mixed Cat, Komatsu, Hitachi and Liebherr fleets. It would read telemetry, oil analysis and work orders and cut time-to-diagnose. The risk: OEM data access (ISO 15143-3/AEMP 2.0 telematics is open but limited).
2. **A pooled rotable and component-exchange marketplace or network** for independent rebuilders. This attacks the capital and logistics bottleneck, not knowledge.
3. **Qualified on-demand manufacturing for obsolete and long-lead mining parts.** It would combine reverse engineering, AM and casting with a certification package. Remote mines are the beachhead.
4. **A labour-multiplier toolset for fitters**: AR and AI guidance plus planning and scheduling optimisation, aimed at the technician shortage.
5. **Autonomy-retrofit or functional-safety assurance tooling** (relevant to the C6 angle).
