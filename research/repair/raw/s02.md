## 1. Hard findings (fetched this session)

F1. FTC, Nixing the Fix (May 2021), report to Congress. "there is scant evidence to support manufacturers' justifications for repair restrictions." Repair restrictions "steered consumers into manufacturers' repair networks or to replace products before the end of their useful lives." The fixes advocates seek (access to information, manuals, spare parts, tools) are "well supported" by the record. (kind: regulator)
URL: https://www.ftc.gov/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions (PDF: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions/nixing_the_fix_report_final_5521_630pm-508_002.pdf)

F2. FTC on quality. Asked whether anyone had studied if authorized providers do higher-quality or more secure repairs than independents, the CTA's Walter Alcorn "said he was not aware that anyone has studied that question." The FTC calls most support for the OEM quality claim "anecdotal." Microsoft said it often received devices that failed after third-party repair, "without citing specific examples or numbers." The one hard number Microsoft gave: off-spec power supplies caused at least 12 serious overheating incidents. (kind: regulator) Same URL.

F3. FTC on parts pairing (2019 workshop record). iFixit testified that replacing an iPhone screen, "even if it's with a brand new OEM screen off of another identical iPhone," disables features such as True Tone. Apple "synchronizes some iPhone parts to the device's logic board, making the part repairable only by Apple." Apple also sent DMCA takedowns to people who posted Apple service manuals. (kind: regulator) Same URL.

F4. Price elasticity of repair (FTC citing Apple). In 2018 Apple cut the out-of-warranty iPhone battery replacement from $79 to $29. Apple reportedly expected 1-2 million replacements and did more than 11 million. Tim Cook's January 2019 investor letter named cheap battery replacements as one cause of weak iPhone sales. (kind: data / history) Same URL; https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2019/01/letter-from-tim-cook-to-apple-investors/
Reasoning: when the price gate drops, repair demand rises by an order of magnitude. That supports the claim that repair is suppressed. But the price here was a policy choice (margin and incentive), not a knowledge gap.

F5. FTC on the IRP contract. Lawyers called Apple's Independent Repair Provider contract "crazy" and "onerous." It allows "unannounced audits and inspections by Apple," and lets Apple keep inspecting a shop for up to five years after it leaves the program. (kind: regulator citing news) Same URL.

F6. FTC citing U.S. PIRG (January 2021): "Repair could reduce household spending on electronics and appliances by 22 percent," about $330 per family per year. (kind: campaigner_view, cited by regulator)

F7. iFixit review of Apple's IRP and Self Service Repair. IRPs pay Apple $270 for a screen. The Apple Store charges $280 for the same screen installed. So an independent cannot compete on genuine parts once labor and overhead are added. Self Service tool rental needs a $1,200 deposit hold, plus up to $1,200 more if the tools come back damaged. After a non-paired battery swap the phone shows "Unable to verify this iPhone has a genuine Apple battery" and battery health disappears. Face ID and Touch ID can stop working. Only Apple and authorized locations can re-pair parts. The workarounds (microsoldering the original battery chip, reprogramming the EEPROM) need specialist skill and tools. (kind: practitioner_view)
URL: https://www.ifixit.com/News/69320/apple-self-service-repair-program-review

F8. iFixit cut the iPhone 14 repairability score from 7/10 to 4/10 because of parts pairing. Hardware design had improved: rear-glass repair fell from $549 to $169. But screen and battery swaps need a software handshake with Apple's servers, so Apple must know about the repair. Aftermarket parts cannot be paired, and warnings appear even for genuine parts. (kind: practitioner_view)
URL: https://www.ifixit.com/News/82493/apple-parts-pairing-oregon
Reasoning: this is the key point for C2 and C3. The gate is cryptographic and server-side, not informational. An AI that knows exactly how to fix the phone still cannot produce the pairing authorization.

F9. Apple Newsroom, November 2021: Self Service Repair announced, starting with iPhone 12 and 13, then M1 Macs, with more than 200 parts and tools. Apple counted more than 2,800 IRPs and more than 5,000 AASPs, and said it had "nearly doubled" its service locations in three years. Apple still says certified professionals are the safest option for most customers. (kind: oem_view)
URL: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2021/11/apple-announces-self-service-repair/

F10. Apple Newsroom, April 2024. Used genuine parts will work in repairs, with on-device calibration. Ordering Self Service parts no longer needs a serial number, except for logic boards. Activation Lock now extends to parts: a part harvested from a locked or lost device has restricted calibration. Apple counts more than 10,000 IRPs and AASPs, with Self Service covering 40 products in 33 countries. (kind: oem_view)
URL: https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2024/04/apple-to-expand-repair-options-with-support-for-used-genuine-parts/
Reasoning: this is the strongest steelman for pairing. It cuts the theft and parts-harvesting economy, which is a real security function (C6-like). Pairing is partly an anti-theft and anti-counterfeit system, not only rent extraction.

F11. Apple Support: Self Service Repair runs read manual, order parts (some need serial or IMEI), repair, then return old parts. "System Configuration" is a post-repair software step needed to finish many repairs. (kind: oem_view)
URL: https://support.apple.com/self-service-repair
Reasoning: Apple's own flow maps onto the founder's chain (C1), and it adds an OEM-controlled software step where inspection or return-to-service would sit. In phones, "safety inspection" is effectively a cryptographic authenticity check run by the OEM.

F12. EU Ecodesign rules for smartphones and tablets, applying from 20 June 2025. Critical spare parts must reach repairers within 5-10 working days, for 7 years after the model's EU sales end. Professional repairers get non-discriminatory access to the software and firmware needed for replacement. Batteries must last at least 800 cycles at 80% or more of initial capacity. OS upgrades must be offered for at least 5 years. (kind: regulator)
URL: https://energy-efficient-products.ec.europa.eu/product-list/smartphones-and-tablets_en
Reasoning: regulators have targeted the "source parts" and "deliver parts" steps (C4), plus software access (pairing), but by mandate, not AI. The EU Right to Repair Directive (2024/1799) is to be transposed by 31 July 2026, with a repair platform due by 2027-2028 (via Wikipedia).

F13. Oregon SB 1596 (2024, Chapter 69). OEMs must make available "on fair and reasonable terms" the documentation, tools and parts they give authorized providers. Penalty: up to $1,000 per day. (kind: regulator)
URL: https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Measures/Overview/SB1596
[BACKGROUND, unverified this session] SB 1596 also bans parts pairing that blocks function or degrades performance, for devices made after 1 January 2025. Apple publicly opposed that provision. The measure summary page did not show the pairing clause, and Wikipedia gives conflicting dates (2025 vs 1 July 2027).

F14. Asurion and uBreakiFix industry structure.
- Asurion: private, Nashville-based. About $8.5B revenue (2019 report). $11.3B of term loans after a 2018 $3.75B leveraged loan. Owned by private equity since 2007. Insurance and protection model across phones, electronics and appliances. Asurion's About page now says "Over 18,000 people," "700+ locations," and same-day in-home service across 85% of the U.S.
- uBreakiFix: founded 2009 in Orlando as a mail-in phone repair business. More than 920 locations by 2016 (a franchise model). Google's only authorized walk-in Pixel repairer from 2016. Samsung authorized in-warranty repair center from 2018, with OEM parts. Bought by Asurion in August 2019. Canadian arm sold to Mobile Klinik in 2023.
URLs: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asurion ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBreakiFix ; https://www.asurion.com/about/
(kind: data / news)
Reasoning: the biggest U.S. independent chain was absorbed into an insurance and financing company. Repair margin sits in the insurance or protection layer (premiums and deductibles), carrier distribution, and OEM authorization deals, not in repair know-how. The winning "repair" company in phones is a capital-heavy, distribution-heavy insurance float business.

F15. Wikipedia (right to repair), citing a Terrance Group study: about 59% of independent repair services still struggle to get parts and diagnostic tools from manufacturers. Wikipedia also notes many small shops avoided the IRP because of its burdensome terms. Apple's IRP launched in August 2019. (kind: data, secondary)
URL: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics_right_to_repair

## 2. Background knowledge (NOT verified this session; flag before use)
- Self Service Repair launched in 2022. Reviewers (The Verge, iFixit) got about 79 lb of tool cases. Rental was $49 per week, with a $1,200 card hold. An iPhone 12 mini battery kit cost about the same as Apple's in-store repair, and the "System Configuration" step needed a chat with an Apple remote team.
- Apple's out-of-warranty screen price for the iPhone 15 and 16 Pro Max is about $379. AppleCare+ cuts screen repair to about $29. The protection-plan price structure, not knowledge, is what steers volume.
- Most phone repairs by volume are screens and batteries, then charge ports and cameras. Diagnosis is usually obvious: a cracked screen or a degraded battery. The cost is mostly the OLED panel (often $150-300 genuine) plus labor plus OEM authorization. Board-level faults (microsoldering) are where real diagnostic skill matters, and they are a minority of jobs.
- Market size: published estimates of the global smartphone repair market range widely, from about $4B to over $25B, depending on whether insurance, refurbishing and parts are counted. Treat any single figure as low-confidence and vendor-driven. U.S. device-protection premiums (Asurion, Assurant, AppleCare) are likely larger than U.S. walk-in repair revenue.
- Pairing beyond Apple: Samsung's contract with independent shops (reported 2024, 404 Media) required shops to share customer data and to disassemble phones with aftermarket parts. That is another sign the gate is contractual and legal, not knowledge.

## 3. Synthesis against the hypotheses

C1 (universal chain): Mostly supported. Apple's own flow (manual, order, repair, System Configuration, return the part) maps onto it. But in phones the "notice" and "diagnose" steps are trivial for most jobs, and the chain gains an extra OEM authorization or pairing step before return to service. The chain holds, but the cost weights differ sharply by domain.

C2 (knowledge monopoly): Mixed. The OEM did hold information (DMCA takedowns of manuals, FTC F3). But the stronger levers today are not knowledge. They are (a) serialized and paired parts gated server-side, (b) parts pricing set so the OEM undercuts independents ($270 vs $280), (c) contract terms (IRP audits), and (d) protection-plan pricing that steers volume. iFixit and other open manuals have long made the know-how for screens and batteries freely available. The monopoly is over authorization and parts, not understanding.

C3 (AI erases the gap): Mostly contradicted for phones. AI cannot produce Apple's pairing handshake or Activation Lock clearance. Where knowledge does matter (board-level diagnosis), AI may help, but the hand skill (microsoldering) remains the bottleneck.

C4 (10x drop on diagnose, understand, source, deliver): Mixed to contradicted. Diagnose and understand are already cheap for most phone repairs, so there is little left to cut. Parts cost is dominated by part price and OEM gating. The EU already mandates 5-10 day parts delivery. The 2018 battery price cut shows that cutting the price gate unlocks large latent demand (more than 11M vs 1-2M expected). But that came from a policy and price lever, not from AI on knowledge.

C5 (bespoke AI parts factory): Contradicted for phones. OLED panels, batteries, cameras and SoCs cannot be made bespoke, and a remade part would be rejected by pairing anyway. The real substitute is a harvested or used genuine part, which Apple now allows (F10), with anti-theft limits.

C6 (inspection and certification essential, must evolve): Supported in a reframed way. In phones, "certification" has become cryptographic part authentication run by the OEM (pairing, Parts and Service History, Activation Lock on parts). The open question is who controls the authentication: the OEM or a neutral party. The FTC found no study comparing authorized and independent repair quality (F2), so there is an evidence vacuum about inspection.

C7 (structurally inefficient, must be rebuilt): Partly supported. The FTC (F1) found that restrictions steer consumers to OEM networks and to early replacement, on scant evidence. But the market is being restructured by regulation (EU Ecodesign, the R2R Directive, state laws in CA, NY, MN and OR) and consolidation (Asurion), not by startups. Phone repair is also arguably efficient for the OEM and insurer, if not socially. It is not "built for inefficiency" so much as built for capture.

## 4. Where money and time go (phones)
Part price (genuine OLED or battery), skilled labor, and OEM authorization or pairing dominate cost. Diagnosis is usually minutes. Delivery is days. The insurance or protection layer captures a large share of consumer spending. Time is lost mainly when parts must be ordered with a serial number and checked, or when the device must go to an authorized depot.

## 5. Surprises
- Apple charges independents nearly its own retail repair price for the bare part ($270 vs $280).
- Cutting battery replacement from $79 to $29 produced more than 11M replacements against 1-2M expected, and visibly hurt iPhone sales.
- No study comparing the quality of authorized and independent repair existed as of the 2019 FTC workshop.
- Apple now frames pairing as anti-theft (Activation Lock on parts). That is a legitimate security role that complicates a pure "knowledge monopoly" story.
- The largest U.S. independent repair chain became part of an insurance and PE-debt company.
