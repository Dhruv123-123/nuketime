## Findings

### 1. Decisiv SRM: scale and how much downtime is "non-wrench" [VERIFIED, vendor claim]
Source: https://www.decisiv.com/ (fetched 2026-10-20)
- "more than 14,000 repair events each day"; "5,000+ Service Locations in North America"; "7M+ Assets in Active Operation"; "nearly 60 percent of commercial vehicles" are managed on the SRM platform.
- Claims a 33% downtime reduction and vehicles returned to service 1-2 days faster. For multi-day repairs the average gain is 3.36 days.
- Uses $1,500 per truck per day as average fleet revenue in its ROI maths.
- The news page shows "34 Million Service Events" (the image filename says 39M, 2026).
- Reasoning: Decisiv's product is communication, approval, estimate and status workflow between fleet, dealer, OEM and component supplier. It does not do diagnosis AI or make parts. If coordination alone saves around a third of downtime, a big share of dwell is *information-flow and approval latency*, not knowledge or wrench time. This supports C7 (the system is badly coordinated). It only partly supports C2: the gap is coordination, not expertise.

### 2. Downtime value per truck-day [VERIFIED for the $1,500 figure; PRIOR for the others]
- Decisiv: about $1,500 revenue per truck per day (verified, above).
- [PRIOR, unverified] An often-cited range is $448-$760 per truck per day in downtime cost (Fleet Advantage / TMC-style estimates). Trade press often quotes ATRI or TMC numbers above $1,000 per day once lost revenue, rental and driver pay are included.
- Implication: the cost of a repair event in trucking is driven mainly by **days out of service**, not parts or labour invoices. A $2k repair that takes 5 days costs a fleet $3.75k-$7.5k in lost revenue.

### 3. Technician shortage is structural and large [VERIFIED]
Source: TechForce Foundation, Technician Supply & Demand Report (9th ed., May 2026): https://techforce.org/supply-demand-report/
- Diesel technician supply gap: **52%**. Median diesel tech wage $60,640 (BLS OEWS May 2024).
- All transport techs: 241,842 annual openings vs 101,743 annual graduates (58% unmet demand). 1.2M new techs needed by 2029. $7.42B in annual economic output lost.
- Reasoning: this is a *labour* bottleneck at the physical "complete the repair" stage, which AI knowledge tools do not remove. It cuts against C4's focus on the four middle steps. AI could raise each tech's productivity: less diagnostic time and a less-skilled tech could do more. That is a partial route.

### 4. Right to repair in heavy trucks: information was legally opened about a decade ago [PRIOR, unverified + VERIFIED context]
- [PRIOR, unverified] In 2015 the Truck & Engine Manufacturers Association (EMA) and the Commercial Vehicle Solutions Network / independent groups signed a Commercial Vehicle Right to Repair MOU. OEMs committed to make diagnostic and repair information available to independents on the same terms as dealers, from about model year 2018. This followed Massachusetts' right-to-repair statute.
- [VERIFIED] FTC *Nixing the Fix* (May 2021): "no industry sector other than the automotive industry has worked to open repair markets through a self-regulatory framework". The auto MOU of Jan 2014 "had the effect of creating a broad, if not complete, right to repair". https://www.ftc.gov/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions
- [VERIFIED] FTC: "there is scant evidence to support manufacturers' justifications for repair restrictions". The automotive sector shows independents can repair complex machines safely.
- Reasoning: this matters for C2/C3. In trucking, *formal* access to OEM information already exists, yet downtime remains high (findings 1, 2, 3). So the knowledge-access monopoly is **weaker** in trucking than in ag or aerospace. The remaining friction is the **cost and usability** of that information: per-OEM subscription diagnostic software (e.g. Cummins INSITE, Detroit DiagnosticLink, PACCAR ESA, Volvo/Mack PTT), the need for multiple tools, and tribal know-how. Independents often pay several thousand dollars a year per OEM tool suite [PRIOR, unverified]. This is a *fragmentation/cost* gap, not a pure monopoly, and it is one AI plus aggregation could compress.

### 5. Emissions systems (DEF/SCR/DPF) as a self-inflicted downtime generator; regulator response [PRIOR, unverified]
- [PRIOR, unverified] In August 2025 EPA announced guidance to end sudden severe "derates" (speed cut to about 5 mph) caused by DEF/SCR sensor faults. It cited farmer and trucker complaints about stranding and downtime. New engines are to avoid abrupt derates, and existing fleets are to get software updates. Fetch of the EPA DEF page returned 503 this run.
- Reasoning: this is a clear case of the repair system being "built to be inefficient". It is not malice: it is a regulatory design, a tamper-proofing inducement, that turns a cheap sensor failure into a multi-day tow-and-dealer event. That supports C7 in a specific, non-conspiratorial way. It also shows the bottleneck can be *regulatory/certification design* (C6) rather than knowledge.

### 6. Telematics is already widespread; the data exists, the action loop is the gap [VERIFIED for Fleetio; PRIOR for mandate and penetration]
- [VERIFIED] Fleetio homepage: "Powering 1,300,000+ fleet assets" (another banner says 1,000,000+). It sells an "AI Service Advisor" (customer Ramos Oil "saves 90+ minutes daily"). Auto-Chlor "avoids $280K+ in repair spend annually by catching unnecessary repairs before approval". It makes no downtime-reduction claim. https://www.fleetio.com/
- [PRIOR, unverified] The FMCSA ELD mandate (compliance Dec 2017 / hard enforcement Dec 2019) put a connected device in almost every interstate Class 8 truck. Samsara, Motive, Geotab and OEM telematics (Detroit Connect, Cummins Connected Diagnostics, Volvo Remote Diagnostics) already push fault codes with severity triage to fleets and dealers.
- Reasoning: "noticing" and first-pass "diagnosing" are already partly automated in trucking (remote diagnostics since about 2013-2015 at OEMs). AI's value here is triage quality and fewer false alarms, not new capability. The Auto-Chlor example points to another inefficiency: *over-repair / unnecessary repair* is a cost leak. That is information asymmetry between fleet and shop: the shop knows more and bills more. It supports a narrower version of C2.

### 7. ATRI operational costs: R&M is a mid-sized line item, but rising [VERIFIED landing pages only; numbers PRIOR, unverified]
- [VERIFIED] ATRI published "Analysis of the Operational Costs of Trucking: 2026 Update" (July 2026). The landing page notes industry-wide costs "accelerating" and gives no figures. Its 2026 Top Industry Issues report (Oct 2026) puts fuel prices as the top concern (diesel "topped $6.50 per gallon", per a TCW quote). https://truckingresearch.org/
- [PRIOR, unverified] ATRI's 2024 edition (2023 data) reported a record repair & maintenance cost of about **$0.20 per mile**, roughly 9% of a total marginal cost of about $2.27 per mile. R&M rose sharply in 2022-2023 (parts inflation, tech wages, more complex emissions/electronic systems). Driver wages and fuel are each about 3-4x bigger.
- Reasoning: if R&M is about 9% of cost per mile and the four middle steps are some fraction of that, then even a 10x cut in them moves *direct* fleet cost per mile only a few percent. The **bigger lever is downtime** (lost revenue, finding 2), which C4 does capture if "delivering parts" and "diagnosing" are the critical path.

### 8. Dealer vs independent vs in-house [PRIOR, unverified]
- Large fleets often do in-house PM and light repair. Warranty, emissions, engine and complex electrical work goes to OEM dealers, partly because warranty requires it. Small fleets and owner-operators (most US carriers run fewer than 10 trucks) depend on dealers and independents.
- Common practitioner complaint (trade press, TMC sessions): dealer bays are clogged, trucks wait days to be *looked at* (triage queue) before diagnosis even starts, and parts back-orders (2021-2023 especially) stretch dwell. Independents are faster but lack some OEM software and warranty authority.
- Reasoning: "time to first touch" and "waiting for parts/approval" dominate the time chain, not "understanding". This is consistent with Decisiv's thesis (finding 1).

### 9. Parts sourcing and delivery: dense distribution already exists for common parts [PRIOR, unverified]
- The heavy-duty aftermarket (FleetPride, TruckPro, OEM parts networks such as PACCAR Parts, plus Rush Enterprises dealer network) gives same-day or next-day delivery for high-runner parts in most metros. The long tail (emissions components, ECMs, model-specific castings, glider/legacy parts) drives the delays. COVID-era shortages (2021-2023) of chips, ECMs and aftertreatment parts left trucks parked for weeks.
- Reasoning on C5: for Class 8, most delay-causing parts are *electronic or emissions-certified* parts: ECMs, NOx sensors, DPFs. These cannot just be fabricated on demand by an "AI parts factory", because they are certified emissions parts, contain semiconductors and run OEM-calibrated firmware. Additive manufacturing helps with brackets, trim and some legacy mechanical parts. Daimler Truck has 3D-printed some spare parts since about 2017-2019 [PRIOR, unverified]. It does not help with the binding constraint. **Contradicts strong C5.**

### 10. Electrification shifts the knowledge gap rather than closing it [PRIOR, unverified]
- NACFE Run on Less Electric (2021) / Electric DEPOT (2023) reports: BEV trucks have fewer wear items but bring new, high-voltage, software-heavy failure modes. Dealer and tech readiness and charging-equipment downtime (a separate repair chain) were major issues. NACFE's homepage (verified) mentions Run on Less Electric DEPOT 2023 but gives no figures.
- Reasoning: each technology transition *creates* a new knowledge gap faster than training closes it. That is a structural argument that the knowledge gap keeps regenerating, and an opening for AI-assisted diagnosis. It contradicts "essentially gone" (C3) and supports the idea that it is a moving target.

### 11. Commercial-vehicle repair is a two-sided coordination market (Decisiv, Fleetio, TMC VMRS) [VERIFIED partial]
- Decisiv serves fleets, OEMs, service providers and component suppliers as distinct markets (verified, about page). Its SRM Alliance and the KEA Advisors acquisition signal consolidation of the workflow layer.
- [PRIOR, unverified] ATA TMC's VMRS (Vehicle Maintenance Reporting Standards) coding has existed since the 1970s as the industry's standard repair-coding taxonomy. The industry has had a data standard for decades. The lack of *real-time* interoperability between fleet systems, shop systems (TMT, Karmak, Procede) and OEM systems is the friction.

### 12. FTC on independent capability and safety arguments [VERIFIED]
- FTC 2021, quoting Vermont Sen. Pearson: safety arguments are "largely bogus" given cars are repaired by independents daily. FTC: "With appropriate parts, repair information, and training, consumers and independent repair shops would similarly be capable of safely" repairing.
- Reasoning: this supports C2 as a *policy* matter: restrictions are mainly commercial, not safety-driven. In heavy trucks, however, the remaining restrictions are mostly warranty and emissions-tamper rules rather than information lock-out.

## Stance summary
- **C1 (universal chain):** Mixed. In trucking the chain largely holds, but the real chain has extra stages the rubric misses: **triage/queueing for a bay**, **authorization/approval** (fleet PO, warranty claim, OEM goodwill), and **towing/logistics**. Several stages also run in parallel. Remote diagnostics notices and pre-diagnoses before the truck arrives, so parts are ordered before teardown.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly):** Mixed. Formal information access was opened by the 2015 CV MOU [unverified] and the FTC sees little justification for restrictions. But the binding inefficiencies in trucking are coordination latency (Decisiv: about 33% downtime cut from workflow alone), technician scarcity (52% gap), parts availability for certified electronic/emissions parts, and emissions derates. Knowledge does matter in the form of fragmented, expensive OEM diagnostic tools and the shop-fleet asymmetry (over-repair).
- **C3 (AI erases gap):** Contradicts the strong form. Telematics plus OEM remote diagnostics already automated much of noticing and pre-diagnosis before LLMs, yet dwell persists. New powertrains (BEV, 2027 NOx rules) regenerate the gap. AI helps (Fleetio "AI Service Advisor" claims time saved) but the evidence is anecdotal.
- **C4 (10x cost drop in four steps):** Mixed. A large share of *time* is in waiting (queue, approvals, parts), which is consistent with focusing on the middle steps. But R&M is only about 9% of cost per mile, and the hands-on labour step is constrained by tech supply, which C4 excludes. The value is in downtime days, so the claim should be reframed as "cut days out of service", not "cut repair cost".
- **C5 (AI parts factory):** Contradicts. The delay-driving parts are certified emissions or electronic components with firmware. Additive manufacturing covers a niche of legacy mechanical parts.
- **C6 (inspection essential and must evolve):** Supports, partially. Emissions certification and anti-tamper rules (EPA derate guidance 2025 [unverified]) and FMCSA annual inspection requirements shape repair. The regulator is already moving to reduce repair-induced downtime.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient):** Mixed, leaning supports. Concrete structural inefficiencies exist: warranty steering to dealers, derate design, per-OEM tool silos, and fax/phone-style approval loops that Decisiv monetises. But a large incumbent coordination layer (Decisiv on about 60% of commercial vehicles, by its own claim) already exists. That suggests incremental, not ground-up, rebuild dynamics.

## Where the money and time go
Time: days out of service, which are dominated by queue/triage, approvals and parts waits rather than wrench time. Decisiv cuts 1-3+ days per event via coordination alone. Money: lost revenue of about $1,500 per truck-day (Decisiv's figure) dwarfs most repair invoices. R&M is about 9% of cost per mile [unverified].

## Real bottleneck
(a) Technician labour (52% diesel supply gap). (b) Coordination/authorization latency across fleet, dealer, OEM and warranty. (c) Availability of certified electronic/emissions parts. Knowledge per se is secondary.

## Company-shape implications
- An AI service writer / triage layer that turns a telematics fault into a confirmed diagnosis, parts list, pre-ordered parts and pre-booked bay *before* the truck arrives. Target: days out of service.
- A tech-productivity copilot across OEM diagnostic tools for independents and in-house fleet shops (multiplies scarce techs).
- Mobile/roadside repair dispatch for emissions faults, timed to EPA derate changes.
- Avoid: generic "AI parts factory" for Class 8. Possible niche: legacy mechanical parts for older or vocational fleets.
- Competition and incumbents: Decisiv, Fleetio, Samsara/Motive/Geotab, OEM uptime centres (Daimler, PACCAR, Volvo), Penske/Ryder outsourced maintenance.
