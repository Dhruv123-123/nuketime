## Findings

### 1. Size: reman is about 2% of manufacturing, and that share has been flat for a decade
- UNEP International Resource Panel, *Re-defining Value: The Manufacturing Revolution* (2018): remanufacturing is "only about 2% of production in the United States and 1.9% in Europe". Value-retention processes (reman, refurbishment, repair, reuse) could cut GHG emissions by 79-99% in some sectors. https://www.resourcepanel.org/reports/re-defining-value-manufacturing-revolution (fetched; hard data, study)
- Wikipedia's Remanufacturing article cites RIT Golisano Institute (2020) for the same 2% US / 1.9% EU figures and says US reman grew 15% between 2009 and 2011. It also cites a UK potential of up to GBP 5.6bn (Carbon Trust, 2014). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remanufacturing (fetched; secondary)

### 2. USITC 2012, Pub 4356, *Remanufactured Goods: An Overview of the U.S. and Global Industries, Markets, and Trade* [RECALLED; usitc.gov returned 403]
- US remanufacturing production grew about 15% from 2009 to 2011, to at least **$43.0bn**, supporting about **180,000 full-time US jobs**.
- US reman exports were about $11.7bn and imports about $10.3bn (2011).
- Largest sectors: aerospace (~$13bn), heavy-duty and off-road equipment (HDOR, ~$7.8bn), motor vehicle parts (~$6.2bn). After those came machinery, IT products, medical devices, retreaded tires and others.
- Reman intensity (reman sales as a share of total sales) was about 2% overall, but much higher in aerospace and HDOR.
- Barriers it identified: (a) core supply and collection; (b) customer perception (reman seen as "used"); (c) foreign trade rules that treat reman goods as used goods or waste (it named Brazil, China, India and others); (d) IP and OEM restrictions in some sectors; (e) uneven standards.
- Structure: in aerospace and HDOR, OEMs and their affiliates dominate reman. In motor vehicle parts, independents (MPA, Cardone and others) are much more common.
- Source URL: https://www.usitc.gov/publications/332/pub4356.pdf

### 3. Europe: ERN 2015 market study [RECALLED; remanufacturing.eu returned 425]
- The European Remanufacturing Network (Horizon 2020) put EU reman turnover at about **EUR 30bn**, with about **190,000 employees** and about **7,000+ firms**.
- Aerospace was the largest sector (~EUR 12bn), then automotive (~EUR 7bn), then HDOR (~EUR 4bn).
- Reman intensity was about 1.9% of manufacturing. The study modelled potential growth to roughly EUR 70-100bn by 2030 if barriers were removed.
- https://www.remanufacturing.eu/assets/pdfs/remanufacturing-market-study.pdf

### 4. Automotive core economy: Motorcar Parts of America FY2026 10-K (fetched; hard data)
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/918251/000114036126024463/ef20070436_10k.htm
- **Revenue and margin.** Net sales were $789.8m, up 4.3% from $757.4m. Gross profit was $159.9m, a **20.2% gross margin** (20.3% the year before). That is a thin, distribution-like margin for a manufacturer.
- **Customer concentration.** The three largest customers took 85% of net sales (86% in FY25, 83% in FY24). The largest single customer took 42% (FY25 39%, FY24 35%). The filing names AutoZone, O'Reilly, Advance and Genuine Parts (NAPA) as major chain customers, with about 25,000 outlets in aggregate.
- **Product range.** About 44,000 SKUs: alternators and starters, brake calipers, boosters, master cylinders, hub assemblies, heavy-duty products and test equipment.
- **Workforce.** About 5,600 employees, of whom about 4,900 are in Mexico (about 4,700 unionised), 300 in the US, 200 in Canada and 200 in Malaysia and China. The labour-intensive steps (teardown, cleaning, test) sit offshore.
- **The core is the raw material.** The filing says "The Used Cores that we acquire and are returned to us from our customers are a necessary raw material for remanufacturing." It also says "Remanufacturing consumes, on average, more than one Used Core for each remanufactured unit", because not all cores are reusable.
- **Core sourcing.** "The majority of our Used Cores are obtained from customers through the core exchange programs." Core brokers are "a critical source for meeting our raw material demands."
- **Balance-sheet weight of cores.** Remanufactured cores held at customers' locations, booked as long-term contract assets, were **$297.6m** (FY25 $301.4m). That is about 38% of a year's sales, parked on retailers' shelves. Total inventory was $380.6m.
- **Paying for shelf space.** Core premiums paid to customers totalled $39.4m and are amortised over 6-8 years ($10.8m in FY26). Customers press for "significant marketing allowances and more favorable delivery and payment terms."
- **Core liabilities.** Accrued core payments were $14.5m and the core bank liability $10.1m.
- **Valuation risk.** Cores on customer shelves were written down $3.6m (FY26), $2.8m (FY25) and $5.4m (FY24), plus a further $27.5m reduction tied to a customer DC inventory realignment.
- **Risks named.** EV transition ("may also reduce demand for our products for combustion engine vehicles"), tariffs, disruption of Used Core supply, and growing Asian competition.
- **Reading.** Automotive reman is a working-capital and logistics business: core float, retailer channel power, and cheap skilled labour. It is not a knowledge business. The knowledge (how to rebuild an alternator) is commoditised. The money is in reverse logistics and channel terms.

### 5. Caterpillar Reman (fetched 10-K plus [RECALLED] details)
- FY2025 10-K: Power & Energy includes "the remanufacturing of Caterpillar engines and components, remanufacturing services for other companies." The Properties section names reman sites including Franklin, Indiana and Corinth, Mississippi. Machines go through 150 dealers (41 US, 109 outside the US) serving 190 countries. Total 2025 sales and revenues were $67.6bn. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/18230/000001823026000008/cat-20251231.htm (fetched)
- 10-K acquisition history (via Wikipedia, fetched): Wealdstone Engineering (UK, 2004), Williams Technologies (SC, 2004, transmissions), Eurenov (France, 2007), Gremada (ND, 2008), and the Nuevo Laredo, Mexico reman centre (since 1988). Cat bought independent remanufacturers to add capability and third-party work. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar_Inc.
- [RECALLED] Cat Reman began in 1973. The model is an exchange: the customer buys a reman part at a fraction of new (often cited as 40-60% of new price) and pays a core deposit, refunded on the core's return according to published acceptance criteria. Cat cites take-back of well over 100m lb of end-of-life material a year. Its "salvage" engineering (thermal spray, laser cladding, machining specs) is proprietary, and it designs new parts for reman ("design for remanufacture").
- [RECALLED] Strategic weight: Cat targeted services revenue of $28bn by 2026 (from about $18bn in 2021; about $24bn in 2024). Reman is a lever that lets dealers offer a lower-cost repair option without losing the customer to will-fit or independent rebuilders. Reman protects the OEM's aftermarket rather than threatening it.

### 6. Cummins FY2025 10-K (fetched)
- The Engine segment sells "New and remanufactured parts and engines, which are sold and serviced primarily through our extensive distribution network." Reman revenue is not broken out. It is folded into OEM aftermarket parts and channelled through Cummins' own distribution. https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26172/000002617226000009/cmi-20251231.htm

### 7. Rematec / industry direction (fetched, headlines only)
- Rematec calls itself "the world's leading platform for automotive remanufacturing" (Amsterdam and Asia editions). Its 2026 headlines are on battery remanufacturing ("JustHyb: battery remanufacturing seen from the inside", "Battery remanufacturing, the current scenario"), European battery supply-chain readiness, Chinese reman parts in the aftermarket, and the new EU End-of-Life Vehicles Regulation (EU) 2026/1738, which affects core supply. https://www.rematec.com/ and https://www.rematec.com/news/
- Interpretation: the automotive reman centre of gravity is moving from ICE rotating electrics toward EV batteries, power electronics and e-motors. China is rising as both a reman producer and a market.

### 8. MEMA Council of Remanufacturing Excellence (CoRE; formerly MERA) (fetched)
- MERA was folded into MEMA as CoRE. It runs the "Manufactured Again" quality certification and a "ReThink Tank" focused on "increasing demand for remanufactured products". Demand and perception, not supply-side knowledge, is the stated problem. https://www.mema.org/networking-groups/council-remanufacturing-excellence

### 9. Academic: closed-loop supply chains (Guide and Van Wassenhove) [RECALLED]
- Guide (2000, *J. Operations Management*), "Production planning and control for remanufacturing", and Guide and Van Wassenhove (2009, *Operations Research*), "The evolution of closed-loop supply chain research", are the standard references.
- Their core finding: the defining difficulty of reman is **uncertainty in the timing, quantity and quality of returned cores**, plus disassembly and yield variability. Profitability hinges on core acquisition management (grading, pricing returns by quality), not on technical know-how.
- Wikipedia (fetched) lists the same open problems: uncertain supply of used products, disassembly sequencing, and parts commonality across generations.

### 10. Lund / Boston University historical survey [RECALLED]
- Robert Lund's 1980s-90s surveys estimated about 73,000 US reman firms with about $53bn in sales. Reman products are often claimed to retain about 85% of the energy and material embodied in the original. Reman is an old, mature, fragmented industry.

### 11. LKQ (fetched)
- LKQ (about 44,000 staff, 1,400+ locations, about 270 acquisitions) lists "remanufactured engines and transmissions" among its parts lines. Reman in the auto aftermarket is consolidating into large distributors via M&A, and scale in logistics is the moat. https://www.lkqcorp.com/

## Synthesis: what makes reman work
1. **Core access and reverse logistics.** Deposits, core banks, broker networks and grading are the scarce input. MPA carries about $300m of cores on customer shelves. Cat's exchange model ties the core return to dealer service.
2. **Design and engineering data** (in HDOR and aerospace). The OEM knows tolerances, salvage specs and part revisions, and can design for reman. That is why OEMs dominate those sectors and independents dominate commoditised auto rotating electrics. This is a real knowledge asymmetry, but it is bundled with core control, warranty and certification, so knowledge alone would not unlock it.
3. **Channel and warranty trust.** Reman sells at a discount (often cited as 40-60% of new) only if it carries a new-equivalent warranty and a trusted brand. Retailers extract premiums and allowances, which squeezes margins to about 20% gross for independents.
4. **Labour-cost arbitrage** (Mexico, Malaysia) for teardown, cleaning and test.
5. **Regulatory treatment.** Trade rules that classify reman as used goods or waste, and ELV rules, can make or break core flows.

## Against or for the hypotheses
- **C1:** Reman shows the chain is not universal. In reman and exchange models, "diagnose → source part → repair" collapses into "swap a known-good exchange unit, then fix the core offline at a factory". Diagnosis moves to the factory, after the fact. The steps get reordered and batched.
- **C2:** Mixed. In HDOR and aerospace, OEM engineering data and salvage specs matter, and OEMs capture reman. In automotive, the knowledge is commoditised, yet margins are still thin and the binding constraints are cores, channel power and working capital, not knowledge.
- **C3:** Contradicted for this domain. AI does not create cores, deposits, warranty backing, trade permissions or retailer shelf space. The 2% share has not moved in over a decade despite much better information technology.
- **C4:** Mostly contradicted. The cost of reman is in core logistics, inventory float (MPA's cores equal about 38% of annual sales), labour and channel allowances. Diagnosis and "understanding" are a small slice. Sourcing and delivery could improve via better core grading and forecasting, but an order-of-magnitude drop is not supported.
- **C5:** Contradicted or partial. Reman exists because restoring an existing high-value core (castings, blocks) is cheaper than making new. Salvage processes (laser cladding, thermal spray, additive repair) do extend this, but qualification and certification gate it, especially in aerospace.
- **C6:** Supports. Reman depends on standards and certification: MEMA's "Manufactured Again" label, Cat's core acceptance criteria, aerospace PMA/FAA. Inspection, test and grading at core intake is a central cost and quality step.
- **C7:** Mixed. Reman is a counterexample of an industry deliberately built for efficiency (exchange units, minimal downtime). But it is also designed by OEMs to protect aftermarket capture. Its stuck 2% share suggests structural barriers (perception, trade rules, core flows), not mere lack of smarts.
