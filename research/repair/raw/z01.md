## Catalogue of software-based repair-control mechanisms

| Mechanism | Example (industry) | What it does | Source status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parts pairing / serialisation | Apple iPhone (screens, batteries, cameras, Face ID / Touch ID) | Part serial numbers are bound to the logic board. An unpaired part either loses features (for example TrueTone) or triggers warnings, unless it is paired through Apple's System Configuration tool | [V] FTC 2021, quoting iFixit |
| VIN burning | GM and several European luxury carmakers (auto) | A control module is locked to a single VIN, so salvaged or remanufactured ECUs will not work | [V] FTC 2021, citing LKQ |
| Encrypted / locked ECU software and reprogramming | Deere (agriculture) | Full diagnostics, calibration and pairing are possible only with dealer-only Service ADVISOR. The owner tool, Customer Service ADVISOR, is restricted | [V] FTC v. Deere press release, 2025 |
| Secure gateway modules | FCA / Stellantis since about 2018 (auto) | Write access through the OBD port needs an authenticated tool and technician (AutoAuth). AutoAuth costs from about $5 to $17.50 a month | [V] autoauth.com. The FCA link is [M] |
| Telematics data control | Carmakers vs. Massachusetts 2020 Question 1 | The ballot measure passed with 74.97% and required open telematics access. Carmakers sued, NHTSA said in 2023 that the law is preempted, and Subaru and Kia disabled telematics in MA | Vote share [V] Wikipedia. The rest is [M], partly [V] via the Wikipedia R2R article |
| Firmware updates gated on service contracts | IBM and other server makers (IT) | Firmware updates are withheld unless the owner buys a maintenance contract, so used servers can't be resold. Vendor-key-signed firmware | [V] FTC 2021 |
| Cartridge chip authentication ("Dynamic Security") | HP printers | Firmware blocks cartridges that lack an HP chip. Settlements: US $1.5M, CA C$700k, EU $1.35M | [V] Wikipedia HP Inc. |
| Time-bomb / geo-fenced locks | Newag Impuls trains (rail, Poland) | Code bricks a train if it spends 10 days or more at one of 7 GPS-defined competitor workshops, or if a component is swapped (checked by serial number). One train was set to fail after 1M km. Of 29 trains analysed, only 5 had no surprises | [V] badcyber.com, Wikipedia |
| Licensed diagnostic / error-code access | Taylor C602 ice-cream machines (McDonald's) | Error codes are opaque and need technician access. Kytch's decoder got about 500 units installed before McDonald's told franchisees to remove them in Nov 2020. About 13,000 of 40,000 stores use the C602. Repairs are about a quarter of Taylor's sales. A DMCA §1201 exemption followed in Oct 2024 | [V] Wikipedia Kytch |
| Service keys / password-locked service menus | Medical imaging and ventilators | Independent service is $150-250/hr vs. OEM $500-600/hr with a 4-hour minimum | [V] FTC 2021, citing IAMERS |
| Remote disable | Deere dealers remotely locked equipment looted from Ukraine in 2022 | Shows the OEM keeps a kill switch after sale | [V] Wikipedia John Deere |
| DMCA §1201 as legal backstop | All of the above (US) | Circumventing a TPM is illegal unless exempted. Repair exemptions were granted in 2015, 2018, 2021 and 2024 for vehicles, ag, medical, devices and food equipment. The exemptions don't cover selling or sharing tools | [V] Wikipedia R2R |
| Sole-source proprietary parts (non-software analog) | TransDigm (aerospace) | DoD found a 9,400% markup on a metal pin, and TransDigm refunded $16M (2019). ## Findings (detailed)

1. **[V] FTC "Nixing the Fix" (May 2021), the regulator's overall verdict.** Quote: "there is scant evidence to support manufacturers' justifications for repair restrictions". The report also says repair restrictions "steered consumers into manufacturers' repair networks or to replace products before the end of their useful lives". On price: "none of the comments or empirical research submitted ... rebuts the right to repair advocates' argument that repair restrictions increase the price consumers pay for repairs." The report also notes that "the car manufacturing industry has taken important steps", meaning the 2014 MOU, while other industries had not. Commissioners Wilson and Phillips noted that IP rights may give legitimate justification. https://www.ftc.gov/reports/nixing-fix-ftc-report-congress-repair-restrictions (PDF extracted locally)
2. **[V] FTC 2021 on software locks.** The report lists software locks, DRM and TPMs, and firmware updates that "disable a computerized device repaired outside of the manufacturer's authorized service networks". Examples it names:
   - Apple synchronises parts to the logic board.
   - LKQ describes VIN burning by GM and European luxury brands. The FTC notes VIN burning may have anti-theft benefits, but "any such benefits could likely be achieved without imposing a substantial burden on independent repair."
   - IBM withholds firmware without a service contract.
   - LKQ: "vehicle manufacturers embed software restricting the reuse, repair and remanufacturing of an electronic control module ... This limits repair options to new OEM replacement parts only."
3. **[V] Price differential in medical equipment (FTC citing IAMERS).** Independent imaging service costs $150-250/hr vs. OEM $500-600/hr with a 4-hour minimum, roughly 2.5-4x. The report also cites the FDA (2018): "the objective evidence indicates that many OEMs and third party entities provide high quality, safe, and effective servicing of medical devices." This undercuts the claim that the restrictions are about safety.
4. **[V] Household-level economic effect (PIRG, cited by FTC).** Repair could cut household spending on electronics and appliances by 22%, about $330 per family per year. This is advocacy data, not a peer-reviewed estimate.
5. **[V] Counter-view in the FTC record (AHAM).** Of 20,000 appliance servicers, over 87% are "authorized local independent" providers. On that basis AHAM says the barriers are "imaginary" for shops willing to certify. In other words, the OEMs' own model is to license the knowledge through authorisation, not to hoard it outright.
6. **[V] FTC v. Deere (Jan 15, 2025).**
   - Service ADVISOR, the only fully functional repair tool, is dealer-only. Customer Service ADVISOR "cannot perform all repairs".
   - Unlike auto and trucking, Deere does not give generic tool makers the information needed to build a full tool.
   - Deere holds a 100% share of repairs that need the full tool.
   - Dealers almost always use Deere parts.
   - The harms named are higher repair costs and delays to planting, spraying and harvest.
   - The vote was 3-2.
   https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/01/ftc-states-sue-deere-company-protect-farmers-unfair-corporate-tactics-high-repair-costs
7. **[V, but unverified secondary] Deere resolutions in 2026, per Wikipedia (fetched 2026-10-20).**
   - Class action, April 2026: a $99M settlement fund covering 26-53% of overcharge damages, plus 10 years of repair digital tools.
   - FTC case, July 2026: Deere must provide fault-code diagnosis, manuals and "previously software-locked features such as pairing new components" on fair and reasonable terms, with 10 years of oversight.
   - The Wikipedia page itself contains a date discrepancy, so check these against primary dockets.
   - Earlier context: the Jan 2023 AFBF MOU was unenforceable, and Judge Johnston denied dismissal in Nov 2023. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deere
8. **[V] Newag trains (Poland, Dec 2023).** This is the starkest case of deliberate software sabotage of third-party repair. The trigger conditions were geo-fences around competitor workshops, a 10-day dwell, serial-number checks on swapped components and a mileage cap. 24 of 29 trains analysed had undocumented behaviour. Newag called it a "safety system", but its 20,000-page manual did not mention it. The trains were stuck at SPS's workshop until hackers bypassed the locks. https://badcyber.com/dieselgate-but-for-trains-some-heavyweight-hardware-hacking/
9. **[V] Taylor / Kytch (food equipment).** About 13,000 McDonald's stores use the C602. A 2000 internal survey found about a quarter of restaurants had non-functional machines. Repairs are about a quarter of Taylor's sales, a direct revenue motive for keeping diagnostics opaque. The FTC opened a probe in July 2021, and a DMCA exemption came in Oct 2024. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kytch
10. **[V] HP Dynamic Security.** Firmware has blocked non-HP-chip cartridges since 2016. Settlements totalled about $3.5M across US, CA and EU, which is small next to ink margins. The lock persisted as of 2023. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HP_Inc.
11. **[V] Legislative pushback.**
    - Oregon SB 1596 (2024) is the first US ban on parts pairing. It applies to devices made after Jan 1, 2025.
    - The EU Right to Repair Directive 2024/1799 bans "hardware or software techniques" that impede repair unless objectively justified, with transposition by July 31, 2026.
    - Colorado HB24-1121 covers digital electronics.
    - Massachusetts 2020 Q1 passed with 74.97%.
    - Terrance Group: about 59% of independent repairers still struggle to get diagnostic tools and parts.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_repair
12. **[V] TransDigm, the non-software analog in aerospace.** Sole-source proprietary parts carry extreme markups: a 9,400% markup on a pin, a $16M refund, and $20.8M excess profit on 105 parts per the 2021 DoD IG report. The company earns an operating margin of about 47% on $8.83B revenue. In aviation, the control point is design authority, certification and data rights, not mainly crypto locks. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransDigm_Group
13. **[V] Secure-gateway monetisation in autos.** AutoAuth charges from $5/month (Standard) to $17.50/month (Plus) per shop. Because the fee is low, the gateway lock adds friction rather than a large cost. https://www.autoauth.com/

### Leads from background knowledge [M] (not re-verified this session)
- **Apple.** The Self Service Repair launch (2022) required a serial-number-tied "System Configuration" step. iOS 18 (2024) allowed used genuine parts and relaxed some pairing. Apple initially lobbied against Oregon's pairing ban.
- **Tesla.** Charges subscription fees for Toolbox diagnostic software, reportedly several hundred to about $3k per year. Its structural battery pack / Cybertruck parts are serialized.
- **Medical (PIRG 2020 hospital survey).** Roughly half of biomeds reported being denied service keys or software.
- **Aviation engines.** Engine OEMs (CFM, Pratt, GE, RR) bundle "power-by-the-hour" service. OEM share of the engine MRO market is rising, and airlines (IATA) have complained about restricted licensing of repair data and DER parts. IATA has estimated the aftermarket concentration costs airlines billions per year (exact figure not verified).
- **Wind turbines.** Siemens Gamesa / Vestas SCADA and controller lock-in has been raised by independent service providers (ISPs) in the EU. Battery-pack serial locks are found on e-bikes (Bosch, Shimano), as reported by iFixit and others.
- **Auto MOU (2014).** Created the national J2534 / OEM service-info subscription model. That model shows that knowledge access was largely solved by regulation, yet independent shops still pay per-OEM subscription fees, and parts pairing has moved the lock from information to authorisation.

## Reasoning for the claims

- **C1 (universal chain): silent / mixed.** Software locks insert an extra step, "authorise / pair / calibrate", between repair and return to service. That step is a control gate, not a natural repair stage, so the chain is incomplete without it.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly): mixed-to-supports, with an important correction.** In the locked cases (Deere, Newag, Apple, VIN burning, IBM firmware), the binding constraint is not knowledge but **authorisation**. Independent shops often know exactly what is wrong and how to fix it. The Newag hackers and Kytch decoded everything, yet the part still won't run without the OEM's cryptographic blessing or legal permission. The barrier is technical (crypto) plus legal (DMCA §1201, licences, warranty). Calling it a "knowledge monopoly" understates the role of the lock and the law.
- **C3 (AI closes the gap): contradicts in this domain.** AI can explain fault codes or decode a protocol, as Kytch did without AI. But AI cannot sign firmware with an OEM private key, and circumventing a lock stays illegal under §1201 unless exempted. Encrypted ECUs, signed firmware and serial pairing are designed to resist exactly the "just know more" attack.
- **C4 (10x cost drop in diagnose / understand / source / deliver): contradicts as stated.** The evidence of 2.5-4x price gaps (medical), 9,400% part markups and the Deere overcharges shows the excess sits in **monopoly rents on authorisation and parts**. Removing those needs legal or market change, not cheaper cognition. A 10x drop is plausible only where locks are removed by law.
- **C5 (AI parts factory): contradicts for locked parts.** A perfectly fabricated replacement ECU, screen or cartridge is rejected if it lacks a paired serial or chip (HP, Apple, VIN burning, Newag serial checks). Fabrication is not the bottleneck. Authentication and certification are, and in aviation design approval (PMA/DER) is.
- **C6 (inspection matters and must evolve): supports.** OEMs frame the locks as safety and cybersecurity measures (Newag's "safety system", NHTSA's 2023 telematics hacking concern, AHAM certification). Any rebuild must replace the OEM's authorisation role with a credible neutral attestation and inspection layer. The FDA's 2018 finding that third-party servicing is safe implies such a layer is feasible.
- **C7 (structurally built inefficient): supports, reframed.** Locks make the system profitably inefficient by design for the OEM (Taylor's repairs are about 25% of sales, TransDigm runs a 47% margin). It is not "not built for smart". It is built smart for rent extraction. The FTC found scant evidence for the justifications. On the other side, cars (MOU) and appliances (AHAM's authorised network) show partial opening without a full rebuild.

## Where the money and time go
In this domain, cost piles up at **authorisation and parts** (pairing, calibration, dealer-only tools, sole-source parts at monopoly prices) and in **downtime while waiting for the authorised technician**. Examples are the Deere harvest delays, the 4-hour OEM minimums in medical, Newag trains stuck in a competitor's depot and broken McDonald's machines. Diagnosis knowledge itself is rarely the dominant cost once a skilled independent can get in.

## Real bottleneck
Cryptographic and legal authorisation: who holds the signing keys, the pairing tool, the type certificate and data rights, and whether DMCA §1201 / contracts let anyone else use them. Knowledge and fabrication are secondary.

## Company shapes suggested
- Neutral, OEM-agnostic authorisation and attestation infrastructure. Examples: a "secure gateway / pairing" clearinghouse like AutoAuth, generalised to ag, medical, rail, energy and robots, which uses the new legal mandates (FTC–Deere settlement terms, Oregon, the EU directive) to give independents logged, auditable access.
- Compliance / repairability tooling for OEMs facing the EU 2024/1799 and Oregon bans. These OEMs need ways to keep anti-theft and cyber goals without pairing locks.
- Independent-repair diagnostic platforms for verticals that have just been opened (Deere tools after the settlement, food equipment after the 2024 exemption, medical).
- Firmware / protocol audit services, of the Dragon Sector / Newag type, for fleet buyers such as rail operators, hospitals and utilities. These services check procured equipment for hidden repair locks, and procurement contracts can require it.
- Avoid "AI knowledge" plays that ignore the lock layer, and "AI parts factory" plays for parts that carry serial authentication or need certification.
