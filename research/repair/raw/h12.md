## Findings

### 1. LLM gives physicians no diagnostic uplift, although the LLM alone scores higher [VERIFIED, study]
Goh et al., JAMA Network Open 2024, RCT. 50 physicians (26 attendings, 24 residents), 244 cases.
- Physicians + GPT-4: median 76% diagnostic reasoning score
- Physicians, conventional resources only: 74% (difference 2 pp, 95% CI -4 to 8, P=.60)
- GPT-4 alone: 92% (+16 pp vs conventional, P=.03; secondary, exploratory)
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2825395
Implication: AI "having the knowledge" does not mean the knowledge reaches the decision-maker. The bottleneck shifts to the human-AI workflow, trust and integration. This cuts against C3 ("knowledge gap gone") as an operational claim, though it supports the idea that raw diagnostic knowledge is now cheap.

### 2. Vendor-supplied diagnostic AI failed external validation (Epic Sepsis Model) [VERIFIED, study]
Wong et al., JAMA Internal Medicine 2021. 38,455 hospitalisations at Michigan Medicine.
- AUC 0.63, against the 0.76-0.83 Epic reported internally
- Sensitivity 33% at the alert threshold, PPV 12%
- Missed 1,709 of 2,552 sepsis patients (67%)
- Alerts fired on 18% of all hospitalisations; 8 patients evaluated per true case
Source: https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2781307
Implication: a model trained on one population degrades on another. Repair analog: a diagnostic model trained on one fleet, OEM or operating environment will drift elsewhere. Independent validation and inspection (C6) becomes more important, not less.

### 3. Task-shifted clinicians differ on average, but variation within each profession is larger than the gap between them [VERIFIED abstract, study]
Chan & Chen, "The Productivity of Professions: Evidence from the Emergency Department", NBER WP 30608 (2022). Quasi-random patient assignment in VHA emergency departments.
- On average, NPs use more resources and have higher 30-day preventable hospitalisations than physicians.
- The gap varies with case complexity and severity.
- Within-profession variation exceeds the average between-profession difference: the NP outperforms the physician in 38% of random pairs.
- [MEMORY] The full paper reports about 7% higher costs and about 11% longer length of stay for NPs, with the gap concentrated in complex cases, and argues that experience and training matter.
Source: https://www.nber.org/papers/w30608
Implication: moving diagnosis to less-trained workers works for routine cases and costs more on complex ones. Repair analog: an independent technician with AI may handle the long tail of routine faults, while complex or novel faults keep the expert premium. This contradicts a uniform order-of-magnitude cost drop (C4).

### 4. Licensing incumbents actively block scope expansion [VERIFIED, practitioner/lobby view]
AMA "scope creep" campaign. The AMA claims "hundreds of victories". In 2023 alone it lists all 11 scope bills defeated in Mississippi, 4 in Indiana (including pharmacists diagnosing illness, HB 1182), 2 in Georgia (nurse anesthetists), and South Dakota SB 175 (PA full practice authority). Its arguments are patient safety, physician-led teams, "scope expansion does not equal access", and truth in advertising.
Source: https://www.ama-assn.org/practice-management/scope-practice/ama-successfully-fights-scope-practice-expansions-threaten
Implication: this supports C2 (knowledge and credential monopoly) and C7 (structural resistance). But the moat is legal licensure and liability, not knowledge itself. Repair analog: OEM dealer networks, FAA/EASA certificated repair stations, and the Right to Repair fights.

### 5. NP supply is growing fast regardless [VERIFIED, data]
BLS: NPs held about 336,300 jobs in 2025, projected to grow 41% to about 474,100 by 2035 (+137,800), one of the fastest-growing occupations.
Source: https://www.bls.gov/ooh/healthcare/nurse-anesthetists-nurse-midwives-and-nurse-practitioners.htm
[MEMORY] AANP: 27 states plus DC grant full practice authority; the rest are "reduced" or "restricted" (requiring collaborative agreements or supervision). Definitions verified at https://www.aanp.org/advocacy/state/state-practice-environment
Implication: decentralisation does happen, slowly, over decades, state by state, through legislation, not through technology. NP full practice authority took about 30-40 years to reach half the states.

### 6. An AI diagnosis company collapsed after overclaiming [VERIFIED, news/history]
Babylon Health went public via SPAC in October 2021 at a $4.2B valuation. In 2018 it claimed its AI diagnosed "as well as a doctor"; a Lancet commentary found "no convincing evidence". UK regulators acknowledged concerns, though the chatbot was a class 1 device needing no formal approval. The US arm filed for bankruptcy in August 2023 (Chapter 7), and the UK arm went into administration in September 2023, its assets sold for £500,000.
Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon_Health (secondary; links to Lancet and BBC)
Implication: "AI replaces the diagnostician" was the failed thesis. The economics of care delivery (Babylon's capitated NHS GP at Hand and US value-based contracts) killed it, not the AI.

### 7. Telehealth spike and retrenchment; Teladoc value destruction [VERIFIED partly, news/investor]
- Teladoc bought Livongo for $13.9B (October 2020). By 2024 it had revenue of about $2.57B and net income of about -$1B, after multibillion goodwill impairments in 2022. [MEMORY: total 2022 impairments about $13.4B.] Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teladoc_Health
- [MEMORY, data] FAIR Health tracker: telehealth peaked at about 13% of commercial medical claim lines in April 2020 and settled at about 4-5% through 2023-2026, dominated by mental health. Source: https://www.fairhealth.org/fh-trackers/telehealth (page verified to exist; figures not readable in fetch)
Implication: remote diagnosis stuck where no physical exam is needed (behavioural health). For everything needing hands, eyes or instruments on the object, remote diagnosis became triage, not resolution. Repair analog: remote diagnosis is valuable for software and sensor-rich assets. Where a physical teardown or inspection is needed, it does not remove the truck roll.

### 8. Remote diagnosis depends on temporary regulatory waivers [MEMORY, regulator]
- Medicare telehealth flexibilities (home as originating site, no rural restriction) have run on short-term extensions since the end of the PHE. They briefly lapsed on 1 October 2025 during the federal government shutdown, causing providers to cancel or hold claims, and were restored by the November 2025 continuing resolution.
- DEA Ryan Haight Act in-person exam requirement for controlled-substance prescribing: the flexibilities have been repeatedly extended (through end of 2025, then 2026), and no permanent special-registration rule has been finalised.
- HHS OCR HIPAA enforcement discretion for non-compliant video platforms ended 11 May 2023.
Implication: rules about who may diagnose, and whether they must be physically present, are the binding constraint, not technology. Repair analog: FAA Part 43/145 sign-off, nuclear (NRC) and pressure-vessel codes, and automotive ADAS calibration rules.

### 9. Autonomous AI diagnosis exists and is approved, but adoption is low [MEMORY, regulator/data]
- IDx-DR (now LumineticsCore, Digital Diagnostics) received FDA De Novo authorisation in April 2018 as the first autonomous AI diagnosis with no physician read. CPT 92229 was created for it (verified on the vendor site: https://www.digitaldiagnostics.com/). The vendor carries its own malpractice liability.
- Wu et al., NEJM AI 2023, "Characterizing the clinical adoption of medical AI devices through US insurance claims": of the roughly 500+ FDA-cleared AI devices, only a handful had meaningful claims volume. FFR-CT (HeartFlow) led with about 67,000 claims; autonomous diabetic retinopathy AI was second, with roughly 15,000 claims over about 4 years. Source: https://ai.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/AIoa2300030 (403 on fetch)
Implication: even the cleanest case (FDA-approved, reimbursed, liability assumed by the vendor) diffuses slowly. Payment codes, workflow integration, camera hardware at the point of care and referral follow-through are the bottlenecks. In repair terms, "notice + diagnose" is solved and "source + repair + inspect" is where things stall: patients flagged positive still have to see an ophthalmologist, and follow-up rates are low.

### 10. Diagnostic error is large, so the problem is real [MEMORY, study]
Newman-Toker et al., BMJ Quality & Safety 2024 (online 2023): an estimated 795,000 Americans die or are permanently disabled each year from diagnostic error. The average error rate across 15 dangerous diseases is about 11%, ranging from 1.5% (heart attack) to 62% (spinal abscess). Stroke is the top cause.
Source: https://qualitysafety.bmj.com/content/33/2/109 (403 on fetch)
Implication: this supports the idea that diagnosis is a high-value bottleneck. Even with credentialed experts, error is substantial, so the "OEM expert" is not the gold standard the monopoly framing implies.

### 11. WHO task shifting: decentralisation works when protocols are standardised [MEMORY, regulator/history]
WHO, "Task Shifting: Global Recommendations and Guidelines" (2008, with PEPFAR/UNAIDS). It was a response to a shortage of about 4.3M health workers and the HIV treatment scale-up. HIV care was shifted to nurses and community health workers using standardised protocols, simplified regimens and supervision, and multiple RCTs (for example the STRETCH trial in South Africa, Lancet 2012) found non-inferior outcomes for nurse-initiated ART.
Implication: task shifting succeeds when the diagnostic decision tree is codified, the treatment is standardised, and there is supervision and referral for exceptions. Repair analog: AI that codifies OEM troubleshooting trees plus standardised parts lets lower-cost techs do most jobs, with an escalation path for the rest.

### 12. Cochrane review: nurse substitution for doctors in primary care [MEMORY, study]
Laurant et al., Cochrane 2018: nurse-led primary care probably gives similar or better health outcomes and higher patient satisfaction, with longer consultations and more tests. Cost savings are uncertain because the lower salary is offset by longer and more frequent visits.
Implication: cheaper labour does not automatically mean cheaper episodes. This contradicts the simple C4 arithmetic.

### 13. AI adoption is fastest where it does not move diagnostic authority (ambient scribes) [MEMORY, news/practitioner]
2024-2026 saw ambient AI scribes (Abridge, Microsoft DAX, Ambience and others) deployed across many large health systems within about two years, much faster than diagnostic AI. They reduce documentation time and do not change who signs the diagnosis or who bears liability.
Implication: AI diffuses fastest into the "understand/document" layer and slowest into the "decide/certify" layer. Repair analog: AI service manuals, work-order writing and parts lookup will diffuse fast. AI as the signatory of return to service will not.

### 14. Payment rules encode the hierarchy [MEMORY, regulator]
Medicare pays NPs and PAs 85% of the physician fee schedule unless they bill "incident-to" under a physician. Payment, not knowledge, shapes who performs diagnosis. Repair analog: warranty reimbursement only through authorised dealers, and insurer DRP networks.

### 15. AI was predicted to replace radiologists; demand rose instead [MEMORY, history/practitioner]
Geoffrey Hinton (2016) said "we should stop training radiologists now". By 2025, US radiology faced shortages and rising imaging volumes. Hundreds of FDA-cleared radiology AI tools (about 75% of all AI devices) act as assistive triage, and the radiologist still signs.
Implication: knowledge automation raised throughput and demand for sign-off rather than eliminating the expert (a Jevons-like effect).

## Synthesis: what limited diagnosis decentralisation in healthcare
1. **Liability and sign-off, not knowledge, is the moat.** Every case of successful decentralisation (NPs, task-shifted HIV care, autonomous DR AI) required a legal change in who may sign the diagnosis or treatment, and who is liable.
2. **Payment codes and reimbursement lock in the hierarchy** (85% NP rate, CPT codes for AI, waiver-dependent telehealth).
3. **Organised incumbent lobbying** (AMA scope creep) slows change state by state over decades.
4. **Physical presence remains needed for most diagnosis.** Telehealth settled at about 5% of claims and is concentrated where no hands-on exam is needed.
5. **Human-AI integration is the gap.** AI alone can beat clinicians on vignettes, yet clinicians with AI do not improve (Goh 2024). Real-world validation fails (Epic sepsis).
6. **Complex cases keep the expert premium**, while routine cases shift successfully (Chan & Chen; Cochrane; WHO).
7. **Downstream steps bottleneck after diagnosis.** Autonomous DR screening still has to route positives to specialists, and follow-through is the weak link. Cheap diagnosis moves the queue to treatment capacity.

## Mapping to the founder's claims
- C1 (universal chain): mixed. Medicine has an analogous chain (symptom, triage, diagnosis, treatment, follow-up, discharge), but it loops (diagnosis is iterative and treatment is diagnostic) rather than being linear.
- C2 (knowledge monopoly): mixed. Incumbents do capture value (AMA, licensure), but the monopoly is legal, credential and liability-based, not informational. Diagnostic error among experts is about 11%.
- C3 (AI removes knowledge gap): contradicts as an operational claim. LLMs know the answer on vignettes, but this does not translate into better decisions in practice or survive external validation.
- C4 (10x cost drop in middle steps): contradicts. Task-shifting evidence shows labour savings offset by more tests, longer visits and higher resource use on complex cases. Telehealth did not cut total cost much.
- C5 (bespoke parts): silent (no healthcare evidence collected; a possible analog is FDA-regulated 3D-printed implants, not researched).
- C6 (inspection essential, must evolve): supports strongly. Sign-off, validation and liability are where the system is anchored, and AI made independent validation more necessary.
- C7 (built to be inefficient, needs rebuild): mixed. The system is built for liability allocation and safety, and the inefficiency is a by-product. The rebuild happened through legislation and payment changes, slowly, not by technology alone.

## Company-shape implications
- A company that **takes on the liability** (like Digital Diagnostics carrying malpractice coverage for autonomous AI) or that becomes the **certified sign-off entity** captures the value. Knowledge alone does not.
- **AI tooling for the "understand/document" layer** (the scribe analog for technicians: auto-generated work orders, manual lookup, parts identification) diffuses fastest.
- **Protocolised task shifting**: AI-codified troubleshooting trees, standard kits and an expert escalation network, as in WHO HIV task shifting, for fleets of energy, robotics or industrial assets.
- **An independent validation and inspection layer** for AI diagnostics on machines (the "Epic sepsis" failure mode will recur).
