## 1. Siemens / Senseye, "The True Cost of Downtime 2024" (primary, vendor survey)
URL: https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:1b43afb5-2d07-47f7-9eb7-893fe7d0bc59/TCOD-2024_original.pdf
Methodology: an ongoing Siemens online survey from April 2019 to March 2023 with 181 completed interviews of maintenance, engineering and IT staff at large industrial organisations in four sectors (Automotive, FMCG, Heavy Industry, Oil & Gas), extrapolated to the Fortune Global 500 using plant and employee counts. The report itself says the year-on-year combined results are "indicative only" because the sector mix changes. This is a vendor (PdM software) marketing white paper. Kind: data, but vendor-sponsored with a small n.

Per-hour cost of unplanned downtime at a large plant, FY23:
- Automotive: $2.3M/hour (">$600 a second"), 2x 2019 (+113% vs 19% US inflation). From the chart: about $0.8M FY19, $2.6M FY20, $2.0M FY21, $2.7M FY22, $2.3M FY23.
- Heavy Industry: about $270k/hour FY23 (chart reading), 4x 2019 (+319%). FY19 about $65k, FY22 about $85k.
- Oil & Gas: about $100k/hour FY23 (chart reading), with a peak of about $650k in FY22. The report says it tracks the oil price.
- FMCG: $36k/hour, stable and below 2019 (about $55k).
- All sectors combined: about $300k (FY19) to about $850k (FY23). The report says this has "doubled" over five years.
- SMEs: "up to $150,000 an hour at the top end."

Per-year figures:
- Average large plant: $253M/year lost to unplanned downtime.
- Automotive plant: $695M/year, "approaching $750m" (1.5x 2019). Heavy Industry plant: $59M/year (1.6x). FMCG plant: just over $10M/year (doubled since 2019). Oil & Gas plant: about $30M FY23, half of 2019, but about $240M in FY22.
- Fortune Global 500: about $1.4 trillion a year, equal to 11% of revenues ("GDP of Spain"). The report says this is down 6% since 2022.

Frequency and duration:
- Incidents per facility fell from 42/month (2019) to 25/month.
- Hours lost fell from 39/month (2019) to 27/month, or 326 hours/year (down nearly a third). Automotive and Heavy Industry hours roughly halved.
- **Mean time to restart rose from 49 minutes to 81 minutes.** Siemens's own explanation: (a) skilled maintenance labour was lost in the "great resignation", "creating a skills and knowledge gap"; (b) "emergency replacements are more challenging to source and take longer to arrive"; (c) PdM removed the easy failures, so "the things that cause downtime now are the more challenging problems that are harder to detect and take longer to fix."

Cost components the report counts: lost revenue, idle wages, wages of repair staff, **emergency replacement parts**, and contractual penalties.

Other points:
- Penalties flow down the supply chain: "those at the bottom of the supply chain face the stiffest penalties."
- About half of firms now have PdM teams (double 2019), and 87% collect machine-health data.
- Vendor claims for its own product: 50% less unplanned downtime, 40% lower maintenance cost, 55% higher maintenance staff productivity, 85% better forecast accuracy, and payback within 3 months. These are vendor claims, not independent data.
- Extrapolated savings potential for the FG500: 2.1M hours, $388B (5% productivity), and $233B (40% lower maintenance cost).

## 2. Siemens / Senseye, "The True Cost of Downtime 2022" (published 2023)
URL: https://assets.new.siemens.com/siemens/assets/api/uuid:3d606495-dbe0-43e4-80b1-d04e27ada920/dics-b10153-00-7600truecostofdowntime2022-144dpi.pdf
- FG500 loss: "almost $1.5tn" a year, 11% of turnover, up from $864bn (8%) in 2019-20.
- Cost per facility: $129M/year (up 65%). Automotive above $600M/plant/year. Heavy Industry $128M (doubled). Oil & Gas $149M (+76%). FMCG just under $10M (+36%).
- Per hour: Automotive above $2M (from $1.3M in 2019-20). Oil & Gas almost $500k (more than doubled). FMCG $39k. Every sector was at least 50% higher than two years earlier.
- 20 incidents/month (6 fewer), 25 hours/month lost.
- PdM teams rose from 11% to 38% of firms in Automotive. More than three-quarters of firms do some condition monitoring.

**Consistency problem (important for anyone quoting these):** the 2022 edition gives 20 incidents/month and $129M/plant in 2021-22, while the 2024 edition gives 42 incidents in 2019, 25 now, and $253M/plant. The per-plant and incident baselines do not reconcile across editions. Heavy Industry is $128M/plant in the 2022 edition but about $36M FY22 in the 2024 chart. Treat the Siemens numbers as order-of-magnitude marketing estimates, not as a statistical series. The $1.4-1.5tn headline is an extrapolation from about 181 interviews.

## 3. NIST AMS 100-34, Thomas & Weiss, "Economics of Manufacturing Machinery Maintenance" (2020; US government; the best independent data)
URL: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ams/NIST.AMS.100-34.pdf (doi 10.6028/NIST.AMS.100-34)
Method: a NIST Machinery Maintenance survey stratified with Census Annual Survey of Manufactures data, covering US discrete manufacturing NAICS 321-339 (excluding 324/325) in 2016.
- Direct maintenance cost was $57.3B (range $47.5-121.7B). Adding $16.3B for faults and failures and $0.9B for buffer inventory gives **$74.5B in total maintenance spending**.
- **Losses from preventable maintenance issues were $119.1B: $18.1B downtime, $0.8B defects, and $100.2B lost sales from delays and defects.** So most of the loss is lost sales, not repair labour or parts.
- On average, 45.7% of maintenance was reactive.
- The top 25% most reactive establishments had 3.3x more downtime, 16x more defects, 2.4x more lost sales from delays, and 4.9x more inventory increases than the bottom 25%.
- Establishments relying mainly on preventive or predictive maintenance had 44% less downtime, a 54% lower defect rate, and 29% fewer lost sales from delays.
- The perceived benefit of more PdM was $6.5B from less downtime plus $67.3B in extra sales.
- Safety: 16.03 injuries and 0.05 deaths per million employees were associated with maintenance issues.
- Interpretation: in US discrete manufacturing, the downtime-related loss (about $119B) is larger than the entire maintenance spend (about $75B). The value sits in avoiding or shortening the outage, not in cheaper repair inputs. This is far smaller than the Siemens $1.4tn (different scope: US only, discrete only, excluding chemicals and petroleum), which suggests the Siemens headline is inflated.

## 4. ITIC 2024 Hourly Cost of Downtime survey (IT/server downtime, cross-industry)
URL: https://itic-corp.com/itic-2024-hourly-cost-of-downtime-report/ (September 3, 2024)
- More than 90% of mid-size and large enterprises say an hour of downtime costs more than $300,000.
- 41% say $1M to more than $5M per hour, excluding litigation and penalties.
- Web survey of more than 1,000 firms, November 2023 to March 2024. Self-reported, so likely upward bias.

## 5. Splunk / Oxford Economics, "The Hidden Costs of Downtime" (June 2024)
URL: https://newsroom.cisco.com/c/r/newsroom/en/us/a/y2024/m06/conf24-splunk-report-shows-downtime-costs-global-2000-companies-400b-annually.html
- Global 2000: $400B a year, 9% of profits. Average annual cost per firm: US $256M, Europe $198M, APAC $187M.
- Causes: 56% security incidents, 44% application or infrastructure failures, with human error the top cause in both.
- Direct line items per firm: lost revenue $49M, regulatory fines $22M, SLA penalties $16M, ransomware $19M.
- 41% say customers are often the first to detect downtime, which is a failure at the "notice" step.
- "Resilience leaders" (top 10%) recover 23-28% faster and spend more on observability and cybersecurity.
- 2,000 executives, 53 countries, 10 industries.

## 6. Aviation MRO context: Oliver Wyman Global Fleet & MRO Market Forecast 2024-2034
URL: https://www.oliverwyman.com/our-expertise/insights/2024/feb/global-fleet-and-mro-market-forecast-2024-2034.html
- Commercial aviation MRO was about $101B in 2023 and $104B in 2024, forecast at $124B by 2034. The global fleet is 28,396 aircraft (2024).
- Constraints named: raw-material and labour shortages, OEM production near capacity, and new-engine durability problems (GTF, LEAP, Trent XWB) that led to A320neo groundings. In other words, airliner downtime is driven by physical capacity (engine shop slots, parts), not by knowledge.

## Synthesis / reasoning vs. the founder's hypotheses
- **Where the money goes:** in every source with a breakdown, the dominant cost of a breakdown is **lost production, lost revenue and penalties during the outage**, not the repair labour, the knowledge, or the parts themselves. NIST: $100B of the $119B loss is lost sales, and direct maintenance is $57B. Siemens lists emergency parts as one of five components, with lost revenue the largest by far. So the economic lever is **time to restore** (and avoiding the failure in the first place), not the bill of materials.
- **The time-to-restore story supports C2 partly.** Siemens attributes the rise in restart time (49 to 81 minutes) to a skills and knowledge gap from lost experienced technicians, AND to parts sourcing and delivery delays, AND to the remaining failures being harder. So knowledge is one of three named drivers; parts logistics is another.
- **Downtime frequency is already falling without AI-native rebuilds:** incremental IoT and PdM adoption cut incidents about 40% and hours about 30% from 2019 to 2023 (vendor data). This undercuts C7: the incumbents' existing toolset is improving downtime, and failures are moving toward the hard tail.
- **Order-of-magnitude claim (C4):** in large plants the outage-time cost (for example $2.3M/hr in Automotive) dwarfs the cost of diagnosis and sourcing. A 10x cut in diagnosis or sourcing *cost* matters little. A 10x cut in diagnosis and sourcing *time* matters enormously, but only for the share of downtime spent waiting for diagnosis or parts. That share is not quantified in these sources, and quantifying it is a needed follow-up.
- **Measurement quality:** downtime cost numbers are mostly vendor-generated, self-reported, and inconsistent between editions. The best independent source (NIST) is about 10-20x smaller in scope-adjusted terms than the vendor headlines. This argues for skepticism about TAM slides built on "$1.4 trillion".
- **Scale dependence:** the per-hour cost ranges from about $36k (FMCG) to $2.3M (Automotive), roughly 60x. That is why PdM ROI is compelling in auto and oil & gas and weak in FMCG (Siemens says so explicitly). The same chain (C1) has very different economics at different scales, so "from washing machines to airliners" spans regimes where the bottleneck differs.
- C3, C5, C6: largely silent in this evidence. NIST notes injuries and deaths linked to maintenance issues, a weak hint that safety matters.
