## Regulatory skeleton (what makes the chain long by design)
1. [VERIFIED] 10 CFR 21.3 definitions (Cornell LII, https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/10/21.3):
   - "basic component" = a safety-related SSC or part thereof.
   - "commercial grade item" = an SSC or part that affects its safety function and was not designed and made under an Appendix B QA program.
   - "dedication" = "an acceptance process undertaken to provide reasonable assurance that a commercial grade item" will perform its safety function.
   - "critical characteristics" = "those important design, material, and performance characteristics of a commercial grade item" that, once verified, give that assurance.
   - Meaning: an off-the-shelf part (relay, valve seat, O-ring, PLC) cannot go into a safety function until a dedicating entity defines its safety function and critical characteristics and verifies them by test, inspection, source survey or performance history. The dedicating entity then takes on Part 21 reporting liability.
2. [VERIFIED] 10 CFR 21.21 timelines (https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/10/21.21): deviations must be evaluated within 60 days of discovery (interim report if not done). Initial NRC notification is due within 2 days of a responsible officer getting the information, the written report within 30 days, and a supplier without evaluation capability must notify purchasers within 5 working days. The defect-information loop is legally mandated and fleet-wide, a knowledge-sharing obligation that most industries lack.
3. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] History. The EPRI NP-5652 (1988) CGD methodology was conditionally endorsed by NRC Generic Letter 89-02. A 1990s round of NRC vendor-inspection findings on poor dedication followed (GL 91-05). EPRI TR-3002002982 (2014, Rev 1 of NP-5652/TR-102260) was endorsed in Reg Guide 1.164 (2017). The 2015 Part 21 rule let accredited commercial calibration/testing labs (ILAC/NVLAP) stand in for an Appendix B supplier audit. The direction of travel has been to make dedication cheaper by trusting outside accreditation.
4. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] Why CGD exists. The pool of N-stamp / Appendix B suppliers shrank sharply after 1980s construction stopped. The commonly cited range is several hundred ASME N-certificate holders in the 1980s down to roughly 200 or fewer later. OEMs left, so utilities increasingly buy commercial items and dedicate them in house or through third-party dedicators (e.g. Curtiss-Wright, Paragon, Westinghouse, Framatome). Practitioners often describe a dedicated part costing several times its commercial twin (multiples like 3-10x are cited anecdotally). I found no systematic dataset in this session.

## Findings: costs, timelines, case studies

5. [VERIFIED] Outages were cut sharply by process, not AI. EIA (https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=37252): the average US refueling outage fell from 46 days (2012) to 34 days (2018). In 2017 six reactors finished in under 20 days, and Peach Bottom 3 took 15 days. Credited to experience and market competition. Shows big efficiency gains within the existing regulated chain, from planning, pre-staging parts and online maintenance. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] NEI's "Delivering the Nuclear Promise" (2015-) cut average generating cost from about $44-45/MWh (2012) to about $30-32/MWh (2020s), roughly 30%, largely through standardised processes, fewer procedures, efficiency bulletins and shared spares.

6. [VERIFIED] Crystal River 3 (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_River_Nuclear_Plant): a planned steam-generator replacement plus uprate outage (Sept 2009, restart due April 2011) cracked and delaminated the containment wall. Engineers had proposed detensioning 97, then 65 tendons (Bechtel agreed with 65). Only 27 were actually loosened, and the delamination was tied to tendon sequencing. Repair estimates ran from $0.9-1.3bn (2012) to $1.5bn, with a $3.4bn worst case (independent review, Oct 2012). Duke retired the plant in Feb 2013 and expected $850m in insurance. Former NRC chair Jaczko blamed "improper work planning". The failure was in executing the repair (work planning and structural understanding), not diagnosis or parts.

7. [VERIFIED] San Onofre (Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Onofre_Nuclear_Generating_Station): a $671m replacement steam-generator project (Mitsubishi, installed 2010-11) showed premature wear on more than 3,000 tubes and leaked in Jan 2012. The outage cost over $300m by Nov 2012, and both units retired in June 2013. The ASLB had ruled restart needed a public hearing, and with licences expiring in 2022 the cost was hard to justify. SCE claimed $7.57bn, but the ICC arbitration (March 2017) awarded only $125m (the contract cap) and ordered claimants to pay MHI $58m in costs. Ratepayer closure bill about $3.3bn. A new-build replacement part designed by a top OEM still failed (a design/thermal-hydraulic modelling error). The regulatory restart pathway, not part sourcing, was what killed the asset.

8. [VERIFIED] Davis-Besse (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis%E2%80%93Besse_Nuclear_Power_Station): boric acid ate through more than 6 inches of reactor-head carbon steel, leaving about 3/8 inch of cladding (found March 2002). Shut 2002-March 2004, with $600m in repairs and upgrades. The replacement head was bought from the never-finished Midland plant. Fines were the NRC's largest at over $5m, plus a $28m DOJ settlement, and individuals were indicted. The noticing step failed for years because of safety culture, not a knowledge gap. The part was sourced by cannibalising a mothballed plant, which is what the parts market looks like for unique heavy components.

9. [VERIFIED] Browns Ferry 1 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Browns_Ferry_Nuclear_Plant): idle from 1985. Restoration ran 2002-2007 at $1.8bn. A full bring-back-to-service is a 5-year program.

10. [VERIFIED] Palisades restart (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palisades_Nuclear_Generating_Station): $1.5bn DOE conditional loan (March 2024) plus $300m state money (Aug 2024). The late-2025 restart target was missed, though status returned to operational on 27 Aug 2025. In 2026 a fuel assembly tilted and bent during loading. The damaged upper tie plate could not take the standard grapple, so Holtec built a custom recovery tool and transfer canister, and the NRC approved a one-time licence amendment for the recovery on 14 Oct 2026. Bespoke tooling was made fast. The gating item was the licensing amendment. This supports C6 and partly C5 (custom tooling is doable), but the binding constraint was regulatory approval.

11. [VERIFIED] South Korea parts-certificate scandal (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_South_Korea). Nov 2012: over 5,000 small parts in five reactors were not properly certified, eight suppliers faked 60 warranties, and two reactors shut to swap parts. June 2013: forged certificates on safety-related control cabling shut or kept offline four reactors and delayed the first APR-1400s by up to a year. About 100 people were indicted (Oct 2013), including a former KHNP CEO. The bottleneck in qualification is document integrity and trust in the paper trail. Faster sourcing without provenance makes things worse. Strong evidence for C6.

12. [VERIFIED] Flamanville 3 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamanville_Nuclear_Power_Plant): €3.3bn (2007, operation 2012) grew to €13.2bn (2022) and €19.1bn including financing (Cour des Comptes, 2020). In April 2015 Areva reported vessel-steel toughness anomalies linked to Creusot Forge. Secondary-circuit welds were found nonconforming in Feb 2018, and ASN required eight steam-line penetration welds repaired in June 2019 (+€1.5bn). 110 defective welds were repaired in total, and a new stress-relief heat-treatment process had to be developed in 2022. Grid connection came in Dec 2024 and full power in Dec 2025. Weld repair and requalification, plus forging QA, added years: the physical repair and inspection stages dominate.

13. [VERIFIED] France 2022 stress-corrosion-cracking crisis (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_in_France): found at Civaux (Oct 2021), then Chooz. 28 of 56 reactors were offline by end-April 2022 and 32 by early Sept (all causes). Output was 279 TWh, the lowest since 1993. EBITDA impact about -€18.5bn, and replacement power bought at market about €29bn. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] EDF lacked qualified pipe welders and brought in about 100 welders from North America (Westinghouse etc.). Repair used cut-out-and-replace of safety-injection/RHR piping, programmed over 2022-2025. Diagnosis (finding the cracking mechanism and which units were affected) needed destructive lab exams of cut pipe, so diagnosis itself needed physical cutting. The scarce input was certified welders and inspection capacity.

14. [VERIFIED] Vogtle 3/4 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogtle_Electric_Generating_Plant): $14bn (2017 certified) grew to about $36.8bn, with commercial operation in Jul 2023 and Apr 2024 against targets of 2016-17. Causes include a post-Fukushima design change while long-lead components were being made, contractor turnover (CB&I, Westinghouse/Fluor, Bechtel), Westinghouse's 2017 bankruptcy, and Georgia PSC concerns (Nov 2017) that drawings lacked approval by licensed engineers. Startup issues (cooling vibrations, RCP motor fault) added months. Construction rather than repair, but it shows the same supply-chain atrophy that drives CGD: the industry forgot how to build and document nuclear-grade work.

15. [VERIFIED] NRC CFSI page (https://www.nrc.gov/about-nrc/cfsi.html): NRC stresses verification "through physical inspections and performance testing" and works with the 23-agency IPR Center on counterfeit parts. Counterfeits are treated as a standing threat, which is why provenance and testing cannot be removed.

16. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] Digital I&C obsolescence is the obsolescence problem that bites hardest. US plants run 1970s analog safety I&C, and vendor parts have been gone for decades. NRC digital safety-system licensing was so slow and uncertain (Diablo Canyon, Oconee took years) that most utilities did like-for-like analog replacement or reverse-engineered boards. Limerick (Constellation) got the first full-plant digital safety-related I&C LAR approval in about 2024 under the "alternate review process". Obsolete-part reverse engineering is a large dedication niche (equivalency evaluations under EPRI 1008256 / 3002018270-type guidance).

17. [BACKGROUND, UNVERIFIED] Additive manufacturing. Westinghouse installed the first 3D-printed safety-related part (a fuel thimble plugging device) at Byron Unit 1 in 2020. ORNL/Framatome 3D-printed fuel-assembly brackets went into TVA's Browns Ferry in 2021. NRC has an AMT action plan and accepts ASME Code Case / NUREG-based qualification case by case. Typical qualification timelines are years per part family because material property data, process qualification, and NDE of AM microstructure must be built. The AI parts factory runs straight into this qualification wall.

## Synthesis: where the time and money go
- Diagnosis and knowledge are rarely the binding step in the costly nuclear cases. Crystal River, San Onofre, Flamanville and France SCC were all diagnosed by competent people. Cost and time ballooned in (a) physical repair execution (tendon sequencing, welds), (b) qualification and requalification (heat treatment process, weld NDE, new grapple and licence amendment), (c) regulatory restart pathways (San Onofre public hearing, Palisades amendments), and (d) replacement-power cost while the unit is offline. Lost generation runs about $1m+/day for a large unit (commonly cited, unverified) and dwarfs the parts bill.
- Sourcing is a real problem, but mostly as qualification and provenance, not search. The parts often physically exist (Midland head, commercial relays). Converting them into "basic components" via CGD paperwork and testing is the friction. The Korea scandal shows paper provenance is the weak link.
- Notice/diagnose failures (Davis-Besse) are about safety culture and incentives, not missing knowledge.
- Process improvements within the rules (outage 46 to 34 days, NEI's roughly 30% cost cut) show the system is not hopelessly built for inefficiency. It responded to market pressure. Gains came from planning, standardisation and shared knowledge (EPRI, INPO, Part 21 fleet reporting), which already pools knowledge across the industry.

## Stance notes
- C1: Mixed. The chain fits, but nuclear adds explicit loops (dedication, Part 21 reporting, licensing amendments, post-maintenance testing) and diagnosis is sometimes destructive (cut-out pipe). Stages are not linear.
- C2: Mixed. OEM exit created a real obsolescence/knowledge gap, but the industry built a shared knowledge commons (EPRI, INPO, Part 21). Costly failures came from execution, not a monopoly of knowledge. OEMs still capture value through dedication and replacement-part monopolies (Mitsubishi steam generators, Framatome/Westinghouse I&C).
- C3: Contradicts. The gating items are physical evidence, accountable sign-off, licensing and qualified labour, none of which AI removes. AI could speed CGD critical-characteristic selection, equivalency evaluations, Part 21 search and outage planning.
- C4: Contradicts for nuclear. Those four steps are a minority of cost. Outage-days, repair execution, requalification and replacement power dominate. A 10x cut in diagnosis and sourcing would shave maybe a few percent off big events, though it could cut the long tail of obsolete-part procurement lead times.
- C5: Contradicts. AM parts take years per part family to qualify, and Korea shows that provenance matters more than the ability to make the part. Bespoke tooling (Palisades grapple) is achievable; bespoke safety-related parts are not "solved".
- C6: Strongly supports. Qualification, dedication, NDE and licensing are the bottleneck, and the AMT and digital I&C frameworks are evolving. This is where AI-era change is needed: digital evidence trails, AI-assisted NDE, dedication automation.
- C7: Mixed. The structure is intentionally conservative (defence in depth), not accidentally inefficient. It has delivered large efficiency gains when pushed. Atrophied suppliers and slow regulatory pathways are real and justify reform (Part 53, ADVANCE Act 2024 [background]).

## Company-shape ideas the evidence points at
- CGD / equivalency-evaluation copilot plus a test-lab network: automate critical-characteristic selection, technical evaluations and Part 21 search, and pair it with accredited labs for verification. This sells to utilities' and dedicators' supply-chain groups and targets the paperwork-plus-test step.
- Obsolete-part reverse engineering and qualified AM-as-a-service for nuclear and defence, with the qualification data package as the product, not the part.
- Digital provenance / chain-of-custody for safety-related parts (anti-CFSI), where the Korea scandal is the use case.
- Outage-execution planning and work-package intelligence (the Crystal River lesson: repair planning, not diagnosis).
- AI-assisted NDE (weld UT, eddy-current steam-generator tube analysis) where certified-analyst capacity is scarce (EDF SCC).
