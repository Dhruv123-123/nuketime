## Findings (hard data from 10-Ks)

### 1. Grainger FY2025 (10-K filed 2026-02-19)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/277135/000027713526000011/gww-20251231.htm
- Net sales were $17,942M, up 4.5%. Gross margin was 39.1% (2024: 39.4%). Operating margin was 13.9% (2024: 15.4%). Operating earnings were $2,495M.
- High-Touch Solutions N.A.: sales $13,993M (+2.0%), gross margin 41.7%, operating earnings $2,354M. That is about 16.8% operating margin (my calculation).
- Endless Assortment (Zoro + MonotaRO): sales $3,625M (+15.7%), gross margin 29.9%, operating earnings $345M (+32.7%). That is about 9.5% operating margin (my calculation).
- Customer and product scale:
  - More than 4.6M customers.
  - About 2M products in High-Touch, about 13M on Zoro and about 29M on MonotaRO.
  - More than 1.5M products stocked.
  - More than 5,000 primary suppliers, none above 5% of purchases.
- The filing describes "the large and fragmented MRO industry."
- High-Touch is built on "product knowledge and customer expertise". Branch staff provide "technical product expertise and search-and-select support."
- Reading: the higher-margin segment is the one that sells knowledge, service and search. Pure online assortment earns about 12 points less gross margin and about 7 points less operating margin. Commoditised "find the part" is a lower-margin business. The premium goes to helping customers pick the right part and handle complex purchasing.

### 2. Fastenal FY2025 (10-K filed 2026-02-05)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/815556/000081555626000009/fast-20251231.htm
- Net sales were $8,200.5M. Gross profit was 45.0% and operating income 20.2%. This is the highest-margin distributor in the set.
- FMI (Fastenal Managed Inventory) sales were $3,712.7M, or 44.7% of sales (2024: 42.5%).
  - FASTBin/FASTVend: $2,675.0M, 32.2% of sales.
  - FASTStock: 12.5% of sales.
- About 124,000 FASTVend devices are in the field. Weighted installs were 136,638 MEUs (+7.6%). Fastenal estimates the market could support up to 1.7M vending units.
- Digital Footprint was 61.4% of sales in 2025.
- About 74% of sales are under contract and national accounts are 65%.
- The 2,653 "$50k+ Sites" (Fastenal's term for Onsite-like customers) generated $4,326M, about 53% of site sales (my calculation).
- Fasteners are 30.5% of sales and safety supplies 22.2%.
- Key quotes:
  - eProcurement provides "a means for our customers to effectively and efficiently procure indirect and unplanned spend."
  - Its inventory models "are designed to improve product availability, automate replenishment, and reduce procurement costs."
  - "the cost and time to manage, procure, and transport these products can be quite meaningful."
  - The goal is to "reduce their total cost of procurement."
- Reading: Fastenal earns the best margins in industrial distribution by sitting physically inside the plant (vending and onsite) and taking over procurement labour. It does not win on product knowledge. The friction it monetises is transactional and logistical: replenishment, small-quantity buys, the trip to the crib. The binding constraint is placement and logistics, not knowledge.

### 3. MSC Industrial FY2025 (FYE 2025-08-30; 10-K filed 2025-10-23)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1003078/000100307825000123/msm-20250830.htm
- Net sales were $3,769.5M (-1.3%). Gross margin was 40.8% (FY24: 41.2%). Operating margin was 8.0% (FY24: 10.2%).
- About 63.8% of orders were placed digitally (web, vending, eProcurement).
- About 2.5M active SKUs.
- 29,611 vending machines in service and 411 in-plant program locations.
- More than 160 metalworking, safety and fluid-connector technical specialists.
- Key quotes:
  - "multiple people often acquire the same item in small quantities via expensive, one-off purchases."
  - "Customers increasingly evaluate their total procurement costs."
  - The industry is "large, fragmented … comprised of national, regional and local distributors" and is expected to consolidate.
- Reading: MSC states the tail-spend problem in its own words: duplicated, small-quantity, one-off buys that cost a lot of administrative effort. Its margin squeeze shows that a gross margin near 41% does not guarantee operating profit when cost-to-serve is high.

### 4. Applied Industrial Technologies FY2026 (FYE 2026-06-30; 10-K filed 2026-08-13)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/109563/000010956326000033/ait-20260630.htm
- Net sales were $4,966.7M (+8.8%). Gross margin was 30.3% and operating margin 11.1%. EBITDA was about 12.4% (my calculation).
- Service Center (mainly MRO break-fix bearings and power transmission): $3,184.2M, 29.2% gross margin, 13.4% operating margin.
- Engineered Solutions (fluid power and automation): $1,782.5M, 32.4% gross margin, 11.8% operating margin. Growth of 15.1% came from automation and fluid power.
- More than 9.4M SKUs, about 580 facilities and about 6,900 associates.
- Addressable market is "over $80 billion" (cross-sell framing). The industry is "highly fragmented."
- Key quotes:
  - Competitive advantage includes "Extensive technical knowledge and domain expertise of our customers' facility and production equipment."
  - Its fluid power operations use "certified fluid power specialists, mechanics, technicians, and engineers."
  - When plants run hot "they tend to wear out machinery more frequently and require replacement parts."
- Reading: this is a direct example of C2. Applied presents tacit knowledge of the customer's installed equipment as its moat. Note, though, that the margins on this "knowledge" business (30% gross, 11% operating) are lower than Fastenal's logistics-led model. Knowledge is valued, but the market does not pay monopoly rents for it.

### 5. Genuine Parts / Motion Industries FY2025 (10-K filed 2026-02-20)
URL: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/40987/000004098726000003/gpc-20251231.htm
- Industrial segment (Motion) sales were about $8.9B (+2.3%), 37% of GPC sales. Segment EBITDA was $1,146.4M, a 12.9% margin (2024: 12.6%).
- Network: 755 locations (32 DCs, 646 branches, 77 service centers). Access to more than 10M replacement parts from about 40,000 suppliers. More than 180,000 customers. National accounts are about 45% of sales.
- Services include specialised repair of gearboxes, fluid power, pumps, drive shafts, electrical panels, and hoses and gaskets. Also VMI and onsite inventory, RFID asset tracking, and Motion AI automation.
- On 2026-02-17 GPC announced a split into Global Automotive and Global Industrial, targeted for Q1 2027.
- Reading: the largest pure MRO "break-fix" distributor runs at about 13% EBITDA. Repair services are bundled in, and the distributor is also a repair shop. An independent Motion would be a standalone public MRO platform from 2027, either a partner or a competitor.

- Takeaway: gross margins of 30-45% on parts mean the distribution layer takes roughly a third or more of every MRO dollar.
- The part is a commodity. The distributor charges for availability, finding the right part and procurement labour.
- The highest margin goes to whoever owns the point of consumption (Fastenal), not to whoever owns the deepest knowledge (Applied).

## Tail and unplanned spend (secondary; could not verify this session)

  - MRO/indirect is often described as about 5-10% of a manufacturer's spend but up to about 60-80% of purchase transactions.
  - Grainger has long said that about 40% of MRO purchases are unplanned (a claim in older investor materials).
  - Typical procurement cost per PO is quoted at about $50-$150+ (APQC and Hackett-style benchmarks). For many MRO line items the transaction cost exceeds the item price.
- Confirmed in primary text:
  - Fastenal names "indirect and unplanned spend" as the target of eProcurement.
  - MSC describes "expensive, one-off purchases" in small quantities by multiple people.
  - Both describe "total cost of procurement" as the competitive axis, not unit price.

## Assessment against hypotheses
- C1 (universal chain): silent or mixed. Distributors show that the "source parts" and "deliver parts" steps are real, large businesses ($50B+ of combined revenue from these five firms). The chain is not linear in practice, though. Fastenal and Grainger KeepStock pre-position parts before failure, so source and deliver collapse into a stocking problem rather than a step after diagnosis.
- C2 (knowledge monopoly): mixed.
  - Applied, Grainger HTS and MSC all sell technical knowledge (search-and-select, specialists, "domain expertise of our customers' facility").
  - But the most profitable firm, Fastenal at 20% operating margin, monetises logistics and procurement labour, not knowledge.
  - The MRO parts market is "large and fragmented", with 5,000-40,000 suppliers per distributor. That is the opposite of a monopoly at the parts level.
- C3 (AI removes the gap): mixed, leaning contradicts for this layer.
  - Digital ordering is already 61-64% (Fastenal, MSC), yet margins have not collapsed.
  - Grainger's online Endless Assortment is growing fast (+15.7%) at lower margin. That suggests commoditising search pushes down margin rather than removing the layer.
  - The remaining value is physical: availability within hours and in-plant inventory.
- C4 (10x cost drop in diagnose, understand, source, deliver): contradicts on "deliver".
  - The physical layer of about 755 locations (Motion), 580 (Applied), more than 120k vending machines and DC networks is capital- and labour-heavy. Fastenal says the cost and time to "manage, procure, and transport" is "quite meaningful".
  - Sourcing and search friction could plausibly fall a lot. Delivery is bound by logistics.
  - Procurement transaction cost is a plausible 10x target: the PO cost is often larger than the item cost [UNVERIFIED benchmark].
- C5 (bespoke parts solved): silent. 10M+ SKUs at Applied and Motion, and 29M at MonotaRO, show that the problem is mostly finding and stocking an existing standard part, not making a new one. Long-tail obsolescence is not addressed in these filings.
- C6 (inspection persists): silent.
- C7 (structurally inefficient): mixed.
  - The friction is real: one-off buys, duplicated purchases, fragmented suppliers.
  - But incumbents have spent 20+ years on it (vending, VMI, eProcurement, digital at 60%+) and earn high returns from it. They are not "slop". The friction is their revenue, which supports a "built to be inefficient for the buyer" reading in part.

## Where the money and time go
- Of the MRO parts dollar, about 30-45% is distributor gross margin. That pays for stocking breadth (millions of SKUs), local availability and procurement labour.
- The customer's own hidden procurement cost (POs, duplicated small buys, emergency buys) is the friction that distributors sell solutions against.

## Real bottleneck
- Physical availability at the point of need: the right part already within reach when it is needed.
- Under that sits the customer's procurement transaction overhead for low-value, high-frequency, unplanned items.
- Knowledge of which part to buy is a real but lower-margin layer.

## Company shapes suggested
- AI procurement agent for tail/unplanned MRO spend. It turns a free-text or photo need into a matched SKU across distributors and an automatic PO, aimed at the cost per PO. It would partner with distributors rather than replace them.
- Part identification and cross-reference: photo, nameplate or BOM to an interchangeable part across 10M+ SKUs, sold to distributors' counter staff (Grainger's "search-and-select") or to plants.
- Predictive stocking: link condition-monitoring data to in-plant vending and VMI replenishment, so parts are pre-positioned before failure. This is the Fastenal model plus prediction.
- Obsolete-part long tail (needs other evidence): a brokerage plus reverse-engineering service for parts no longer in any catalogue.
