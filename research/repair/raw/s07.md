## Findings: wheelchairs (the best-documented part of this domain)

1. **Median 14 days to repair; 14% of users got none of their needed repairs done; "vendor failure to complete after being contacted" was the top reason (30%).**
   Worobey LA et al., "Wheelchair Repairs: Delays, Causes, and Associated Outcomes," Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2025;106(3):379-386. PMID 39427781. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39427781/
   - N=301 SCI wheelchair users across 9 SCI Model Systems centers. 76% said all needed repairs were completed, 7% some, 14% none.
   - Vendors did 56% of completed repairs. The most common consequences were being forced into a backup chair or confined at home.
   - "A greater percentage of participants experienced each type of consequence, except injury, for repairs completed by vendors."
   - The authors conclude that this "seems to indicate structural inadequacies within the repair process."
   - Time to repair did not differ significantly across components. This hints that the delay is a process/system delay, not one tied to the technical difficulty of any component.
   - Kind: study (hard data, cross-sectional survey).

2. **Breakdowns are frequent and getting worse: 44.8% of full-time users needed a repair within 6 months (2009); repairs and consequences rose significantly between 2004-06 and 2006-11.**
   - McClure LA et al., Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2009;90(12):2034-8, PMID 19969165, N=2213. 44.8% repaired in 6 months; 8.7% had an adverse consequence; power chairs needed significantly more repairs. The authors call for mandatory ANSI/RESNA standards compliance and **changes in insurance reimbursement policy**.
   - Worobey L et al., Am J Phys Med Rehabil 2012;91(6):463-9, PMID 22549473, N=723. Significant increases of 7.8% in participants reporting repairs and 23.5% in adverse consequences versus historical data. Medicare/Medicaid-funded chairs had more consequences than VA, workers' comp and vocational rehab chairs.
   - Kind: study.

3. **63.8% needed ≥1 repair in 6 months, and 18.2% of those were stranded. Power chair failures are mostly electrical/power/controls. 40% of manual chair users repair at home themselves, versus 14% of power chair users.**
   Toro ML et al., Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2016;97(10):1753-60, PMID 27153763, N=591. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27153763/
   - Interpretation: self-repair collapses when the device is electronic/powered. That is consistent with a knowledge, tools and parts lock on power chairs, and also with genuine complexity.
   - Kind: study.

4. **56% reported repairs. 42% of those had an adverse consequence lasting a median 5 days. Median out-of-pocket cost was $150 (IQR $50-620). Public insurance raised the odds of repair (OR 1.70); Black users had OR 2.42.**
   Worobey LA et al., Arch Phys Med Rehabil 2022;103(4):779-789, PMID 33845000, N=533. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33845000/
   - The authors call for "higher standards, access to quicker service, and better training of users on wheelchair maintenance and repair." They also note that a working backup chair minimizes consequences.
   - Kind: study.

5. **Veterans: 88.3% needed a repair in 6 months, with a median 7-day time to repair (IQR 0.8-30). Vendors did 82.1% of repairs. Longer repair times went with more adverse consequences (p<0.001).**
   Henderson GV et al., Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 2022;17(3):331-337, PMID 32762567, n=60.
   - The VA-funded population waits half as long as the SCIMS population (7 vs 14 days median). That points to payer/process design, not device technology, as a key variable.
   - Kind: study.

6. **The rural repair market is hollowing out. Traditional Medicare power wheelchair vendors fell from 1,292 to 986 between 2015 and 2022; rural vendors fell from 374 to 182 (-51%). Repairs per sale or rental fell from 0.31 to 0.18 for rural vendors and rose from 0.32 to 0.44 for urban ones.**
   Skinner RJ, Gupta A, Keohane LM, "Power wheelchair vendors: Declines in rural market activity," Disabil Health J 2026;19(4):102119, PMID 42303535.
   - The authors attribute the regulatory pressure to Medicare competitive bidding and prior authorization.
   - Kind: study (claims data, hard).

7. **Stakeholders (clients, clinicians, CRT suppliers) name prior authorization for repairs, insufficient funding for repair services and backup devices, redundant paperwork, inconsistent policy interpretations, and the "in the home" coverage rule as the bottlenecks.**
   Breaux R, Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 2026;21(2):651-667, PMID 40955950. 37 interviews in Colorado and beyond.
   - Kind: study (qualitative; practitioner views).
   - None of these bottlenecks is a knowledge gap. All of them are payer/administrative.

8. **Colorado HB22-1031, the first US right-to-repair law of any kind (wheelchairs only), effective 1 Jan 2023.**
   It requires powered-wheelchair manufacturers to provide "parts, embedded software, firmware, tools, or documentation" (diagnostic, maintenance and repair manuals) to independent repairers and owners. Contract terms that limit this are void. Manufacturers are not liable for independent repairs. Violation is a deceptive trade practice. Trade secrets are exempt.
   https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb22-1031
   - Kind: regulator/legislation.
   - It supports C2: legislators judged that a lock on information, tools and firmware existed.
   - It does not touch prior authorization, reimbursement or technician supply. Those bottlenecks persist (see finding 7, which is Colorado-heavy and post-law).

9. **[MEMORY, UNVERIFIED THIS SESSION] US PIRG "Stuck in Place" (2022) survey.**
   - Roughly 93% of respondents needed a repair in the past year. About 68% waited 4+ weeks, and about 40% waited 7+ weeks.
   - It describes a US complex-rehab market dominated by two suppliers, Numotion and National Seating & Mobility, both private-equity-backed.
   - It describes manufacturer restriction of parts and diagnostic software to authorized dealers.
   - URL (403 to fetch): https://pirg.org/resources/stuck-in-place/
   - Kind: campaigner_view.

10. **Prevention and predictive maintenance work.** With NHS-service power chair telemonitoring plus charging coaching, 54/119 users charged badly and 26 changed behaviour. Battery repairs fell 18%, total repairs 11%, and costs fell £3,092 versus a matched cohort. Drive time predicted time-to-failure (AUC 0.71).
    Mhatre A et al., Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 2025;20(1):127-134, PMID 38754034.
    A related study (PMID 39540758, Mhatre 2025) says "no tools or technologies exist to inform data-driven inspection schedules for wheelchairs." It found 13.4 ± 14.8 self-reported part failures per user per year, 4.7 of them high-risk.
    - Kind: study.
    - The "notice" stage is under-instrumented: sensor data plus AI could move repair from reactive to scheduled. This is an addition to C1, not a replacement for it.

11. **In low-resource settings (Kenya, Uganda, Sierra Leone, Indonesia), community/informal repair is the dominant repair route.** Specialist AT repair is scarce, and repair is "often overlooked in the planning and design of assistive products and services."
    Oldfrey B et al., Disabil Rehabil Assist Technol 2024;19(5):1945-55, PMID 37466362.
    - Kind: study.
    - Informal repairers already fill the knowledge gap locally. The binding constraint there is parts and design-for-repair.

## Findings: e-bikes, bicycles, scooters

12. **VanMoof (proprietary-everything e-bike) went bankrupt in July 2023, owing €143.8M.**
    - Before the collapse, customers reported frame damage, electrical malfunctions, and "difficulty getting timely repairs or replacement parts."
    - Lavoie (McLaren Applied) bought it in August 2023.
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VanMoof
    - [MEMORY, UNVERIFIED] Owners feared bikes would be bricked because unlocking depended on VanMoof's servers/app. Rival Cowboy and the third-party "Bikey" app shipped tools to export digital keys.
    - Kind: news/history.
    - This is the textbook case for C5 (orphaned proprietary parts). It also shows that the lock is digital (keys and firmware), not just physical parts.

13. **Rad Power Bikes (the largest US DTC e-bike brand) filed Chapter 11 on 15 Dec 2025.**
    - Assets were $32M against $72M in liabilities.
    - It "could not afford the cash needed for a battery recall" after a CPSC warning (24 Nov 2025) about two battery models linked to at least 31 fires.
    - It said it would not honor warranties for pre-bankruptcy purchases. It was sold to Life EV Group for about $13.3M (closed 5 Mar 2026, renamed Rad Life Mobility).
    - https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rad_Power_Bikes
    - Kind: news.
    - The bottleneck for e-bikes is the safety-critical battery: a certified replacement battery is the scarce part, and an AI-designed or bespoke-made lithium pack runs into safety certification (UL 2849/2271, required in NYC and SF). This supports C6 and contradicts C5.

14. **BionX (a major e-bike hub-motor system supplier) went into receivership on 27 Feb 2018 and dismissed 80 staff.** Its IP, software and inventory were sold to Amego/Leisger in Oct 2018. Owners of BionX-equipped bikes from many brands were left reliant on residual inventory.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BionX
    - Kind: history.
    - This is another orphaned-proprietary-electronics case (a motor controller plus firmware).

15. **The EU Battery Regulation (EU) 2023/1542, Art. 11, requires light-means-of-transport batteries (e-bikes, e-scooters) to be replaceable by an independent professional from 18 Feb 2027.** This also covers individual cells.
    https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32023R1542

16. **Bosch / Shimano dealer-only diagnostics** [MEMORY, UNVERIFIED THIS SESSION].
    - Bosch's "DiagnosticTool 3" and Shimano's E-TUBE Project Professional give dealers deeper access than the consumer apps.
    - Bosch's Smart System (2022+) supports component registration/anti-theft (eBike Lock, eBike Alarm). Right-to-repair advocates have criticised this as a parts-pairing risk.

## Synthesis / reasoning

- **What is the bottleneck?** For wheelchairs (power chairs above all), the evidence points to the payer-and-vendor system as the dominant bottleneck, not knowledge:
  - prior authorization for repairs (Breaux 2026)
  - reimbursement that does not pay for repair labor, travel or backup chairs (McClure 2009, Breaux 2026)
  - a consolidating, thinning vendor network, especially in rural areas (Skinner 2026: rural vendors down 51%)
  - vendors failing to show up (Worobey 2025: the top reason)
  - The VA vs SCIMS difference (7 vs 14 day medians) and the finding that time-to-repair does not vary by component both say the delay is organisational.
  - Manufacturer information and tool lock is real (Colorado legislated against it), but it is one of several layers.
- **For e-bikes**, the scarce layer is proprietary electronics plus batteries:
  - motor, controller and battery are tied to firmware and dealer-only diagnostics, and brands go bust (VanMoof, BionX, Rad)
  - battery replacement is safety-certified, so you cannot simply fabricate one
- **For plain bicycles**, the knowledge is open and parts are mostly standardised. The constraints are mechanic labor/margins and supply-chain shocks.
- **C1 (chain):** It roughly fits, but this domain adds two stages the rubric omits:
  - authorization/funding approval (a hard gate before parts are even ordered)
  - logistics of getting a technician to a person who cannot move without the device (an in-home visit)
  - "Notice" is also weak: there are no inspection schedules (Mhatre 2025), so failures are discovered reactively.
- **C3/C4:** AI could collapse diagnosis (telemonitoring already predicts failures, AUC 0.71) and manuals. But the dominant time sinks (authorization, a vendor dispatch backlog, parts held by dealers, battery certification) are not knowledge problems. Cutting knowledge cost tenfold would not cut a 14-day median tenfold.
- **C5:** It is contradicted for safety-critical parts (batteries, motor controllers with firmware, seating systems that need clinical fitting). It is plausible for simple mechanical bits (casters, brackets, armrests, 3D-printable clips). Even these need standards compliance (ISO 7176 / RESNA) if they are load-bearing.
- **C7:** It is partially supported. The system is not "built to be inefficient" by design. It is the emergent result of Medicare competitive bidding, prior authorization, PE consolidation of CRT suppliers, and OEM dealer channels. That is incentive misalignment rather than a deficit in "smart."

## Company shapes suggested by the evidence
- Mobile, insurer-agnostic power-wheelchair repair network with parts inventory and backup-chair loans, enabled by Colorado-style RTR (rural focus, where vendor counts halved).
- Telemetry plus predictive maintenance for power chairs and e-bike fleets: the sensor turns "notice" into a schedule and can auto-generate prior-auth documentation.
- Prior-authorization / claims automation for DME repairs (AI that drafts the paperwork). It attacks the actual time sink.
- Certified, universal replacement battery packs / BMS for orphaned e-bike brands (VanMoof, Rad, BionX), with UL 2849/2271 certification as the moat. The EU 2027 replaceability rule creates demand.
- Open diagnostic tooling for e-bike motors (a cross-brand "OBD" layer for Bosch/Shimano/Bafang).
