# v07: iFixit, Repair.org (Gay Gordon-Byrne), US PIRG, Restart Project, Repair Cafe: what are their core arguments about why repair is inefficient, and what data do they cite?

Researched 2026-10-20. All five groups are advocates. Their surveys are self-selected and not nationally representative. Restart/Repair Cafe logs are the best hard data, though they cover only consumer goods brought to volunteer events.

## Core thesis these groups share
They do not argue that repair is "built to be inefficient" by accident or by structure. They argue that **manufacturers deliberately restrict repair to control aftermarket revenue**. The restrictions come in five forms: parts, information, tools and diagnostics, software locks (parts pairing), and design (glue, unopenable products). Their remedy is legal, not technological: right-to-repair laws, FTC enforcement, EU ecodesign and repairability scores.

## Findings

1. **Repair.org / Gordon-Byrne: repair as an OEM monopoly.** She estimates about 90% of products on the market cannot be repaired without the manufacturer, if they can be repaired at all. This is her own estimate, not measured. Her history: OEMs moved documentation online, then added logins and paywalls, then shut out independents, while also restricting parts, tools and software. In her Vermont testimony she added that some OEMs train only dealer-employed technicians, and that training is lost when those technicians go independent. Her asks: manuals and parts at fair prices, the right to bypass software locks, and no warranty voiding.
   - NPR/KUOW 2022: https://kuow.org/stories/gay-gordon-byrne-why-do-big-manufacturers-prevent-you-from-repairing-your-own-stuff
   - Vermont S.180 testimony 2018: https://legislature.vermont.gov/Documents/2018/WorkGroups/Senate%20Economic%20Development/Bills/S.180/S.180~Gay%20Gordon-Byrne~Testimony~2-7-2018.pdf
   - Her legal frame is the aftermarket lock-in doctrine of Eastman Kodak v. Image Technical Services (1992): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastman_Kodak_Co._v._Image_Technical_Services,_Inc.

2. **Open Repair Alliance / Restart Project, "The rise of community repair" (Oct 2024).** The dataset is 208,491 repair attempts by 1,158 groups in 31 countries, across 19,986 events. 53% of products were fixed and 25% ended as end-of-life. Among failed repairs with a recorded barrier: **no spare parts 25%, parts too expensive 18%, design (cannot open) 16%, missing repair information 12%.** https://openrepair.org/open-data/insights/2024-report/
   - KEY for C2/C3: in the best real-world dataset, missing information is the **smallest** named barrier. Parts (availability plus price, 43%) and physical design (16%) dominate.

3. **Restart 2025 dataset.** 441,068 broken items from 31,436 events in 32 countries; about 11,000 items logged per month. 43% of electrical devices seen went unrepaired, and lack of spare parts was again the most common barrier. Their framing: products are "not designed for repair," plus business models that push replacement. The EU Joint Research Centre uses this data to prioritise repairability scoring. https://therestartproject.org/data-and-insights/how-our-real-world-data-is-making-the-case-for-the-right-to-repair/

4. **Repair Cafe International RepairMonitor.** The average success rate is about 65% (electrical appliances about 55%) in the 2017-18 Dutch data. More than 60% of 2022 repairs succeeded and about a quarter failed. Spare parts were the leading cause of failure, and a reason was recorded for 60% of failures. In 2017-18, **manuals or repair tips were found in only 16% of the cases where volunteers looked for them**, so information does bite when sought. Textiles have a 95% success rate, which shows that physical simplicity matters more than knowledge.
   - https://www.repaircafe.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/Factsheet_RepairMonitor_2022_EN.pdf
   - https://repaircafe.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/RepairMonitor-analyse_2018_02042019.pdf
   - EEA textiles: https://www.eea.europa.eu/en/circularity/sectoral-modules/textiles/repairability-of-textile-products

5. **US PIRG, "Repair Saves Families Big" (2020) and its sequel II (2022).** Households spend about $1,480 a year on new electronics and appliances. PIRG says repair could cut that by 22%, worth about $330 a year per household and $40B nationally, rising to $382 and $49.6B in the sequel. https://pirg.org/media-center/report-fixing-instead-of-replacing-devices-could-save-americans-nearly-50-billion-a-year/
   - CONTRA: the Competitive Enterprise Institute says the figure counts housewares, assumes no warranty-covered repairs and no current repairs, and puts the real saving at about $55.74 a year. https://cei.org/blog/right-to-repair-will-not-save-households-over-300-a-year/

6. **PIRG, "Hospital Repair Restrictions" (2020).** Survey of 222 biomedical technicians. 91.8% said they had been denied service information for critical equipment such as ventilators, defibrillators and imaging. Nearly half were denied manuals, parts or service keys since March 2020. Named restriction types: passwords and service keys needed to read diagnostics, and refused manuals. A later edition surveyed more than 100 servicers: about 83% said repair barriers increase downtime at least somewhat frequently, and 70% "commonly" hit diagnostic tool restrictions that delay patient care. https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/hospital-repair-restrictions/ ; https://pirg.org/edfund/media-center/new-report-repair-restrictions-lead-to-equipment-downtime-less-timely-patient-care-in-hospitals/
   - Here the knowledge is **locked**, not missing: service keys and diagnostic software. AI cannot read data it has no access to.

7. **PIRG, "Deere in the Headlights II."** Farmers depend on dealers because dealer technicians have diagnostic and calibration software that farmers and independents lack. Dealer consolidation adds distance and downtime, which is worst at planting and harvest. https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/deere-in-the-headlights-ii/
   - Again the bottleneck is a software and authorisation lock plus a shrinking dealer network, not tacit knowledge. The FTC sued Deere in Jan 2025 on this theory.

8. **PIRG, "Stranded" (wheelchairs).** 141 users. 93% needed a repair in the past year and 68% needed two or more. 62% said the average repair takes 4 or more weeks and 40% said 7 or more weeks. 77% named "long wait for service and parts" among their biggest challenges. https://pirg.org/resources/stranded/
   - The delay is in sourcing, authorisation and logistics (the insurance prior-authorisation chain plus dealer parts). Diagnosis is easy, and an order-of-magnitude cut in diagnosis cost does little here.

9. **PIRG "Failing the Fix" scorecards (2022-2025).** Phones improved: Apple went from F (2022) to B- (2025) and Google from D to B-. Laptops are "pretty stagnant." 8 of the 10 brands belong to anti-right-to-repair trade groups. https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/failing-the-fix/
   - This is evidence that regulation (NY, CA, MN, OR laws and the EU) shifts OEM behaviour within a few years, and that this lever is not AI.

10. **iFixit (Kyle Wiens).** The core claim: "If this information exists in the world, I should have access to it." When OEMs refused to publish, iFixit built community guides. iFixit retroactively cut the iPhone 14 score from 7 to 4 (Sept 2023) over **parts pairing**: even genuine Apple parts lose Face ID, True Tone and so on without Apple's System Configuration tool. Oregon (2024) and Colorado (2024) banned parts pairing. https://9to5mac.com/2023/09/20/iphone-14-ifixit-repairability-rating/ ; https://9to5mac.com/2024/05/29/parts-pairing-banned-by-colorado/
    - **iFixit FixBot (2025)** is an AI assistant over about 125k guides, the Q&A forum and PDF manuals. Wiens says it "sometimes gets things wrong," and testers reported incorrect or unsafe CRT advice. https://pcworld.com/article/3002716/dropped-it-smashed-it-ifixits-new-ai-powered-app-can-help-you-repair-it.html
    - So the leading repair-information company has shipped the AI layer and itself treats it as a complement. It does not remove parts pairing, parts supply or design barriers.

11. **FTC, "Nixing the Fix" (May 2021), the regulator view these groups all cite.** It found "scant evidence" for OEM justifications based on safety, security or quality, and no evidence that independents misuse data more often. Restrictions catalogued: adhesives, limited spare parts, unavailable diagnostic software. A July 2021 policy statement prioritised enforcement. https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P194400-Nixing-the-Fix-California-Senate-Judiciary-Committee-Testimony.pdf

12. **Cost relative to a new product is the dominant consumer barrier, a point the advocates underweight.** In a cross-country NIM study, consumers tolerate repair costs of only about 15-20% of the new price; "too high costs" was by far the top reason for not repairing. BEUC (2026): a €400 phone display costs about 30% of the device price, and a vacuum battery at €281 costs 150% of the vacuum's price. https://www.nim.org/en/studies/detail/repair-or-buy-new ; https://www.beuc.eu/reports/repairing-the-market
    - Implication: for cheap goods, labour plus parts price relative to cheap new manufacturing decides repair, and better knowledge does not change that.

13. **Policy momentum.** PIRG reports that 25.75% of Americans were covered by some right-to-repair law as of Jan 2026, rising to at least 35.5% by fall 2026 (CT, TX). 2025 was the most productive year, with wheelchair laws in OR, WA and NV. https://pirg.org/articles/more-than-one-quarter-of-americans-covered-by-right-to-repair-come-jan-1/

## My reasoning against the hypotheses
- C2 is partly supported, but the advocates frame it as **access control** (locked manuals, service keys, diagnostic software, pairing), not a capability gap. That knowledge exists; the OEM withholds it.
- C3 is contradicted. AI cannot reconstruct cryptographic pairing, dealer-only calibration software or service keys. In the community-repair data, information is only 12% of failures; parts and design are about 59%. Repair Cafe's 16% "info found" rate shows a real but secondary gap that AI search over manuals (FixBot) could help close.
- C4: none of these groups' evidence points to diagnosis or understanding as the dominant cost. The dominant costs are parts availability and price, labour cost relative to the new-product price, wait times for parts and authorisation, and dealer distance.
- C5 is silent or contradicted: parts unavailability is the #1 barrier, but nobody claims on-demand fabrication solves it. Many parts are electronic, serialised or certified.
- C7 is supported in a modified form: the advocates say the inefficiency is engineered for aftermarket profit, so the fix is legal and economic rather than a rebuild.
- C6: the advocates say OEM "safety" arguments are mostly pretext (FTC: scant evidence). That cuts against inspection as a necessary gate for consumer goods, while hospitals and aviation are a different matter.
- C1: no one disputes the chain, but in the data the wait is concentrated in sourcing and authorisation, and the authorisation step (insurance, OEM service key, software unlock) is missing from the founder's chain.
