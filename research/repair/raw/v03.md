# v03: US state right-to-repair laws (NY Digital Fair Repair, Minnesota, California SB 244, Colorado ag and wheelchair laws, Oregon parts pairing): what do they require and what early evidence of effect exists?

Researched 2026-10-20. Sources are a mix of primary (FTC, legislature summaries, court filings via trade press) and secondary (law-firm alerts, advocacy groups, trade press). Hard data is labelled as such. Advocacy (PIRG, Repair.org, iFixit) and industry (NAM, Deere, Apple) material is labelled as opinion.

## Bottom line

- The laws are almost entirely **information/access mandates**: make manuals, diagnostic software, tools and parts available to owners and independent repairers on "fair and reasonable" terms (generally the same terms authorized dealers get). They directly target the "understand" and "diagnose" stages and partly "source parts". They say nothing about delivery logistics, skilled labour, or making parts from scratch.
- They are full of carve-outs: motor vehicles, medical devices, off-road and construction equipment, aviation, marine, game consoles (in CA/MN/CO/OR), security/alarm systems, and anything that would bypass anti-theft or security features. Aviation (the founder's airliner example) is excluded everywhere.
- **Early evidence of effect is thin and mostly about compliance, not outcomes.** No published study measures repair price, turnaround time or downtime before/after any of these laws. The best hard data are: PIRG's 2024 audit (no manual for 48% of covered products, no parts for 44%), the Deere class-action overcharge estimate ($190M-$387M; $99M settlement), and the FTC-Deere consent order (July 2026), which is federal antitrust enforcement, not the state laws.
- As of mid-2026, law-firm surveys report no public state AG enforcement action under any of the digital electronics laws (as of Aug 2024 confirmed by Grist; Faegre Drinker July 2026 lists none). The real teeth so far came from **litigation and the FTC**, not the statutes.
- Manufacturers' strongest response was **anticipatory**: Apple backed SB 244 (it already had Self Service Repair), and loosened iPhone parts pairing weeks after Oregon passed SB 1596 (used genuine parts, on-device calibration). That suggests the law shapes product design at the margin, but the OEM still controls calibration and the parts channel.

## Findings

### F1. New York Digital Fair Repair Act (GBL 399-nn): first, and weakened
- Signed Dec 2022 by Gov. Hochul after passing in June 2022; effective Dec 2023. Covers "digital electronic equipment" first sold/manufactured on or after **July 1, 2023** (not retroactive).
- Last-minute chapter amendments at the governor's request: dropped the requirement to provide passwords/security codes/materials to override security features; limited to products sold after July 1, 2023; exempted business-to-business and business-to-government equipment; allowed OEMs to sell assemblies rather than individual components; excluded home appliances, motor vehicles, medical devices, off-road equipment, public safety communications, home security.
- Penalty: **$500 per violation** (weakest of the group). AG enforcement.
- Proposed fix bill A8955 (Fahy) would remove B2B/B2G exemptions, backdate to July 1, 2021, add home appliances. I could not confirm enactment; Faegre Drinker's July 2026 survey lists no 2025-26 NY amendment.
- Sources: Fight to Repair, https://fighttorepair.substack.com/p/ny-governor-bows-to-big-tech-signs ; PhoneArena, https://www.phonearena.com/news/ny-passes-digital-fair-repair-act_id144528 ; SDM, https://www.sdmmag.com/articles/101717-first-in-the-nation-ny-right-to-repair-law-exempts-security-devices-and-alarm-systems ; NWIDA on A8955, https://nwida.org/ny-looks-to-beef-up-r2r-law ; Faegre Drinker July 2026, https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2026/7/unlocking-the-right-to-repair-the-expanding-compliance-landscape
- Reading: an example of the incumbent lobbying successfully on scope. Evidence for C7 (the structure resists change) more than for C2/C3.

### F2. Minnesota Digital Fair Repair Act (Minn. Stat. 325E.72)
- Enforceable July 2024; covers digital electronic equipment manufactured/sold on or after **July 1, 2021**, including business computers (unlike NY). Excludes medical, agricultural, motor vehicles, game consoles, cybersecurity tools.
- Manufacturers must provide documentation, tools and parts to owners and independent providers on "fair and reasonable terms"; cannot condition parts on licensing/registration or require use of particular providers. AG-only enforcement; penalties up to $20,000-$25,000 per violation (sources differ).
- A 2024 bill (HF 4418) proposed parts-pairing language; not confirmed enacted. 2025 SF2484 extended to motorcycles and added a liability shield for OEMs from independent repairs, and a 60-day window to provide resources after first sale.
- Sources: Gislason & Hunter, https://www.gislason.com/minnesotas-new-right-to-repair-law/ ; Repair.org MN, https://www.repair.org/minnesota ; MN revisor HF 4418, https://www.revisor.mn.gov/bills/93/2024/0/HF/4418/versions/latest ; SF2484 tracker, https://track.mn/legislative_instruments/0942025-sf2484 ; Crowell, https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/right-to-repair-a-growing-trend-for-states-creating-compliance-challenges-for-manufacturers

### F3. California SB 244 (Right to Repair Act, Cal. Pub. Res. Code 42488)
- Signed Oct 2023, enforceable July 1, 2024. For products sold after July 1, 2021: wholesale price **$50-$99.99 -> parts/tools/docs for 3 years** after last manufacture; **>$100 -> 7 years** (longest in the US). Excludes video game consoles, alarm systems, and anything that disables anti-theft (e.g. Face ID/Touch ID). Independent repairers must disclose use of non-genuine or used parts.
- Penalties: $1,000/day first violation, $2,000/day second, up to $5,000/day thereafter; AG, city and county prosecutors.
- Apple publicly supported it (Aug 2023 letter) because its Self Service Repair program (launched April 2022) already met the requirements, and it asked for the disclosure clause and protection of security features. Commentators note the disclosure clause can work in Apple's favour.
- Sources: AppleInsider, https://appleinsider.com/articles/23/10/11/apple-backed-right-to-repair-bill-now-law-in-california ; Apple letter, https://s3.documentcloud.org/documents/23925286/apple-support-sb-244.pdf ; Mactrast, https://www.mactrast.com/2023/10/apple-backed-california-right-to-repair-bill-signed-into-law/amp/ ; Faegre Drinker (above).
- Reading: the most powerful OEM chose to support the law because it already owned the compliant channel. Access mandate != dismantling of control over parts/calibration.

### F4. Colorado agricultural right to repair (HB23-1011), effective Jan 1, 2024 - first ag law in the US
- Requires OEMs (Deere, CNH, AGCO etc.) to provide parts, embedded software, firmware, tools, manuals and diagnostics to owners and independent repairers. Amended during passage to bar modifications to security or emissions functions. Passed after a 2023 Deere-American Farm Bureau MOU that lawmakers saw as "vague, incomplete and unenforceable."
- Deere: law is "unnecessary and will carry unintended consequences." Sponsor Rep. Brianna Titone: absence of lawsuits or industry collapse would show it doesn't cause chaos.
- **Effect evidence: none found.** No Colorado AG enforcement, no state evaluation, no farmer survey after the effective date. Advocates still report independent shops cannot provision software tools (Repair.org, Jan 2025).
- Pre-law baseline (opinion/survey, national): PIRG/NFU survey: farmers lose avg **$3,348/yr** to repair downtime and restrictions.
- Sources: DTN, https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/equipment/article/2023/04/12/colorado-becomes-first-state-pass-ag ; Colorado Sun, https://coloradosun.com/2023/02/13/right-to-repair-farming-equipment-colorado/ ; Repair.org, https://repair.org/blog/2025/1/21/john-deere-reaps-what-they-sow ; PIRG on Deere change, https://pirg.org/articles/deere-right-to-repair-change/

### F5. Deere: the real ag outcomes came from litigation, not state law (hard numbers)
- **Class action (N.D. Ill., filed 2022 by 17+ farms):** $99M settlement announced April 2026; preliminary approval May 2026 (objection pending). Plaintiffs' expert (Russell Lamb, yardstick method) estimated **overcharge damages of $190M-$387.3M** on large ag equipment repairs since Jan 10, 2018; class "well in excess of 200,000 farmers"; recovery = **26%-53% of estimated overcharge** (vs. typical antitrust 5-15%). Deere agreed to make digital diagnostic/repair tools available to farmers and IRPs for 10 years. No admission of wrongdoing.
- **FTC + 5 states (IL, AZ, MN, MI, WI) v. Deere (filed Jan 2025):** judge denied Deere's motion for judgment on the pleadings June 2025 (weak source); settled **July 8, 2026**. 10-year order: Deere must give owners and independent shops the same tools dealers have, nationwide: view DTCs, program/reprogram ECUs, reset immobilizers/security locks, calibrate to factory spec, replace engines/ECUs, live/stored data, DTAC knowledge base, Machine Health Insights, emissions inducement overrides, offline mode; PIPs by Dec 31, 2026. Compliance reports every 60 days, CEO-certified. $1M to each state. Caveat: third-party software in the equipment needs "reasonable best efforts" approval before ECU capabilities are provided; "dealer business resources" carve-out.
- Sources: DTN settlement, https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/equipment/article/2026/04/07/farmers-recover-53-overcharge-john ; PIRG, https://pirg.org/articles/deere-announces-99-million-settlement-in-right-to-repair-lawsuit/ ; DTN FTC order, https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/equipment/article/2026/07/08/ftc-orders-deere-open-repair-tools ; Equipment World, https://www.equipmentworld.com/business/article/15829573/john-deere-settles-ftc-states-lawsuit-over-right-to-repair
- Reading: strongest quantitative evidence in the whole domain that a knowledge/tool monopoly produced a measurable rent ($190-387M overcharge over ~8 years on one OEM's large ag equipment). Supports C2 in the ag case. Notice the monopoly was on **software tools and authorizations** (ECU programming, immobilizer reset, calibration), not on know-how per se: AI cannot generate an OEM's signed ECU firmware or reset an immobilizer. This undercuts C3.

### F6. Colorado powered wheelchair law (HB22-1031), effective Jan 1, 2023 - first in US
- Manufacturers must provide parts, embedded software, tools, manuals and digital access to owners and independent repairers; non-compliance is a deceptive trade practice. Now copied by Oregon (wheelchair rules effective Jan 1, 2026), Maryland (May 2025), Nevada AB 407 (May 2025), Washington SB 5680 (May 2025).
- Baseline (PIRG "Stranded" survey, n=141, opinion/self-report): **62% waited 4+ weeks** for a repair, **40% waited 7+ weeks**, 68% needed 2+ repairs in last year; 83% expected the law to help. Market concentrated in two national suppliers (Numotion, National Seating & Mobility); rural users report weeks of waiting because the supplier won't send a tech for one customer.
- **Effect evidence: none found.** No post-2023 measurement of wait times. Important structural point: much of the wheelchair delay comes from Medicare/insurer prior-authorization and supplier technician capacity, which a parts/manuals law does not touch.
- Sources: PIRG, https://pirg.org/media-center/colorados-nation-leading-right-to-repair-wheelchair-bill-goes-into-effect-on-january-1/ ; Stranded, https://pirg.org/resources/stranded/ ; Harvard Health, https://www.health.harvard.edu/blog/millions-rely-on-wheelchairs-for-mobility-but-repair-delays-are-hurting-users-202207212785 ; iFixit, https://fr.ifixit.com/News/60846/colorado-fixes-wheelchair-repair-with-new-bill
- Reading: the bottleneck here is labour, logistics and payer authorization (time-to-technician), not knowledge. Contradicts C2/C3 for this category; supports C1's chain but shows the delays sit in "deliver" and "repair labour" and "payer approval" stages that the chain omits.

### F7. Colorado consumer electronics (HB24-1121), effective Jan 1, 2026
- Covers digital electronic equipment first sold/used in CO on/after July 1, 2021. For equipment first sold after Jan 1, 2026: bans parts pairing that prevents installing replacement parts, reduces functionality, or triggers misleading warnings. Carve-outs: pairing to log repairs; standalone biometric components. Excludes vehicles, marine, aviation, medical (except powered wheelchairs), certain security, construction and energy-related equipment, game consoles. Software tools must be free; physical tools can be charged. Independent repairers must disclose non-authorized status and non-OEM parts.
- Sources: Colorado General Assembly, https://leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1121 ; Proskauer, https://www.proskauer.com/blog/colorado-expands-right-to-repair-law
- Note: "energy-related equipment" is excluded. Relevant for a founder in energy: the state laws mostly do not reach grid/energy equipment.

### F8. Oregon SB 1596 (2024): first parts-pairing ban
- Signed March 2024. Covers consumer electronics sold in OR after July 1, 2021 (cell phones back to July 1, 2015). **Parts-pairing ban** for equipment manufactured after **Jan 1, 2025**: OEMs cannot use software unique-ID matching to (a) prevent installation of a part, (b) reduce functionality/performance, or (c) display misleading alerts. Excludes motor vehicles, medical devices, HVAC, off-road/farm equipment, game consoles, electric toothbrushes, etc. AG civil penalties up to **$1,000/day** for violations on/after **July 1, 2027** (so no enforcement possible yet).
- Faegre Drinker's table lists cell phones as excluded and July 2025 as the date: this conflicts with the legislature's summary and press; treat that table cell as a likely error.
- Washington (effective Jan 1, 2026) is the second state to ban specified forms of pairing.
- Sources: Oregon measure analysis, https://apps.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/2024R1/Downloads/MeasureAnalysisDocument/82383 ; Resource Recycling, https://resource-recycling.com/e-scrap/?p=24183 ; PCWorld, https://www.pcworld.com/article/2282443/oregon-passes-right-to-repair-law-outlawing-parts-pairing.html ; etcentric, https://www.etcentric.org/oregons-right-to-repair-law-is-the-first-to-ban-parts-pairing/

### F9. Early effect: Apple's anticipatory product change after Oregon
- Weeks after Oregon's law, Apple (April 2024) announced iOS changes: used genuine parts get full functionality, calibration happens on-device after install, parts from Activation-Locked phones restricted. Apple's hardware chief argued pairing is needed for authentication and calibration and that most third-party parts still work. Critics (404 Media) called it a "half measure" that still lets Apple gate parts by calibration and does not fully comply with a total pairing ban. Apple lobbied against pairing bans in Oregon and Colorado.
- Sources: 404 Media, https://404media.co/apple-announces-half-measure-to-make-iphones-more-repairable-immediately-after-law-banned-its-repair-practices ; Mactrast, https://www.mactrast.com/2024/04/apples-hardware-engineering-chief-discusses-parts-pairing-process/ ; Techfinitive, https://www.techfinitive.com/features/parts-pairing-and-the-apple-iphone-16-how-apples-next-phone-will-be-designed-by-oregons-senate/
- Reading: the control point has shifted from "knowledge" (manuals) to **cryptographic identity and calibration of parts**. That's a technical gate, not a knowledge gap.

### F10. Compliance audit: PIRG "Leaders and Laggards II" (2024) - the main hard early-effect data
- Graded 25 products (21 covered by NY law) in 5 categories on manual and parts availability. **Could not access a repair manual for 48%; no spare parts for 44%.** 32% graded A, 28% B/C, 40% D/F. Laptops all A/B; no dishwasher above C; no manuals for recent Sony, Nikon, Fujifilm, Canon cameras; Apple Vision Pro had no manuals/parts; Meta Quest 3 no manual. Smartphones best.
- Sony said it had published ~300 service manuals; customer service told PIRG to check YouTube/iFixit. Loophole: a company that offered no repair support before has no obligation to start (laws generally require parity with what authorized providers get; if no authorized network exists, little is required).
- As of Aug 2024 no state had brought a public enforcement action; NY AG declined comment. CA and MN AGs said they are committed to enforcement.
- iFixit's Kyle Wiens: "the public reputational risks are as significant as the fines." Repair Association's Gay Gordon-Byrne: "We're expecting there will be lots of holes."
- Sources: Route Fifty/Grist, https://route-fifty.com/emerging-tech/2024/08/right-repair-electronics-now-law-3-states-big-tech-complying/399119 ; PIRG, https://pirg.org/edfund/media-center/new-report-despite-more-repair-laws-many-companies-still-do-not-support-repair/ ; MyChesCo, https://www.mychesco.com/a/news/pennsylvania/new-report-finds-manufacturers-falling-short-on-right-to-repair-laws-despite-growing-state-mandates/
- I found no 2025 or 2026 edition grading Oregon/Minnesota compliance.

### F11. The patchwork in 2026 (13 states per Faegre Drinker, July 2026)
- Electronics: NY, MN, CA, CO, OR, WA (eff. Jan 2026, pairing ban), CT (July 2026, includes major home appliances), TX HB 2963 (eff. Sept 1, 2026; AG-only with mandatory 30-day cure notice), KS HB 2700 (signed Apr 2026, eff. July 2027, injunctive relief only; reimbursement/replacement as alternative compliance). Autos: MA, ME. Wheelchairs/mobility: CO, MD, NV, WA, OR.
- PIRG: more than a quarter of Americans covered by electronics repair laws as of Jan 1, 2026.
- Enforcement: Faegre lists no state AG action under any electronics law. Massachusetts auto telematics law (Question 1, 2020, 75% yes): industry suit dismissed Feb 2025, appealed to First Circuit; GM/Stellantis testified compliance was "impossible" because the required standardized platform did not exist. Five-plus years of delay.
- Federal: EPA "freedom to fix" emissions guidance July 1, 2026; Motor Vehicle Modernization Act (H.R. 7389) advanced in committee May 2026.
- Sources: Faegre Drinker (above); PIRG Jan 2026, https://fairshareonline.org/updates/more-than-one-quarter-of-americans-covered-by-right-to-repair-in-2026 ; Wikipedia MA Q1, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Massachusetts_Question_1 ; Fight to Repair on MA, https://fighttorepair.substack.com/p/auto-industry-wins-more-delays-in
- Reading: the patchwork itself is a compliance cost; laws trend toward weaker enforcement (TX cure period, KS injunctive only) as industry adapts its lobbying.

### F12. FTC "Nixing the Fix" (May 2021): the evidentiary basis behind the laws
- Unanimous bipartisan report to Congress: "scant evidence to support manufacturers' justifications for repair restrictions"; no empirical evidence independent shops are more likely to compromise data; restrictions raise costs and limit choice. Followed by July 2021 policy statement prioritising enforcement.
- Sources: DotMed, https://www.dotmed.com/legal/print/story.html?nid=54775 ; FTC testimony, https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P194400-Nixing-the-Fix-California-Senate-Judiciary-Committee-Testimony.pdf
- Supports C2 and C7 (restrictions are a business choice, not a technical necessity) - regulator view, not a causal measurement.

### F13. Counter-evidence: economics of mandates are ambiguous
- Jin, Yang & Zhu (Management Science, 2023) model: when independent repair gets cheaper, a monopolist manufacturer re-prices both new products and repair; outcomes can be "lose-lose-lose" for manufacturer, consumer and environment depending on production costs. Theoretical, not empirical.
- NAM-commissioned study argues repair mandates raise costs and emissions (industry advocacy).
- No empirical study found of phone repair prices or independent shop revenues before/after any state law.
- Sources: Berkeley Haas, https://newsroom.haas.berkeley.edu/research/right-to-repair-laws-have-potential-downsides-for-consumers-and-the-environment/ ; ServiceMag, https://www.servicemag.org/news/right-to-repair-economics-management-science ; NAM, https://nam.org/right-to-repair-laws-harm-manufacturers-and-consumers-29659

## Stance reasoning vs. hypotheses

- **C1 (universal chain):** Mixed. The laws implicitly map onto diagnose/understand/source parts (manuals, diagnostics, parts). But real delays in the evidence sit in stages the chain lacks: **authorization/unlocking** (ECU programming, immobilizer reset, parts pairing/calibration), **payer approval** (wheelchairs), and **technician availability/dispatch** (wheelchairs). Also "disclosure" duties for independent repairers.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly):** Supports, with refinement. FTC found restrictions mostly unjustified; Deere overcharge $190-387M; PIRG found 48% no manuals. But the monopoly is increasingly over **software keys, calibration, and authorized-only tooling**, i.e. access/permission, not just know-how.
- **C3 (AI closes the gap):** Contradicts. The gated items are cryptographic pairing, signed firmware, dealer-only software, immobilizer reset, emissions overrides. AI cannot produce these; only legal mandate/settlement did. Statutes also explicitly exclude security-bypass materials, so even with the laws some gates remain.
- **C4 (order-of-magnitude cost drop):** Silent/mixed. No measured cost change from any state law. Deere settlement implies overcharge on the order of tens of percent, not 10x. Laws touch info/parts access but not delivery.
- **C5 (AI parts factory):** Contradicts indirectly: laws require OEM parts availability (3-7 years in CA) and disclosures of non-genuine parts; parts pairing bans exist because OEM identity is enforced in software. A third-party fabricated part still faces pairing/calibration and certification gates (and aviation, medical, vehicles excluded entirely).
- **C6 (inspection stays essential):** Supports mildly. Laws carve out safety/security/emissions modifications; CA/CO require disclosure of non-OEM parts; regulated sectors (aviation, medical) excluded precisely because certification regimes govern repair.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient):** Supports in part. FTC found restrictions unjustified; NY law gutted at signing; MA auto law delayed 5+ years; Deere monopoly priced. But it's "built for rent capture via access control" more than "built dumb", and the fix that worked was antitrust + litigation, not technology.

## Where money and time go (from this evidence)
- Ag: dealer-only diagnostics/programming create downtime (PIRG/NFU: ~$3,348/farm/yr) and price premium (Deere overcharge estimate $190-387M across 200k+ farmers over ~8 years, i.e. on the order of $1-2k per farmer total).
- Wheelchairs: time dominated by waiting for a technician and approval (62% wait 4+ weeks), not diagnosis.
- Electronics: barrier is parts availability (44% none) and pairing/calibration, then manuals.

## Real bottleneck
Permission and authorization layer: software keys, parts pairing, calibration, dealer-only tools, warranty/emissions/safety liability, plus enforcement capacity (AGs have brought ~no actions). Second: skilled technician capacity and dispatch in thin markets (wheelchairs, rural ag).

## Company shapes suggested
- Compliance/repair-resource infrastructure for OEMs facing a 13-state patchwork + FTC-Deere-style orders (portals that serve manuals, diagnostics, parts-pairing tools to independents; audit trails for the CEO-certified 60-day reports).
- Independent-repair tooling for heavy/ag equipment now that Deere must expose DTCs, ECU programming, calibration (AI-assisted diagnostics on top of newly mandated access) - the access is now legally guaranteed for 10 years.
- Calibration/part-authentication-as-a-service neutral to OEMs (the pairing ban moves the gate to calibration).
- Mobile technician networks for powered wheelchairs / mobility devices in states with new laws (labour/dispatch bottleneck, not knowledge).
- Note: energy, aviation, medical, construction, vehicles mostly exempt, so a deeptech/energy repair startup gets little tailwind from state R2R laws and must rely on contracts or certification pathways instead.
