# v04: Agricultural right to repair (John Deere, CNH; FTC v Deere 2025; US PIRG farmer surveys): quantify downtime and dealer-repair cost premiums, and what the knowledge/software lock actually is

Researched 2026-10-20. Hard data is marked [DATA]. Court or regulator allegations are marked [ALLEGATION/REGULATOR]. Advocacy estimates are marked [ADVOCACY]. Opinion is marked [OPINION].

## 1. What the lock actually is: FTC v. Deere amended complaint (N.D. Ill. 3:25-cv-50017, filed Feb 7 2025, public version Feb 19 2025)
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/006020250219AmendedComplaint.pdf (case page: https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/cases-proceedings/211-0191-deere-company-ftc-v)

[ALLEGATION/REGULATOR, based on Deere internal documents]
- **Two tools.** Full-Function Service ADVISOR (built around 1999) goes to dealers only. Dealers pay a monthly fee per location (amount redacted). The degraded Customer Service ADVISOR (launched for ag in 2017) is licensed to owners and independent repair providers (IRPs).
- **What the dealer tool does that the customer tool does not** (para 49-53):
  - reads and clears all diagnostic trouble codes (DTCs);
  - calibrates ECUs;
  - downloads and installs Deere "payload files", i.e. reprograms ECUs;
  - gives access to CCMS/DTAC, Deere's solutions database plus a helpdesk where Deere engineers resolve problems.
  - Reprogramming is required when an ECU is replaced, when some sensors and valves are replaced ("parts pairing"), and when ECU software is reflashed. The reprogramming function "does not require or enable a dealer technician to modify the software payload ... or access the source code."
- **This lock is about authorization, not knowledge.** The scarce item is a cryptographic or entitlement gate to Deere's payload files and DTAC case base. It is not general know-how.
- **Third-party tools hit the same wall** (para 56). Bosch, Cojali and Texa interoperate partly, but cannot read or clear all DTCs, cannot reprogram Deere ECUs, and cannot reach DTAC.
- **Price of the customer tool** (para 54): $3,160 for the first year, $2,560 to renew. A Deere employee said the price points "will be cost prohibitive for nearly all customers but then we can't be accused of not making it available," and "this does not deteriorate the service business at dealerships."
- **Dealer labor rates** (para 66): $130-$200/hour as of 2024, with field rates higher than shop rates. Dealers also charge travel time and per-mile fees. A dealer's service area is generally 30-60 miles.
- **Independent labor rates** (para 69): a Deere internal analysis found "substantial rate differences." The average IRP rate equalled or exceeded the dealer rate in only two states.
- **Diagnosis opacity** (para 76): Deere keeps no dictionary of which DTCs need a dealer. Building one would take a multi-department team about 16 hours of engineering effort per equipment model. Across 14 equipment families, only 6% of the DTCs that need dealer resolution (185 of 2,958) tell the owner to contact a dealer. **This is a real knowledge gap that the OEM itself has not codified.** Owners therefore haul machines to dealers for repairs that do not need a dealer (para 113).
- **Installed base** (para 88): about 1.8M pieces of Deere ag equipment in the US, carrying 6.3M ECUs. A combine can have more than 20 ECUs.
- **Volume of restricted repairs** (para 110): 1.8M reprogramming events on Deere ag equipment globally in the 12 months to June 2023.
- **Scale of the business**: in FY2023 the PPA segment had $26.8B in net sales and $7.0B in operating profit.
- **Parts motive** (para 79-87, 94-100):
  - Deere offers about 500,000 OE parts.
  - Generic parts are typically some % cheaper (redacted), and their quality is "increasingly comparable."
  - Deere ran a CEO-level "Win in Aftermarket" initiative from about 2015. Its strategies were "Capture the Service" and "Control the Wrench."
  - A consultant on the initiative: "win the service and you win the parts."
  - A Deere executive: "if we get the machine in the shop we can control the parts going on the machine."
  - Self-repairers and IRPs use far fewer Deere OE parts (figures redacted).
  - Parts were a large share of Deere's FY2016 operating profit (figure redacted).
- **History** (para 92): ECU-related repairs, "including repairs equivalent to those that farmers historically completed themselves, such as replacing mechanical parts," became dealer-exclusive.
- **Lifecycle pricing** (para 75): customers cannot do it. Repair needs are unpredictable and depend on weather and field conditions.

Reasoning: the dealer premium has three stacked parts:
- a labor-rate premium;
- an OE-parts premium, because dealers almost always fit OE parts;
- logistics: transporting the machine 30-60 miles or paying field travel, plus waiting through a technician queue.

The software gate is the hinge that steers service, and through service, parts.

## 2. Ferguson dissent from filing the complaint (Jan 15 2025): even the skeptic agrees on the mechanism
Source: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/deere-ferguson-dissent-final.pdf

[REGULATOR/OPINION]
- Ferguson, joined by Holyoak, wrote that "repairs are no longer a strictly mechanical exercise. Rather than simply replacing a part, repairs require software tools to diagnose a problem and to calibrate and, at times, reprogram any affected components."
- He dissented on procedure, not substance. He called the repair market "extremely complicated, comprised of a web of authorized dealers, independent repair shops, customers, and parts manufacturers," and said staff lacked the evidence to sue "with any real confidence." He favored settlement.

## 3. Settlements (2026): the lock is being opened by law and contract, not by AI
- **Class action** (In re Deere & Co. Repair Services Antitrust Litigation, MDL 3030):
  - $99M fund for buyers of large-ag repairs from Deere or its dealers between Jan 10 2018 and May 18 2026.
  - More than 200,000 class members. Money is distributed pro rata to repair labor spend.
  - Preliminary approval May 18 2026; amended order Aug 2026; fairness hearing Jan 19 2027.
  - Includes 10 years of injunctive access to digital repair tools.
  - Sources: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2026/08/18/court-sets-january-2027-hearing-date ; https://www.kcrg.com/2026/04/07/deere-settles-right-repair-lawsuit-99-million/ ; https://agrisk.umd.edu/post/in-re-deere-company-repair-services-antitrust-litigation-understanding-the-preliminary-settleme
- **Rough scale check (my arithmetic):** $99M over about 200k members is about $500 per member over about 8 years. That is small next to PIRG's $4.2B/yr claim. Settlement amounts reflect litigation risk, not harm, but this is a useful sanity check on advocacy numbers.
- **FTC + 5 states (IL, AZ, MI, MN, WI) stipulated order**, July 8 2026, 10 years. Source: https://www.paulweiss.com/insights/client-memos/ftc-settles-right-to-repair-monopolization-case-against-deere ; Joint motion: https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Deere-JointMotion-StipOrd.pdf
  - Deere must offer owners and IRPs, at parity with dealers, these "Repair Resources": DTC view and clear, ECU reprogramming, calibration, interactive tests, engine replacement, troubleshooting resources, **emissions inducement overrides**, and offline diagnostics.
  - Pricing must be "fair and reasonable." The benchmarks are dealer net cost, other OEMs' prices, and affordability.
  - New tools must be offered once more than 50% of US dealer locations have them.
  - Deere pays the states $1M.
  - Status: proposed order, subject to court entry.
- **Deere's pre-emptive product move:** Operations Center PRO Service, July 31 2025.
  - Annual license from $195 per machine. Includes reprogramming when replacing controllers.
  - With the owner's permission, an IRP can access diagnostic and repair information.
  - Replaces Customer Service ADVISOR.
  - Source: https://www.deere.com/en/news/all-news/enhanced-digital-self-repair-tool
  - Note: $195/machine/yr against $3,160/yr is an order-of-magnitude cut in tool access price, achieved by regulatory pressure.

## 4. Downtime and premium quantification: PIRG / NFU surveys [ADVOCACY]
- **PIRG Ed Fund + National Farmers Union survey, about 2023**: 53 farmers in 14 states.
  - Average loss of **$3,348/yr** per farmer from repair downtime and restrictions.
  - Extrapolated to all US farms: **more than $3B/yr**.
  - One-third worried about losing the farm because of equipment failures, downtime and repair costs.
  - Source: https://thefern.org/ag_insider/lack-of-right-to-repair-may-cost-farmers-more-than-3-billion-says-survey/amp
- **"Out to Pasture" (PIRG; Kevin O'Reilly)**:
  - $3B downtime plus **$1.2B excess repair cost = $4.2B/yr**.
  - The $1.2B rests on surveyed farmers reporting that dealer mechanics charge **$58.90/hr more** than independent mechanics, scaled up nationally.
  - Source: https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/out-to-pasture/ ; https://pirg.org/media-center/report-tractor-right-to-repair-would-save-u-s-farmers-4-2-billion/
  - Caveat: small self-selected samples, extrapolated linearly.
- **Earlier PIRG/NFU survey**: 74 farmers in 14 states.
  - 95% support right to repair.
  - 92% think they could save money with independent repair or self-repair.
  - **77% have bought older equipment to avoid the software.** This is demand-side evidence that the software lock is priced in at purchase.
  - Source: https://pirg.org/edfund/resources/why-farmers-need-right-to-repair-2/
- **Cross-check of the labor premium**: the FTC's $130-200 dealer rate and PIRG's $58.90 gap imply independents at roughly $70-140/hr. The **labor premium is about 30-45%**, not 10x.

## 5. Dealer consolidation, the structural channel lock [ADVOCACY with countable data]
- PIRG "Deere in the Headlights II", Feb 2022:
  - **82%** of Deere's 1,357 ag dealer locations belong to chains of 7 or more stores. Comparisons: Case IH 37%, AGCO 22%, Kubota 5.8%.
  - Many farmers have only one dealer chain for their brand within 100 miles.
  - One chain per 5.3M acres, or per 12,018 farms.
  - Source: https://pirg.org/illinois/edfund/media-center/hog-tied-and-fed-up-new-report-shows-dealership-consolidation-makes-farmers-lives-harder/ ; https://lists.stevens.edu/pipermail/themaintainers/2022-February/001017.html
- Reasoning: the dealer is the single funnel for the software gate, OE parts, technicians and logistics. Consolidation lowers bargaining power and lengthens queues.

## 6. Repair costs are rising for reasons beyond the lock [DATA]
- farmdoc daily, Illinois FBFM farms, Dec 2024: https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2024/12/machinery-costs-on-illinois-grain-farms.html
  - Corn machinery cost rose from $44/acre (2003) to $162/acre (2023).
  - The repairs price index rose 1.8x since 2003, and faster since 2020.
  - Repair cost increases outpace the index, which suggests a growing volume of *hired* repair services.
  - Attribution: larger, more technologically advanced machines "require skilled labor to service." This is consistent with both a lock story and a complexity story.
- Purdue/FINBIN 2024: corn machinery cost about $178-185/acre. Between 2021 and 2024, machinery cost per acre rose 25% for corn and 22% for soybeans on farms over 1,000 acres. Source: https://www.farm-equipment.com/articles/24865-datapoint-machinery-costs-per-acre-for-small-and-large-producers

## 7. Downtime cost per day [DATA, older extension estimates]
- Iowa State's Mark Hanna, cited in trade press: about $1,400 per planting day and about $500 per harvest day of breakdown for 12-row equipment on an 8-hour day. The figure depends on how much yield a late pass loses. Source: https://www.proquest.com/docview/229786806
- A vendor blog cites $900 per harvest day and $2,400 per planting day; unverified.
- Timeliness is the dominant cost in farming. A two-day wait in the planting window is worth more than the labor-rate premium on the repair itself.

## 8. Emissions software (DEF derates) as a second lock and major downtime driver [REGULATOR]
- EPA guidance, Aug 12 2025, issued at Deere's request: temporary emissions overrides during repair are allowed.
- EPA guidance, Feb 2 2026: the Clean Air Act does not prohibit farmers from fixing their own nonroad diesel equipment, including temporary overrides.
- EPA sent information demands to 14 manufacturers about DEF failures, and plans a proposal to remove DEF derates on new engines. Model year 2027 on-road trucks must avoid sudden severe power loss.
- Sources: https://www.epa.gov/newsreleases/administrator-zeldin-takes-additional-measures-address-diesel-exhaust-fluid-def-issues ; https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2026-03/def-sensor-factsheet_0.pdf ; https://www.agweb.com/news/machinery/epa-backs-farmers-affirms-right-repair-equipment
- Reasoning: part of the "software lock" is regulatory compliance logic. Emissions inducement shuts machines down on a sensor fault. Unlocking it required the regulator, not knowledge.
- Industry counter-argument [OEM_VIEW]: open access enables tampering. ETF testimony cites an EPA estimate of 570,000 tons of excess NOx over a decade from diesel tampering, plus safety controls (braking, anti-rollover) and cybersecurity risks. Source: https://rilegislature.gov/Special/comdoc/House%20Corporations%202025/02-11-2025--H5159--Engine%20Technology%20Forum%20-%20Kara%20Gundel.pdf
- This is why inspection and authorization (C6) persist: someone must certify that emissions and safety functions were restored.

## 9. Contradicting / complicating evidence
- **Deere claims 98% of repairs can be done by customers** and only 2% need a dealer [OEM_VIEW]. Shareholder Green Century disputed this. Source: https://www.vice.com/en/article/john-deere-blows-off-shareholders-asking-about-right-to-repair/
  - If even roughly true by count, the restricted repairs are a minority by count but may be a large share by value and timeliness (ECU, engine, calibration on large tractors and combines).
  - The FTC counters that **uncertainty** about which DTCs need a dealer pulls many non-restricted repairs to dealers too.
- **Technician scarcity is a binding constraint independent of the lock.**
  - AED Foundation, 2020: dealers need up to 73,500 technician hires over 5 years.
  - AED 2016: technician job-opening rate of 11.34%, more than 3x the national average; about $2.4B/yr in lost revenue.
  - NAEDA, Jan 2024: each dealer would hire 3-5 mechanics immediately if it could.
  - Sources: https://www.enr.com/articles/48858-equipment-dealers-say-certified-technician-shortage-is-getting-worse ; https://www.forconstructionpros.com/business/news/12160580/aedf-report-skilled-worker-shortage-costs-equipment-industry-billions-each-year ; https://www.producer.com/?p=280575
  - The dealer complaint quoted by the FTC ("the more we enable the [IRPs] the more techs we will lose to start their own business") shows that hands, not knowledge, are the contested resource.
- **Parts sourcing delays in 2021-22 were supply-chain driven** (chips, steel, tires), not knowledge driven. Dealers expected 2-9 month delays, and farmers bought spares months ahead. Source: https://www.dtnpf.com/agriculture/web/ag/news/article/2021/11/05/farmers-mostly-managing-parts-delays
- **The lock is not technically deep.** At DEF CON 30 (2022), Sick Codes jailbroke a Deere 4240 display: weak encryption and checksums, root everywhere, dealer-authentication bypass. The barrier is legal and contractual (DMCA 1201, licenses, warranty) more than engineering. Source: https://www.theregister.com/2022/08/16/john_deere_doom
  - Implication: AI does not need to "know" anything new. The question is permission.
- **Voluntary MOUs (Deere-AFBF Jan 2023; CNH/Case IH/New Holland-AFBF Mar 9 2023)** had no enforcement mechanism and preserved IP and the bar on emissions and safety alteration. The FTC sued anyway two years later. Source: https://www.gfb.org/news/ag-news/post/afbf-signs-right-to-repair-mou-with-case-ih-and-new-holland
- **Colorado HB23-1011** (effective Jan 1 2024) was the first state ag right-to-repair law. I found no published post-implementation evaluation, so its impact is undocumented.
- **Deere's own predictive maintenance** (Connected Support / Expert Alerts, from 2016; AE50 award) uses telematics to predict failures and pre-stage parts and technicians.
  - This is the "AI diagnosis" thesis already in production, but owned by the OEM and dealer and reinforcing the dealer channel.
  - No published downtime-reduction percentage.
  - Source: https://www.equipmentworld.com/as-its-precision-ag-business-grows-deere-adds-predictive-maintenance-alerts/ ; https://hayandforage.com/article-1683-John-Deere-Connected-Supporttrade-ndash-Expert-Alerts-Earns-Ag-Engineering-Award.html
  - Data access, not inference capability, decides who captures that value.

## 10. Synthesis against the hypotheses
- **C1 (universal chain)**: mixed, mostly supports. The chain fits. But the case shows extra non-physical steps: authorization/entitlement, software pairing and calibration, and emissions re-enablement. Transport (moving a 20-ton combine 30-60 miles) is a separate step. In farming, the dominant cost is timeliness: downtime during a narrow window, not the repair itself.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly)**: supports, with a correction. The monopoly is mostly an *access/permission* monopoly (payload files, DTC dictionary, DTAC case base, emissions overrides), enforced by software and contract, and amplified by dealer consolidation and an OE-parts capture strategy. Part of it is genuine knowledge opacity: 94% of dealer-required DTCs do not say so, and Deere has no DTC dictionary.
- **C3 (AI erases the gap)**: contradicts. AI cannot produce Deere's signed payload files or reach its case database. The gap closed in 2025-26 through litigation, FTC orders and EPA guidance. AI could help with the DTC triage problem (which faults need a dealer) and with troubleshooting, but only once access exists.
- **C4 (10x cost drop in four steps)**: mixed.
  - Tool access price fell about 16x ($3,160 to $195/machine/yr).
  - The labor premium is about 30-45%. The OE-parts premium is real but redacted.
  - Downtime ($3,348/farm/yr, advocacy estimate) is the larger cost and is driven by technician scarcity and distance as well as the lock.
  - A 10x overall drop is not supported. A 1.5-3x cut in restricted repair cost plus large timeliness gains is plausible.
- **C5 (bespoke parts)**: silent to mildly contradicting. The parts issue in ag is not unavailability. About 500k OE parts exist, generics are cheaper and increasingly comparable, and Deere's lever is steering. Electronic parts need pairing and reprogramming, which a parts factory cannot provide without software access.
- **C6 (inspection persists and evolves)**: supports. Emissions and safety re-certification is the OEMs' central argument, and the settlement explicitly covers emissions inducement overrides under rules. EPA is redefining what counts as lawful repair.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient)**: supports for Deere specifically. Internal documents show deliberate design: priced to be "cost prohibitive," "Control the Wrench," "Capture the Service." But it was rebuilt incrementally through regulation, not torn down. Lower consolidation at Kubota and AGCO shows the structure is a choice, not a law of the industry.

## Where the money and time go
- Time is dominated by downtime during the planting and harvest windows: waiting for a dealer technician, hauling equipment, and uncertainty over whether a fault needs a dealer.
- Money comes from the dealer labor premium (about $59/hr, or $130-200 vs roughly $70-140) and the OE-parts premium.
- The steering of all service into dealers is driven by the parts motive (Win in Aftermarket).
- Advocacy estimate: $3B downtime plus $1.2B excess cost per year, about 70% of it downtime.
