## Findings

### 1. Aquant 2026 Field Service KPI Benchmark (largest dataset found)
Source: Aquant press post, "Aquant's 2026 Field Service Benchmark: Companies Can Unlock up to 26% in Service Cost Savings by Scaling Knowledge Across the Workforce"
https://www.aquant.ai/resources/post/aquants-2026-field-service-benchmark-companies-can-unlock-up-to-26-in-service-cost-savings-by-scaling-knowledge-across-the-workforce
- Scope: 161 service organizations, ~30 million service events, 7 million assets, $8.3B in service costs, 3 years of anonymized data. Savings are modeled, not measured.
- First-time fix (FTF): industry 77%; top performers 88%; bottom 60% (28-point spread).
- Mean time to resolution: industry 4.5 days; top 2.5 days; bottom 10 days.
- Failed visits as share of total service cost: 25% at median; 44% bottom; 14% top.
- Multi-visit cases: top teams resolve in under two weeks; bottom close to a month.
- ~1 in 5 cases could be resolved remotely; unnecessary truck rolls raise cost impact from 1.4% to 18.3%.
- Modeled savings: "typically in the low teens", up to 26% in some segments (23% previous year).
- Retention: 87% top vs 66% bottom.
- Skills gap (FTF difference between top technicians and everyone else inside an org): 2.9 points in best-in-class teams vs 10 points in underperformers.
- Quotes: "service excellence can't scale if expertise lives in a few heads." "Leaders make knowledge portable so more technicians perform like top technicians."
Kind: data (vendor).

Reasoning: The total addressable waste in this dataset is the failed-visit cost (25% of spend at median) plus unnecessary dispatch. Even moving every org to top-performer level (14% failed-visit cost) yields ~11-26% savings, not 90%. That is a strong quantitative counterpoint to C4's "order of magnitude" claim, at least for the dispatch/diagnosis layer measured by FSM data.

### 2. Aquant 2025 Industrial Machinery benchmark
Source: https://discover.aquant.ai/industrial-machinery-service-benchmarks
- 4.8M service events, $3.5B of costs.
- Leaders (top 20%): FTF 87%; time to resolve 2 days vs 8 days for bottom.
- Cost per service event: $688 leaders vs $5,706 bottom (8x).
- Days between visits: 123 (top) vs 48 (bottom).
- 25% of assets (bottom performers) fail within 3 months of a preventive maintenance visit.
- >$13M annual savings potential if every site performed at the median.
Kind: data (vendor).
Note: 8x cost-per-event spread between top and bottom quintile is an order of magnitude, but that is the spread between organizations; the average org's gap to best is far smaller. Interesting partial support for C4 at the tail (worst performers) only.

### 3. Aquant 2026 Industrial Equipment and Medical Device benchmarks (landing pages)
- Industrial equipment: https://discover.aquant.ai/industrial-equipment-field-service-benchmark-report : "18% of truck rolls are unnecessary"; failed visits = 52% of total service costs for lower performers; retention 84% vs 53%.
- Medical device: https://discover.aquant.ai/medical-device-field-service-benchmark-report : 20% of truck rolls avoidable; failed visits 44% (47% in another callout) of costs for lower performers; bottom performers could cut cost up to 23%.

### 4. FTF has been flat at ~75% for over a decade; failed first visit triggers 2.5 more visits and 20 days to resolve
Source: Field Service News, "What First Time Fix Rate Can't Tell You About Service Performance" (Aug 2022), citing Aquant 2022 Service Intelligence Benchmark (6M+ work orders, 75+ orgs).
https://www.fieldservicenews.com/what-first-time-fix-rate-cant-tell-you-about-service-performance/
- "FTF rates have held at about 75% for over a decade" (despite a decade of FSM software: ServiceMax, Salesforce FS, IFS, Dynamics, mobile apps, scheduling optimization).
- A failed first visit leads to 2.5 additional visits on average; mean time to resolution after a failed first visit: 20 days.
- Bottom workforce quartile costs 84% more than top quartile.
- Top 20% of technicians: 75% FTF; bottom 20%: 59% FTF (on a 30-day window).
- Short windows (7/14 days) overstate FTF; 30-day window recommended. Measurement gaming is common.
Kind: data (vendor) + practitioner_view.
Reasoning: The decade-long plateau is important. A wave of digital FSM tools (scheduling, mobility, knowledge bases) did not move the industry average. Either the bottleneck is not information availability (contradicts C2/C3), or the tools did not deliver knowledge at the point of work (Aquant's argument). Both readings are present; the plateau is a cautionary data point for C3.

### 6. Causes of repeat visits: consumer study (Trimble, UK, 2015)
Source: Field Service News, "Consumers say Fix It First Time" (Oct 2015)
https://www.fieldservicenews.com/consumers-say-fix-it-first-time/
- 2,000 UK adults; 25% needed a return visit.
- 40% of return visits: technician lacked the right tools or parts.
- 27% of return visits: technician lacked the right skills.
- 52% say first-visit fix is the leading satisfaction factor.
Kind: study (vendor-commissioned survey).
Reasoning: Parts/tools (logistics) outweigh skills (knowledge) as the stated cause. That splits C2: knowledge is a cause but not the largest one in this survey; parts availability is. AI diagnosis helps only to the extent it predicts the right part before dispatch.

### 8. Expertise concentration and retirement
Sources: https://www.aquant.ai/resources/post/the-expertise-gap-isnt-a-future-problem-its-the-problem ; https://www.aquant.ai/resources/post/training-has-become-continuous-most-service-organizations-havent-caught-up
- About half of field service technicians are over 50 (unsourced).
- 70% of service organizations expect retirement-driven knowledge loss within 5-10 years.
- 47% say acquiring quality technicians is their biggest challenge.
- "Roughly one in seven truck rolls is unnecessary" (wrong tech, part or diagnosis).
- Anecdote: VP of service: "We used to escalate two out of three of these calls"; AI "gets us 80% of the way there"; the last 20% still needs experts.
- Anecdote: new techs ready in 6 weeks vs 12 weeks with AI-assisted training.
Kind: practitioner_view / vendor.
Reasoning: Supports C2 (knowledge concentrated in few, aging heads). Partial support for C3: the most bullish vendor itself frames AI as getting "80% of the way" with experts retained for the hard tail, not "gap gone".

### 9. Salesforce State of Service, 7th edition (survey Apr-Jun 2025)
Source: https://www.salesforce.com/resources/research-reports/state-of-service/
- Mobile workers waste ~18% of hours (>7 h/week) on admin; 37% say admin keeps them from their jobs.
- 96% of field service teams plan to use AI for knowledge retrieval; 45% already use AI for AR-guided repairs, 43% more plan to.
- 85% expect AI investment to increase.
Kind: data (vendor survey).
Reasoning: A big chunk of field labor waste is admin/paperwork and scheduling, not diagnosis. AI hits this too, but it is a different layer from the knowledge monopoly framing.

### 10. "System, not technician" view
Source: Field Service News, "From Technician Blame to System Accountability" (Jul 2026)
https://www.fieldservicenews.com/from-technician-blame-to-system-accountability-rethinking-field-service-performance/
- FTF depends multiplicatively on technician capability x parts availability x information quality x customer readiness; one weak factor collapses the result.
- Technicians improvising around gaps hide logistics/data failures; spreadsheet reliance signals bad data for stocking decisions.
- Customers praise technicians but criticize pricing, logistics, appointment timing.
Kind: practitioner_view.
Reasoning: Contradicts a pure knowledge-monopoly story; supports C7 in the sense that the system (parts logistics, planning data, access) is the inefficiency, and C1 in showing a chain where each link matters.

## Synthesis: quantify waste
- Average FSM org: ~23-25% of first visits fail (FTF ~75-77%, flat for a decade).
- Each failure triggers ~2.5 more visits and ~20 days to resolution.
- Failed visits = ~25% of total service cost at median (14% best, 44-52% worst).
- 14-20% of truck rolls are unnecessary (remote-resolvable).
- Admin consumes ~18% of technician hours.
- Rough waste envelope for a median org: 25-35% of field service cost. Realistic recoverable (moving median to top-quintile): low teens to ~26% (Aquant modeled). An order-of-magnitude (90%) drop is not supported by this data for the average org; it only appears in the bottom-vs-top spread ($5,706 vs $688 per event, 8x).
- Causes: parts/tools (40% in consumer survey) > skills (27%); diagnosis error and knowledge gaps cited by vendors; system factors (parts logistics, data quality, customer access, scheduling) emphasised by practitioners.

## Stance reasoning
- C1: mixed/supports. FSM data reflects a chain (triage/remote resolution, dispatch, diagnose, parts, fix, verify), but the real chain has loops (2.5 extra visits) and a pre-dispatch triage step (remote resolution) that can collapse the chain entirely; also PM visits that don't prevent failure.
- C2: supports, partially. Knowledge concentration (top vs bottom tech gap, retirements) is real and drives cost; but parts availability is as large or larger a cause.
- C3: contradicts. Vendors say AI gets ~80% of the way; FTF flat 10+ years despite digital tools; physical parts and access remain.
- C4: contradicts for the average. Recoverable waste ~10-26%; only the worst performers show 8x gaps.
- C5: silent (only an FSN teaser that spare parts availability is a "critical risk factor" and points to AM).
- C6: mixed/supports weakly. Work verification before job closure is weak (ServicePower); 25% of assets fail within 3 months of PM visit.
- C7: mixed. The system is fragmented and multiplicative failures persist; but it is not "built to be inefficient": top quintile orgs in the same structure achieve 88% FTF and 8x lower cost, suggesting execution, not structure.
