# v05: Automotive repair data access: Massachusetts 2013 and 2020 telematics ballot laws, the 2014 national MOU, REPAIR Act; independent vs dealer share of repair; what is actually locked (data, tools, parts, calibrations)?

Research date: 2026-10-20. Sources come from web search plus fetches of a few primary pages. Much of what is below is trade press and advocacy, so each item is labelled as hard data or as opinion/advocacy.

## 1. Legal and regulatory timeline

### Massachusetts 2012/2013 law (Chapter 93K)
- 2012 ballot Question 1 (right to repair) passed with about 86%. The legislature then enacted a compromise in 2013. It required OEMs to sell independents the same service information and diagnostic tools that dealers get, on "fair and reasonable terms", and to move to a standardized, non-proprietary interface (J2534 / PC-based pass-through) by MY2018.
  Source: Wikipedia, 2020 Massachusetts Question 1 (background) https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Massachusetts_Question_1 ; ACCC/GPC history https://accc.gov.au/system/files/GPC%2520-%2520The%2520History%2520of%2520Right%2520to%2520Repair.pdf
- HARD DATA: 86% vote share in 2012.

### 2014 national MOU
- Signed January 15, 2014 by the Alliance of Automobile Manufacturers and Global Automakers on one side, and the aftermarket (Auto Care Association/CARE, plus the Coalition for Auto Repair Equality) on the other. The OEMs agreed to apply the Massachusetts requirements nationwide: service information and diagnostic tools for MY2002+, and a standardized pass-through interface by MY2018.
  Signed MOU PDF: https://etools.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/R2R-MOU-and-Agreement-SIGNED-1.15.14.pdf
- The MOU is VOLUNTARY and has no enforcement mechanism. In 2017 Auto Care/CARE wrote to 18 OEMs about MY2018 compliance and only 10 replied. Tesla was named specifically.
  https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2017/06/07/auto-care-association-tells-mass-panel-its-concerned-by-some-oems-r2r-silence-calls-out-tesla-specifically/
- The MOU did NOT cover wirelessly transmitted (telematics) data. That gap is why the 2020 ballot happened.
- The 2023 "Automotive Repair Data Sharing Commitment" (Alliance for Automotive Innovation with the Society of Collision Repair Specialists and the Automotive Service Association) reaffirmed the MOU and said OEMs won't use telematics to "circumvent" it. The Auto Care Association did not sign it.
  https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/history-repeats-itself-as-automakers-strike-deal-with-independent-repair-facilities-to-provide-access-to-telematics-data.html

### Massachusetts 2020 Question 1 (telematics)
- Passed with 74.97% (2,599,182 yes votes). Starting with MY2022, vehicles that use telematics must have an "inter-operable, standardized and open access platform" that lets owners and independent shops reach mechanical data, with the owner's consent.
- The campaign drew at least $43M in combined spending, the most ever for a Massachusetts ballot question (Boston Globe). The proponent committee raised at least $24M.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Massachusetts_Question_1 ; https://www.bostonglobe.com/2020/11/03/metro/voters-get-their-say-right-repair-ranked-choice-ballot-questions
- OEM response: Subaru disabled Starlink on MY2022 vehicles delivered in Massachusetts, and Kia disabled Kia Connect in January 2022. Both said compliance was "impossible" because no open platform exists. The AG argued that disabling telematics was itself a lawful way to comply. Consumers lost remote start and stolen-vehicle features (Boston Globe, 2023).
  https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2022/01/18/aai-ag-respond-to-new-evidence-in-mass-right-to-repair-suit-kia-joins-subaru-in-disabling-telematics/ ; https://www.bostonglobe.com/2023/06/15/business/subaru-buyers-take-hit-mass-right-to-repair-fight
- Litigation: in Alliance for Automotive Innovation v. Campbell, the trial was in June 2021 and decisions were delayed for years. NHTSA wrote letters warning of safety and cybersecurity conflict and then revised that guidance. AG enforcement began June 1, 2023. Judge Casper dismissed the remaining claims on February 11, 2025, finding no clear conflict with federal law. The Alliance filed its notice of appeal on March 14, 2025. The First Circuit heard argument on February 3, 2026 and urged mediation. The AG rebuffed compromise. As of about August 2026 there was no decision. Net result: the law has been on the books for six years and no open telematics platform is in use.
  https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/judge-denies-industry-challenge-to-massachusetts-data-access-law.html ; https://www.wbur.org/news/2025/02/12/massachusetts-right-to-repair-carr-law-challenges-dismissed ; https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/blogs/driving-forward-developments-in-transportation-law-and-innovation/all/first-circuit-appears-skeptical-about-aspects-of-mass-data-law-urges-negotiated-resolution ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/02/10/auto-innovators-massachusetts-ag-agree-to-consider-data-access-law-suit-settlement/

### Maine 2023
- The November 2023 ballot initiative (LD 1677) required an interoperable telematics access platform from January 5, 2025, and an AG-designated "independent entity" to set standards for it. No entity was designated by the effective date. In June 2025, LD 1228 replaced the entity with a commission that has no rulemaking authority, and per one report it removed the requirement to equip vehicles immediately. Auto Innovators sued in January 2025, and the case is still being pressed as of August 2026.
  https://www.nelsonmullins.com/insights/insights/amended-maine-right-to-repair-lawsuit-in-limbo-until-jan-2026 ; https://www.maine.gov/ag/docs/AG%20telematics%20notice%20final%20version%2001.02.25.pdf
- Lesson: a statutory right to data does not create the governance and trust infrastructure (standards body, credentialing, cybersecurity) needed to use it. That infrastructure is the missing layer.

### Federal REPAIR Act (H.R. 1566, 119th Congress)
- The bill gives owners and independents access to vehicle-generated data, tools and parts, and covers telematics. The House E&C subcommittee advanced it by voice vote on February 10, 2026. On May 21, 2026, the full committee passed the Motor Vehicle Modernization Act (H.R. 7389) 48-1 but EXCLUDED most REPAIR Act provisions. What it kept: codify the 2014 MOU, give the FTC enforcement power, and have the FTC study access. Insurer-backed provisions were dropped. NADA supports the narrower version, and Auto Care/MEMA wanted the full act. It may come back via the floor or surface transportation reauthorization.
  https://www.autocare.org/news/latest-news/details/2026/02/10/repair-act-(h.r.-1566)-advances-to-full-congressional-committee-hearing ; https://www.autobodynews.com/news/house-committee-advances-motor-vehicle-modernization-act-excluding-full-repair-act-provisions ; https://www.nada.org/legislative/oppose-repair-act-hr-1556s-1379 ; bill text https://www.congress.gov/119/bills/hr1566/BILLS-119hr1566ih.htm
- Note who backs it: insurers (State Farm, Allstate, via CAPS/Repair groups) and parts distributors (LKQ, AutoZone, etc.). Part of the fight is over who captures repair margin, not only over knowledge.

### FTC "Nixing the Fix" (May 2021)
- Regulator finding: "scant evidence to support manufacturer justifications for repair restrictions". There was no empirical evidence that independents misuse data more than authorized shops do. The report cited LKQ evidence that some OEMs (for example Volvo) restrict key parts to their authorized networks. Restrictions listed: parts unavailability, manuals, diagnostic software/tools, and disparagement of non-OEM parts. The FTC noted that autos are relatively successful at self-regulation thanks to the MOU, which partly contradicts the "structurally broken" framing.
  https://www.ftc.gov/node/80837 ; https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2021/05/12/ftc-promotes-lkq-abpa-safelite-collision-repair-arguments-in-report-to-congress/

### EU analog
- Regulation (EU) 2018/858 (with Delegated Reg. 2021/1244) obliges OEMs to give independent operators standardized repair and maintenance information (RMI) access. Security-related RMI (immobilizers, key programming and so on) goes through SERMI. To get it, an independent operator needs accreditation from a conformity assessment body under ISO/IEC 17020 and per-employee authorization, and access runs through a trust centre with certificates and tokens. National rollout has been phased since about 2023-2024 (Cofrac accreditation from March 1, 2024).
  https://www.hella-gutmann.com/en/sermi ; https://ilac.org/latest_ilac_news/sermi-scheme-a-european-accreditation-scheme-for-access-to-safety-information-for-vehicle-repair-and-maintenance
- Lesson: even with legal access, the EU had to build a credentialing and trust layer. The bottleneck shifts from knowledge to authorization and identity.

## 2. Who does the repair: independent vs dealer share
- IMR Automotive Research (January 2024), HARD DATA (consumer survey): the share of consumers using dealer service fell from 25.6% (2013) to 20.3% (2023). For vehicles 0-3 years old, 35.8% go to the dealer and only 7.2% to independent shops (11.1% to tire dealers).
  https://fleetmaintenance.com/in-the-bay/article/53082717/aftermarket-repair-shops-take-market-share-from-auto-dealerships
- Industry report cited by GoodCarBadCar: the dealer share of total vehicle service market dollars fell from 33% (2018) to 29%.
- AASA: dealers have 17% of aftermarket product volume and independents 57% (dollar/product basis, older).
- Marqstats (single source with opaque method, so treat as OPINION/estimate): independents hold about 70% of out-of-warranty ICE volume, but for EVs franchised dealers capture more than 75% of service and diagnostic spend. Even in the upside scenario, independents only reach parity (52%) by 2030. HV tooling for a 2-bay shop costs $30-70k against average independent revenue of about $312k. There are about 230k independent shops.
  https://marqstats.com/insights/us-ev-dealership-channel-inversion-independent-shops/
- NADA 2025: franchised dealers wrote more than 276M repair orders, with more than $164B in service and parts sales. Fixed ops were about 50% of dealer gross profit on about 12-13% of revenue (2019: 12.4% of sales, 50.5% of gross, per Mercer Capital on NADA data). This is why dealers lobby hard: service is their profit engine.
  https://www.nada.org/NADAData ; https://mercercapital.com/insights/auto-dealer-valuation-insights/2022/don-t-turn-a-blind-eye-to-fixed-operations/
- Auto Care 2027 Factbook: the US total aftermarket grew 3.9% in 2025, is forecast to reach about $599.7B in 2026 and $676.5B by 2029. The light-duty aftermarket was $413.7B in 2024.
  https://www.autocare.org/news/latest-news/details/2026/06/11/new-report--5.4--growth-expected-for-total-auto-care-industry-in-2026--reaching--676.5-billion-in-2029

Reading: independents ALREADY win most post-warranty ICE work even with incomplete data access. Dealer dominance is concentrated in (a) warranty-age vehicles, because warranty work is legally and contractually tied to dealers, and (b) EVs and new tech. Warranty tie-in and capital cost explain more of the dealer share than the knowledge gap does.

## 3. What is actually locked (layer by layer)

1. **Service information (manuals, wiring diagrams, TSBs)**: mostly AVAILABLE for a fee since the MOU. OEM portals cost money: Tesla $3,188/yr, Ford about $2,500/yr, GM about $1,200/yr (Detroit News via Carscoops, July 2026). Short-term subscriptions exist, and Kia is now free while Honda cut its rates (FenderBender). Aggregators (ALLDATA about $179-239/mo per bay, Mitchell1, Motor) repackage the information. A 2017 TechGroup profile found 44% of shops subscribe to OEM sites, averaging 3 sites at about $271/mo. Crowdsourced fix databases (Identifix Direct-Hit, built from 2M+ hotline calls with "millions of confirmed fixes") have existed for 20+ years. So the KNOWLEDGE layer is already largely commoditized and aggregated, well before AI.
   https://www.carscoops.com/2026/07/independent-repair-software-costs/ ; https://fenderbender.com/running-a-shop/operations/article/33022277/honda-reduces-subscription-rates-kia-now-free ; https://www.nxtbook.com/mercury/autocare/AutoCareInsiderVol111/index.php?startid=27 ; https://www.brakeandfrontend.com/solera-xtool-partner-on-diagnostic-scan-tools/
2. **Diagnostic and bidirectional tool access**: pass-through J2534 is available. But **secure gateway modules** (Stellantis 2018+, Nissan, and others) block code clearing and service functions unless the tool is authenticated through AutoAuth: about $50-60/yr per shop per brand, plus the scan-tool vendor subscription, plus technician identity. This is an AUTHORIZATION lock, not a knowledge lock.
   https://astech.com/autoauth/ ; https://autel.us/security-gateways/ ; https://www.hunter.com/adaslink-gateway
3. **Module programming and parts pairing (VIN-lock, component protection, immobilizer/keys)**: replacement modules and some parts must be coded to the VIN using OEM credentials. Security-related functions require NASTF Vehicle Security Professional registration in the US (SERMI in the EU). A New Jersey bill would force OEMs to provide part-pairing codes. Parts pairing is the cutting edge of the lock, the same pattern as Apple's iPhone parts pairing.
   https://www.theadanews.com/news/repair-roadblocks-congress-debating-access-to-data-for-some-auto-parts/article_629d717d-39bb-5d81-b7a0-c6337c242d8f.html ; https://accc.gov.au/system/files/AAAA%2520Supplementary%2520Submission%25203%2520summary%2520of%2520the%2520repair%2520and%2520service%2520information%2520that%2520is%2520withheld%2520-February%25202017.pdf
4. **Parts**: some OEMs restrict certain parts to authorized dealers (FTC/LKQ on Volvo). Captive collision parts are protected by design patents, which is the PARTS Act fight. Parts sourcing for mainstream cars is otherwise a mature, efficient distribution network (AutoZone, O'Reilly, LKQ, Genuine Parts deliver multiple times a day). That is evidence against "sourcing and delivering parts is broken" for automotive.
5. **ADAS calibration**: windshield replacements needing calibration rose 64% since MY2016 (AutoBolt). IIHS puts a simple windshield at about $250, while ADAS vehicles are much more likely to see glass claims over $1,000, and AAA estimates about $360 average for calibration on a windshield job alone. A calibration center costs $150-250k to set up. Many low-end shops skip calibration or customers refuse it. This is a CAPITAL, EQUIPMENT, FACILITY and VERIFICATION lock. The knowledge is in OEM position statements and is not the bottleneck.
   https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2023/04/03/autobolt-reports-64-increase-in-required-windshield-replacement-calibrations-since-my-2016/ ; https://www.wapcar.my/news/insurance-body-windscreen-replacement-for-cars-with-adas-costs-4x-more-improper-calibration-postrepair-a-concern-63380
6. **Telematics and remote data**: STILL LOCKED in practice everywhere, including Massachusetts after six years. This is the frontier for prognostics ("notice" and pre-diagnosis). OEMs monetize vehicle data, and the data is also a vehicle for lead generation to dealers.
7. **EV high-voltage systems**: warranty plus capital ($30-70k) plus training. Tesla is vertically integrated with no dealers.

### Quantified pain (advocacy surveys)
- Auto Care/Hanover survey (n=407, February 2024): 84% call data access their top business issue (ahead of technician shortage and inflation). 63% have difficulty with routine repairs daily or weekly. 51% send up to 5 vehicles/month to dealers. The estimated cost to independents is $3.1B/yr. A Babcox survey found 2-4 uncompensated labor hours per vehicle before referral. CAVEAT: commissioned by an advocacy group during a lobbying campaign.
  https://www.autocare.org/news/latest-news/details/2024/04/10/survey-84-of-independent-repair-shops-view-vehicle-data-access-as-top-issue-for-their-business
- Scale check: $3.1B is about 0.75% of the $414B light-duty aftermarket. The access lock is real but small compared with total repair spend. That cuts against "order of magnitude" savings from removing it.
- New Zealand analog: 48% of independents had turned away vehicles for lack of information, tools or data (Choice of Repairer group, 2026).

### Labor bottleneck (counter-evidence to a knowledge-only framing)
- TechForce: there are about 805,600 auto technicians in the US. There are about 70,865 annual openings against about 50,085 new supply, a gap of about 20.8k/yr. The collision pipeline fills about 42% of demand, with about 73k new collision technicians needed 2025-2029. Average fleet age is about 12.6-12.8 years.
  https://www.repairerdrivennews.com/2026/06/08/techforce-more-than-73000-collision-techs-needed-by-2029-retention-partially-to-blame/ ; https://techforce.org/sdmedia/
- Shops in the Auto Care survey still put data ABOVE technician shortage, but labor hours dominate the repair-order dollar.

### AI in auto diagnostics (2025-2026)
- Bosch is adding a conversational AI assistant to ESI[tronic], rolling out globally with release 2026/4. KPIT/Microsoft have the Trace2Fix copilot. Launch has PredictaFix. One anecdotal report claims a shop cut labor 25% and diagnostic time by up to 90% "in some cases". There are no controlled studies. NOTE: AI copilots sit ON TOP of OEM and aggregator data. They do not remove the gateway, pairing, calibration or telematics locks. Pieces such as "Why General AI falls short in the repair bay" argue that LLMs without vehicle-specific data and live access hallucinate.
  https://autosphere.ca/mechanical/2026/07/23/bosch-augments-esitronic-diagnostic-platform-with-conversational-ai-assistant/ ; https://www.webpronews.com/why-general-ai-falls-short-in-the-auto-repair-bay/

## 4. Reasoning vs the founder's hypotheses

- **C1 (universal chain)**: auto repair fits the chain. But "notice" is increasingly done by the car itself, through DTCs and telematics, and the OEM controls that channel. Calibration and verification is a distinct step after repair, before return to service. MIXED: the chain holds, but the locks don't map evenly onto steps.
- **C2 (knowledge monopoly)**: partially supported. OEMs DO control access, and surveys show real friction ($3.1B, 51% referring to dealers). But the 2013 law and 2014 MOU already made most service KNOWLEDGE purchasable. What remains locked is authorization (gateways, security functions), part pairing, telematics, capital equipment (ADAS, EV) and warranty tie-in. The monopoly is on PERMISSION and CREDENTIALS, not on understanding. MIXED.
- **C3 (AI closes the gap)**: CONTRADICTED for automotive. AI can digest manuals that are already available, but it cannot authenticate to a secure gateway, code a VIN-locked module, read locked telematics, or substitute for a $150k calibration bay. In addition, 13 years of laws have not unlocked telematics, because OEMs can comply by turning features off and can litigate for years.
- **C4 (order-of-magnitude cost drop in diagnose, understand, source, deliver)**: weak or contradictory. Parts sourcing and delivery in automotive is already highly efficient (same-day, multiple daily drops). The measurable data-access tax is about $3.1B on a market of about $414B or more. Diagnostic time savings from AI are plausible (anecdotes up to 90% on some jobs), but labor shortage and calibration capex dominate.
- **C5 (bespoke parts)**: largely silent here. Parts are patent and design protected (captive collision parts) and increasingly electronic and VIN-paired. A 3D-printed bracket doesn't solve a coded module. Legal (design patent) and electronic pairing barriers are the issue, not fabrication.
- **C6 (inspection and certification essential and evolving)**: SUPPORTED. ADAS calibration verification, SERMI/NASTF credentialing and AutoAuth are all "trust and verification" layers that are GROWING. Regulators keep building identity and accreditation schemes rather than removing gates.
- **C7 (built to be inefficient)**: MIXED. There is strong incentive misalignment: dealers earn about 50% of gross profit from fixed ops, and OEMs disable features rather than open data. But the FTC praised auto as relatively successful self-regulation, and independents already handle most post-warranty work. "Inefficiency" is better described as rent-seeking at specific choke points than as structural slop across the board.

## 5. Surprises
- Automakers chose to DISABLE telematics (Subaru, Kia) rather than open it, and the AG said that was fine. The "right" to data can mean that no one gets the data.
- Six years after a 75% vote and more than $43M spent, the Massachusetts telematics platform still doesn't exist, and the case is at the First Circuit.
- The House committee stripped the REPAIR Act down to "codify the 2014 MOU", which is close to the industry's preferred position.
- EVs are re-concentrating service at dealers (more than 75% of EV service spend, single source).
- The pain number most often cited by advocates ($3.1B) is under 1% of the aftermarket.

## 6. Company-shape implications
- A neutral, cybersecure "data access platform plus credentialing" layer: the thing Massachusetts and Maine required but nobody built. This is security tech, fits the YC deeptech/security brief, and is regulator-pulled. Risk: OEMs must cooperate and litigation is ongoing.
- An independent ADAS calibration and verification network or tooling: a mobile calibration plus a digital verification record that insurers accept. This sits on C6.
- An EV high-voltage service enablement kit for independents: tooling, training and a remote OEM-procedure copilot, attacking the $30-70k capex barrier.
- An AI diagnostic copilot for heavy fleets and independents, BUT only defensible with proprietary confirmed-fix data and telematics integration. Incumbents (Bosch, Solera/Identifix, Snap-on, Mitchell1) are shipping this now.
