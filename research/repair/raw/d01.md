## Findings

### 1. Generative AI at Work (Brynjolfsson, Li, Raymond, NBER w31161), the best causal analog [VERIFIED]
- URL: https://www.nber.org/papers/w31161
- 5,179 customer-support agents (the work is technical troubleshooting of software, not physical repair). With a GPT-based assistant, issues resolved per hour rose about 14% on average.
- Novice and low-skilled workers gained 34%. Experienced and highly skilled workers saw "minimal impact".
- The authors give suggestive evidence that the AI spreads the tacit practices of top workers to newer ones, so new workers move faster along the experience curve.
- Relevance: this is the strongest causal evidence that AI shrinks the knowledge gap between novices and experts, which is the mechanism behind C2/C3. The size of the effect is about +14% throughput, not 10x, and the work was purely informational with no parts or physical labor.

### 2. Siemens Industrial Copilot for maintenance (vendor claim) [VERIFIED]
- URL: https://press.siemens.com/global/en/pressrelease/siemens-expands-industrial-copilot-new-generative-ai-powered-maintenance-offering
- "First pilot use cases have shown that the Industrial Copilot for maintenance helps save on average 25% reactive maintenance time."
- The release does not say how many pilots there were, names no customers and gives no methodology. This is opinion or marketing from an OEM, not a study.
- Relevance: the headline industrial number is about 25% (1.33x), not an order of magnitude. Note also that the OEM, Siemens, is the one shipping the knowledge tool. Incumbents are using AI to deepen their hold on knowledge, which cuts against the claim that the monopoly dissolves (C2/C3).

### 3. Aquant (field-service AI vendor) benchmark claims [VERIFIED as claims; data not seen]
- URL: https://www.aquant.ai/blog/
- The headline of the 2026 Field Service Benchmark says companies "can unlock up to 26% in service cost savings" by spreading knowledge across the workforce. A blog headline says half of senior technicians are over 50; it gives no source.
- Relevance: a vendor framing the problem as a gap in knowledge distribution between top and bottom technicians supports C2. The savings ceiling is about 26%, not 10x. The retirement of tacit expertise is the practical form the "knowledge gap" takes.

### 4. Lufthansa Technik AVIATAR (aviation predictive maintenance platform) [VERIFIED page; no numbers]
- URL: https://www.lufthansa-technik.com/en/aviatar
- The page claims more reliable operations and lower costs but gives no quantified results. It notes that the latest aircraft types produce 50x more data than older generations.
- Relevance: one of the largest MROs publishes no public outcome metrics. Publicly checkable evidence on AI in aviation maintenance is thin.

### 5. Navigating the Jagged Technological Frontier (Dell'Acqua et al., HBS WP 24-013, 2023) [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- URL: https://www.hbs.edu/ris/Publication%20Files/24-013_d9b45b68-9e74-42d6-a1c6-c72fb70c7282.pdf (403 this session)
- 758 BCG consultants. On tasks inside the frontier, GPT-4 users completed about 12% more tasks, about 25% faster, at about 40% higher quality. Below-average performers gained about 43%, above-average about 17%.
- On a task outside the frontier, AI users were about 19 percentage points less likely to reach the correct answer.
- Relevance to diagnosis: when AI is confidently wrong, it degrades the human's judgment. Novel or rare failures (the long tail where OEM field teams get flown in) are exactly where models are weakest. This contradicts C3's "essentially gone".

### 6. Delta TechOps predictive maintenance [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- Delta reported cutting maintenance-related cancellations from about 5,600 a year (2010) to about 55 (2018). It credited predictive analytics on sensor data, together with process, parts-positioning and operational changes.
- Relevance: a real order-of-magnitude result in aviation. It came from noticing and pre-positioning (predict, then have the part and technician ready), not from cheaper diagnosis. It took most of a decade of data infrastructure and operational change. That supports C4's direction but points to the notice and logistics stages as the lever.

### 7. McKinsey and industry estimates for predictive maintenance [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- Commonly cited figures: predictive maintenance cuts machine downtime by 30-50% and maintenance costs by 10-40%. These are consultant estimates, not controlled studies.
- Relevance: even optimistic estimates fall short of 10x on total maintenance cost.

### 8. Enterprise genAI deployment failure rates [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- MIT NANDA, "The GenAI Divide" (Aug 2025): about 95% of enterprise genAI pilots showed no measurable P&L impact. Gartner (Jun 2025): over 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled by the end of 2027. Aquant's blog also cites the Gartner figure.
- Relevance: the gap between pilot and production is the norm. Integration, data access and workflow fit bind, not model capability.

### 9. FTC "Nixing the Fix" (2021) and right-to-repair [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- The FTC found little evidence to support manufacturers' repair restrictions. The restrictions it names are restricted access to diagnostic software and tools, service information, parts, and firmware locks or parts pairing.
- Relevance: the "knowledge monopoly" is largely a monopoly on access and rights (data, software, keys, parts), not on cognition. An LLM cannot unlock a parts-pairing lock or legally get proprietary fault-code meanings. This partly contradicts C3 even if AI reasoning is excellent.

### 10. FAA Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance (2024) and EASA AI Roadmap 2.0 (2023) [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE; FAA URL 403]
- URL: https://www.faa.gov/aircraft/air_cert/step/roadmap_for_AI_safety_assurance
- Both regulators treat AI as needing new assurance methods. They take an incremental, learning-assurance approach, starting with "assistance to humans" and keeping human authority over airworthiness decisions.
- Relevance: supports C6. In aviation, AI diagnosis can advise, but a certificated person or organization still signs the return to service. Regulators are building new assurance frameworks rather than removing inspection.

### 11. Technician shortage [PRIOR KNOWLEDGE]
- TechForce Foundation and BLS data show persistent shortfalls of automotive, diesel and collision technicians, with demand for new entrants in the hundreds of thousands over 5 years. Aviation MRO forecasts (e.g. Oliver Wyman) predict mechanic shortages through the 2020s.
- Relevance: the scarce input is skilled hands and time on the asset, alongside knowledge. Novice uplift (finding 1) matters most here, because it lets less experienced technicians do expert-level diagnosis.

## Synthesis
- Measured gains from AI diagnostic and knowledge assistants cluster at 14-34% productivity, about 25% less reactive maintenance time and about 26% lower service cost. None of the controlled or vendor-reported figures is an order of magnitude.
- Order-of-magnitude outcomes (Delta) came from predictive noticing combined with logistics and process, built over years. They did not come from a chatbot making diagnosis cheaper.
- Gains concentrate in novices. AI spreads expert tacit knowledge, which supports C2's diagnosis of the problem, but performance degrades on novel or out-of-distribution problems, which is the long tail.
- The "monopoly" is partly a matter of access and rights (FTC). AI does not dissolve those barriers.
- Inspection and sign-off stay human, and regulators are building AI assurance frameworks (C6).
- C5 (bespoke parts) and C1 (universal chain) are not addressed by this question's evidence.

## Stance notes
- C1 silent; C2 supports (novice-expert gap is real and bridgeable); C3 contradicts (gains are modest, there is a frontier failure mode, and access barriers remain); C4 contradicts or mixed (no 10x evidence on diagnosis; 10x only where noticing and logistics were rebuilt); C5 silent; C6 supports; C7 mixed.
