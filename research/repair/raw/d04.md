## Findings

### F1. Microsoft is killing Dynamics 365 Remote Assist and Guides (hard fact, regulator-grade: vendor lifecycle notice)
- Source: Microsoft Learn lifecycle announcement, "Dynamics 365 Guides and Remote Assist reaching end of support on December 31, 2026" (dated 2025-10-14, updated 2026-07-15). https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/dynamics-365-guides-remote-assist-end-of-support
- Text: "Dynamics 365 Guides and Dynamics 365 Remote Assist will reach end of support on December 31, 2026 ... subscriptions may be purchased or renewed until November 1, 2025 ... The apps will no longer be functional." Microsoft sends customers to the Marketplace for alternatives and to "spatial annotation" in Teams Mobile.
- Remote Assist mobile was deprecated March 25, 2025, and its functions moved into Teams mobile (Remote Assist overview page: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/mixed-reality/remote-assist/).
- [MEMORY, unverified this session] Microsoft stopped making HoloLens 2 in October 2024 and has no successor device.
- What it means: one of the three flagship products in this question is being discontinued. The headset-AR form of remote expert support did not become a big standalone product. What survives is plain video calling with annotation built into general tools (Teams, a phone camera). That counts against "AR remote assist is transformative" as a stand-alone business, but not against remote expert support as a practice.

### F10. Structure of the claims (my analysis)
- Every number found is vendor-reported. None is independent, comes from a regulator, or is peer-reviewed. The analyst recognitions (Frost, IDC, PAC) rank vendors and do not measure outcomes.
- The vendor numbers cluster at 15% (a named customer's measured travel-cost cut) and "up to 50%" fewer dispatches (unnamed, marketing). Nothing approaches an order of magnitude (90%).
- Where remote expert support works, it removes the specialist's second trip and repeat visits. It does not remove the first trip or the hands-on repair. Remote inspection does remove some of the inspector's travel (Microsoft's inspection use case, SGS on Librestream).
- The tools that stand alone (HoloLens Remote Assist) are being shut down, while the capability moves into generic video and chat plus AI (Teams spatial annotation, TeamViewer AI, ResolveGrid). Remote expert support is becoming a commodity feature. The hard parts are physical access, parts, and the authority to sign off.

## Stance reasoning
- C1 (universal chain): mixed. The Microsoft workflow confirms the chain (arrive, diagnose with a remote expert, repair, document). But remote support shows the steps overlapping and looping: diagnosis happens during the repair, and inspection happens remotely.
- C2 (knowledge monopoly): supports. The whole remote-expert category exists because front-line technicians lack the knowledge and scarce experts have to be flown in. Microsoft's pitch is "Scale expert knowledge." But the scarcity here is the OEM's or the firm's own experts, often inside the same company, not necessarily a monopoly used against owners.
- C3 (AI removes the gap): mixed, leaning contradicts. AI session counts are growing fast (TeamViewer ninefold), but no outcome data shows the gap is gone. The numbers are partial (15-50%), and the products still route to a human expert.
- C4 (order-of-magnitude cut): contradicts. The best claims are about 50% of dispatches avoided (vendor), and the measured named case is 15% of travel cost. These reach the diagnose/understand steps, not parts sourcing or delivery.
- C5 (AI parts factory): silent.
- C6 (inspection persists and must evolve): supports. Remote inspection is an explicit use case (Microsoft, SGS), and the Airbus gearbox inspection was 40% faster. Inspection is changing in form (remote witness, digital records) rather than going away.
- C7 (built to be inefficient): mixed. The evidence shows incremental, self-funded efficiency adoption by OEMs and service firms, which goes against "built to be inefficient". But the failure of standalone AR products suggests incumbents absorb the tools without restructuring.

## Where the money and time go (from this evidence)
In field service, a dispatch (truck roll), and especially a repeat dispatch or a flown-in specialist, is the expensive unit. Remote expert tools target it: vendors claim up to 50% of dispatches avoided, and the measured named case is 15% of travel cost. The cost the tools cannot touch is the physical presence needed to do the repair, plus waiting for parts.

## Bottleneck
Not knowledge transmission. Video plus annotation is now a free feature in Teams and a commodity at TeamViewer. The scarce layers are (a) diagnostic data captured from the asset itself, (b) a person with hands and tools on site, (c) parts availability, and (d) the authority to sign off and the liability for remote decisions. The AR headset layer failed commercially (HoloLens 2 and Remote Assist were discontinued).
