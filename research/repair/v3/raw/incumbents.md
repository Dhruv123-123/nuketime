# Bottom-up from incumbent revenues: US nuclear safety-related replacement parts and CGD

Date: 2026-10-21. Scope: what the incumbent dedicators and nuclear-parts OEMs earn, used to build a bottom-up estimate of the US safety-related replacement parts and dedication services market.

Legend:
- **(sourced)**: figure taken from a filing or press article, with the URL given.
- **(recalled)**: from memory and not verified this session.
- **(est)**: my own estimate or arithmetic.

---

## 1. Mirion / Paragon Energy Solutions (the best comparable)

This is the cleanest public datapoint on a pure-play US dedicator, safety-related parts supplier and obsolescence-solutions company.

- **Close date:** December 1, 2025. Mirion bought 100% of the membership interests of WCI-Gigawatt Intermediate Holdco, LLC, the indirect parent of Paragon Energy Solutions, LLC. (sourced: Mirion FY2025 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1809987/000162828026009869/mir-20251231.htm)
- **Consideration:** $588.4M gross, or $581.3M net of cash and net working capital adjustments. It was funded by the September 2025 Class A equity offering (17.3M shares at $21.35) plus convertible notes (about $316M net). (sourced: 10-K above; financing 8-K at https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1809987/000162828025043231/exhibit992-8xk.htm)
- **Headline price and financials:** The headline price was $585M. That is about 18x expected 2026 adjusted EBITDA, or about 14x after about $10M of synergies.
  - Paragon 2026E revenue: about $150M.
  - Adjusted EBITDA margin: 20–22%, which means about $30–33M of EBITDA.
  - EPS accretion: $0.02–0.03 in the first full year.
  - (sourced: Mirion/BusinessWire press release, 2025-09-23, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250923824037/en/ ; Dallas Innovates, https://dallasinnovates.com/fort-worths-nuclear-focused-paragon-energy-solutions-acquired-for-585m/)
- **Implied revenue multiple:** about 3.9x 2026E revenue. (est)
- **Seller:** Windjammer Capital Investors, which had bought Paragon in December 2021 (price undisclosed). Advisers: Baird (lead), Moelis, and Kirkland & Ellis. (sourced: Dallas Innovates)
- **Business:**
  - Safety-related products and components.
  - Commercial-grade dedication and qualification, plus testing and inspection.
  - "Value-add distribution through a proprietary platform."
  - More than 20,000 proprietary parts.
  - Systems "in 100% of nuclear reactors in North America."
  - The HIPS reactor protection platform, and neutron monitoring for NuScale.
  - Headcount: more than 100 engineers (Dallas Innovates); about 150 engineering and technician staff added (Mirion 10-K).
  - The 10-K frames Paragon as "proven solutions for obsolescence and parts availability challenges."
- **Lineage:** In February 2020 Paragon bought AZZ's Nuclear Logistics business (NLI, Fort Worth, more than 100 employees, a 200,000 sq ft facility). (sourced: AZZ 8-K EX-99.1, 2020-02-11, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8947/000000894720000036/azz-nlipr.htm)
- **Mirion context:**
  - FY2025 consolidated: revenue $925.4M, adjusted EBITDA $227.9M (24.6%).
  - Nuclear & Safety segment: revenue $614.6M (2024: $561.1M) and adjusted EBITDA $177.7M, about 28.9% (2024: $159.8M).
  - More than 80% of nuclear power end-market revenue comes from the installed base.
  - 2026 guidance: total revenue growth of 22–24%, organic growth of 5–7%, adjusted EBITDA of $285–300M. The growth includes full-year Paragon and Certrec.
  - (sourced: 10-K; Q4 release https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1809987/000162828026006844/a2026-02x10exhibit991.htm)

## 2. AZZ Nuclear Logistics Inc. (NLI): a cautionary datapoint

- **Purchase by AZZ:** AZZ bought NLI on June 1, 2012 for $80M plus a $20M earn-out, which closed at $77.0M net of cash plus $3.8M of notes paid off.
  - Expected first-year revenue: $70–80M.
  - Implied multiple: about 1.1–1.2x revenue. (sourced: AZZ 8-K EX-99.1 2012, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000008947/000000894712000021/ex991nlipressrelease.htm ; 8-K https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8947/000000894712000021/nliacquisition.htm)
- **Earn-out:** reversed in FY2015 because performance came in below plan. (sourced: AZZ XBRL R25, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8947/000000894715000035/R25.htm)
- **Sale:** sold to Paragon in February 2020 for net cash proceeds of $23.6M, with an $18.6M loss on disposal. AZZ said its sales and profit were "not significant" enough to report it as a discontinued operation. (sourced: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/8947/000000894720000045/R24.htm)
- **Takeaway:** a standalone dedication and qualification shop with $70–80M of revenue was worth only about $24M by 2020, during the fleet-shrinkage era. Windjammer then grew the combined Paragon to about $150M of revenue at 20%+ EBITDA and sold it for $585M. Valuations have re-rated sharply with the nuclear revival.

## 3. Westinghouse (parts business / New Stanton CGD)

- **CGD volume (company-published, figures inconsistent across pages):**
  - "CGD services for 100% of the US market and 75% of global utilities throughput of ~60,000 parts per year" (https://info.westinghousenuclear.com/parts-engineering).
  - "50,000+ parts each year" (data sheet, https://westinghousenuclear.com/data-sheet-library/commercial-grade-dedication/).
  - "over 100,000 individual items per year" (ANS sponsored article, Jan 2023, https://www.ans.org/news/article-4611/westinghouse-parts-business-wpb-overview-parts-quality-program-pqp/).
- **Capacity and staff:** more than 4,000 EPRI-compliant CGD instructions covering about 100,000 unique part numbers; more than 125 inspectors, technicians and engineers; a 100,000 sq ft facility in New Stanton, PA.
- **Fleet coverage and lead time (ANS article):**
  - Each unit has about 1,000–2,000 parts with elevated quality requirements.
  - Westinghouse has supplied about 20% of that population at PWR sites.
  - It claims an "8+ week schedule improvement" for PQP parts versus sourcing them otherwise.
- **Westinghouse as a whole:** Cameco's 49% share was revenue of C$3,458M in 2025 (2024: C$2,892M) and adjusted EBITDA of C$780M (2024: C$483M). 100% equivalent: about C$7.1B revenue (about US$5.1B) and about C$1.6B adjusted EBITDA (about 22.5%). (est) 2025 includes Dukovany new build. (sourced: Cameco 2025 results, https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260212233756/en/Cameco-Announces-2025-Results)
- **Business mix (recalled):** Cameco has guided that about 85% of Westinghouse's core revenue is long-term, recurring operating-plant work (fuel plus plant services). Parts are not broken out.
- **Parts business size (est):** US$500–900M globally, of which the US is about $350–600M. This rests on about 60,000 dedicated parts per year at roughly $3–10K average, plus OEM spare components (RCP parts, CRDM, I&C cards). Treat it as a wide-range estimate.

## 4. Curtiss-Wright (Naval & Power)

- **Naval & Power segment:** 2025 sales $1,503.0M (2024: $1,278.4M, +18%), operating income $231.3M (15.4% margin; 2024: 15.6%). (sourced: CW FY2025 10-K, https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/26324/000162828026007587/cw-20251231.htm)
- **Power & Process end market (company-wide):** 2025 $635.1M (2024: $540.8M, +17%). The growth came from the I&C Solutions acquisition and "higher organic sales of commercial nuclear products" (operating-reactor maintenance and advanced reactors). The 10-K says "the majority of our revenues in this market today support the maintenance of nuclear reactors across North America." Commercial nuclear orders also rose. (sourced: 10-K)
- **I&C Solutions (Ultra Energy):** bought for $200M cash, completed around January 2025. It comprises Ultra Nuclear Ltd and Weed Instrument (Round Rock, TX): about $65M of 2023 revenue, about 300 employees, and reactor protection, neutron monitoring and temperature/pressure sensors. Price was about 3.1x revenue. (sourced: https://pulse2.com/curtiss-wright-200-million-acquisition-of-ultra-energy-completed)
- **CW nuclear-qualification businesses (recalled):** Qualtech NP (CGD, EQ, seismic and reverse engineering in Huntsville and Cincinnati), Target Rock (safety relief valves), Scientech, and NuVision.
- **Commercial nuclear revenue (est):** about $450–500M, roughly 70–80% of Power & Process. The rest is process valves and industrial.
  - About $100M+ is new build: AP1000 reactor coolant pumps (RCPs) and the Vogtle tail.
  - The US operating-fleet aftermarket is about $250–350M. (est)
  - (recalled) CW investor materials have described commercial nuclear as roughly $400M+ with the majority in the aftermarket.

**Point estimate: about $2.0B a year** for US safety-related replacement parts plus dedication services. (est)

- **Third-party dedication and qualification services only** (the value-add layer, excluding OEM part content): about $300–600M. (est) This rests on dedication content being about 20–35% of the dedicated part's price, plus in-house utility labor.
- **Top-down cross-check:**
  - 94 US units × about $15–25M a year of safety-related material ≈ $1.4–2.4B. (est)
  - (recalled) NEI puts total US fleet generating cost at about $30/MWh × about 780 TWh ≈ $23–24B a year. Of that, capital is about $5/MWh, O&M about $16/MWh and fuel about $5–6/MWh.
  - If materials and parts are about 15% of O&M plus capital, they come to about $3–3.5B. If 50–60% of that is safety-related or augmented quality, the result is about $1.6–2.1B.
  - This is consistent with the bottom-up figure.

Pricing (recalled industry lore, unverified):
- A dedicated part typically sells for 2–10x the price of its commercial-grade equivalent.
- Dedication engineering per new part number (technical evaluation, critical characteristics, CGD plan) costs about $5–30K.
- Per-lot acceptance testing costs hundreds to thousands of dollars.
- Lead times run 8–26+ weeks; Westinghouse markets an 8+ week improvement as a differentiator.

Gross margins at dedicators are likely 35–50%, and EBITDA 15–25%. (est)

## 9. Implications for an AI-native dedicator

- The market is mid-sized, about $2B in the US. The pure dedication-services layer is about $0.3–0.6B, and incumbents earn about 20% EBITDA.
- The strongest proof of value is Paragon: $150M of revenue sold for $585M (18x EBITDA) in December 2025. Strategic acquirers (Mirion, Curtiss-Wright, BWXT, Westinghouse) are paying up for nuclear-qualified installed-base franchises.
- The cautionary datapoint is AZZ NLI. Pre-revival, a $70–80M dedication and qualification business was sold for $24M. Economics depend on fleet health and on owning a proprietary part library, not on generic dedication labor.
- Westinghouse's scale (about 60K parts a year and 4,000+ CGD plans) is the moat to beat. Speed (days versus 8–26 weeks) is the explicit marketing axis that incumbents already compete on.
