### Key takeaways from section 1
- Wellfield capex is the dominant sustaining capital line in Western ISR: $15.20/lb at Lost Creek; $178M at Dewey Burdock. KAP spends ~US$0.8bn/yr capex, mostly drilling & well construction.
- 2024–26 shortfalls had mixed causes: KAP = acid supply + wellfield development lag + regulatory paperwork (Inkai); Ur-Energy = staffing + flow; enCore = Texas permitting timing for new wellfields; Boss = rain/logistics + plant ramp. Pure "head grade/geology" misses are rarely the stated cause publicly — but wellfield flow/recovery underperformance shows up as "flow rates" and "wellfield development pace".

## 2. Software in ISR today

### Reactive-transport / wellfield simulation (incumbents are in-house or academic)
- Kazakhstan: Satbayev University / Joldasbekov Institute group "developed specialized software for Kazatomprom ... employed for modeling and simulation of uranium deposits at Inkai and Ortalyk". They note existing ISL software from Seversk (Russia), Orano's HYTEC (France), and Kazatomprom's own; PINN warm-starts cut solve time 2.8–7.1x. Kurmanseiit et al., Minerals, 2024-10-18 (https://www.mdpi.com/2075-163X/14/10/1043)
- Same group: ANN surrogate trained on 125 simulated cases from KAP lab configuration, ~99% accuracy vs reactive-transport sim, which "can take up to 20 h" per run. Aizhulov et al., Processes, 2024-10-18 (https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9717/12/10/2285)
- Orano/Mines ParisTech: HYTEC deployed industrially at KATCO (Tortkuduk, Chu-Sarysu) — "Industrial Deployment of Reactive Transport Simulation: An Application to Uranium In situ Recovery", Reviews in Mineralogy & Geochemistry v85, 2019 (https://dx.doi.org/10.2138/rmg.2019.85.16) and 3D large-scale well-field application at Tortkuduk, Hydrometallurgy 2022 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304386X22000585). Orano posted a 2023 postdoc for ML in ISR (SFdS listing, page down at time of fetch).
- Chinese academic stream (CNNC-linked, East China Univ. of Technology): ML-based optimal design of ISL process parameters (J. Hydrology 2023, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169423011769); deep-learning multiobjective wellfield design under uncertainty (J. Hydrology 2024, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169424019723); deep learning + clustering field-scale optimisation (Water Resources Research 2026, https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2025WR041741).
- KAP "Digital Mine" at Kazatomprom-Sauran (2016-05-30) — monitoring information system only; no quantified benefits published (https://www.kazatomprom.kz/en/media/view/kazatomprom-zapustil-cifrovoy-rudnik). KAP FY2025 results mention no digital programme (2026-03).

### Western ISR: consultancies + generic SCADA, no product company
- Hydrogeology/wellfield design is done by consultancies: Petrotek (Colorado; injection-well/ISR specialist; engaged by American Uranium, and on NRC/Ur-Energy filings) (https://petrotek.com/ ; https://mining.com.au/american-uranium-brings-on-petrotek/), plus WWC Engineering, Intera, BRS, etc., using MODFLOW/MT3D/PHREEQC-class open tools. Plant/wellfield control is standard PLC/SCADA (Rockwell/Allen-Bradley-class) integrated by the operator.
- I found NO venture-backed startup selling ISR wellfield optimisation/closed-loop control software, and no published Western closed-loop control trial with quantified NPV uplift. Search "uranium in-situ recovery AI startup wellfield software 2025 2026" returned only operator news (2026-10-01).
- Closest analog evidence: Kazakh/Chinese academic work shows simulation surrogates, but published value is computational speed (2.8–7.1x) and forecast accuracy, not $ uplift.

## 3. Downhole logging, disequilibrium, log-to-grade

- Standard practice: total-count gamma probes give eU3O8 (radiometric equivalent); in young roll-front (ISR) deposits uranium and its daughters are in disequilibrium, so gamma mis-states grade. PFN measures uranium directly.
- PFN commercial supply is tiny and concentrated:
  - Energy Fuels bought PFN tech from GeoInstruments Logging LLC for **US$500k** (4 PFN tools + 9 gamma tools + global exclusive rights), 2020-05-06 (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/energy-fuels-enters-into-agreement-to-acquire-prompt-fission-neutron-pfn-borehole-logging-technology-and-equipment-securing-control-over-this-critical-technology-for-us-isr-uranium-production-301053254.html)
  - enCore then bought it from Energy Fuels for **US$3.1M** (6 PFN probes, 9 gamma probes w/ resistivity+deviation, 2 logging trucks, IP incl. firmware/software, global exclusive licence), 2023-05-08. Rationale quote: "without accurate in-situ measurement of uranium, significant high-grade ore has been missed using traditional downhole techniques"; Alta Mesa produced 4.9 Mlb 2005–2013 on PFN-defined resources (https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/encore-energy-announces-acquisition-of-proprietary-pfn-technology-equipment-and-global-licensing-rights-301817638.html)
  - => The US PFN fleet is effectively one operator-owned asset (enCore). Others rely on gamma + core chemical assays + disequilibrium factors.
- Disequilibrium magnitude example: Alta Mesa historical resource applied a disequilibrium factor (DEF) of **1.13** (2007, Total); positive disequilibrium (eU3O8 > chemical U) shown in TRS Fig 8.2. Alta Mesa S-K 1300 TRS, eff. 2024-12-31, dated 2025-02-25 (https://encoreuranium.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Alta-Mesa-S-K-1300-Technical-Report-Summary_ROpt_2025.02.25.pdf). Calibration at DOE test pits (George West, TX) — infrastructure is decades old.
- Logging service providers: Century Wireline Services (Tulsa, OK; uranium/minerals gamma, resistivity, deviation logging) (https://www.centurywirelineservices.com/services/); DGI Geoscience (Canada; borehole geophysics for mining, Athabasca) (https://www.dgigeoscience.com/); operators frequently own trucks (enCore, UEC). Kazakhstan uses in-house geophysical services (KAP subsidiaries) and Russian-origin fission-neutron (KND) logging.
- ML log-to-grade: academic only. East China University of Technology: BP neural net on spectral gamma, 86.99% accuracy low-grade / 93.48% medium-high grade; permits logging at 6 m/min vs 2–4 m/min conventional. Nuclear Engineering & Technology, 2024-12-01 (https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S173857332400319X). I found no commercial ML log-to-grade product for uranium (2026-10-01). Adjacent mining-log ML vendors (e.g. core-imaging firms) do not specialise in disequilibrium.
- Wedge read: technically real pain (DEF errors of ~10–15%+ directly move reserve pounds), but buyer pool = the few dozen companies drilling roll-front U, and the hardware incumbent (PFN) was sold for $0.5M then $3.1M — sets a low ceiling on what "better grade logs" are worth as a standalone product, unless bundled with resource modelling.

## 4. Drill targeting & sequential drilling

- Evidence for value-of-information drilling:
  - "Intelligent Prospector v2.0" (Mern, Corso [Terra AI], Burch, House [KoBold], Caers [Stanford]), 2024: POMDP drill planning needed <half the boreholes of grid drilling; in demo ~9 vs ~36 holes to a go/no-go decision; methodology contributed to KoBold's 2023 Zambia (Mingomba) ultra-high-grade copper characterisation (https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10610)
  - Stanford Mineral-X: "Sequential efficacy of information for optimized geophysical and drilling strategies" (Li, Muir, Olivier, Yin, Caers), GJI 2026-05-25 — framework to choose sequences of geophysics + drill holes without an economic model (https://academic.oup.com/gji/article/246/1/ggag176/8692778). Mineral-X is industry-sponsored (https://mineralx.stanford.edu/)
  - Note: Terra AI (Corso) is a startup spun from this line of work — a direct competitor archetype for an "AI drill planning" pitch.
- Drilling costs:
  - Athabasca: IsoEnergy summer 2025 program 24 holes / 11,000 m / C$11M => ~C$1,000/m all-in, 2025-06-13 (https://www.cruxinvestor.com/posts/isoenergy-24-hole-summer-2025-program-in-athabasca-basin-expanding-uranium-exploration)
  - NexGen PCE: 35,366 m in 2025, 42,000 m planned 2026 (2026-01-15) (https://thedeepdive.ca/nexgen-launches-42000-metre-drill-program-at-pce-while-expanding-mineralized-footprint/) => ~C$35–45M/yr at C$1,000/m for one discovery.
  - Denison-operated Wheeler North JV 7,500 m 2026 (2026-03-20) (https://www.streetwisereports.com/article/2026/03/20/saskatchewan-uranium-drilling-heats-up-skyharbour-resources-wheeler-north-jv-targets-7-500-meters-in-the-athabasca-basin.html)
  - Texas ISR (Alta Mesa): **US$7,026 per hole** all-in (drilling $4,400, materials $969, cement $900, bits/underream $150, reclamation $300, washout $908); 433 holes 2023, 647 holes 2024; ~11,800 holes total on property. Wellfield capex in TRS $23.4M; opex $27.44/lb (Alta Mesa TRS, 2025-02-25, link above)
  - UEC rig count: 17 (WY) + 21 (TX) at FY-end Jul-2026 (UEC FY2026 results, link above); enCore 22 rigs (Mar-2025).
- Global uranium exploration + mine development spend: US$380M (2020) -> US$800M (2022) -> US$840M (2023 prelim) — Red Book via WNN 2025-04-08 (https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/invest-now-to-secure-long-term-uranium-future-red-book-says); 2023–2024 combined US$1.78bn (+46% vs 2021–22) — Red Book 2026 via ANS 2026-09-15 (https://www.ans.org/news/2026-09-15/article-8391/newest-red-book-notes-the-need-for-more-u-investment/)
- Read: drilling is the single biggest discretionary spend for juniors and for ISR producers (delineation + production holes). A 20% reduction in delineation holes at Alta Mesa-scale (~650 holes x $7k = ~$4.5M/yr) = ~$0.9M/yr saved — small. In Athabasca a 20% cut on a 40,000 m program = ~C$8M — larger, but buyers are juniors with episodic, financing-dependent budgets.

## 5. Uranium recovery permitting

Jurisdictions: NRC licenses ISR directly in Nebraska, South Dakota, New Mexico (pending), etc.; Agreement States license themselves — Texas (TCEQ radioactive material licence + mine permit + production area authorisation + EPA aquifer exemption concurrence), Wyoming (became the uranium-recovery Agreement State in 2018; WDEQ Uranium Recovery Program + LQD permit to mine + EPA aquifer exemption), Colorado, Utah. EPA UIC Class III permits are needed where EPA retains primacy (e.g. South Dakota).

Evidence on timelines:
- Texas — Goliad (UEC): mine permit application 2007-10-24 -> contested-case hearing granted 2009-02-25 -> mine permit + PAA 2010-12-14 -> RML 2011-12-20 -> EPA aquifer exemption concurrence 2012-12-05 (EPA first declined 2012-05-16): **~5.1 years to full authorisation; still not producing in 2026**; renewal preliminary hearing 2026-02-05 — WISE Uranium Texas page (https://www.wise-uranium.org/upusatx.html); TCEQ hearing notice 2026-02-05 (https://www.tceq.texas.gov/downloads/agency/decisions/hearings/notices/2026/2026-02-05-uranium-energy-corp-ur03075-ph.pdf)
- Texas — Burke Hollow (UEC): disposal-well permits 2015-07-15; mine area permit 2016-12-13; EPA aquifer exemption 2017-04-05; RML 2019-02-20; **production 2026-04-08** (~4 yrs permitting, ~11 yrs to production). Same WISE source.
- Texas — Palangana: RML 2010-01-20 -> wellfield dev 2010-06 -> production 2010-11-17 (fast when brownfield). Same source.
- Texas — enCore Q1 2026: drummed output fell 32% QoQ because "timing of permitting in Texas for administrative reasons" delayed new wellfields (Holdco Markets 2026). => even PAA-level (production area) approvals for incremental wellfields gate output.
- Wyoming — Lost Creek expansion (6 more mine units, HJ/KM horizons): WDEQ URP licence amendment 2021 -> WDEQ LQD approval late Apr-2025 -> EPA aquifer exemption 2025-05-01: **~4 years** — Ur-Energy Q1-2025 release (https://www.ur-energy.com/news-media/press-releases/detail/381/ur-energy-releases-2025-q1-results-and-announces-receipt-of); ANS 2025-05-12 (https://www.ans.org/news/2025-05-12/article-7016/epa-wyoming-approve-future-expansion-of-urenergys-lost-creek-mine/)
- NRC/EPA — Dewey Burdock (SD): NRC licence Apr-2014 (challenged/appealed); EPA UIC Class III/V Nov-2020, under appeal; "permitting completion" forecast Q3 2026 (TRS eff. 2024-10-08, link in §1). Effectively >15 years from first application (~2009).
- Cost proxies: NRC FY2026 professional hourly rate **$337/hr** (ANS 2026-06-25, https://www.ans.org/news/article-8148/nrc-news-fy-2026-fees-finalized-facility-licensing-reviews-launched/). NRC proposed ISR groundwater rule (Criterion 14) published 2026-09-10, comments to 2026-10-13; estimated savings per ISR application **$288,600 industry + $316,200 NRC** (https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/09/10/2026-18504/in-situ-recovery-monitoring-and-decommissioning-timeliness). Implies per-application regulatory cost is in the low millions including consultants; I did not find a published total.
- Policy tailwind: EO 14300 (May 2025) ordering NRC reform & fixed deadlines (https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/05/ordering-the-reform-of-the-nuclear-regulatory-commission/); NRC claims ~40% schedule and 35% staff-hour reductions in 2025 (NRC 2025-12-29, https://www.nrc.gov/sites/default/files/cdn/doc-collection-news/2025/25-071.pdf) — mostly reactor/fuel facility reviews, not uranium recovery.
- AI in permitting: PNNL PermitAI / NEPATEC 2.0 (2025-08-21): ~120,000 NEPA documents from 60,000 projects made machine-readable; DOE/DOI/USACE users; "minutes instead of months" for data access (https://www.pnnl.gov/news-media/permitai-ushers-new-era-faster-better-federal-permitting). Open-source MCP toolkit (https://github.com/pnnl/nepa-mcp). No uranium-recovery-specific permitting software product found. Incumbent = environmental consultancies (Petrotek, WWC, Inter-Mountain Labs, etc.) billed hourly.
- Read: the binding delays are state contested-case hearings, EPA aquifer-exemption concurrence and litigation — political/legal, not document-drafting throughput. Software can shave consultant hours and RAI cycles, but cannot remove the hearing calendar.

Observations:
1. The biggest dollar pool tied to *geology-in-the-loop* decisions is ISR wellfield development capex (tens of $M per Western operation over life; ~US$0.8bn/yr at KAP), and it is where 2025–26 operational misses actually occurred (Peninsula flow/gassing; Ur-Energy flow; KAP wellfield development lag; enCore new-wellfield cadence).
2. A founder with ML-for-uranium-prospecting already has the core asset for "where to drill the next production pattern and at what spacing" — that is the bridge from exploration ML into ISR operations: delineation-drilling optimisation -> pattern placement -> flow/recovery forecasting -> closed-loop injection/extraction balancing.
3. Upstream (pure prospecting/drill targeting) is now crowded: Terranox AI (YC W26, uranium-specific), Terra AI ($20M, Khosla/BHP), KoBold. YC will likely ask "why not Terranox?".
4. Log-to-grade is a useful *feature* (and data moat: pairs of gamma/PFN/assay) but a thin standalone business.
5. Permitting AI is a weak standalone wedge in uranium: small buyer count; time lost is legal/political.
