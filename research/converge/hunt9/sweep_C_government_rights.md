# Hunt 9, Sweep C: government, defense and regulatory rights held by small or dormant firms

Method: ~40 WebSearch calls plus ~45 WebFetch reads (the shared search budget ran out near the end; the last few checks were done by fetching URLs already surfaced). Numbers are quoted with sources; "(my estimate)" marks inference. No transactions are invented; where a price is redacted or undisclosed I say so.

Summary verdict for the cluster: the pools are large (DLA alone manages ~4M NSNs and bought $7.9B of sole-source spares on 136 large contracts in FY15-19; ~37% of all US generic approvals never launch; DoD Phase III is >$2B/yr), but almost every proving step here is a *regulatory decision* whose predictors the regulator already publishes in a legible field (AMSC code, Orange Book status, Phase III coding). The one place where the unproven right is genuinely orphaned and un-indexed is the technical-data/tooling of defunct sole-source part makers, which must be found by joining FLIS CAGE codes to SAM expiry, bankruptcy dockets and image-only drawings. That is my top candidate (6/10). Nothing in this cluster reaches Terranox's 10^4 step-up; the best R1 values are 5-20x (my estimate) on small dollars.

## 1. Orphaned-source NSNs: buy the drawings/tooling of defunct sole-source part makers, get re-approved by DLA (NEW) — fit 6/10

**Proving step.** A Source Approval Request (SAR) plus first-article test to become an approved source for an NSN coded AMSC B/C/D/P/R (source-controlled, no usable government data). Vendor pays for reverse engineering, the test article, tooling and lab work; DLA cannot fund drawings ([DLA SAR guide via GovPath](https://govpathstrategies.com/source-approval-request-sar-guide); [NDIA 2012 DLA L&M SAR deck](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/supply/Source_Approval_Request.pdf)). Time: DLA review ≥90-180 days then military ESA review ≥180 days, "toward a year"; DLA's own target chain sums to ~190 days. Hit rate: DLA Land & Maritime baseline Aug 2009-Oct 2011: 363 SARs received, 10.2% approved, "less than 10%" of contractor packages even accepted for ESA review; top rejection reasons: not economically feasible 21%, OEM data missing 17%, unapproved drawing 15%, no test plan 14%, drawing missing 10% ([NDIA 2012](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2012/supply/Source_Approval_Request.pdf)). FY17 (NDIA 2018 briefing): 282 received, 111 approved, 114 rejected, 144 pending, i.e. ~49% of decided packages, ~39% of received ([NDIA 2018 SAR briefing](https://ndia.dtic.mil/wp-content/uploads/2018/dla/SARBriefing_SC.pdf)). So p ≈ 0.1-0.5 depending on package quality, lever cap 2-10x. Cost per attempt: not published; $25k-250k per NSN (my estimate from the package contents: RE, first article, AS9100 QMS). GAO counted >1,600 reverse-engineering projects FY15-18 (1,000 DLA-funded, 600 contractor-funded) across 124 contractors, 103 of them small ([GAO-19-586](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-19-586-highlights.pdf)).

**Signal.** Legible parts: DIBBS solicitations and procurement history, FLIS/PUB LOG (NSN, AMSC code, approved CAGE codes, annual demand), FPDS, SAM (whether a CAGE is active or expired). Illegible parts: the drawings in cFolders are image files that need DLA's IADS viewer and JCP certification (DD 2345) to open, export-controlled for Distribution C/D ([DLA cFolders review](https://www.dla.mil:443/portals/104/Images/Aviation/AviationEngineering/Engineering/cFolders-Review-(20210310).pdf)); the ownership trail of a dead supplier's IP sits in PACER bankruptcy dockets, UCC filings and state corporate records (PDF). The AMSC code already tells you an item is sole-source (that is a lookup table, no edge). The edge, if any, is the join: NSNs with sustained demand whose only approved CAGE is dead or dying (SAM expired, no bids on DIBBS) and whose drawings/tooling can be located and bought. Nobody publishes that list; DLA's RPPOB library has only ~200 items with the $40k annual-demand threshold, approving 65 items in 2016 and 43 in 2017; DoD owns data for only about half of them ([DLA news 2019](https://www.dla.mil/About-DLA/News/News-Article-View/Article/1771709/new-sources-faster-parts-for-potential-dla-suppliers/)).

**Unproven right.** The technical data package, tooling and (where it exists) the approved-source status of a defunct or retiring small manufacturer. Bought from estates, bankruptcy trustees, retiring owners. Price: not systematically disclosed; the aviation analog is True Flight paying $925,000 at auction for the whole former-Grumman light-aircraft type certificates, tooling and equipment in 2007 ("I thought it was a bargain") ([AvWeb](https://avweb.com/?p=102251)). Exclusive (you own the drawings and tooling), not auctioned by a regulator, transferable. Sellers (trustees, heirs) cannot read FLIS demand data.

**Proven state.** An approved source on an NSN with recurring DLA demand, which is the asset class TransDigm, HEICO and Ontic are built on. TransDigm: ≥88 acquisitions since 1993, business model "highly engineered, sole-source spare parts"; DoD IG found ≥$20.8M excess profit on 105 of 107 parts (150 contracts, 2017-19), >95% of contracts below the TINA threshold ([DoD IG](https://www.dodig.mil/reports.html/Article/2871623/audit-of-the-business-model-for-transdigm-group-inc-and-its-impact-on-departmen/); [Warren letter](https://warren.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/final_-_warren_garamendi_pocan_letter_to_dod_ftc_doj_re_transdigms_recent_acquisitions.pdf)). Ontic (licensed manufacture of legacy OEM parts) sold to CVC for $1.365B in 2019 ([CVC](https://www.cvc.com/media/news/2019/2019-07-30-proposed-sale-of-ontic-for-1-365-million-to-cvc-fund-vll/)). HEICO: >20,000 PMA parts, 500+ new approvals a year, >100 acquisitions, Wencor for $2B in 2023 ([Expanse Stocks](https://expansestocks.substack.com/p/deep-dive-brief-heico)). R1: unknown; 5-20x on IP bought at scrap value (my estimate). The proven asset is tradable before "building the mine" only partly: approved-source status attaches to a CAGE/plant, so the buyer must either manufacture or license to a qualified maker (Ontic's model is exactly that licence).

**Market ceiling.** DLA ≈ 4M consumable NSNs, ~80% of DoD spare-parts buying; 32% of large spare-parts contracts (136 of 426, $7.9B) were sole-source FY15-19, 50% after mid-2018 ([GAO-21-388](https://www.gao.gov/assets/d21388.pdf)). Incumbents: TransDigm, HEICO/Wencor, Ontic/CVC, Chromalloy, PE roll-ups; DLA's own RE program competes (it funded 1,000 projects). **Kill test:** pull FLIS for AMSC B/C/P/R NSNs with ≥$40k annual demand; join approved CAGEs to SAM; count NSNs whose sole approved CAGE is expired/deactivated and whose DIBBS history shows unfilled solicitations. If <2,000 NSNs or <$200M/yr demand, or if DLA's RPPOB/limited-source lists already enumerate them, kill. **Biggest doubt:** the proven state is an operating business, not a paper option; the step-up per NSN is small dollars, so this is a roll-up, not a Terranox.

## 2. Approved-but-unlaunched / discontinued ANDAs (NEW) — fit 5/10

**Proving step.** Reactivating a dormant generic approval: site transfer or new testing site, prior-approval supplement, re-validation, relaunch. The Padagis cyclosporine case shows the gating item: it could not re-release the product without FDA approval of a supplement adding a qualified related-substances testing facility; the 2022 supplement was withdrawn in 2023 ([Padagis letter to FDA](https://fda.gov/media/175548/download)). Cost $0.5-3M and 12-30 months per ANDA (my estimate); hit rate unknown (CMC supplements mostly approve, but commercial success is the real failure mode; 70% of generics take >4 years to reach market, [Lachman/IQVIA 2025](https://www.lachmanconsultants.com/2025/07/generic-approvals-vs-launches-iqiva-sheds-light-on-a-problem/)).

**Signal.** Orange Book discontinued list, FDA shortage list, approval letters, 483s and warning letters (PDF), NDC directory, state Medicaid utilization (legible), IQVIA sales (paid). The regulator's "discontinued"/"RLD"/"shortage" fields are legible; the illegible part is the inspection history and the CMC content of the file, which the buyer only sees in diligence. Edge is thin.

**Unproven right.** An ANDA held by a firm that will not launch. Pool: 37% of generics approved 2013-Q1 2024 had not launched ([Lachman/IQVIA](https://www.lachmanconsultants.com/2025/07/generic-approvals-vs-launches-iqiva-sheds-light-on-a-problem/)); of 735 ANDAs approved in 2018 only 261 had launched by December 2018 ([IQVIA via CfB](https://centerforbiosimilars.com/view/iqvia-data-show-anda-approvals-on-the-rise-biosimilars-fighting-for-market-share)); >700 of ~1,600 approvals in 2017-18 (43%) were not on the market, 157 of them injectables ([KFF Health News](https://kffhealthnews.org/news/trump-administration-salutes-parade-of-generic-drug-approvals-but-hundreds-arent-for-sale/)); 62% of shortage drugs have approved generics and 84% of those have ≥1 unlaunched product. Order of magnitude 3,000 unlaunched ANDAs (my estimate). ANDAs are transferable by letter; prices mostly undisclosed. Comparable buyer: ANI Pharmaceuticals bought 4 ANDAs from Oakrum (2022), 2 ANDAs + 1 pipeline product from the Akorn Chapter 7 trustee, 1 from Slayback, 1 from PAI (2023), and Novitium for $163.5M; it pursues CGT 180-day exclusivity on low-competition products and had 116 commercial products at end-2023 ([ANI 10-K FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1023024/000102302425000015/anip-20241231.htm); [PrivSource](https://www.privsource.com/acquisitions/deal/ani-pharmaceuticals-acquires-four-andas-from-oakrum-pharma-llc-D8SkJj)).

**Proven state.** A marketed generic in a 1-3 player market, bought by ANI, Amneal, Padagis, Hikma, PE platforms. R1 unknown; a dormant ANDA at $0.1-2M vs. a relaunched product in a thin market at $5-50M gives 5-25x (my estimate, no comps found). EU equivalent: the sunset clause lapses an MA after 3 years unmarketed; the Czech SUKL publishes an Excel list of MAs "jeopardised by the sunset clause" ([SUKL](https://sukl.gov.cz/en/pharmaceutical-industry/medicines/marketing-authorisation-of-medicines/sunset-clause-2/list-of-medicines-jeopardised-by-sunset-clause/); [Taylor Wessing](https://microsites.taylorwessing.com/synapse/regulatory_sunsetclause.html)), i.e. the regulator itself lists the dormant rights (legible).

**Ceiling.** US generics ~$100B+ at invoice; thousands of dormant files. Incumbents: ANI (explicit model), Elite, Lannett, Civica, Padagis, Cosette. **Kill test:** take the 2018 cohort (474 unlaunched at Dec 2018); in today's Orange Book count how many later launched, how many changed applicant name (a transfer), and at what disclosed prices in 10-Ks; if transfers <50/yr or the regulator's fields predict relaunch as well as any model, kill. **Biggest doubt:** the hit rate is commercial, not regulatory; the base rate of "launch after dormancy" may be high enough that the lever cap is ~2-3x, and incumbents already read the same tables.

## 3. SBIR lineage: buying dormant Phase II firms for Phase III sole-source eligibility (SEEN; new numbers only) — fit 5/10

New numbers: Navy FY23 Phase III obligations $1,200,696,845 across 440 contracting actions to 158 small businesses; FY22 also >$1B ([DON FY23 Year in Review](https://navysbir.com/docs/DON-FY-23-SBIR-Year_in_Review.pdf)). DoD-wide: >$10B of identified Phase III over four years; FY22: 2,153 Phase III contracts >$250K vs 1,590 Phase II; FY21: 1,733 vs 1,264; >600 small businesses got a Phase III in 2022 ([SBTC 2023](https://sbtc.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/07/SBTC-White-Paper-Unleashing-DODs-Small-Business-Technology-Potential.pdf)). NPS: cumulative DoD Phase III to SBIR firms $10.28B; 748 of 4,703 SBIR companies (16%) have ≥1 Phase III; Progeny Systems alone $875M ([NPS SYM-AM-23-162](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/4929/1/SYM-AM-23-162.pdf)). Incumetrics 2025: about 6% of all SBIR companies have Phase III dollars exceeding SBIR funding; 2,120 SBIR firms acquired by 2024 (L3 43; GE, SAIC, BAE, Lockheed, Raytheon, GD, Philips, Teledyne ≥10 each); after acquisition, follow-on contracts are "often not certified" Phase III and become invisible in FPDS ([Incumetrics](https://sbtc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Incumetrics-Strategic-Options-for-the-DOD-SBIR-Program.pdf)). TechLink: 16,959 Phase II contracts to 4,412 firms (1995-2018), 58% produced sales, 39% zero sales, 496 firms acquired "primarily because of" the SBIR tech for ≥$35.6B disclosed, 172 firms (3.9%) ceased to exist or untraceable ([DoD SBIR economic impact study](https://media.defense.gov/2022/Mar/10/2002954100/-1/-1/0/DOD%20SBIR-STTR%20ECONOMIC%20IMPACT%20STUDY.PDF)). NASA: ~75 Phase III/yr, ~$56M/yr, Phase III count ≈ half of Phase II ([Payload](https://payloadspace.com/inside-nasas-sbir-phase-iii-program-by-the-numbers-payload-research/)). Eligibility: GAO holds the Phase III awardee must have performed, or been novated, the prior Phase I/II award, so a stock purchase or novation preserves the right.

Do primes price lineage? For live firms yes: 2,120 acquisitions, primes the top acquirers. For dormant firms: no evidence either way (no acquisition of a dormant Phase II firm producing a Phase III was found). Dormant pool: ~6,600 zero-sales Phase II contracts; 3.9% of firms defunct; "dormant but alive" unknown. New angle on the signal: Phase III demand is decided by program offices whose intentions sit in RDT&E/procurement justification books (thousands of PDF pages per year) that could be NLP-joined to SBIR topics; nobody does that join. **Kill test:** from SBIR.gov take Phase II awardees 2010-18 with no award since 2019 and expired SAM; search FPDS for later Phase III-coded awards on their topics; if zero, the step is a sales process, not a predictable proving step. **Doubt:** hit rate (6-16%) gives a 6-16x cap but the step is "a program office wants it", p is not revealed by public data, and the right decays (SBIR data-rights protection is 20 years from award).

## 4. Orphaned FAA type certificates / STCs (NEW) — fit 3/10

Proving step: reviving production or obtaining PMA/STC parts approval for an unsupported fleet; STC ≈ 9-12 months ([AviationPros](https://www.aviationpros.com/aircraft-maintenance-technology/engines-parts/aircraft-airframe-accessories/article/11150520/the-complexity-of-pma-and-stc-approvals)); PMA by test-and-computation 9-18 months, identicality "months" ([Prime Propulsion](https://www.primepropulsion.com/post/faa-pma-certification-timeline-delays)); costs not published (MARPA "not collecting any numbers"). Signal: FAA TCDS, STC database, registry (fleet counts), ADs, SDRs, all legible except the engineering data itself. Unproven right: a TC/STC held by a dormant company; True Flight paid $925,000 for the Grumman-line TCs plus tooling in 2007; EASA moved to revoke the Aero Commander 100 TC in 2021 because the holder stopped paying the annual fee ([EASA CI 2021-24](https://WWW.EASA.EUROPA.EU/sites/default/files/dfu/easa_certification_information_2021-24_notice_of_intent_to_revoke_easa_tc_for_the_aero_commander_100_aircraft_model_faa_tc_no._us_1a21_issued_to_dynac_aerospace_corporation.pdf)). Important structural fact: under 49 U.S.C. 44704(a)(5) the FAA now declares certificates abandoned and releases the engineering data to anyone who asks (42 STCs currently listed: New Systems 30, Javelin 11, Aero Enhancements 1; deadlines Aug 2026-Jan 2027) ([FAA abandoned TC page](https://www.faa.gov/about/office_org/headquarters_offices/avs/offices/air/abandoned_tc); [FR 2026-03046](https://policyrisk.com/federal-register/2026-03046)). So once abandoned the right is non-exclusive (fails the brief); the only exclusive window is buying from the holder before abandonment. Proven state: parts monopoly over a fleet of tens to hundreds of aircraft (Adam A500 <12 built; Varga Kachina 175) ([AOPA](https://www.aopa.org/news-and-media/all-news/2012/september/01/ownership-no-tc-no-problem)). Ceiling: too niche; the $B version (HEICO/Wencor PMA on airliner parts) is not a bought right but a created one. **Kill test:** list TCs/STCs whose holder's state registration is dissolved and whose fleet >200 registered aircraft; if <50 such certificates, kill. **Doubt:** too niche, and the FAA's abandonment process is turning these into public goods.

## 5. PMA "second source" creation on airliner parts (NEW, adjacent) — fit 3/10

Not a bought right: the applicant creates the PMA. Economics are attractive (a unique PMA gets "35% or 40%" benefit, a second PMA only "5% or 6%"; airlines rarely approve a second PMA; parts "almost become an annuity", [Wencor interview](https://inpractise.com/articles/wencor-ip)). HEICO already does 500+/yr. Fails the "unproven right you buy" test; listed for completeness.

## 6. CMMC / facility clearance arbitrage: buy uncertified small defense suppliers, certify, keep the sole-source slot (NEW) — fit 2/10

Numbers: 337,968 in-scope entities, 35% (≈118,000, my arithmetic) need Level 2 third-party certification, 68% small; 1,866 final Level 2 certificates by July 2026, 2,362 by 29 Sep 2026 (<2% of those needing it); DoD cost estimate $101,752 for a small-firm Level 2 certification, SBA estimate $593,800 including remediation ([BrightDefense](https://www.brightdefense.com/?p=22350); [Secureframe](https://secureframe.com/blog/federal-cybersecurity-report-2026); [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/what-cmmc-pause-means-defense-industrial-base)). IPC survey: 24% of electronics firms said CMMC costs could push them out of the DoD supply chain ([IPC](https://ipc.org/news-release/industry-survey-finds-us-electronics-manufacturers-may-exit-defense-market-due-high)). Kill: on 13 July 2026 DoD suspended CMMC Phase II (the 10 Nov 2026 deadline) pending a 60-day review, and C3PAO requirements must be removed from active solicitations ([Troutman](https://www.troutman.com/insights/years-in-the-making-suspended-in-a-day-dod-w-halts-cmmc-phase-ii-but-keeps-baseline-cybersecurity-obligations/)). FCLs are not transferable; they survive a stock sale subject to FOCI review ([PilieroMazza](https://www.pilieromazza.com/govcon-ma-and-security-clearances-what-buyers-need-to-know-when-acquiring-a-cleared-contractor-the-govcon-ma-playbook-part-5/)). Hit rate of certification is high for firms that reach assessment (58 conditional vs 1,866 final), so the lever cap is ~1.1-1.5x. Signal (SAM, FPDS) is fully legible. **Doubt:** forcing function paused; step is cheap and usually succeeds.

## 7. Pesticide registrations facing data call-ins (NEW) — fit 4/10

AMVAC's stated model: acquire "established chemistries" in "smaller niche markets which are no longer of strong focus to larger companies", e.g. permethrin (Ambush/Prelude) registrations, data, trademarks and the EU DMF from Syngenta in 2006 (price redacted), 2003 acquisitions totalling $10.7M; FIFRA data-gathering spend $4.7M in 2003 ([AMVAC 10-K FY2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000005981/000119312504053903/d10k.htm); [Permethrin asset sale](https://www.lawinsider.com/contracts/eRF4PCaNJy); [AMVAC 10-K FY2018](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/5981/000156459019007329/avd-10k_20181231.htm)). Rights structure: 10-year exclusive use, 15-year data compensation with binding arbitration, registrations transferable by EPA notification ([EPA Ch.10](https://19january2025snapshot.epa.gov/pesticide-registration/pesticide-registration-manual-chapter-10-data-compensation-requirements)). Proving step: funding registration-review studies and surviving EPA's risk decision (cost $1-10M, 2-5 years, my estimate; p unknown). Signal: registration-review dockets (PDF), 6(a)(2) adverse-effect reports, USGS use maps, state registration databases. **Doubt:** sellers are Bayer/Syngenta/Corteva who read the data perfectly; small-registrant distress cases exist but counts are unknown; step-up undocumented.

## 8. Fragmented FCC licences re-purposed by rulemaking (Anterix model) (NEW) — fit 3/10

Anterix bought ~6 MHz of nationwide 900 MHz from Sprint in Sept 2014 (price undisclosed; $10M paid in stock) plus channels from small operators, won the FCC May 2020 Report & Order creating a 3x3 MHz broadband segment, and now sells/leases to utilities: SDG&E $50M sale (≈$2.31/MHz-pop, my estimate), Oncor $102.5M, LCRA $30M + $13.5M, Xcel $80M lease, Ameren $47.7M, Evergy $30.2M, TECO $34.5M; the FCC 600 MHz auction averaged $0.93/MHz-pop; broadband licences in 194 counties, "essential party" in all 3,233 counties ([Anterix 10-K FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1304492/000130449225000056/atex-20250331.htm); [PDV release 2014](https://anterix.com/pacific-datavision-inc-completes-equity-raise-of-218-million-and-acquires-nationwide-900-mhz-licenses-to-expand-its-business-and-offer-2/)). This is the right structure (buy fragmented narrowband rights from small holders who cannot see the broadband future, regulatory step, 10x+) but the proving step was a one-off rulemaking that took six years, the signal (ULS) is legible, and the next such band is a policy bet, not a data edge. **Doubt:** not repeatable per asset.

## 9. Shelved orphan-designated programs (overlaps "foreign-approved drugs") — fit 2/10

>4,975 designations vs ~780 approvals since 1983 (≈16% conversion); 526 requests and 476 designations in 2017 ([FDA OOPD deck](https://fda.gov/media/128018/download); [FDA Law Blog](https://www.thefdalawblog.com/2018/02/going-strong-orphan-drug-approvals-and-designations-skyrocketed-in-2017-while-orphan-drug-designation-requests-dipped-slightly/)). The proving step is a clinical trial ($20-100M, p ≈ 0.3-0.5 per phase, cap ≤3x) and the data is not public. Roivant/BridgeBio already do this.

## 10. EU/UK sunset-clause marketing authorisations (NEW) — fit 2/10
Regulators publish the at-risk list (SUKL Excel); the step (relaunch) is not uncertain; low.

## 11. Unlicensed Bayh-Dole inventions (NEW) — fit 1/10
GAO-26-107971: 21% of recipients declined title FY2020-24, mostly for low commercial potential ([GAO](https://www.gao.gov/assets/gao-26-107971-highlights.pdf)). Universities license non-exclusively or exclusively by negotiation; nothing to buy cheap and prove.

## 12. India dormant defence industrial licences (NEW) — fit 1/10
539 licences to 333 companies, only 110 producing (67% dormant) ([Pioneer 2021](https://www.dailypioneer.com/2021/india/333-pvt-companies-into-defence-production-in-india--says-centre.html)). Licences are not scarce and carry no proving step.

## 13. Medical-device 510(k) predicates held by defunct firms (NEW) — fit 1/10
Transferable, but a cleared 510(k) is already the proven state; no numbers found; low.

## 14. UK MOD single-source spares (NEW, mirror of #1) — fit 2/10
SSRO estimated £1.8bn recoverable from expanding the Single Source Contract Regulations ([Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/mod-supply-chain-to-come-under-pressure-from-need-to-save-costs-and-expansion-of-single-source-contracts-regulations)); no public NSN-level demand data equivalent to FLIS/DIBBS, so the join in #1 cannot be run.

Footnote (excluded by standing rule): NRC source-material licences for dormant uranium mills are drilling-adjacent and were not researched.

## Answers to the brief's specific questions

- **Dormant Phase II firms:** 3.9% of 4,412 DoD Phase II firms (1995-2018) ceased to exist or became untraceable; 39% of 16,959 Phase II contracts produced zero sales; "alive but dormant" firm count unknown.
- **Phase III size/frequency:** Navy $1.2B over 440 actions to 158 firms in FY23; DoD 2,153 actions >$250K in FY22; NASA ~75/yr at ~$747K average (my arithmetic).
- **Phase II→III hit rate:** 16% of SBIR firms have ≥1 Phase III; 6% have Phase III > SBIR funding; Navy's Phase III actions ≈ 0.95x its Phase II contracts (my arithmetic).
- **Named roll-ups:** no roll-up buying dormant firms for Phase III rights was found; acquirers of live firms: L3 (43), GE, SAIC, BAE, Lockheed, Raytheon, GD, Philips, Teledyne (≥10 each); GD bought Progeny ($875M Phase III) in 2022.
- **Do primes price lineage?** Yes for live firms (2,120 acquisitions); after acquisition the Phase III coding disappears from FPDS, so the value is captured but invisible. For dormant firms: no evidence.
- **Is DLA data illegible or all tables?** Demand, AMSC code, approved sources and award history are tables (DIBBS, FLIS/PUB LOG, FPDS). Drawings are image files behind JCP/export-control gates; the ownership of a dead supplier's IP is in bankruptcy/UCC PDFs. The edge is the join, not any single file.
- **SAR success rate and cost:** 10% (2009-11), ~39-49% (FY17); ~1 year; vendor-paid, dollar cost unpublished.
