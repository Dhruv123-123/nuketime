## F3. Mitigation banking on hydric farmland (NEW; best structural fit in this cluster)

**What it is.** US Clean Water Act 404 and Endangered Species Act permits require developers, DOTs and pipelines to buy "credits" from approved mitigation banks (restored wetlands, streams, species habitat). A bank sponsor buys land, gets a Mitigation Banking Instrument approved by a US Army Corps-led Interagency Review Team, restores the site, and sells credits over years.

**1. Proving step.** Instrument approval plus credit demand. The 2008 Mitigation Rule prescribes 225 days of Corps-led review (90 prospectus + 90 draft + 45 final) (Corps slides, mitchellwilliamslaw.com). Observed: sponsors wait 45 to 1,000 days after submitting a complete draft instrument, and the Corps "has exceeded its timeline goals for at least a decade" (EPIC, policyinnovation.org). Realistic prospectus-to-first-credit-release: 2-5 years (my estimate). Cost per attempt: land at agricultural value (median wetland bank 174 acres, conservation bank 333 acres, valuewetlands.tamu.edu), design/permitting/legal and financial assurances (no public figure; $0.5-1.5M my estimate), restoration $10-60k/acre (my estimate); Florida releases ~20% of credits at instrument approval and the rest on restoration milestones (mitigationbankinginc.com). Base hit rate: unknown nationally; RIBITS carries statuses Pending / Approved / Sold Out / Suspended / Terminated, and CDFW's list shows "Suspended", "Closed", "Credits not for sale". The failure mode that matters is demand: a bank that is approved but sells few credits. EASI reports ~70% of released credits have been consumed over 35 years and $130B of credit inventory is still held (easillc.com 2026), which implies a long tail of slow or dead banks; I estimate p(sells out within 5 years) at 0.3-0.5, so lever cap 2-3x. Who pays today: ~1,500 sponsors, from EIP (49 projects) down to single-bank farmers (ecosystempartners.com 2020).

**2. Signal.** Two layers. (a) Site suitability: hydric soils (SSURGO), NWI wetlands, LiDAR are legible, but the decisive evidence for "restorable" is historical: pre-drainage aerials (1930s-50s USDA photos), county drainage-district and ditch records, old NRCS wetland determinations, all scanned or paper, scattered by county. (b) Demand: USACE public notices (PDF per district), ORM permit data, state DOT STIPs (PDF), FERC pipeline filings, and RIBITS credit ledgers (per-bank PDF/CSV, public but not joined). No regulator writes "this parcel would make a good bank in a credit-short service area" into any field; EASI sells price-and-availability reports but by hand (easillc.com). Prices vary 80x within the country and are driven "mainly by supply and demand, not land value" (EASI): Mississippi wetland credits average $8,600/acre, South Carolina $14,000, North Carolina $134,000, New Jersey $675,000; Florida banks list $50,000-$550,000 per UMAM credit (Alafia $500k, Florida Gulf Coast saltwater $550k combined, Barberville $50-70k); Iowa forested $65-95k, Louisiana coastal $60-100k, Texas $12.5-22.5k (Fenstermaker; mitigationbankinginc.com). Species credits: Florida scrub jay $20k/credit at 2 credits per impacted acre; Texas cave invertebrates up to $400k/credit (valuewetlands.tamu.edu); California species credits $50-250k/acre (memory, unverified).

**3. Unproven right.** Farmland or ranchland with hydric soils, drained decades ago, in a watershed with permit demand. Bought privately from farmers at crop value ($3-10k/acre in the Southeast and Midwest, my estimate) or optioned. Farmers price corn yield, not credit yield, and cannot read drainage history, permit pipelines or ledgers at scale. Exclusive, negotiated, not auctioned. Passes.

**4. Proven state.** An approved bank with released credits. Gross value per acre = credits/acre (0.5-1.5 for restoration, my estimate) x price. Examples: NC $134k/acre average vs $5k/acre land = ~25x gross; Florida 10-100x; Mississippi ~1-2x (fails there). Do proven banks trade before credits are sold? Yes: EIP's own sale "points to deeper institutional market for mitigation bank" and Landvest ran a "mitigation bank portfolio sale" (ecosystempartners.com); EIP raised $181M, $303M and $454.5M funds (42 LPs, 2020); RES is KKR-backed (memory). So the option on an approved bank is sellable without waiting for the last credit.

**Market ceiling.** EASI's estimates: $415B of credits released since the 1990s, $285B transacted, ~$8B/yr of demand, 2.5M acres, Virginia alone 164 banks, Texas 48 wetland/stream + 6 conservation banks, California 105 conservation/mitigation banks (CDFW). Enough for a multi-billion company; EIP got to ~$940M of funds on 49 projects.

**Incumbents.** Ecosystem Investment Partners, Resource Environmental Solutions (KKR), Westervelt Ecological, Wildlands Inc., Mitigation Solutions USA, Restoration Systems, plus consultants (Burns & McDonnell, Great Ecology) and data vendors (EASI, Mitigation Desk). Tools are GIS and spreadsheets; I found no evidence of ML on drainage history or permit-pipeline demand.

**Kill test (days).** Export RIBITS (public). For banks approved 2008-2018: (i) months from prospectus to approval, (ii) share that had sold <20% of credits after 5 years, (iii) price dispersion within one service area. If >70% sell out in 5 years and approval is >90%, there is no class imbalance (lever <1.4x) and the business is ordinary land development. Second check: whether farmland adjoining approved banks already trades at a premium (sellers pricing the option).

**Biggest doubt.** Sackett v. EPA (2023) removed federal jurisdiction over many isolated wetlands, cutting wetland-credit demand in some states; stream and species credits are less affected. Also the step is partly political (IRT discretion), not a hidden physical state, and EASI's dollar totals are "authorized" values, not cash.

**Score 7/10.**

## F1. Shelved clinical-stage compounds (Roivant model), extended with numbers

**Roivant's record (adus.substack.com; Roivant releases; STAT; Fierce).** In-licensed from pharma restructurings: intepirdine (GSK, $5M, 2014; Phase 3 failed 2017 after ~$315M raised), relugolix (Takeda; approved 2020/2021; Pfizer co-commercialisation $650M upfront; Sumitomo bought Myovant 2022), vibegron (Merck, ~$10M; approved 2020, ~$400M/yr sales), tapinarof (GSK, $150M upfront 2018; approved 2022; sold to Organon 2024 for $250M + up to $1.2B, "not outstanding"), RVT-3101 (Pfizer, Nov/Dec 2022, $45M plus a development commitment, Pfizer kept 25%; sold to Roche Oct 2023 for $7.1B upfront, Roivant net ~$5.2B, 11 months after purchase), brepocitinib (Pfizer), mosliciguat (Bayer), namilumab (failed Phase 2 2024), rodatristat (failed 2022), Rethymic (Duke; approved 2021), batoclimab (HanAll; paused 2021 then continued). Sumitomo paid $3B for five vants in 2019. Tally: ~4 approvals, 1 pre-approval mega-exit, 5-7 failures or shutdowns out of ~15 clinical programmes, i.e. a hit rate near the industry base rate; returns come from one fat-tail event.

**Peers.** BridgeBio: academic sourcing (Stanford acoramidis via Eidos 2016; Novartis infigratinib $15M upfront + $95M milestones 2018); 19 INDs since 2015; 3 FDA approvals (Attruby Nov 2024, Truseltiq 2021 later withdrawn, Nulibry 2021 (memory)); out-licensing: Bayer EU acoramidis $135M upfront, Kyowa Kirin $100M, Alexion $25M (BridgeBio 10-K FY2024). Cullinan: zipalertinib, US rights sold to Taiho for $275M upfront in 2022 after Phase 1/2a (memory, unverified). "Pharmakon" in the task brief is Pharmakon Advisors, a royalty/debt lender (BioPharma Credit), not an asset buyer; the financial version of the model is XOMA Royalty (buys milestones/royalties on partnered early assets; 22 assets added in 2025, royalty income $33.6M, being bought by Ligand for ~$739M) and Royalty Pharma. Others running the shelved-asset play: Formation Bio (ex-TrialSpark, buys assets and runs AI-assisted trials), Centessa, Aditum Bio (Novartis cast-offs), Lantern Pharma (RADR, rescued oncology compounds), Fortress, PureTech, Arrivent (China assets).

**1. Proving step.** Phase 2 (or 2b). Cost: Moore et al. JAMA 2018: median pivotal trial $19M (n=138); trials <=100 patients $5.9M; placebo-controlled mean $28.8M (medcitynews.com). Phase 2 for a shelved asset: $6-20M, 2-3 years. Base rate: BIO 2011-2020, n=12,728 transitions: Phase I->II 52.0%, II->III 28.9%, III->NDA 57.8%, NDA->approval 90.6%, LOA from Phase I 7.9%; non-oncology rare disease II->III 44.6%, LOA 17.0%; biomarker-selected II->III 46.3% (bio.org PDF). Lever cap = 1/0.29 = 3.5x; for rare disease 2.2x. Deloitte: externally sourced assets launch at 12.7%/20.5%/63.1% from Phase 1/2/3 vs benchmarks 9.6%/15.3%/49.6%, so in-licensing already captures a selection effect (Deloitte external innovation PDF; lifescivc.com cites ~2x).

**2. Signal.** ClinicalTrials.gov results (structured), FDA approval packages and Complete Response letters (hundreds of PDF pages per drug on Drugs@FDA), EMA EPARs and withdrawal assessment reports (PDF), FAERS (structured), patents (text), sponsor 10-K/8-K discontinuation language (text), conference abstracts. Is the answer in a legible field? Partly: Cortellis, Pharmaprojects, Biomedtracker and GlobalData tag every asset "discontinued / no development reported" with a reason code, and every pharma BD team subscribes; the Deloitte/BIO numbers above come from Biomedtracker. Who reads the illegible layer with ML: Every Cure ($48.3M ARPA-H, knowledge graph + LLM literature mining, does not own assets or run trials yet), Healx (~$115M raised, owns repurposed HLX-1502, Phase 2 in NF1 from late 2024, 55 staff), BenevolentAI, Formation Bio, Lantern, Insilico, Recursion, plus NCATS' 2012 "New Therapeutic Uses" programme that opened shelved pharma assets to academics (memory).

**3. Unproven right.** Exclusive licence or outright purchase from pharma, negotiated: $5M (intepirdine), ~$10M (vibegron), $15M (infigratinib), $45M (RVT-3101), $150M (tapinarof). Passes on exclusivity and negotiated price, but fails the "seller cannot read the data" test: the seller generated the data and is the best-informed party on earth; it sells for portfolio reasons, and it typically keeps equity or milestones (Pfizer 25% of Telavant).

**4. Proven state.** After positive Phase 2 the option trades before Phase 3: Telavant $7.1B (R1 ~150x on the $45M licence; ~20-50x on total money in, my estimate), zipalertinib $275M upfront. Buyers: every large pharma; liquidity is the best of any candidate here.

**Market ceiling.** Thousands of shelved clinical-stage compounds (the often-quoted figure is >5,000 shelved for non-safety reasons; memory, unverified); global in-licensing >$100B/yr headline value (memory). Roivant market cap ~$8B.

**Kill test (days).** Take Phase 2 assets discontinued 2012-2017 "for strategic reasons" (Cortellis field or press releases), find which were re-licensed and what fraction reached Phase 3/approval; then test whether a two-variable legible model (had positive Phase 2 signal, rare/biomarker indication) explains outcomes as well as anything in the PDFs. If re-licensing is already common for the good ones, pharma BD and the ML shops are the market and there is no cheap unproven right.

**Biggest doubt.** p=0.29 caps the lever at 3.5x, the sellers are the most informed party, and five ML companies plus every BD team read the same PDFs. The structure works (cheap right, expensive failing step, option trades at Phase 2) but the "illegible" data is already semi-legible in commercial databases.

**Score 5/10.** Sub-variants: dormant orphan designations (FDA ODD database is a legible lookup; designations are transferable; p for rare disease 0.45 so lever 2.2x); old antibiotics (non-inferiority Phase 3 $30-80M, Phase III success ~0.6, demand side broken: UK pays ~GBP 10M/yr subscription (memory), PASTEUR Act not passed) 2/10; biosimilars (patents and expiry legible, $100-300M, p>0.8) 1/10; dormant ANDAs and 505(b)(2) see F8.

## F4. Senior western water rights, change-of-use decree as the proving step (NEW)

**1. Proving step.** Converting an agricultural right to municipal/industrial use requires a Colorado water-court change case or a state-engineer change application (NV, NM, UT, ID). Historic consumptive use (HCU) analysis "can cost hundreds of thousands of dollars" (the-journal.com); Pueblo West's Hill Ranch acquisition (~1,900 AF of consumptive-use water) has been in court support since 2013 and is described as "rather lengthy" (respec.com); Vidler spent ~$6M on a 700 AF application in New Mexico and had its ~2,000 AF Sandy Valley (NV) application only partly granted (grist.org). Quantity credited is cut: the streamlined HCU method under-credits by 10-15% (the-journal.com); Supreme Court decisions (Wolfe v. Sedalia 2015, Busk-Ivanhoe 2016) reduced or voided claimed quantities. p unknown: I estimate 0.5-0.8 of applications get a decree with 10-40% of quantity lost, so lever cap 1.3-2x. Who pays: municipalities, developers, water investors.

**2. Signal.** Diversion records (Colorado HydroBase, structured, legible), decrees and ditch-company records, historical aerials, ditch-rider books, state-engineer well logs and permit files (scanned PDFs, illegible). Partially legible; the key variable (HCU) is computable from public diversion records by any water engineer.

**3. Unproven right.** Irrigated farms with senior ditch shares, bought privately. C-BT units are a public index: $7,175/AF (2010), $15,065 (2013), $47,662 (2019), $65,946 (2022), $67,000 (2023), $57,487 (2025) (Northern Water); Colorado Sun reports ~$85,000/AF in 2025 and a 2022 peak of ~$101,000. Transactions: Carlson Family Trust auction, 96 units $5.47M (~$57k/unit) sold separately from 154 acres at $990k; Lavin ranch, 100 units for $7M to North Weld County Water District, 1,547 acres for $9M to Larimer County (hallhall.com; businessden.com). C-BT is already a single liquid market, so R1 there is 1; the edge, if any, is in native ditch rights priced with yield uncertainty ($5-30k/AF, memory).

**4. Proven state.** A decreed, transferable consumptive-use right. Buyers: municipalities, water districts, D.R. Horton (bought Vidler for $291M in 2022), Water Asset Management, Greenstone/MassMutual, Renewable Water Resources, Pure Cycle. Vidler: fivefold on one Colorado deal after six years, more than threefold in Arizona, ~$25M profit flipping an aquifer stake (grist.org). Trades before any pipeline is built: yes.

**Ceiling.** WestWater Research counts ~2.5M AF of leases and sales worth ~$1B/yr (Colorado Sun). Total western water-right value is tens of billions (memory).

**Incumbents.** WAM, Vidler/D.R. Horton, Greenstone, RWR, Pure Cycle, Cadiz; brokers WestWater. Politics: Colorado discourages "buy and dry" (alternative transfer methods); counties sue.

**Kill test.** Pull Colorado DWR change-case decrees 2005-2020 (public PDFs): compute decreed/applied AF and time; if >85% of quantity is granted and is predictable from HydroBase alone, there is no illegible edge and the profit is urban-growth beta.

**Biggest doubt.** p is high, the proven price is a published index, and most of the return is waiting for growth, not reading files. Probably overlaps the real-estate/infrastructure cluster. **Score 5/10.**

## F2. Veterinary repurposing of human-validated molecules (NEW)

**1. Proving step.** Pilot field study in dogs or cats (Nexvet: 126 cats for frunevetmab), then pivotal field efficacy and target-animal safety studies: "$5-10 million" and "2-4 years" per Anivive; manufacturing held to human-drug standards. Base rate unknown; Kindred had two approvals (Mirataz 2018, Zimeta 2019) and at least one pivotal failure (CereKin/diacerein 2016, memory), Nexvet's ranevetmab and frunevetmab both hit; I estimate p 0.4-0.6 for human-validated mechanisms, so lever cap ~2x.

**2. Signal.** Human clinical and pharmacology data (FDA review PDFs, literature), expired compound patents, FDA CVM Freedom-of-Information summaries (PDF), EMA veterinary EPARs. Illegible-ish but it is scientific judgment on translation, not a dataset about a specific cheap asset.

**3. Unproven right.** Weak: an off-patent human molecule is free, so there is nothing bought cheap from an uninformed owner; exclusivity comes from new formulation/use patents, caninised antibody sequences, and CVM's 5-year new-animal-drug exclusivity (memory). Fails the "buy the right" test unless the play is buying stalled vet-stage assets from failed startups.

**4. Proven state.** Zoetis bought Nexvet for ~$85M (66% premium) after ranevetmab met its pivotal endpoint (investor.zoetis.com; Nexvet R&D was $14.9M in FY2016); the resulting Librela and Solensia are now a franchise of several hundred $M/yr (memory, unverified). Elanco bought Kindred for $440M (52% premium) with two approvals and three dermatology candidates (businesswire). Buyers: Zoetis, Elanco, Boehringer, Merck AH, Ceva, Dechra; liquid.

**Ceiling.** Companion-animal therapeutics ~$20B/yr (memory). **Incumbents.** Zoetis in-house, Elanco, Anivive, Petmedix (bought by Zoetis 2023), Invetx (bought by Dechra 2024), Scout Bio. **Kill test.** From CVM FOI summaries 2015-2025, classify approvals by origin (human-repurposed vs novel) and count pivotal failures disclosed in SEC filings of Kindred, Aratana, Nexvet, Jaguar; if p>0.5 the lever is <2x. **Doubt.** Low class imbalance and no cheap unproven right. **Score 4/10.**

## F5. Orphaned EU novel-food dossiers (NEW)

EU novel-food authorisation: ~40 applications/yr since 2018 (foodnavigator.com); of 156 received Jan 2018 to Jan 2021, 39 approved, 3 rejected, 114 pending (lifeboat.com); under the 2021 Transparency Regulation, 10 of 19 evaluated applications (53%) were terminated at intake for study-notification non-compliance, with a 6-month penalty (nutraingredients.com); EFSA has 9 months on paper but clock-stops stretch assessments to years (EFSA 2025 plenary). Authorisation carries 5-year proprietary-data protection (Reg. 2015/2283 Art. 26), which makes it an exclusive right. Cost of a dossier incl. tox studies: EUR 0.5-3M (my estimate). The play: buy data packages and IP from insolvent or withdrawn applicants (insect protein, algae, cultivated-cell firms that failed 2023-2025), fix the dossier, resubmit. Signal: EFSA Register of Questions (legible status), published opinions and termination letters (PDF), the applicant's own studies (private until bought). Proven state: an authorised ingredient with data protection; buyers DSM-Firmenich, ADM, Kerry, IFF; comparables unknown. Ceiling modest; the US GRAS route is self-affirmed and has no failing step. **Kill test:** list withdrawn/terminated applications 2018-2025 and check whether any were later resubmitted by a different owner. **Doubt:** niche; p is high once a dossier is complete. **Score 3/10.**

## F9. Chilean salmon concessions, relocation and sanitary status as the proving step (NEW)

1,320 concessions operated; Subpesca wants ~500 (38%) relocated and total space cut 18%; 270 relocation applications pending (74 Los Lagos, 183 Aysén, 13 Magallanes); a Senate bill would halt new concessions until at least 2030 (seafoodsource.com). Relocation proposals cost $6M (Cooke) to $18M (Salmones Antártica) and have been held up by government. A 2025 law allows micro-relocations up to 350 m without new environmental approval (~100 farms qualify) and imposes non-use fees of 6 UTM/ha/yr rising to 16 UTM after 54 months (fishfarmingexpert.com). Concessions have been leased and transferred between producers since the 2012 reform; UC Temuco built an 11-variable valuation model because prices are opaque. Signal: INFA sanitary reports, RCAs, oceanographic data (Spanish PDFs). Unproven right: inactive or badly sited concessions, price unknown. Proven: operable concession in a good macro-zone; buyers Mowi, AquaChile, Cooke, Camanchaca. **Doubt:** the buyers know the sites far better than an outsider, and the step is political. **Score 3/10.**

## F10. LatAm farmland title regularisation (NEW, low)

BrasilAgro's listed model buys Cerrado land cheap, transforms it (clearing, liming, three crops) and sells at roughly 2-3x (memory, unverified); that is a build, not a drill. The drill-like slice is title: possession-only ("posse") land trades at a discount and regularisation (CAR, SIGEF georeferencing, INCRA, cartório) is costly, slow and fails on overlapping claims or IBAMA embargoes. Signal: SIGEF polygons, CAR declarations, embargo lists, PRODES (all legible) plus scanned cartório books (illegible). Unproven right: posse parcels bought privately. Proven: titled parcel, 2-5x (my estimate). **Doubt:** grilagem (land-grabbing) risk and the brownfield lesson that the discount may already equal the expected cost. **Score 3/10.**

## F6. Biocontrol strains from public collections to EPA registration (NEW, low)

Biopesticide: 3-5 years and under $10M to develop, EPA registration ~13 months, vs ~12 years and ~$300M for a conventional pesticide; Marrone's funnel screened ~2,203 microbes, tested 14, developed 2; biopesticides $9B growing ~12%/yr (Marrone lecture PDF). Strains in NRRL/ATCC/DSMZ are orderable by anyone, so the unproven right is non-exclusive unless patented or licensed from USDA ARS (exclusive-by-field licences, cheap; memory). The expensive step (field trials + registration) has p ~0.5-0.7, so lever ~1.5x. Marrone exited to Bioceres for ~$236M after 15 years (memory). **Score 3/10.**

## F7. Public-breeder crop varieties and PVP (NEW, low)

State variety-trial reports (one PDF table per state per crop per year) and GRIN evaluation data are illegible at scale, but the sellers (university breeding programmes) are the best-informed party and licences are often non-exclusive; released-variety royalties are $1-4/acre (memory). ~1-2% of advanced lines become varieties (memory). Fails the "seller can't read the data" test. **Score 2/10.**

## F8. Paragraph IV challenge rights and dormant ANDAs (NEW-ish)

Generic merits win rate 48% (82/171, RBC 2010), "success" incl. settlements 76%, 36-37% in the busiest districts; 2024 data: brands win 20% on the merits, generics 2%, the rest settle; 312 ANDA suits in 2024; 180-day exclusivity worth "hundreds of millions" on a blockbuster; PTAB invalidation 60-70% (drugpatentwatch.com; patentdocs.org). First-to-file status is first-come (killed by the brief) and litigation analytics (Lex Machina, Docket Navigator) already structure the dockets. Dormant approved ANDAs are a legible FDA list, and the profitable version (reviving a sole-source generic to raise price) is the Turing/Valeant pattern. **Score 2/10.**

## F11. Orphaned REACH/TSCA registrations (NEW, weak)

REACH registrations cost EUR 50k-2M+ per substance and are transferable with the legal entity; as plants close in Europe some substances could lose their only registrant, making the holder the sole legal supplier (memory, unverified). But registration almost always succeeds; there is no failing discovery step, only cost. **Score 2/10.**

## F12. Recycled-material streams (weak)

EPA e-Manifest and RCRA biennial reports are structured; metal content of a specific stream is known to the generator and the refiner (Umicore, Heraeus, Eramet/Gulf Chemical, Johnson Matthey). No failing step. **Score 1/10.**

## F13. Chinese/Indian-approved drugs missing in the West (seen; one paragraph)

New numbers only: Akeso licensed ivonescimab to Summit for $500M upfront (2022) and Summit's market cap rose above $15B after HARMONi-2 (2024) (memory, unverified); China-origin assets were roughly 30% of big-pharma in-licensing by count in 2024 (memory). The illegible layer is the Chinese-language CDE registry and NMPA review reports, now read by Arrivent, Kelun/Merck, Hengrui partners and every BD team. Crowded. **Score 3/10.**

## Killed in one line each

- Farmland productivity mispricing (SSURGO, yields, satellite): NCCPI/CSR2 are legible lookup indices already used by AcreTrader/Acres.com, Farmland Partners, Tillable, Nuveen; farming a season has p~1, no lever.
- Timber: a cruise costs $5-15/acre with p~1; FIA plots are structured; forest-carbon eligibility is a registry lookup.
- Livestock genetics: $50 genomic tests replaced progeny testing; CDCB/AGIL evaluations are legible; nothing left to read.
- Orchard/vineyard replant: no public signal; virus risk is a $50-200 PCR panel.
- Agricultural easements: formula payments, no failing step.
- US federal-waters aquaculture permits: first-come and essentially never approved.
- Orphan designations: FDA ODD database is a legible lookup; included under F1.
