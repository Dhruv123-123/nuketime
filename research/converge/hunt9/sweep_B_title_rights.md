# Hunt 9, Sweep B: legal title and rights whose value is revealed by an expensive adjudication

Scope: rights (water, land, planning, IP, claims, corridors, numbers) that are cheap while clouded and valuable once a slow, costly legal or administrative step proves them. Drilling/prospecting excluded. 32 web searches plus ~40 page fetches; the shared per-turn search budget ran out before the Australian and Chilean water registers, 2025 IPv4 pricing, US lot-banking and bankruptcy-claims trading could be searched. Statements from memory are marked "(from memory)"; inferred numbers "(my estimate)".

Scoring convention: fit 0-10 against the brief's four-part drill test. The brief's lever cap is 1/p where p is the base hit rate of the proving step. The recurring finding of this sweep: adjudications of title are expensive and slow but, unlike drilling, they usually SUCCEED (they settle), so p is 0.5-0.9 and the lever is capped at 1.1-2x. The exceptions with low p (restitution claims, ITU filings, IPR survival, out-of-basin water transfers) are small, political or already legible.

## 1. UK strategic land promotion: consent is the drill hole (fit 6/10)

**Proving step.** An outline planning application, usually refused by the council and won at appeal before the Planning Inspectorate. Promotion cost £25,000-£40,000 per gross acre, £7-20M for a new settlement (Knight Frank, 2024: https://www.knightfrank.co.uk/research/article/2024/9/benchmark-land-value-fine-margins). Time 2-5 years (Catesby's Pentland Farm: application April 2013, refused, won on appeal January 2015, sold to Redrow later that year; Gladman's Snowley Park: agreement April 2013, first application refused, approval September 2014; source: https://eprints.glos.ac.uk/7050/1/7050%20Jones%20A%20Commentary%20on%20Strategic%20Land%20Promotion%20in%20the%20UK.pdf). Base hit rate: Gladman says about two-thirds of its sites get through appeal; a CPRE 2019 study found 60% of promoter appeals approved where the council lacked a five-year housing land supply versus 30% where it had one (https://www.favershameye.co.uk/amp/how-land-speculators-0perate-at-our-expense). So p = 0.3-0.67, lever cap 1.5-3x. The promoter pays ("no win, no fee" to the landowner).

**Signal.** Local plans, HELAA/SHLAA site assessments, five-year housing land supply statements, housing delivery test results, 40 years of appeal decisions, all PDFs scattered across 300+ local planning authorities. Partly legible already: the Housing Delivery Test is a published table, and LandTech/LandInsight, Nimbus and Searchland sell ownership and planning-history layers. What is NOT a lookup field is p(consent) for a specific unallocated field given the council's evidence base and inspectors' reasoning in comparable appeals. That is a document-understanding problem of the Terranox kind.

**Unproven right.** An option or promotion agreement on farmland. Upfront payment nominal; the promoter takes 20-25% of net sale proceeds after costs (Faversham Eye; Knight Frank says the split is contractual and promoters target 5x on costs, with one case study at 1.3x). Bought bilaterally from farmers, exclusive by contract, not auctioned. Farmers cannot read the planning evidence base.

**Proven state.** Agricultural land £21,000/ha (about £8,500/acre) versus greenfield with residential permission £1.95M/ha (about £789,000/acre) in 2015: a ~93x uplift (Faversham Eye, citing MHCLG land value estimates; the Knight Frank benchmark is "at least 20x existing use value"). Buyers are the volume housebuilders: Gladman sold 2,796 plots across 18 sites in one year to Barratt, Bovis, Redrow, Persimmon, Morris, David Wilson, Bloor, Miller. Consented land trades before anything is built; that is the entire model. Named comparable: Barratt bought Gladman for £250M in 2022 for a pipeline of ~100,000 plots on 406 sites, which had consented 2,760 plots and earned £6.9M pre-tax on £29.9M turnover in FY2021 (average £18.9M PBT on £52.5M sales in FY2017-19) (https://housingtoday.co.uk/news/barratt-buys-land-trader-gladman/5115838.article).

**Market ceiling.** England needs 240,000-300,000 homes a year; at ~£200k-£800k per consented acre and 12-35 homes/acre the annual consented-land market is many £bn (my estimate £5-10bn/yr). Hallam Land alone reports ~50,000 consented plots. US analogue (lot banking and entitlement, Walton Global, Phoenix/Dallas) is larger but was not searched.

**Incumbents.** Gladman (Barratt), Catesby (Urban&Civic), Richborough, Hallam Land (Henry Boot), Rosconn, L&Q Estates, Wallace Land, Dandara; data vendors LandTech, Nimbus, Searchland. None is ML-first; all rely on planning consultants and the same council door-knocking.

**Kill test (days).** Scrape 2,000 PINS appeal decisions for residential sites of 50+ units 2015-2025; join to the council's 5YHLS position and HDT score at decision date; if a logistic model on those two legible fields alone reaches within 0.05 AUC of a model that reads the decision letters and local plan evidence, there is no ML edge (the brief's 0.03-0.08 pattern) and the candidate dies. Second check: compare promoters' site-level win rate (Gladman ~67%) with the all-appeals residential rate; if promoters already sit at the ceiling, the lever is below 1.5x.

**Biggest doubt.** p of 0.5-0.67 caps the lever below 2x, and 2024-25 planning reform (mandatory targets, grey belt) raises p further, which shrinks the ML edge while enlarging the pool.

## 2. Western US ag-to-urban water rights: the change case (fit 5/10)

**Proving step.** In Colorado, a water court change-of-use case that quantifies historical consumptive use (HCU) and decrees the transferable amount; in Arizona, ADWR hearing plus Bureau of Reclamation approval for Colorado River entitlements; in Nevada/Utah/New Mexico, a State Engineer change application. Costs: legal fees "will exceed $100,000" for a small town's single change case with ~$10,000 engineering (Wiggins, 2010: https://coyotegulch.blog/2010/08/16/wiggins-water-court-application-update/); "well over $100,000" and three years for Park Forest Water District (https://parkforestwd.colorado.gov/sites/parkforestwd/files/Letter%20to%20residents%202-19-20_litigation_sumary.pdf); Fort Collins' change case 2011-2015, four years (EDF ATM report, https://edf.org/ATMreport); American Water Development's San Luis Valley export: nine years, several million dollars, dismissed (https://elr.info/sites/default/files/article/2012/08/42.10800.pdf); Hill Ranch (Pueblo West, 1,900 AF): in process since 2013 (https://respec.com/?p=1582); Greenstone's Cibola transfer: farms bought 2013-14, ADWR hearing 2019, Reclamation approval 2022, vacated by a federal judge February 2024 for inadequate NEPA review, so 10+ years and still open (https://www.motherjones.com/?p=1051824). Hit rate: in Colorado most change cases end in stipulated decrees ("settlement happens more often than not", Colorado Water Trust, https://coloradowatertrust.org/?p=10198), so p for in-basin cases is perhaps 0.8-0.9 (my estimate), lever ~1.2x. The hidden state is continuous rather than binary: how many acre-feet of HCU the decree grants versus what the seller believes. For large out-of-basin or interstate transfers p is low (AWDI dismissed; Vidler's Sandy Valley and New Mexico applications both lost in court; Fish Springs needed ~$100M and an act of Congress: https://truthout.org/articles/this-little-known-company-is-making-millions-off-the-western-water-shortage/), lever 2-3x, but those are political, not data, problems.

**Signal.** Scanned decrees, ditch-company diversion records, historical aerial photographs of irrigated acreage, share-to-parcel mappings, well logs. Partly legible: Colorado's HydroBase has digitized daily diversion records for most structures (from memory), and OpenET now publishes Landsat consumptive use at 30 m for the 17 western states from 2016, already accepted for regulatory reporting in the California Delta (since 2022), Nevada's Diamond Valley and Oregon's Harney Basin (https://democrats-naturalresources.house.gov/download/porterfield-testimony_-wow-leg-hrg-110421pdf&download=1). What stays illegible: the pre-2016 period of record that change cases use (30-50 years), the parcel-level irrigated-acreage history, and the ditch-wide versus parcel-specific share analysis (https://mountainscholar.org/items/912da44a-c4fe-4468-9f97-85411fd85cc0/full). Colorado's decennial abandonment list is a legible regulator field, so "forfeiture risk" is not an edge.

**Unproven right.** Farmland with appurtenant senior rights or ditch shares, bought bilaterally from farmers. Prices: Greenstone paid ~$9.8M for 485 acres in Cibola carrying 2,033 AF of fourth-priority Colorado River entitlement (about $4,800/AF embedded, my calculation); Water Asset Management paid $16.6M for 2,220 Grand Valley acres (~$7,500/acre) and $100M for 12,793 acres in Arizona's McMullen Valley (~$7,800/acre) (https://www.watereducation.org/aquafornia-news/why-hedge-funds-are-eyeing-utahs-shrinking-water-supply, https://www.kdminer.com/news/hedge-fund-s-100m-arizona-farmland-buy-stirs-water-grab-fears/article_0598ffeb-81d0-5277-ac6a-56830cf1ec3d.html). Farmers do not read HCU engineering; the cities' lawyers do.

**Proven state.** Perpetual municipal water. Colorado-Big Thompson units (which avoid water court; transfers take 2-3 months): $7,175 per unit in 2010, $59,609 in 2020, $67,000 in 2023, $57,487 in 2025 (Northern Water, https://www.northernwater.org/getmedia/45190426-0f57-4325-8320-82199f21b984/Colorado-Big-Thompson-Units-Market-Price-Historical-Data); $85,000 per acre-foot in 2025 off a $101,000 peak in 2022 (WestWater via Colorado Sun, https://coloradosun.com/2026/04/24/colorado-water-prices/; the two figures reconcile because a unit yields ~0.7 AF). Queen Creek paid Greenstone $24M, more than $11,500/AF, for 2,033 AF of perpetual Colorado River water: a 2.4x gross on the land price, with Greenstone keeping the land. Vidler reports fivefold on a Colorado right over six years and threefold on Arizona banked water; D.R. Horton bought Vidler for $291M in 2022 (https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/830122/000119312522105010/d315776dex991.htm). Arizona long-term storage credits trade at only $209-300/AF (Phoenix AMA, 2023: https://waterbank.az.gov/sites/default/files/2023-09/04a-2023.09.13_LTSC-Pricing.pdf) and $500-600/AF in CAWCD's 2026-28 purchase from Vail Water (https://library.cap-az.com/cagrd/documents/Vail-Water-CAWCD-2026-LTSC-PSA.pdf), so credits are not the proven asset; perpetual rights are. Liquidity: WestWater tracks 2.5M AF and ~$1bn/yr of leases and sales; 353 permanent transfers in the West in 2007-09, 61% of them CBT units. Proven rights trade before any pipeline is built (Cibola sold on paper in 2018, diversion began 2023).

**Market ceiling.** ~$1bn/yr transacted, tens of $bn of agricultural rights in the Colorado, Rio Grande, Platte and Arkansas basins; growing demand from data centres and semiconductor fabs in Arizona and Colorado (Colorado Sun notes prices pulling water from farms to industry).

**Incumbents.** Water Asset Management (~$300M farmland by 2020 across CO, CA, AZ, NV; hired Colorado's former state engineer as counsel), Greenstone/MassMutual-Barings (Rabo AgriFinance debt), Vidler (now D.R. Horton), Western Water LLC, Renewable Water Resources (San Luis Valley export, failed), PICO, Cadiz; WestWater Research for data.

**Kill test.** Pull 100 decreed Division 1 and 2 change cases 2010-2025; compare decreed HCU/share with the applicant's claimed HCU and with a model fitted on public diversion records plus aerial imagery. If decreed/claimed clusters tightly around 0.8-0.9 regardless of inputs, the hidden state is small and the candidate is a 1.2x business. Also check broker listings: if ditch shares are quoted per acre-foot of engineered HCU, the market already prices the hidden state.

**Biggest doubt.** The proving step usually succeeds; the real risk is political (anti-speculation doctrine, county lawsuits, NEPA), which ML does not predict. OpenET is actively erasing the illegibility.

## 3. Mexico ejido land -> dominio pleno on nearshoring corridors (fit 5/10)

**Proving step.** Adoption of dominio pleno (full private ownership) by a qualified ejido assembly (quorum 75%+1 of rights-holders, with a Procuraduría Agraria representative and a notary present), registration at the Registro Agrario Nacional, then issuance of a private title that can be sold to anyone, including a FIBRA or a foreign manufacturer via a fideicomiso (https://ccmss.org.mx/wp-content/uploads/2023_02_GradosPrivatizacion.pdf). Only 3.4% of the 1991 social-property area had become private by 2021 (~3.5M ha, my calculation); 1.21M ha of parcelled land converted 2010-2021; 882 dominio pleno procedures approved in 2020. Cost: assembly organisation, surveying, notarial fees and side payments; time 1-3 years (my estimate; not found in sources). Hit rate unknown; many ejidos vote no or are blocked by internal disputes at the Tribunales Agrarios.

**Signal.** RAN's per-ejido registry (PHINA: certification status, number of ejidatarios, parcelled area), assembly minutes (actas) in RAN archives (scanned), agrarian tribunal rulings (PDF), PROCEDE/FANAR certification maps (92.4M ha certified across 30,434 núcleos agrarios). The hidden state is "will this assembly vote for conversion and is the parcel chain clean", which lives in minutes and litigation files, not in a status field.

**Unproven right.** Certificados parcelarios (parcel rights). Legally transferable only to other ejidatarios or avecindados of the same ejido, so an outsider must become an avecindado, buy through a local nominee, or hold a promise-of-sale contract with an irrevocable power of attorney (from memory; this is the standard and risky practice). Foreigners are restricted near borders and coasts. Listing evidence of the unproven price with industrial feasibility: 18.7 ha of ejidal land in Mexicali asking $25/m² ($250k/ha); 96 ha of ejido parcels in Querétaro, half zoned industrial, asking MXN 650/m² (~$35/m²) (https://www.pincali.com/en/home/18-5-hectareas-en-venta-mexicali, https://www.pincali.com/en/home/terreno-industrial-en-venta-en-queretaro-santa-rosa-de-jauregui-queretaro). Plain ejidal farmland away from corridors trades at $1-5/m² (my estimate).

**Proven state.** Serviced industrial land in the north at $60-150/m² (from memory) with under 2% availability in northern Mexico and 18-24 months to develop a park (Colliers/AMPIP via Reforma: https://comercial.reforma.com/libre/comercial/campanas/Industrialparks_23/1.Demand_for_industrial_land_is_growing_in_northern_Mexico.html). Buyers: Prologis, Vesta, Fibra Uno, Fibra Macquarie, Finsa, Grupo Frisa and the park developers that list on AMPIP. Titled land trades before any park is built.

**Market ceiling.** 99.5M ha of social property (50.7% of Mexico); the corridor-relevant slice (Monterrey-Saltillo, Bajío, Juárez, Tijuana-Mexicali) is hundreds of thousands of hectares, worth tens of $bn once titled (my estimate).

**Incumbents.** Local land assemblers and "coyotes", park developers' own land teams, agrarian law firms. No data-driven player found.

**Kill test.** Take RAN's dominio pleno approvals 2015-2024 by municipality, overlay the AMPIP park inventory and highway corridors; measure the share of corridor ejidos that have already converted. If most corridor ejidos converted years ago, the pool is picked over; if conversion is rare on the corridor, check three assembly minutes for whether the blocking variable is predictable or purely personal politics.

**Biggest doubt.** Pre-conversion rights are not legally purchasable by the company, and the practice around that is where the corruption and dispossession stories live.

## 4. Orphaned legacy IPv4 blocks, re-evaluated (fit 4/10)

Numbers: legacy space is ~81.8 /8s, about 1.37 billion addresses or 37% of routable space; only ~50% of it was routed in 2015, implying ~690M unrouted legacy addresses (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.2649). ARIN found 53% of ~25,000 legacy networks had no or unverified points of contact; RIPE found 730 unrouted /16s (~48M addresses) held by 400 organisations in 2010, of which only 160 updated records and 16 returned space (https://www.ipxo.com/blog/ip-legacy-space/). Price per address was roughly $30-35 in 2024-25, down from ~$55 in 2021 (from memory; not searched). A /16 is ~$2M, a /8 ~$500M.

Four-part test. (1) Proving step: an ARIN 8.2 merger/acquisition transfer proving an unbroken chain of legal succession from the 1990s registrant; ARIN accepts affidavits and indemnities where records are missing (Synoptek case, where the ImagiNet sale was never reported and the name was misspelled: https://ipv4.global/case-studies/synoptek). Cost $5-50k legal (my estimate), weeks to months; ARIN publishes no abandonment statistics (https://btw.media/en/arin-documentation-burden). (2) Signal: ARIN WHOIS and BGP routing tables are fully legible, so "unrouted legacy /16 with stale POC" is a trivial query every broker already runs; the illegible part is the successor chain through state corporate registries, probate files and bankruptcy dockets. (3) Unproven right: the successor entity's rights, bought from an estate, trustee or dormant shell for near zero because the owner does not know it owns anything. This is a bilateral purchase, not a tender, which answers the earlier hunt's objection; the earlier kill stands for blocks whose owners are alive and use brokers. (4) Proven state: hyperscalers and cloud providers buy via IPv4.Global, Hilco Streambank, Kalorama, Addrex; deep liquidity.

Ceiling: 300-600M unrouted legacy addresses times $30 is $9-18bn on paper, but the orphaned share with a findable successor is perhaps 10-20% (my estimate), so $1-3bn and falling as prices fall. Incumbents: the brokers' "IP hunters" already cold-call stale POCs; AFRINIC's Cloud Innovation scandal shows the policy risk of registries reclaiming space. Kill test: take 200 unrouted legacy /16s with invalid POCs from ARIN bulk WHOIS, look up the registrant in state corporate registries; count how many have a dissolved registrant with a traceable successor and no ARIN transfer-log entry. If fewer than 10%, the pool is too small; if brokers' names appear in the transfer log for most, it is hunted out.

## 5. Japan owner-unknown land (fit 4/10)

4.1M ha in 2016 (larger than Kyushu), projected 7.2M ha by 2040; ¥180bn annual loss in 2016 and ¥6tn cumulative 2017-2040 (Masuda committee: https://fpcj.jp/en/j_views-en/recent_editorials-en/p=60219, https://www.tokyofoundation.org/research/detail.php?id=680). Niseko: 2.3% of area unclaimed, 38% of ordinance notices undeliverable. Proving step: trace heirs through koseki family registers (paper, held by each municipality of each ancestor), obtain consents from dozens of co-heirs, or since 2023 petition the court for an owner-unknown-land manager empowered to sell; cost ¥0.3-3M and 1-3 years (my estimate). The unproven right is one identified heir's undivided share (purchasable; a co-owner can then demand partition, the Japanese analogue of US heirs' property). Proven state: clean title for solar, logistics, data centres in Hokkaido, or Niseko resort land. Incumbents: Katitas buys 3,451 houses a year directly from owners and resells at ¥10-15M (https://www.porterprize.org/english/pastwinner/2017/12/05174615.html), judicial scriveners, akiya banks. Doubt: most owner-unknown land is mountain forest with no proven value; the step-up exists only at growth nodes, which are few. Kill test: overlay the MLIT owner-unknown survey municipalities with solar/data-centre siting and Niseko-type resort zones; count parcels.

## 6. Rail corridor title: new numbers only (fit 4/10, unchanged)

14,184 miles abandoned 1983-2008 (STB), 56.8% negotiated for railbanking, only 35.8% actually railbanked, 64.2% never railbanked (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railbanking). About 85% of US rail rights-of-way were acquired by easement from abutting owners; Brandt v. US (2014) confirmed 1875-Act corridors revert to adjacent landowners (Stewart Title 2024 webinar: https://www.stewart.com/-/media/files/stewartdotcom/webinar-pdfs/2024/2024maywebinar.pdf). A single Olympia rail-trail needed 35-40 title reports plus a quiet-title action to get one consolidated policy. What a fibre occupant pays when the corridor turns out to be an easement: Norfolk Southern's settlement $6,000 per mile plus stock; AT&T's $1-145 per linear foot ($5k-$766k per mile) in ME, CT, IN, OH (https://www.photonics.com/a9730/Landowners_to_Consider_Fiber_Optics_Offer). No $/mile for SOO Green on CPKC or Grid United was found; title-opinion cost per mile not found (my estimate $2-10k/mile from abstracts). The 1875-Act reversion plus the AT&T settlement scale suggests the adjacent owner's reversionary interest is the cheap, clouded, bilaterally purchasable right; ICC valuation maps remain the illegible signal.

## 7. Romanian ANRP restitution points (fit 3/10)

About 5.7bn points (1 leu nominal each) after discounting, a ~€1bn market in 2017; 2.2bn points already issued (~€500M nominal); ~50,000 files pending, of which ~12,000 expected to be accepted (24%), averaging 290,000 points; points sold at 20% of nominal in 2013-14, 0.40 lei in late 2016, Alpha Quest's firm offer 0.50 lei (50% discount) for up to 400M points; the state pays 20% a year over five years; ANRP publishes no centralised data (https://profit.ro/stiri/social/noul-val-de-retrocedari-o-piata-de-un-miliard-de-euro-16626098, https://www.wall-street.ro/articol/Social/207644/cum-se-tranzactioneaza-punctele-de-despagubire-emise-de-anrp.html). Structure fits: claim files are purchasable by notarial assignment, acceptance is a 24% hit rate (lever ~4x), and the signal (1945-89 confiscation decrees, land books, court files) is illegible. Fails on ceiling: by 2026 most issued points have been paid and the remaining pool is a few hundred €M (my estimate); Poland closed Warsaw-decree claims in 2021 after the reprivatisation scandal. Incumbent: Alpha Quest (Malta).

## 8. California pre-1914 and riparian claims after SB 389 (fit 3/10)

Pre-1914 and riparian claims are self-reported, exempt from permitting, and the State Water Board "lacks comprehensive data"; SB 389 (2023) lets the Board investigate a claimant, puts the burden of proof on the claimant, and allows forfeiture findings without a competing claim (https://www.pcl.org/media/2023/06/SB-389-Surface-Water-Right-Verification-5.11.23.pdf). Claims statewide total several times actual runoff (Grantham & Viers 2014, from memory). The hidden state (does the 19th-century chain of notices, maps and continuous use exist?) is genuinely in scanned county-recorder books. But the Board, not the owner, initiates verification, pre-1914 rights are appurtenant to land, and out-of-watershed transfers need Board approval, so the proven option is hard to sell. Buyers of proven permanent water: Westlands-area and Kern districts at $3-9k/AF (from memory).

## 9. US heirs' property and tax-deed quiet title (fit 3/10)

3.5M acres of heirs' property across the South with ~$28bn assessed value, 1.6M acres and $6.6bn in Black Belt counties (https://www.atlantafed.org/news/pressreleases/atlantafed/2019/10/08/community-development-forest-service-publishes-heirs-property-land-fractionation-proceedings); $32.3bn identified by HAC/Fannie Mae 2023, up to 42% of property in some counties; average tangled-title resolution $9,200 (Pew, Philadelphia); UPHPA adopted in 23 states gives co-heirs first refusal and forces appraisal and open-market sale (https://www.lincolninst.edu/publications/land-lines-magazine/articles/understanding-heirs-property/). The mechanism (buy one heir's share, force partition) is exactly the dispossession the Act was written to stop; the only defensible version is a promoter model that funds title clearing for a share. Tax-deed properties are bought at auction (killed by the brief) and the quiet-title step is cheap ($1.5-5k, 3-12 months, >90% success, from memory), so the step-up is ~1.5-2x.

## 10. ITU NGSO priority filings (fit 3/10)

Resolution 35: bring into use within 7 years, then 10% of satellites at +2 years, 50% at +5, 100% at +7, with penalties reducing the notified constellation (https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/seminars/wrs/Documents/2024/Space-Workshops/Resolution%208%20and%2035.pdf). Rivada's Liechtenstein filing (576 satellites, $2.4bn Terran Orbital order) was withdrawn by the regulator in 2025 over a performance-bond dispute; a German filing covers 400 MHz of Ka-band (https://spacenews.com/?p=510113). The filing's value is real (OneWeb's priority was the asset Bharti and the UK government paid ~$1bn for in 2020, from memory) and most filings lapse (p well under 0.2, my estimate), but the proving step costs a constellation, the right belongs to an administration not the company, and the ITU filing database is legible. Dormant trademarks and revivable abandoned applications fail outright: cancellation and revival are cheap and first-come.

## 11. Small-entity and bankruptcy patents (fit 2/10)

FY2025 PTAB: 1,433 petitions, 58% instituted by petition (54% by patent); of 387 final written decisions 65% found all claims unpatentable, 19% mixed, 16% none; 32% settled (https://prod-web-alb-tg.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/Trial_StatsFY25_Q4.pdf). So a litigated patent fully survives IPR ~16-35% of the time (lever 3-6x) and the step-up on a win is 10x+ over brokered prices (~$100-250k per asset, from memory, Richardson Oliver data). Fails part 2: Lex Machina, Docket Navigator, Unified Patents and PatentAdvisor already sell exactly this prediction, and Fortress, Burford and the NPEs act on it. Also reputationally poisonous for a YC pitch.

## 12. Bona vacantia and escheat land (fit 2/10)

Land of dissolved companies passes to the Crown; the Bona Vacantia Division sells only at full market value with no title guarantee, or disclaims it into escheat where the Crown Estate "is not obliged to sell" (https://www.freeths.co.uk/insights-events/legal-articles/2025/understanding-bona-vacantia-ownerless-land-and-key-issues-for-developers/, https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/bona-vacantia-dissolved-companies-bvc1/bona-vacantia-dissolved-companies-bvc1). The join that finds it (HMLR's corporate-ownership CSV against Companies House dissolutions) is legible, and the seller sets the price. UK unregistered land is ~10% of England and Wales (https://www.farmersguide.co.uk/?p=120746) but first registration is cheap, so no proving step; UK ransom strips (Stokes v Cambridge, a third of the uplift) are a cottage industry with no quantifiable pool.

## 13. One-liners (fit 1-3)

Australia's water market: entitlements are registered securities on public state registers with published prices, no proving step (not searched; from memory). Carbon rights on Australian land: the proving step (ACCU project registration) is cheap and GreenCollar/Climate Friendly already optimise it with satellite data. NYC air rights/TDR: MapPLUTO publishes built and allowed FAR, so unused development rights are a lookup field. Copyright termination (17 USC 203/304): the termination right cannot be assigned before it vests, non-transferable. Bankruptcy claims trading: PACER is illegible but Contrarian, Cherokee and dozens of funds already price claims; not searched. Chile's 2022 Water Code reform forces registration of unregistered rights on a deadline with extinction for non-use (from memory, unverified); potentially a real "regularise then sell to miners" play but not researched. Footnote per the brief: Nevada-style applications for unappropriated groundwater (Vidler's model) are first-come applications and are excluded.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. Adjudication is not drilling. Title steps settle; p is 0.5-0.9 almost everywhere, so the lever is 1.1-2x. The exceptions with low p (restitution 24%, IPR survival 16-35%, ITU filings <20%) have small pools, political owners or already-legible data.
2. Regulators are writing the answer down. OpenET, HydroBase, the Housing Delivery Test, HMLR's corporate dataset, ARIN WHOIS plus BGP: in each case the cheap half of the signal is now a table, leaving ML only the residual (pre-2016 water records, appeal-decision reasoning, corporate successor chains, assembly minutes).
3. The two with a credible path to a large company are land promotion (UK, and the US entitlement analogue) and water, because the proven asset trades at scale to named buyers before anything is built and the step-up is 20-90x (land) or 2-5x on very large dollars (water). Both have well-funded incumbents without an ML edge.
4. The ethically clean version of every "clouded title" play is the promoter model: fund the proving step for a contracted share of the uplift. The predatory versions (heirs' property partition, ejido nominees, Warsaw decree claims) are the ones that got legislated away.
