# Hunt 9, Sweep E: legal and financial claims, credits and entitlements as purchasable assets

Method: ~45 WebSearch calls plus ~45 WebFetch reads (the shared search budget ran out before the last six queries; the gaps they would have filled are marked "unknown" or "(my estimate)"). Numbers carry a URL where I found one. No invented transactions.

Funding cost per case: not disclosed by any of the three; one established funder spends $75-100k on due diligence per claim ([Yale LJ note](https://www.yalelawjournal.org/note/the-growth-of-litigation-finance-in-doj-whistleblower-suits-implications-and-recommendations)).

Conclusion for the drill test: funded litigation has p = 0.65-0.9, so the ML lever is capped at about 1.1-1.5x. The brief's class-imbalance structure (p of a few percent, price of the unproven right near zero) exists only where claims are SOLD CHEAP by holders who have written them off: unenforced judgments, catastrophe subrogation, awards against sovereigns, trade claims in messy estates. Those are the classes examined below.

## 1. Unenforced civil money judgments bought at pennies, enforced with public-record asset discovery (NEW) - 6/10

1. PROVING STEP: post-judgment enforcement: asset discovery (property, bank, business, wages), then liens, levies, garnishment, debtor exams. Cost $1-10k per judgment (my estimate: attorney, skip-trace, sheriff fees), 6-24 months. Base rate: "80% of U.S. civil money judgments, year after year, continue to be unenforced" ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/voice/article/why-do-80-of-judgments-remain-uncollected)); Philadelphia sample: 13% of judgments satisfied two years after filing (11.6% of defaults) ([Fels capstone](https://www.fels.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2023-04/Default-Justice_McGlazer-Capstone_FINAL.pdf)); DOJ collects 22-28% of federal judgments ([Senate PSI](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/?p=107550)). So p ~ 0.15-0.25, lever cap 4-7x. Paid today by the creditor or a contingency enforcement firm.
2. SIGNAL: county civil dockets (3,000+ counties, many scanned, no national judgment register) joined to assessor rolls and recorder deed images, UCC filings, Secretary of State officer lists, professional licences, PPP/SBA loan lists, vehicle liens, other dockets (the debtor as plaintiff elsewhere), probate. Public, scattered, needs OCR and entity resolution. No regulator writes "collectible" into a field. Big caveat: credit bureaus (private, legible) already hold most of this for consumers, which is why consumer debt buyers do not need ML; the illegible residue is COMMERCIAL judgments against small businesses and their principals.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT: the judgment itself. Holders: small businesses, landlords, contractors, individuals, plus debt buyers. Assignment of judgment is routine (filed with the court; FDCPA and state collection licensing apply to consumer paper). Prices: a ~$1,100 judgment listed for $25 (2c), a $16.5M judgment listed at 73c ([Judgment Marketplace](https://smallbiztrends.com/judgment-marketplace/)); typical range unknown. Bilateral, exclusive, and sellers generally have not run a property search (that is why it is unenforced).
4. PROVEN STATE: cash at 100c plus post-judgment interest (6% PA cap; 10% CA). R1 for a hit: 10-50x at 2-10c purchase; blended at p=0.2 roughly 2-10x. A judgment with a located asset can be resold to collection law firms at 40-70c (thin market), so the proven option is tradable but mostly you collect yourself (cheap "build").

MARKET CEILING: $97B unpaid federal judgments alone ([Senate PSI](https://www.hsgac.senate.gov/?p=107550)); New Jersey courts entered ~140,000 debt-buyer judgments/yr; ~4M collection suits/yr nationally ([Fels](https://www.fels.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/2023-04/Default-Justice_McGlazer-Capstone_FINAL.pdf)). Unenforced commercial judgment face value: unknown, my estimate >$100B. INCUMBENTS: Encore/Midland and PRA (consumer, bureau data), small judgment-recovery shops, JudgmentMarketplace, Recovery of Judgment; no ML-scale commercial judgment buyer found (search budget ended before confirming). KILL TEST: pull 500 commercial judgments >$25k from one county's online civil index, match debtor names to the assessor roll and Secretary of State; if >60% of debtors with recorded real estate already show a recorded abstract/satisfaction, creditors are already using the public signal; also check whether Judgment Marketplace asking prices track assessor-matched equity (if they do, sellers price the signal). BIGGEST DOUBT: reputational (debt collection) and whether private bureau data makes the "illegible" join legible for incumbents.

## 2. Wetland, stream and species mitigation banking (on list) - 5/10

1. PROVING STEP: Interagency Review Team approval of a bank (prospectus -> MBI) then credit release on performance. Time: 1,099 days average full approval (496 banks, FY2014-21; Corps-responsible 336 days vs the 225-day rule) ([EPIC](https://www.policyinnovation.org/blog/new-research-finds-that-wetland-and-stream-mitigation-banks-take-over-1000-days-to-approve)); 1,149-1,195 days in the 2024 update, slowest-quartile districts >1,506 days, rising 18-27 days/yr ([EPIC 2024](https://policyinnovation.org/blog/mbi-timelines-2024-update)). Prospectus to full credit release 5-10 yrs; ~15% of credits released at signing ([guide](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/09/27/wetland-mitigation-banking-section-404-credits-guide)). Cost: no published figure; design/permitting/construction $30-80k per restored acre (my estimate). p: no denominator published (EPIC's data is approved banks only); professional sponsors mostly get approved eventually, my estimate p = 0.6-0.8, lever cap 1.3-1.7x. The bigger "failure" is demand: CA conservation banks took 2.9 yrs average to final sale, some 12 yrs; 12 of 26 CA banks had negative NPV and with land cost only 3 of 8 were positive ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/22/12441/html)).
2. SIGNAL: RIBITS (public; credit ledgers, service areas, availability; NO prices; rumoured stale), 404 permit decisions by district (PDF), NWI, SSURGO hydric soils, LiDAR, parcel data; agency bid results require FOIA ([Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/opinion-nine-steps-towards-doubling-the-value-of-us-mitigation-markets/)). Prices assembled by EASI from "bid results, contracts, news stories" into a private database. Partly legible: soils/NWI are lookup layers; the illegible part is demand (permit pipeline in PDFs) and price.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT: degraded farm/ranch land in a high-demand service area, $2-15k/acre (MDPI land-cost mean $9,557/acre, range $156-51,450), bought bilaterally from farmers who do not read RIBITS.
4. PROVEN STATE: credits sell to developers/DOTs: wetland $8,600/acre (MS) to $675,000 (NJ), VA ~$263k/credit, FL $125-300k, Sacramento ILF ~$500k ([EASI](https://www.easillc.com/2026/06/22/2026-review-u-s-mitigation-credit-marketplace-nears-500-billion/)); 10-yr average $95k, ~11,000 wetland credits/yr = >$1B/yr ([Forest Trends](https://www.forest-trends.org/articles/opinion-nine-steps-towards-doubling-the-value-of-us-mitigation-markets/)); CA vernal pool preservation ~$325k/credit (Placer County 2014, [doc](https://www.placer.ca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/22468/18a-PDF)) but CA species credits median only $554/acre ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/13/22/12441/html)). Only 5-20% of bank acres yield wetland credits (Van Vleck: 43 vernal-pool credits on 765 acres, [PDF](https://www.conservationfund.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/Van_Vleck_Mitigation_Bank.pdf)), so R1 on the whole parcel is ~3-10x gross. Approved banks trade before sell-out (EIP funds, RES/Onex, Westervelt, Wildlands), so the proven option is sellable.

MARKET CEILING: >$1B/yr wetland credits; EASI claims ~$8B/yr average across types and $130B remaining inventory (their own valuation method; treat with caution). INCUMBENTS: Ecosystem Investment Partners, RES, Westervelt Ecological, Wildlands, GreenVest, Mitigation Resources; GIS-literate, no known ML. KILL TEST: in RIBITS for Jacksonville or Norfolk district, list banks approved 2015-20, compute years-to-sell-out vs permits issued in the service area; if sell-out time is predictable from the ledger alone, demand is legible and already priced; count "pending" vs "approved" banks to estimate p; if p >0.7, kill. BIGGEST DOUBT: p is high and the hidden state is half in lookup layers; the money is in demand forecasting, which incumbents already do by hand.

## 3. Data-centre / industrial land: power plus entitlement (seen; new numbers) - 5/10

Northern Virginia entitled-but-unpowered land ~$4M/acre vs powered >=$6M/acre (1.5x); sites on 230/500kV traded 2.4x sites on <=138kV (Altus, 80 deals); rural land ~$10k/acre reaches six figures with a power path; $6B of US data-centre land bought in H1 2026 (+79%) ([DCK](https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/ai-data-center-land-buying-hits-6b-as-power-premiums-rise)). Residential land medians $94-125k/acre vs $3.5-6.3M/acre for data-centre deals in the same counties (28-50x); Texas Rt-67 $20-40k -> $350k+/acre (9-17x) ([NAHB](https://www.nahb.org/blog/2026/07/servers-over-shelter-ai-data-centers-outbidding-home-builders-for-land)). The step-up is the largest in this sweep, but the proving step is the interconnection queue (seen: GridCARE, Cloverleaf, Tract, PowerHouse) plus rezoning. No new angle found beyond the numbers; kept for comparison.

## 4. Catastrophe subrogation claims (NEW) - 4/10

1. PROVING: litigation against the at-fault utility or manufacturer; 2-5 yrs; for CA utility wildfires inverse condemnation makes liability near-strict once origin is attributed; insurers and buyers recovered 87% of insured payments on PG&E 2017-18 fires ([Banham](https://www.russbanham.com/?p=1726)). Generic product subrogation: ~70% of pursued files recover something, ~20% of loss dollars ([Canadian Underwriter](https://www.canadianunderwriter.ca/news/claims/insurers-leaving-hundreds-of-millions-on-the-table-by-not-pursuing-subrogation-claims)). 2. SIGNAL: Cal Fire/LAFD origin reports, CPUC filings, utility 8-Ks, dockets, CPSC/NHTSA incident data. The price is set by the attribution: Palisades claims ~3c, Eaton (SCE named) ~60c ([Hedgeweek](https://hedgeweek.com/news/hedge-funds-up-investments-in-la-wildfire-claims)). The only ML edge is predicting attribution before the public report (ignition point vs line geometry, VIIRS timing). 3. UNPROVEN RIGHT: subrogation rights sold by mid-size insurers under capital pressure: CSAA sold $1B at <=35c; Hippo's floor ~40c; some "pennies on the dollar". Sellers CAN read the data; the discount is a liquidity price. 4. PROVEN STATE: Baupost put $6.8B into PG&E subro for ~$3B profit (1.44x); 35c -> 87c = 2.5x. Pool: LA fires ~$40B insured losses; episodic. INCUMBENTS: Baupost, unnamed hedge funds; assignability contested (Hawaii barred suits vs utility). KILL TEST: did Palisades prices move only after the official origin report? If yes, the hidden state is revealed by the regulator and the edge is a race. DOUBT: informed sellers, R1 <3x, episodic.

## 5. Art "sleepers" (NEW, outside the listed classes) - 4/10

Proving: attribution (cleaning, IRR/X-ray/dendro, scholar or committee opinion); cost $5-50k (my estimate; sources give none), 6-24 months; p unknown but low. Signal: images and provenance text in thousands of regional auction catalogues, catalogues raisonnes, RKD, Getty Provenance Index; illegible at scale (image connoisseurship). Examples: Stubbs pair GBP840 -> GBP88,000 (105x); Chardin GBP1.15M -> $7.5-10M (6-8x) ([Duke](https://law.duke.edu/news/when-sleepers-awake-how-law-governs-sellers-regret-art-market)); "Circle of Rembrandt" est. EUR10-15k, sold EUR860k, resold as Rembrandt GBP10.9M (12.7x) ([AHN](https://arthistorynews.com/articles/8668_Sleeper_alert_ctd)). Pool: ~$25-30B/yr global auction sales (my recollection); sleeper share unknown. Incumbents: dealers (Mould, Moretti), Art Recognition (AI service). Fails the brief's auction rule half-way: the owner does not run the price, uninformed bidders do, but sleeper hunters compete at the same sale. DOUBT: subjective proof, no path to many billions.

## 6. International arbitration awards against sovereigns (on list) - 3/10

Compliance: of 151 ICSID damages awards, 66% paid voluntarily or settled; enforcement pursued on 31%; of enforcement actions with known outcomes 73% succeed; 97% of awards with known outcome satisfied ([Freshfields/ICSID](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/icsid-background-paper-on-compliance-and-enforcement-of-awards-valuable-insights-102jh8j)) -> p high, cap ~1.1x, survivorship aside. Discounts: an $800M award changing hands at 10c (illustrative, [EMTA](https://dev.emta.org/media/0otl4ymd/soveregin-awards-untapped-yield-for-the-intrepid-investor-september-2020.pdf)); Rockhopper monetised a EUR190M award for EUR45M upfront + EUR65M contingent, then annulled ([IISD](https://www.iisd.org/itn/2026/09/09/unregulated-rise-of-award-monetization-lukas-schaugg/)). Assignability under attack (OperaFund v Spain, Blasket v Spain, 2025, on appeal). Sellers are listed companies who can price; buyers Tenor, Gramercy, Burford, Blasket, Elliott. Pool: single awards $1-11B (P&ID, Tethyan, Yukos) but unknown aggregate. Occupied by hedge funds; informed sellers.

## 7. Bankruptcy trade claims (on list) - 3/10

Claims register is public and structured (Kroll/Epiq/Stretto), so the "signal" is legible; prices rise with competition in large cases ([Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/media/6418/nathanpluscargill-a-primer-on-selling-bankruptcy-trade-claims-business-credit-22021.pdf)). Illustrative 20c purchase vs 85% distribution (4x). Risks: disallowance, preference, setoff (buyer gets repurchase rights). p high once a plan is visible. Buyers: Cherokee, Argo, Hain, Contrarian, Jefferies, Seaport. Sellers (small vendors) cannot price, but buyers already canvass every register. FTX claim price history unverified (budget).

## 8. EU cartel damages claims, CDC model (NEW) - 3/10

CDC "purchased and bundles" claims of 700+ companies in the trucks cartel, redistributes >65% of damages, no-win-no-fee, overcharge ~10%, suit filed 2017, still running ([CDC](https://carteldamageclaims.com/on-going-cases/)). Signal that nobody extracts: public procurement records (TED, national portals) identify who bought cartelised goods. But liability is fixed by the Commission decision, so p on liability is high; uncertainty is quantum and pass-on; 8+ year duration. Revenue share, not purchase.

## 9. Residential land entitlement, California (on list) - 3/10

237 projects, 2014-16: SF median 23 months, Oakland 7, San Jose EIR 24; EIR share 2-20% by city; denial and litigation rates not reported ([Getting It Right](https://www.law.berkeley.edu/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Getting_It_Right.pdf)). Entitlement "can double value overnight" (unquantified, [Zuegel](https://devonzuegel.com/land-development-math-is-alchemy)); raw-to-entitled multiple industry rule of thumb 2-5x (my estimate, unsourced). p high (most applications eventually approved) so cap ~1.3x; signal partly in city planning databases. Incumbents: Walton, IHP, Forestar, Starwood Land, Hearthstone.

## 10. Medical lien / letter-of-protection receivables - 3/10
Buyers exist (funding companies), resolution 2-3 yrs ([Becker's](https://www.beckersspine.com/orthopedic-spine-practices-improving-profits/selling-lien-lop-receivables-what-orthopedic-surgeons-and-practices-should-know/)); the underlying PI docket is public but the receivable is private and providers already have funders; discounts unknown; transparency attacks ([WLF](https://wlf.org/2020/08/20/publishing/greater-transparency-can-expose-the-illusion-of-medical-receivable-funding-in-tort-litigation)).

## 11. Carbon and biodiversity credits (on list) - 2/10
Verra fees are trivial (registration $0.10/t capped $10k; issuance $0.05-0.14/VCU, [fee schedule](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-program-fee-schedule-v4-2)); VVB audit $30-100k (my estimate). Only 95 of 2,485 Verra projects later rejected or suspended (3.8%) ([Verra](https://verra.org/verra-views/beyond-the-headlines-a-constructive-response-to-criticism-of-validation-and-verification-in-the-vcm/)), so validation rarely "fails"; the failure mode is integrity and price collapse (West et al.: ~10% of REDD+ credits real). Carbon stock is cheaply measured by LiDAR by incumbents (Anew, Finite, NCX, Chestnut, Aurora Sustainable Lands, Pachama). No class imbalance; fails part 1.

## 12. Government contract claims, ASBCA/COFC (on list) - 2/10
Anti-Assignment Act (31 USC 3727) bars buying claims against the US; only funding. ASBCA FY22: 400 appeals filed, 142 decided on merits, 71% sustained in whole or part (52.7-52.8% prior years), majority of 255 dismissals were settlements ([Pillsbury](https://pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/asbca-fy-2022-annual-report.html)). p high. Legalist already lends here.

## 13. Qui tam (on list) - 2/10
Relator share is arguably partially assignable, funders use non-recourse loans; DOJ declined 78% of qui tams (1987-2004) and courts dismissed 92% of declined suits ([Yale LJ](https://www.yalelawjournal.org/note/the-growth-of-litigation-finance-in-doj-whistleblower-suits-implications-and-recommendations)) so p is low, but cases are sealed (no public signal), first-to-file and original-source rules stop buying before filing. Fails part 3.

## 14. Unclaimed property / escheat (on list) - 2/10
California holds $15.0B across 84M properties; finder fee capped at 10%, 12-month waiting period, agreements voidable for "unjust consideration" ([ungovr CA](https://www.ungovr.org/unclaimed/ca)). Non-exclusive race, legible name search. Corporate-revival variant (buy a dissolved company's shell to claim its escheated assets) is legally fragile; not pursued.

## 15. Zombie second mortgages - 2/10
Buyers (e.g. ARC Private Equity) paid "pennies on the dollar"; MA data: 31% of second-mortgage foreclosures had balances above origination, 89% average balance growth on the worst cohort ([NCLC](https://www.nclc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/202510_Fact-Sheet_MA-Zombie-Mortgage.pdf), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/transcripts/nx-s1-4973763)). Signal (county records + AVMs) is legible, incumbents active, legislation closing in. Occupied and toxic.

## 16. Cautionary analog: MSP Recovery (LifeWallet)
Took assignment of Medicare Advantage recovery rights with $1.5T billed / $364B paid value, paying assignors 50% of recoveries ([SEC 425](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1802450/000110465922062191/tm2215834d1_425.htm)); recoveries proved tiny and the SPAC collapsed. Lesson: a huge pool of cheaply assigned claims without a legible, affordable proving step is worth nothing.

## 17. Rights with NO proving step (fail part 1 by construction)
- IRA transferable tax credits: $42B transferred in 2025; ITC 93-94c, PTC 94-95c, 45Z 90.8c, 45U 96.2c ([Crux](https://www.crux.com/blog/cruxs-2025-market-intelligence-report-what-tax-credit-buyers-need-to-know)). The credit is earned by the project; buyer risk (recapture, PFE) is insured. No uncertain step.
- TDRs/air rights: transfer is legible (ACRIS); the only uncertain step (landmark transfer special permit, zoning lot merger) is run by sophisticated owners and brokers. Fail.
- ITQs (fishing quotas), taxi medallions, liquor licences, agricultural base acres, grazing permits, airport slots: defined rights, transfer at posted or auction prices, no hidden state revealed by an expensive step. Fail. (Water skipped per brief.)
- Life settlements, structured settlements, pre-settlement funding, securities class-action claim filing (2-3% of losses, service), royalty audits (contingency service), construction liens (assignable but no hidden state beyond priority), tax refund/ERC claims (IRS processing, no public signal): all fail on one of parts 1-3.
- Patents: sweep B; the brief's logic applies (claims bought cheap, proven by litigation with p ~0.3-0.5 and $3-5M cost) but incumbents (Acacia, Intellectual Ventures, Fortress) are dense.
- Footnote (excluded): treasure/shipwreck salvage and mineral-royalty underpayment audits are prospecting-adjacent.

1. Unenforced commercial judgments: 6/10. 2. Mitigation banking: 5/10. 3. Data-centre/industrial entitlement: 5/10 (seen). 4. Catastrophe subrogation: 4/10. 5. Art sleepers: 4/10. 6-10. Sovereign awards, trade claims, cartel claims, residential entitlement, medical liens: 3/10. 11-15. Carbon, government contract claims, qui tam, unclaimed property, zombie seconds: 2/10. Everything in section 17: 0-1/10.
