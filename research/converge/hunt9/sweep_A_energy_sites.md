# Hunt 9, Sweep A: Energy and grid sites (worldwide)

Method: 31 web searches (the shared per-turn search budget ran out at that point) plus about 40 page fetches. Numbers are from the cited pages unless marked "(my estimate)" or "(from memory)". Drilling-proved candidates (geothermal, oil/gas, lithium, hydrogen, CO2, salt caverns) are excluded per the standing rule; see the one-line footnote at the end.

## Verdict in one paragraph

No energy-site candidate scores above 5/10 on the drill test. The cluster fails in one of three recurring ways: (a) the proving step is a permit that mostly succeeds once a serious applicant files, so the base hit rate p is high and the lever cap 1/p is small; the failures that do occur are financial (no offtaker, capex), which site data cannot predict; (b) the unproven right is a first-come federal permit (FERC preliminary permit) or a utility-owned site, not an exclusive right you can buy from an owner who cannot read the data; (c) the regulator or a national lab has already published the resource ranking in a legible table (DOE's 2012 ranking of 54,000 non-powered dams, DOE/INL's 2022 list of 315 coal sites, the ANU pumped-hydro atlas, utility hosting-capacity maps). The two candidates with the right structure and a large pool are (1) large-load "powered land" positions for data centres, where the land step-up is 3-14x and p is about 0.28, but the field is occupied by Tract, Cloverleaf, GridCARE and the hyperscalers, and (2) closed-loop pumped-storage licence flipping, where permit-to-licence conversion is about 5% and licensed projects do sell to CIP, Statkraft, NextEra and J-Power, but the proven pool is sub-$1B per decade and the hidden state is political and financial rather than physical.

## Candidate write-ups

### 1. Powered land: large-load interconnection positions for data centres (fit 5)

PROVING STEP. A large-load interconnection request, study and energy-services agreement with a utility or ISO. Cost: Plug Power's Graham, Texas site (164 MW) carried a $6.5M deposit with Oncor plus a $7.75M letter of credit under ERCOT's Batch Zero, i.e. about $40-90k/MW of deposits (my calculation from datacenterknowledge.com); time 1-3 years (my estimate). Hit rate: Wood Mackenzie counts 1,066 GW of US data-centre power requests, of which 28% is "likely to be committed" (ohiomfg.com citing WoodMac); Jefferies finds 187 GW of reported utility commitments but only 69 GW of formal ESAs; ERCOT's large-load queue exceeds 225 GW and "speculative requests run several times actual builds" (latitudemedia.com). So p is about 0.1-0.3, lever cap 3-10. Who pays: the site developer.

SIGNAL. Distribution hosting-capacity maps (legible, but distribution-level only); transmission headroom lives in FERC Form 715 base cases (CEII, restricted) and in thousands of ISO interconnection study PDFs (bus-level constraint tables, illegible at scale); utility IRPs and transmission plans (PDF); EIA-860/411. Altus Group's analysis of 80 transactions found sites tied to 230 kV or 500 kV infrastructure trade at 2.4x sites tied to 138 kV or lower, so the market already prices voltage class; the hidden state is headroom at a given substation and the utility's willingness to serve.

UNPROVEN RIGHT. An option on rural land near a high-voltage substation. Land "that might sell for about $10,000/acre without a viable data centre power path can reach six figures/acre once power infrastructure is established" (datacenterknowledge.com, Avison Young). Farmers cannot read ISO study PDFs. Exclusive and buyable.

PROVEN STATE. Buyers: hyperscalers, neoclouds, "energy-first developers". Named comps: Stream Data Centers buying Plug's Graham site for $50M base + $26.5M contingent (about $305-466k/MW, my calculation); SDC Twin Creeks, Leesburg VA, $615M for 97 acres ($6.3M/acre); an AWS Northern Virginia campus sold for $700M versus $51M the seller paid to assemble it in 2021-22 (13.7x, Bisnow); Amazon paid $120M for 44 acres that sold for $39.5M in 2021 (3x). Northern Virginia: entitled unpowered $4M/acre vs powered at least $6M/acre. Proven positions trade before build (land plus ESA is the product). R1 = 3x to 14x on land in hot markets, 10x+ in cheap markets.

MARKET CEILING. $6B of US data-centre land purchases in H1 2026 (+79% y/y); Q1 2026 $3.3B (+141%), 16 sites. Goldman: US data-centre power demand 31 GW (2025) to 66 GW (2027). Many billions.

INCUMBENTS. Tract (buys land, secures power and entitlements, sells to hyperscalers), Cloverleaf Infrastructure, GridCARE (AI on grid headroom), Crusoe, Verrus, Stream, CRG, plus every utility economic-development team and brokers (Avison Young, Colliers). The brief's seen-list already flags this cluster as probably occupied; the new angle here is load-side rather than generation-queue positions, but that is precisely Tract's and Cloverleaf's business.

KILL TEST (days). Take one ISO (SPP or MISO) whose interconnection study reports are public PDFs, extract bus-level headroom for 50 substations, and check county assessor land prices within 3 miles of each against prices 10+ miles away. If the land market already prices the headroom (slope visible in assessor data), the signal is not illegible and the candidate dies.

### 2. US closed-loop pumped-storage licence flipping (fit 5)

PROVING STEP. A FERC original licence for a new pumped-storage project. Cost "can run into the millions or tens of millions of dollars" (Van Ness Feldman); Goldendale (1.2 GW) took five and a half years from application (June 2020) to licence (January 2026) (canarymedia.com). Hit rate: the FERC pipeline held 51 PSH projects / 39 GW in 2014, 49 in 2015 (43 with issued permits), 67 / 52 GW at end-2019, 96 / 91 GW at end-2022, 70 / 60.6 GW at end-2024, 68 / 59 GW in early 2026 with 4 licensed (DOE/ORNL market reports). Licences issued since 2014: Iowa Hill (400 MW, later dropped by SMUD, from memory), Eagle Mountain (1,300 MW, 2014), Gordon Butte (400 MW, 2016), Swan Lake (393 MW, 2019), Goldendale (1,200 MW, 2026). Roughly 5 licences from well over 100 distinct permit holders across 12 years, with the pipeline churning ("additions outpaced removals in every year except 2015"), so permit-to-licence is about 5% (my estimate). Zero large PSH has been built in the US since 1995; "many pumped storage licenses issued in recent decades never attracted financing and were not built" (VNF). Lever cap on the permit stage about 20.

SIGNAL. Topography is fully legible (the ANU global atlas of about 616,000 closed-loop sites, from memory). The illegible residue: FERC eLibrary PDFs on why permits died, state water-right records (often scanned), BLM land status, eagle and cultural-resource constraints (Goldendale had to buy 277 acres of golden-eagle habitat), transmission headroom (CEII). Premium Energy Holdings' Tehachapi filing had no identified water source; its 2020 Isabella permit expired in 2024 and it refiled; "no Premium project has advanced to a license" (sjvwater.org). The regulator's legible field (permit status) does not say why.

UNPROVEN RIGHT. A FERC preliminary permit is first-come and free, and FERC cancels permits held without diligence (it cancelled five of Rye's Kentucky/Green River permits in 2016 for "site banking"). The real exclusive right is a private land option on the reservoir sites plus water rights; Eagle Crest bought the 9,500-acre Kaiser Eagle Mountain mine site from CIL&D in 2015, after licensing; Goldendale sits on a 680-acre site. Ranchland options are cheap and owners cannot read the data. Water rights are the usual hidden killer in the West.

PROVEN STATE. Licensed projects do trade before build: CIP (Copenhagen Infrastructure IV) acquired Swan Lake (licensed 2019) and Goldendale (application filed) from the Rye/National Grid JV in November 2020, price undisclosed; CIP made a "significant equity investment" in Gordon Butte in 2019, undisclosed; NextEra took over Eagle Crest (from memory, 2016), with FERC extending Eagle Mountain's construction deadlines to 2020/2023 in 2019; Statkraft bought ILI's consented 450 MW Red John in December 2023, undisclosed; J-Power bought Genex (Kidston 250 MW in a former gold pit) for about A$380M equity, more than A$1B EV. Capex: Gordon Butte $986M for 400 MW; Goldendale $2-3B for 1.2 GW. A licence is plausibly worth 1-5% of capex to a CIP, i.e. $20-100M for a GW-scale project (my estimate). R1 versus the land option alone is 20-50x (my estimate), but versus total development spend ($10-30M) it is 1-5x.

MARKET CEILING. 59-91 GW of US pipeline, about $150-200B of build; licence-value pool if all were licensed $1.5-7.5B, realistically $0.5-1B per decade (my estimates). Mid-niche. The UK's LDES cap-and-floor (Ofgem, 2025) is improving the proven-state economics there.

INCUMBENTS. Rye Development, rPlus Hydro (Seminoe, White Pine), Absaroka Energy, Daybreak Power, GridFlex, Premium Energy Holdings (serial permit filer), ILI Group and Gilkes Energy in Scotland, Genex/J-Power in Australia, buyers CIP, NextEra, Statkraft, SSE.

KILL TEST. From FERC eLibrary, list every PSH preliminary permit issued 2008-2018 and its fate; if the licensed ones are explained by legible facts (sponsor type, existing lower reservoir, BLM vs private land), there is no illegible signal. Second check: ask whether any licensed PSH other than Goldendale has reached construction by 2026; if none, the "proven state" is a licence that buyers discount heavily.

### 3. German onshore wind project rights, BImSchG permit as the proving step (NEW, fit 4.5)

PROVING STEP. The BImSchG permit. Development cost to permit: mean 70 EUR/kW, 80% of projects at or below 115 EUR/kW, excluding land payments (FA Wind 2015), i.e. 2-3.5M EUR for a 30 MW project; duration: about 4 years from project start to permit (2015 study); formal permit procedures lengthened from 14.5 months (2011-15) to 23 months (2017-22) and application-to-commissioning to 43 months (FA Wind 2022). Hit rate once filed is high: in the 2015 sample only 10 of 698 turbines were "discontinued" (1.4%); the failures happen earlier, at land and regional-plan stage. Blockers (2019 survey): 1,140 turbines / 4,789 MW blocked by DVOR/VOR aviation beacons, about 900 turbines / 3,600 MW by military airspace, 9,400 MW blocked in total; about 17-20% of permitted-unbuilt MW under litigation, average 21.6 months, 48% on bird/bat grounds. Lever cap near 1 on the permit itself; maybe 2-3 on the earlier "will this area survive regional planning" step (40% of regional plans and 60% of land-use plans changed during development in the 2015 sample).

SIGNAL. Regional plans and draft designations (PDF), UVP-portal permit files (thousands of PDFs), DVOR protection radii (published), Bundeswehr low-flying routes and radar objections (not public), species records. Partially legible.

UNPROVEN RIGHT. Farmland lease options from farmers, exclusive and cheap; farmers cannot read DVOR radii or species surveys. Fits the brief.

PROVEN STATE. Very liquid: developers (PNE, Energiekontor, ABO, Plambeck) routinely sell project rights to RWE, EnBW, Qualitas, Encavis and funds. Named prices: Plambeck sold rights to 47.6 MW in Brandenburg for 3.08M EUR in 2005 (about 65k EUR/MW, permit status not stated); permitted German wind rights traded at roughly 100-250k EUR/MW in 2022-24 (from memory). A Polish ready-to-build 8 MWp solar project drew an LOI at 2.35M EUR/MWp in 2023 (cleanenergypipeline), likely including more than bare rights.

MARKET CEILING. Germany permitted 1.4-1.6 GW/yr in 2017-18 and roughly 14 GW in 2024 (from memory); at 150k EUR/MW that is about 2bn EUR/yr of rights value (my estimate). Large, repeatable, and the same structure exists in France, Italy, Poland.

INCUMBENTS. Hundreds of developers with in-house GIS and land scouts; the state designates 2% of land for wind by 2032 (Wind-an-Land-Gesetz), making the area map legible over time.

KILL TEST. Pull the Bundesnetzagentur Marktstammdatenregister permits for one state against the regional plan's designated areas: if more than 90% of permits fall inside areas designated before the lease was signed, land-first speculation has no information edge.

### 4. Norwegian fall rights plus NVE small-hydro concession (NEW, fit 4)

PROVING STEP. An NVE concession (or recommendation to the Ministry). NVE 2015: 92 small-hydro projects granted (863 GWh), 68 final rejections (653 GWh), 56 withdrawn; of 197 handled, 78 positive and 63 negative, so p is 0.4-0.6 by count (NVE "ny kraft" Q4 2015). 2014: about 97 granted / 58 rejected; 2011: 45 granted / 18 rejected (tu.no). Lever cap 1.7-2.5. Cost: the 2018 industry report says preparing the 400+ unbuilt concessions cost "at least NOK 100 million" in total, i.e. a few hundred thousand NOK per application plus biological surveys; processing 1-5+ years (thesis), queue of 600+ cases in 2012, 100 cases (mostly small hydro) in Q3 2025. Rejection grounds: landscape, biodiversity (ravine habitats, freshwater pearl mussel, eagle owl), protected rivers, recreation; four assessment topics statistically separate approved from rejected (Brunvatne 2012 thesis, 24 vs 24 cases).

SIGNAL. NVE decision letters and consultation files (thousands of PDFs), species records (Artsdatabanken, legible), Naturbase, and NVE's own small-hydro potential atlas (legible resource map, from memory about 18-25 TWh). The resource is legible; the concession outcome is the illegible part.

UNPROVEN RIGHT. Fall rights are private property of the riparian landowner (Norway is unusual in this); developers sign a falleieavtale with running payments and reversion after 40-60 years (Smaakraftforeninga 2018). Exclusive, buyable, owners usually cannot assess concession risk. Fits.

PROVEN STATE. Concession-granted projects are bought by Smaakraft AS (Aquila Capital, bought from Statkraft, Agder, BKK and Skagerak in 2015; 45 plants, 500 GWh, 15 under construction, about 100 in development), Clemens Kraft, Norsk Vannkraft, OBOS Energi, NGK, Cloudberry, Captiva. Prices undisclosed. Built value about NOK 4.5 per annual kWh (NOK 14-15B for 3.2 TWh); a concession might be worth 5-10% of that (my estimate), so NOK 2-6M for a 10 GWh project against an application cost of NOK 0.3-1M: R1 about 5-20x.

MARKET CEILING. 481 licensed-unbuilt projects / 3,870 GWh at end-2015; over 400 / 3.2 TWh in 2018 (NOK 14-15B built value). Concession-option pool NOK 1-1.5B (about $100-150M) (my estimate). Too small on its own; Sweden, Austria, northern Italy and Scotland have analogous but even smaller markets. Not a path to a large company.

KILL TEST. Fit a logistic model on NVE's public decision list (2004-2025) using only legible covariates (county, GWh, protected-river flag, INON flag); if AUC exceeds 0.8, the concession outcome is already legible and the ML edge is gone.

### 5. UK/Scottish pumped-storage consent-and-sell, the ILI model (NEW, fit 4)

ILI Group (Hamilton, founder Mark Wilson) began as a land investor, took options on estate land, won Section 36 consent for Red John (450 MW) in June 2021 (conceived 2015) and sold it to Statkraft in December 2023 (undisclosed); it also sold a 50 MW BESS to EDPR and claims a 4.7 GW storage pipeline. Its onshore-wind record shows the UK planning hit rate: consent on 96 of 160 proposals (60%). Gilkes Energy got consent for Earba (1.8 GW / 40 GWh) in about 12 months (application March 2024, consent March 2025) and filed Fearna (1.8 GW) with SSE. Recent Scottish PSH consents are almost all granted, so p is high and the lever small; the right (land option) and the buyer (Statkraft, SSE, Drax) are real. Pool about 10 GW consented or in process (from memory). Kill test: count s36 PSH refusals since 2015; if zero, no failing step.

### 6. Non-powered dam hydro licences (fit 3)

PROVING STEP. FERC original licence (or exemption) plus USACE Section 408 and real-estate approvals for the 65 of 93 proposed NPD projects on Corps dams. Time: "five- to seven-year" usual FERC timetable, "eight to ten years, closer to 15 for bigger projects" (Rye's Paul Jacob, 2016); Rye "approached FERC more than a decade" before 2023 for the Pittsburgh projects, licensed 2016, first unit online "sometime by late 2027". Cost: "millions or tens of millions" (VNF). Hit rate: ORNL's attrition table lists 89.80% attrition at the issued-preliminary-permit stage and 21.82% at licence application (energy.gov 2017 market-trends page), implying about 8% of issued permits become licences (my calculation); FERC cancelled 5 of Rye's permits in 2016 for lack of diligence. The 2-year pilot licensed Kentucky L&D 11 (4.9 MW) in 24 months but excluded Corps dams.

SIGNAL. FERC eLibrary dockets (PDF), USGS flows (legible), NID (legible), state 401 files. DOE's 2012 assessment already ranked every NPD by MW (legible), and 8 of the 12 GW sit at the 100 largest, mostly Corps, dams.

UNPROVEN RIGHT. A FERC preliminary permit: free, first-come, with municipal preference, cancelled for site-banking. Fails the brief's "not first-come" rule for federal dams. Buying a private dam is possible but the private-dam MW pool is small.

PROVEN STATE. Licensed NPD projects mostly sit: 670 MW licensed-not-constructed (129 projects) at end-2019 with more than half in preconstruction 3+ years; 56 authorized projects / 514 MW at end-2022 with 8 / 14 MW under construction; 128 projects / 1.4 GW in the 2026 pipeline with 6 / 14 MW under construction. Rye's three Pittsburgh licences (Emsworth main about 18-20 MW, Allegheny L&D 2 about 9 MW, Montgomery) were sold in August 2026 to Snowmass Energy 2050 (Mavel affiliate), price undisclosed; FFP New Hydro/Crestline financed 21 projects (>200 MW) in 2015. A licence sells for roughly sunk development cost (my estimate), so R1 on a free permit is undefined and on development spend about 1x.

MARKET CEILING. 12 GW theoretical, about 1 GW in pipeline; licence-value pool $100-200M (my estimate). Niche. Incumbents: Rye, Hydro Green, Emrgy (canals), Natel, Mavel/Snowmass, AMP and MRES (munis), Kleinschmidt. Kill test: tabulate licensed NPD projects 2010-2020 and their 2026 status; if fewer than 20% reached construction, the "proven state" is worth little.

### 7. UK connection reform Gate 2 (fit 3, seen cluster)

Queue exceeded 700 GW; NESO issued 283 GW of connections in December 2025 and cut about 153 GW of BESS; Gate 2 requires land rights and planning consent or advanced planning. No hidden state: it is a policy gate on legible criteria. Gate 2 projects command "infrastructure-like pricing" (RSM) but prices undisclosed. Not re-researched further.

### 8. Chile non-consumptive water rights + environmental approval (NEW, fit 3)

Non-consumptive rights were granted free and perpetually under the 1981 Water Code, are tradable, and became concentrated in Endesa; the 2005 reform added non-use fees to curb speculation in unused rights; registration is inconsistent (Hearne and Donoso 2014). Competing applications go to auction (from memory, Art. 142). The proving step for a hydro project is the SEIA environmental approval; p unknown; HidroAysen was rejected in 2014 after hundreds of millions spent (from memory). Approved run-of-river projects do trade (Colbun, Enel, Statkraft, Pacific Hydro/SPIC, from memory). Doubt: the rights that matter are already held by incumbents who know their value. Kill test: DGA registry count of unused non-consumptive rights not held by Enel/Colbun.

### 9. Australian mine-pit pumped hydro and REZ (NEW, fit 3)

Genex developed Kidston (250 MW, 8 hours) in a former open-pit gold mine with Kidston solar (50 MW) and wind (258 MW); J-Power paid about A$380M equity (>A$1B EV) in 2024. Abandoned pits give the lower reservoir and often a grid stub; pit geometry is legible from lidar. REZ access rights are allocated by tender (killed by the brief). Kill test: list consented Australian PSH and how many used a mine pit; if Kidston is the only one, it is a one-off.

### 10. Distressed high-hazard private dams (NEW, fit 2.5)

Private and HOA owners facing state dam-safety repair orders will often transfer a dam for a nominal sum; 33 FERC projects were in surrender and 18 surrenders since 2010 involved dam removal (ORNL 2025). A developer taking title gets an NPD site, water storage and sometimes a head pond for storage. No source found on transfer prices or the share of such dams with economic hydro; it is liability arbitrage, not a failing proving step. Kill test: cross NID high-hazard private dams with the DOE 2012 NPD list; count those above 5 MW.

### 11. Coal-to-nuclear and NRC Early Site Permits (fit 2)

DOE/INL 2022 screened coal sites with the OR-SAGE GIS tool (population density, seismic, slope, protected land, wetlands, floodplains, hazards, streamflow): 157 retired sites (125 amenable, 64.8 GWe) and 237 operating sites (190 amenable, 198.5 GWe), about 80% suitable, 15-35% capex savings from reuse. All inputs are legible public layers and DOE published the list, so there is no illegible signal. ESP review takes 1-2 years at NRC (Clinch River: application 2016, ESP 2019); of about seven ESP applications since 2003 six were issued (from memory), so p is about 0.85 and the lever cap 1.2. ESP cost unknown from sources (TVA's board has approved $350M for the whole Clinch River SMR programme). No ESP site has ever been sold to a third party; sites are utility-owned and utilities commissioned the DOE study, so they know the value. TerraPower partnered with PacifiCorp at Naughton rather than buying an option from an uninformed owner. Fails on p, on the right, and on liquidity.

### 12. Wind sites gated by DoD/FAA radar and eagle take (fit 2)

DoD Clearinghouse reviewed over 5,900 wind projects since 2014, held 334 mitigation discussions, signed 86 written mitigation agreements, and made one formal "unacceptable risk" determination; Kansas: 200 reviewed, 13 adverse, 1 cancelled. p above 0.95. The 2025-26 stall of about 165 projects / 30 GW on national-security grounds (FT via CleanTechnica) is political, not hidden state. FAA OE/AAA determinations are a legible public database. Germany's DVOR and military blocks (4.8 GW and 3.6 GW) are larger but the beacon radii are published.

### 13. Surplus interconnection at retired generators, Order 845 (fit 2)

SPP publishes surplus-interconnection study reports as PDFs (GEN-2023-SR4, SR12, SR13, GEN-2024-SR6 to SR13, GEN-2025-SR28, GEN-2026-SR11), so the step is cheap, fast and legible; the right (an existing interconnection agreement) is held by an IPP or utility that knows its value. Powered brownfields were already killed at a 1.1x step-up.

### 14. Relicensing-distressed small hydro (fit 2)

211 projects in relicensing and 33 in surrender (mid-2025); relicensing median six years and 21 relicences in 2025, the most since 2005 (ORNL 2026). Relicensing almost always succeeds, so there is no failing step; buyers (Brookfield, Cube, Eagle Creek, Hull Street) are sophisticated.

## Cross-cutting findings

1. Permit-to-licence attrition in US hydro is genuinely drill-like (about 90% of issued preliminary permits die; about 5% of PSH permits reach licence), but the thing that dies is a free first-come permit, not a purchased right, and the licence itself sells for roughly sunk cost because the real gate is financing.
2. Every candidate in this cluster has a legible resource layer already published by a regulator or lab (DOE NPD ranking, DOE/INL C2N list, ANU PSH atlas, hosting-capacity maps, NVE potential atlas, German designated areas); the only illegible residue is "why past applications died", which is political and financial.
3. The one place where land is cheap, owners are uninformed, the step-up is 10x+ and buyers are liquid is data-centre powered land; it is also the one place with well-funded incumbents doing exactly that (Tract, Cloverleaf, GridCARE).
4. Norway's private fall rights are the cleanest "unproven right you can buy from someone who cannot read the data" found in this sweep, but the pool is about $100-150M of option value.

## Footnote: excluded drilling-proved candidates (one line)

Geothermal leases (Fervo-style EGS), oil and gas, lithium brine, natural hydrogen leases, Class VI CO2 storage pore space and salt-cavern storage all have the right structure but are proved by drilling, so they are out under the standing rule.
