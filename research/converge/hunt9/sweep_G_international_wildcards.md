# Hunt 9, Sweep G: non-US forced-disclosure regimes and wildcards

Method: 21 web searches (the shared per-turn search budget ran out at that point; the rest is from 23 WebFetch reads of the result pages plus prior knowledge, marked where recalled). Every candidate is run through the four-part drill test; numbers I infer are marked "(my estimate)". Drilling-type regimes (Norway/UK petroleum data, Australian state surveys, Coal Authority mine-water heat) are footnotes only, per the standing rule. Nine of the fourteen candidates are not on the brief's seen list.

Headline, honestly: nothing in this cluster reproduces Terranox's 10^4 step-up on a many-billion pool. The best structural fits (art sleepers, looted-art claims) sit on pools of a few hundred million dollars a year; the big pools (Chilean water, Japanese unknown-owner land, Indian land) have proving steps that are either too cheap/high-hit (lever cap under 2x) or too slow (decades). Ranked by fit.

## 1. Old Master "sleepers" at scale: vision ML over regional-auction lot images, then buy, authenticate, resell (fit 5/10, NEW)

1. PROVING STEP. Buy the lot, clean it, technical analysis (IR/X-ray/dendro), expert dossier and committee opinion. Cost $5k-50k per attempt (my estimate; Turquin's firm charges 5% of hammer for attributions), 6-24 months; Caravaggio (2014) took about five years to win the field. Base hit rate p: unknown; the Art Law Podcast hosts only float "one in a thousand" lots as a hypothetical. Among a model-filtered shortlist p might be 2-10% (my estimate). Lever cap 10-50x. Dealers pay today.
2. SIGNAL. Lot photographs and catalogue text across online aggregators (the-saleroom, Invaluable, Drouot.com, Lot-tissimo) covering thousands of regional houses; Eric Turquin's office alone processes about 15,000 appraisals a year for about 350 small and mid-size houses and books EUR 20-30m revenue (artnet profile). The "legible field" is the house's own attribution ("19th C Continental School", "Italian school"), which is wrong by definition for a sleeper, so no regulator has pre-written the answer. The data is images, which is exactly the illegible-at-scale form.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. The lot, bought at auction for EUR 1k-50k. The auction objection in the brief (owner runs the price) does not bite: the seller prices on a false belief and the uncertainty resolves after purchase. Examples: Pierozzi miniature EUR 7,800 at Millon, Artemisia Gentileschi GBP 30,000 at a Danish house, Salvator Mundi $1,175 in 2005, Constable $5,000 at Christie's (The Art Newspaper; artnet).
4. PROVEN STATE. Resold at Christie's/Sotheby's/Colnaghi or privately: Artemisia GBP 1.9m (63x, <1 year), Constable $5m (1,000x), Cimabue found above a stove $26.8m, Fragonard $9.1m, Strigel $4m at a Toulouse house. Liquid at the top houses; sellable with the dossier before any "build".

Market ceiling: Old Master auction turnover is roughly $0.5-1bn a year (my estimate; consistent with one attribution firm touching EUR 400-600m of hammer); sleeper value creation maybe $100-300m a year. Not a many-billion pool. Incumbents: Turquin (Paris), Philip Mould and Bendor Grosvenor (London), Colnaghi, a dozen known "sleeper hunters", Art Recognition (Zurich, AI authentication as a service), Art Analysis & Research, the big houses' specialist desks. Kill test (days): scrape 24 months of "circle of / follower of / school" lots over EUR 2k on Invaluable and the-saleroom, image-match against subsequent Christie's/Sotheby's/Dorotheum catalogues; if fewer than 0.2% re-sold at over 10x, or if the same five bidders already took every one (the Nye & Co "Rembrandt" estimated at $500-800 hammered at $870k because the whole trade saw it online), the edge is priced. Biggest doubt: ceiling; and the online trade already bids up anything three connoisseurs can spot.

Sources: https://news.artnet.com/art-world/eric-turquin-profile-2085629 ; https://theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/14/its-time-to-end-the-predatory-practices-of-sleeper-hunters ; https://news.artnet.com/market/800-painting-sold-870000-rembrandt-334799 ; https://artlawpodcast.com/2025/04/24/the-problem-of-sleepers/

## 2. Chilean water rights facing the 2027 registration/regularization cliff (fit 4/10, NEW)

1. PROVING STEP. Regularization of an unregistered or customary right (Código de Aguas art. 2 transitorio) through the DGA and the civil courts, then inscription in the Conservador de Bienes Raíces and the Catastro Público de Aguas. Cost USD 5-30k legal and technical per right (my estimate); duration 18 months to several years (the statutory window was 18 months, extended). Hit rate p: unknown; DGA rejections for lack of proof of five years' use or for restricted aquifers are common but I found no published grant rate. Holder pays. Lever cap unknown (if p is 50%, only 2x).
2. SIGNAL. Rural Conservador registers are not digitized (camara.cl), DGA resolutions are PDFs, the CSIRO/DGA Copiapó catastro of uses and users is a scanned report, and proof of historical use needs satellite imagery over time. Legible trap: the CPA inscription status is itself a regulator field, but only for already-registered rights; the hidden state is whether an unregistered use will be recognized.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Unregistered or defectively registered rights held by farmers and small users. Ley 21.435 (2022) made unregistered rights subject to caducidad; the deadline was moved to 6 April 2027 (Leyes 21.586 and 21.727) and a bill proposes 2030; INDAP users get five years. The deadline manufactures forced sellers who cannot run the process. Price discount for rights "en trámite": unknown. Purchasable with the land or by cession; regularization must then be pursued by the possessor.
4. PROVEN STATE. Registered consumptive rights listed at CLP 1.5m-68m per L/s (Compragua via La Tercera, 2018), about USD 2k-90k per L/s; Calama-El Loa highest, Santiago CLP 5-35m. Buyers: mining, sanitary utilities, real-estate developers, agribusiness; brokered market exists.

Ceiling: total stock of consumptive rights is worth USD 10bn+ (my estimate). Incumbents: water brokers (Compragua), water-law boutiques, mining-company water desks. Kill test: pull DGA's art. 2T resolutions 2015-2025 and compute the grant rate; scrape Compragua for discounts on rights in trámite; if grant rate over 70% or discount under 30%, dead. Biggest doubt: ethics and politics (buying water from campesinos ahead of a caducidad deadline during a megadrought), plus constitutional risk to the whole regime.

Sources: https://www.latercera.com/pulso/noticia/derechos-agua-68-millones-puede-costar-litro-segundo-chile/270548/ ; https://www.diarioconstitucional.cl/2026/04/14/proyecto-de-ley-propone-extender-hasta-2030-plazo-para-regularizar-e-inscribir-derechos-de-aprovechamiento-de-aguas/ ; https://www.camara.cl/cms/2024/10/30/extienden-plazo-para-regularizar-derechos-de-aprovechamiento-de-aguas/

## 3. Nazi-era looted-art claims: machine matching of looting records to current holdings, claims bought on contingency (fit 4/10, NEW)

1. PROVING STEP. Provenance research plus the claim (museum negotiation, national commission, or litigation). Mondex's $24m Chagall case: 3,858 historian-hours, over $130k research cost, 2018 to 2024. p: Mondex claims 97-98% on filed claims, which is after selection; on raw candidate matches p is maybe 10-30% (my estimate). Mondex pays and takes a cut of sale (39% alleged in the Matthiesen suit).
2. SIGNAL. ERR project cards, Munich Central Collecting Point property cards, Linz database, Lost Art (Germany), MNR (France), 1933-45 German auction catalogues (Heidelberg), Getty Provenance Index, joined to museum online catalogues and auction appearances; the join is image matching against handwritten cards, illegible at scale. National commission decisions are legible but cover only filed claims.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Heirs' claims, acquired by exclusive contingency mandate (20-40%). Heirs usually do not know the object exists.
4. PROVEN STATE. Restituted works sell at the big houses ($24m Chagall via Matthiesen, 2023) or settle with museums; step-up is from zero.

Ceiling: about 100,000 works still unrecovered (commonly cited figure, recalled), "tens of billions" of value (my estimate $10-30bn), only a fraction findable. Incumbents: Mondex (Toronto, since 1993), Art Recovery International, Commission for Looted Art in Europe, Herrick Feinstein, the Art Loss Register. Kill test: one week of off-the-shelf perceptual hashing of ERR/CCP images against the 20 largest museum online catalogues; count unclaimed hits; check the US HEAR Act sunset (1 Jan 2027, recalled) and German Beratende Kommission throughput. Biggest doubt: optics of a profit-taking intermediary (the 39% fee was called "well outside industry norms"), glacial processes, a handful of cases a year.

Sources: https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/08/27/holocaust-restitution-firm-mondex-settles-legal-feud-with-heir-over-fees-for-24m-chagall-painting ; https://www.mondexcorp.com/results/

## 4. Swedish small hydro under the national relicensing plan (NAP): buy distressed plants whose permit will survive (fit 4/10, NEW)

1. PROVING STEP. Retrial in the Land and Environment Court under the NAP, which covers about 2,000 plants and dams, roughly 1,800 of them under 10 MW (about 1,030 under 125 kW). Cost per case: not published; SEK 1-5m including fish-passage works (my estimate), of which the industry-funded Vattenkraftens Miljöfond (EUR 50m a year) covers about 90% (damremoval.eu). Time 2-5 years per case; the programme is paused 2023-2025, restarted August 2025, planned for 20 years, EU infringement opened December 2024. p: of the 33 cases completed by end-2024, 22 (67%) ended in permit withdrawal and dam removal, 11 (33%) continued with conditions, 10 of those under 1.5 MW (KMAE 2026). Lever cap about 3x.
2. SIGNAL. Pre-1980s water-court judgments (vattendomar) in paper archives, Länsstyrelsen fish and dam inventories, the NAP schedule. Legible trap: VISS (the water information system) already designates heavily-modified water bodies and fish-passage priority per stretch, which may predict most outcomes.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. The plant, owned by about 1,500 farmers and small companies; distressed (municipalities are buying stations to decommission; owners cite "costly environmental reviews and stalled funding", Energywatch). Purchasable; owners cannot run the court process.
4. PROVEN STATE. Plant with a modern 40-year permit. Buyers: small-hydro aggregators, utilities; thin market in Sweden. Step-up 2-3x (my estimate).

Ceiling: small plants are 6-8% of Swedish hydro (about 4-5 TWh); whole pool SEK 20-40bn (USD 2-4bn, my estimate). Niche. Incumbents: none at scale; Norwegian aggregators (Småkraft, Aneo) in theory. Kill test: regress the 33 decided outcomes on VISS status; if VISS alone predicts over 90%, no ML edge. Biggest doubt: pool size and a 20-year, politically paused process.

Sources: https://www.kmae-journal.org/articles/kmae/full_html/2026/01/kmae250140/kmae250140.html ; https://damremoval.eu/wp-content/uploads/2022/06/Peter-Rudberg_Swedens-national-plan-for-hydropower-reviews.pdf ; https://energywatch.com/EnergyNews/Renewables/article19027611.ece

## 5. German onshore wind repowering sites (fit 3/10, NEW angle on an adjacent seen item)

1. PROVING STEP. BImSchG permit for a repowered turbine: species surveys EUR 120k plus EUR 80k re-survey in Uckermark (EUR 33k/MW), EUR 45k spent and EUR 120k more estimated in the Eifel, "up to 5 years", one Ostwürttemberg case about ten years (DIHK; Reuters Events). p: not published; in Uckermark-Barnim over 50% of suitable repowering areas are no longer available for species reasons; since the 2023 Wind-an-Land law approvals have surged (record permits in 2024, recalled), which shrinks the lever. Cap about 1.5-2x.
2. SIGNAL. State species-protection registers (red kite nests), regional plans, aviation-beacon exclusion zones, EEG end dates per turbine (Marktstammdatenregister, legible). Mixed legibility.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Old turbines and their land leases, owned by Bürgerwind and farmers; about 4 GW over 20 years old in 2021 plus 2.4 GW a year through 2025 (BWE). Purchasable, but developers already canvass every site.
4. PROVEN STATE. Permitted project rights sell to IPPs/funds at a development premium of EUR 100-300k/MW (my estimate); one modern turbine replaces up to six old ones; 102 repowering projects (339 MW) won 24% of 2020 auction awards.

Ceiling: tens of GW, many billions in asset value, but the premium pool is EUR 1-3bn. Incumbents: wpd, Energiequelle, ABO Energy, PNE, UKA, Juwi, Bürgerwind associations. Kill test: compare 2022-2025 permit decisions to the species-register distance rules; if deterministic, no edge. Doubt: approvals are now high (lever gone) and incumbents own the relationships.

Sources: https://www.dihk.de/resource/blob/50168/2b1197c5041021dd3739eab0984c03ee/energie-dihk-papier-repowering-data.pdf ; https://www.reutersevents.com/renewables/node/61523

## 6. Japan unknown-owner land: buy fractional shares, consolidate through the 2023 court procedure (fit 3/10, NEW)

1. PROVING STEP. Petition under the 2021 Civil Code amendment (in force 1 April 2023) to acquire the share of a co-owner whose whereabouts are unknown: filing fee JPY 1,000 per share, stamps JPY 6,150, Gazette advance JPY 7,847, three-month notice, deposit set by the court at the appraised market value of the share (Osaka District Court Q&A); in a published case a manager was appointed three months after filing and sale completed six months later, with a JPY 300k deposit mostly returned (Keio). p is high and cost low, so the lever is small; the real cost is lawyer and judicial-scrivener time (JPY 0.3-1m, my estimate).
2. SIGNAL. Registry entries (online, legible) show stale owners; the inheritance tree lives in koseki family registers (not public; scriveners can pull). Unknown-owner land is 23% of surveyed parcels (MLIT 2024), 63% from unregistered inheritance; 4.1m ha in 2016, projected 7.2m ha by 2040 (2017 Masuda study, recalled). Mandatory inheritance registration from 1 April 2024 (JPY 100k fine) will slowly make this legible.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. A known heir's fractional share, which is nearly worthless to anyone else; purchasable cheap.
4. PROVEN STATE. Whole parcel with clean title, sold to developers, solar/forestry operators, or Katitas-style refurbishers. Step-up: whole-parcel price versus fractional-share price, perhaps 3-10x on the share (my estimate), but the deposit is paid at full market value for the missing shares, so the gain is only on the known heir's discount.

Ceiling: large in area, low in value per hectare (rural). Incumbents: judicial scriveners, Katitas (listed, buys about 6,000 vacant homes a year, recalled), akiya banks, local brokers. Kill test: count petitions filed under the new procedure in 2023-2025 (courts publish statistics) and compare to the number of co-owned stale-registry parcels; if petitions are trivial, the pipeline exists but nobody wants rural land. Doubt: the asset is mostly unwanted land; the lever is in sourcing, not proving.

Sources: https://www.japaneselawtranslation.go.jp/en/outlines/view/50 ; https://www.courts.go.jp/osaka/vc-files/osaka/c_10_syozaihumei_QA.pdf ; https://www.keio.ac.jp/en/about/public-relations/mita-hyoron/featured-topic/07-2-3

## 7. Norwegian small-hydro fall rights ahead of NVE concession (fit 3/10)

1. PROVING STEP. NVE concession: 2015 decisions 70 granted, 45 refused (61%; 2014 97 vs 58, 63%), 516 pending, "many years" in NVE (NVE 2016 via Trondsen thesis); application cost NOK 0.5-1.5m (my estimate). Cap 1.6x, which kills the ML lever.
2. SIGNAL. NVE's concession database and small-hydro resource maps are fully legible, and NVE itself runs the hydrology.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Fall rights (fallrettigheter) owned by farmers, leased 40-75 years for a revenue share (Småkraft paid EUR 5m to about 1,100 landowners in 2024); selling is constrained by farm-property rules.
4. PROVEN STATE. Operating plants trade actively: Småkraft (Aquila Capital) owns 238 plants, 2.16 TWh, EUR 864m total assets, bought 4 companies for EUR 6.7m in 2024; construction cost NOK 5-6.5m/MW.

Incumbents: Småkraft, Clemens Kraft, Aneo, Tussa, Norsk Grønnkraft. Kill test: compute NVE grant rate by county and the price per annual kWh paid for concessioned vs unconcessioned projects from Småkraft's filings. Doubt: p of 0.6 and a legible regulator.

Sources: https://static02.nmbu.no/mina/studier/moppgaver/2016-Trondsen.pdf ; https://live.euronext.com/sites/default/files/company_press_releases/attachments_oslo/2025/03/28/642539_2025-03-25%20Sm%C3%A5kraftAnnualReport.pdf ; https://www.tu.no/artikler/store-penger-i-smakraft/322296

## 8. UK insolvency-estate claims bought from insolvency practitioners (Manolete model; fit 3/10)

1. PROVING STEP. Litigating the claim (misfeasance, preference, bounce-back-loan fraud): average 12.8-15.5 months, FY23 revenue GBP 26.8m over 193 completions, about GBP 139k per case (my estimate); 2.2x money multiple, 125% ROI across 689 completed cases; 93% of new cases are purchased outright. p is high (most settle; one large trial loss), so the cap is about 1.2x.
2. SIGNAL. Companies House accounts and charges (PDF), Gazette notices, IP progress reports; IPs already know their own cases.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Claims assigned by IPs for a share of recoveries; purchasable and exclusive.
4. PROVEN STATE. Cash settlement; no onward buyer needed.

Ceiling: Manolete (AIM) books GBP 30.5m revenue, 282 new cases in FY25; whole UK pool perhaps GBP 100-200m a year (my estimate). Incumbents: Manolete, Henderson & Jones, Harbour. Kill test: none needed; the structure fails the "usually fails" criterion. Doubt: a high-hit, small-pool business already listed.

Sources: https://www-proxy.stockopedia.com/share-prices/manolete-partners-LON:MANO/news/reg-manolete-partners-audited-results-for-the-year-ended-31-march-2023-urn:newsml:reuters.com:20230622:nRSV5053Da ; https://www.lawgazette.co.uk/news/insolvency-litigation-finance-firm-thrilled-by-record-revenues/5123695.article

## 9. Chain-of-title clearing of pre-1978 films and books from the Copyright Office card catalog (fit 3/10, NEW, US wildcard)

1. PROVING STEP. Title search and quiet-title/registration work per title: $5-30k, months (my estimate); p unknown.
2. SIGNAL. About 41.5m index cards, 1870-1977, registrations, renewals and assignments; only about 1m registration cards (1909-1945) are in the CPRS pilot with searchable metadata, the VCC proof-of-concept OCR/ICR covers 1955-1977 "corrected to the extent practical", and the Office says neither may be relied on for legal purposes. Genuinely illegible at scale.
3. UNPROVEN RIGHT. Rights bought from heirs and successors of defunct studios and publishers whose status is murky; cheap because unknowable.
4. PROVEN STATE. A cleared library licensed to streamers; step-up unknown; buyers Kino Lorber, Cohen Media, Shout!, Warner Archive, Nightdive (games).

Ceiling: unknown, probably hundreds of millions. Kill test: pick 200 pre-1964 features with no documented renewal and check how many have an identifiable successor-in-interest; if most are either clearly public domain or clearly owned, there is no middle to buy. Doubt: no evidence the middle is large.

Sources: https://vcc.copyright.gov/about ; https://blogs.loc.gov/copyright/2023/11/over-one-million-card-catalog-records-digitized-in-copyright-public-records-system-pilot/

## 10. India: disputed-title land and eCourts (fit 2/10)

Two-thirds of pending civil cases are land disputes (World Bank 2007 via PRS); about 20 years to resolve (NITI Aayog); records 86% computerized but cadastral maps 46% digitized, 9% of villages resurveyed, title presumptive not conclusive. The proving step (litigation) takes two decades, which fails the test on time alone; local aggregators do this with muscle, not data. Kill test unnecessary. Source: https://prsindia.org/policy/analytical-reports/land-records-and-titles-india

## 11. England & Wales unregistered land (about 12-13% by area, recalled) and Scottish a non domino dispositions (fit 2/10)

Prescriptive routes (10 years in Scotland under the 2012 Act, 10-12 years adverse possession in England) are cheap, slow and reputationally toxic; the proving step is neither expensive nor exclusive. No search budget left to quantify a non domino volumes. Footnote-grade.

## 12. Romanian and Polish restitution claims (fit 1/10)

Harvard's vehicles bought at least 33,600 acres of Romanian forest from 1997, invested over $100m, and sold for about $62m to an IKEA vehicle in 2015 after deeds were invalidated for corrupt restitution (Atlas Obscura; OCCRP). The structure is real (claims bought cheap from heirs, titled forest worth EUR 3-5k/ha, recalled) but the proven examples are frauds. Kill. Source: https://www.atlasobscura.com/articles/harvard-ikea-and-the-battle-for-romanias-forests

## 13. Brazil CAR and Terra Legal titling (fit 1/10)

Terra Legal registered 87,992 possessions on 10.3m ha in its second year and issued 335 titles (1.4% of target; roughly 260 years at that pace, my arithmetic); Amapá sells public land to individuals at 4-59% of the R$1,312/ha market (Imazon; IHU). The step-up is real and the data (CAR polygons, INCRA PDFs) illegible, but the model is buying possession from invaders and the regularization "rewards invaders and deforesters". Kill on ethics. Sources: https://imazon.fly.storage.tigris.dev/wp-backup/PDFimazon/Portugues/livros/did-land-regularization-advance-in-the-amazon-pdf.pdf ; https://ihu.unisinos.br/610016-grilagem-areas-publicas-da-amazonia-no-amapa-chegam-a-ser-vendidas-por-4-do-valor-de-mercado

## 14. ITU satellite filings and bringing into use (fit 1/10)

400-500 new network filings a year, about one in ten launched; a 2009 review removed 145 networks and flagged 325; Tonga leased slots at $2m a year; BIU needs 90 days at the position within seven years (NYU GlobaLex). The right belongs to administrations, not companies; BR IFIC is a structured database (legible); the proving step is a $100m+ satellite or a drift lease. Kill. Source: https://www.nyulawglobal.org/globalex/paper_satellites_free_use_outer_space.html

## Footnotes (checked and dropped)

- UK Coal Authority mine-water heat: the proving step is a borehole, so excluded; the Authority's mine-plan archive is scanned and the heat-network step-up is real.
- Norway and UK petroleum data releases, Australian state surveys, marine salvage: drilling/prospecting, excluded.
- Australian water register, Equasis/ICAO/flag registries, Handelsregister, EPREL, TED, Scandinavian income records, Singapore/HK registries: fully legible, no hidden state or no purchasable right.
- Companies House dormant-company asset hunt: restoration costs GBP 0.5-5k with about 95% success, so the "proving step" is cheap and the lever is absent; Bona Vacantia sells at market value.
- Heir hunting (Treasury Solicitor unclaimed-estates list, Finders International, Fraser & Fraser): legible daily list, cheap genealogy, commission model; existing GBP tens-of-millions industry.
- Music black-box royalties (MLC unmatched about $424m, recalled): legible database, audits succeed most of the time (cap under 1.3x); Hipgnosis/Primary Wave/Concord own the catalogue trade.
- EU airport slots: non-transferable outside the UK; telecom licences, toll roads, ports, Alpine lifts, Swiss Heimfall: owners know their own renewal odds.
- Bloodstock, classic cars, rare books, domains, eSports player rights: auction-priced with legible data or non-transferable (FIFA banned third-party ownership in 2015).
- Burgundy appellation upgrades (village EUR 1m/ha to grand cru EUR 10m+/ha): no purchasable test, decades per INAO review.
- Video-game IP chain of title: Nightdive/Ziggurat already do it on a pool of tens of millions.
