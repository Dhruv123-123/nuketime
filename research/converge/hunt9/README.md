## Dhruv's rubric (verbatim, project chat, 17 Oct 21:36Z)

> Terranox works because drilling is trivial/abstracted. Discovery is 95% of the game, so we need to find other applications
> where discovery is 80%+. For example, setting up geothermal plants in abandoned coal mines is not good because setting up
> bespoke geothermal plants is much harder than finding the right coal mine.
>
> At the same time, when you zoom out, discovery becomes like 1%. Terranox only works because there is a discrete, defined
> level of vertical integration to sell into where that 95-5 applies. So i was looking at it wrong. It isn't is discovery
> 80+% (typically no, even in a smaller than the full frame of reference) its is there a frame of reference where it is.
> That means that anything that got close on full frame of reference is very very promising. And then if thats self
> sustaining theres so much growth potential from there.
>
> If something has steps of vertical integration, we can win at the smallest step and then there is a path to continually
> step up and ultimately do it from start to finish becoming a trillion dollar company.
>
> The initial part of the uranium discovery process is an exploratory drill such that the land value goes from a dollar a
> hectare to 10,000 dollars a hectare (10^4). [...] The company that acquires said land [...] is in for 40,000/ha and
> collects 100,000/ha. [...] The initial capex is dropped 10 fold using such models. That single drop of an order of
> magnitude allows said company to do 10 more iterations to get that initial 10^4 jump.

Two refinements the sweeps did not have (added by the coordinator at hand-off): the test is whether a discrete, sellable frame
of reference exists in which discovery is 80%+ of the work (zoomed out to the full chain it may be 1%); anything that got
close on the full frame is very promising. And the vertical-integration ladder matters: win the smallest rung, then step up.

The thread's own sharpening, agreed with Dhruv at 21:19Z: the model does not make the drill cheaper, it raises the hit rate,
so the lever is capped at 1 / base hit rate (look for expensive, usually-failing proving steps); the step-up lands only on
whoever owns the unproven right before proving (buy it from people who cannot read the record); and the discoverer's 10^4 on
a tiny base against the developer's 2.5x on big dollars is the junior/major split, which is why a capitalised downstream
buyer is a precondition.

First principles (sweep H): enumerate every "cheap because unknown, expensive step reveals it, proven state sells at a
