# Closed-loop pumped storage sites via FERC preliminary permits: feasibility deep-dive (round 5, relaxed C3)

*Note: the shared web-search budget ran out before this deep-dive started (0 of 2 searches ran). Facts below come from direct fetches I made (marked "fetched") or from the scorer and verifier sources in the finalist record. Everything else is marked as an estimate.*

## Frame of reference
The rung is a closed-loop pumped storage (PSH) site held under a FERC preliminary permit, with geotech, a makeup-water study, an interconnection screen and an NOI/PAD done. Picking the reservoir pair (head, geology, grid access) is about 40-50% of the work (estimate). The rest is process: NOI/PAD consultation, the interconnection queue, water rights and NEPA. The physical step is not trivial. Geotech borings at the dam, tunnel and powerhouse take several field seasons. The rung can be sold, but discovery is well under 80% of it.

## Step-up ratio
- **Unproven price:** about $0. A preliminary permit is issued by filing order at no market price. Land is BLM/USFS ROW at schedule rent (https://www.blm.gov/programs/lands-and-realty/rights-way/obtaining-right-of-way/row-fees) or a private option at farmland price (estimate).
- **Proven price, Tent Mountain (Alberta, 320 MW, 15 h):** TransAlta paid about C$8M upfront for 50%, plus up to C$17M on development and commercial milestones (fetched: https://www.tipranks.com/news/press-releases/transalta-announces-acquisition-of-50-interest-in-early-stage-pumped-hydro-energy-storage-development-project). That is about C$50k/MW upfront. Only hydrology was done at sale, and geotech was still planned. The site is a brownfield mine pit.
- **Proven price, Kanmantoo (South Australia, 250 MW):** AGL signed for A$31M. The deal was scrapped in Feb 2020 and the amount actually paid was not disclosed (fetched: http://www.pv-magazine-australia.com/2020/02/22/agl-hillgrove-resources-scrap-250-mw-pumped-hydro-plans/).
- **Proven price, Swan Lake and Goldendale (US):** CIP bought them in 2020 at an undisclosed price. Swan Lake was already licensed and Goldendale's licence application was pending, so this is a later rung (fetched: https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-storage/pumped-storage/cip-acquires-393-mw-swan-lake-1200-mw-goldendale-pumped-storage-hydro-projects-in-u-s/).
- **Multiple:** about 3-8x upfront on real proving capital, and about 10-15x if every milestone is paid (estimate, assuming C$2-5M of seller spend). It looks like 1000x only if measured against the free permit.
- **Transactions per year:** about 0-2 disclosed, priced early-stage PSH sales worldwide. None is a US greenfield site (verifier 1, citing https://www.energy.gov/sites/prod/files/2021/01/f82/us-hydropower-market-report-full-2021.pdf).

## Hit rate and proving capex today and with ML
- **Capex:** about $0.5-5M over 1-3 years for borings, survey, water, an interconnection pre-study and the NOI/PAD (estimate). A full licence costs another $10-30M over 5+ years (estimate).
- **Base rate:** about 67 projects (52 GW) were in the pipeline at end-2019 and only 3 were FERC-authorised (https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2021-04/hydropower_market_report_slides.pdf). Most permits lapse or are surrendered.
- **Why permits die:** missed progress reports or NOI/PAD (Maysville https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-denies-permit-for-1000-mw-pumped-storage-project-in-kentucky/411549; Montezuma https://www.enerdata.net/publications/daily-energy-news/us-ferc-cancels-preliminary-permit-21-gw-pumped-storage-project-arizona.html), or market and ownership problems (Mount Hope https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/policy-and-regulation/ferc-rejects-new-permit-for-developer-of-2000-mw-mount-hope/). These are commercial and diligence failures, not geology.
- **With ML:** a 1.5-2x lift on cost per proven site is plausible only if site-specific technical causes (geology, water, transmission) explain at least about 30% of deaths. That is untested. Today's evidence leans commercial, so I put the lift at about 1.2-1.5x (estimate).

## Data source
- **Datasets:** FERC eLibrary dockets (scanned feasibility reports, progress reports, surrender letters); 1970s-80s USBR/USACE reconnaissance scans; state dam-safety files and borehole logs; USGS 3DEP LiDAR; EIA-860; FERC Form 715.
- **Access:** all free.
- **Why illegible:** the useful parts are scanned and unstructured.
- **Is the outcome summarised?** Partly. Permit status shows "surrendered" or "cancelled" but not why. However, NREL's closed-loop atlas (about 15,000 site pairs with head, volume, $/kW and transmission spur cost, from https://nrel.gov/docs/fy22osti/81277.pdf) and ANU's atlas already make the geometric screen public. The remaining edge is geology and failure text only.

## How the right is bought
It is not bought. It is filed for.
- Under 16 U.S.C. 798, preliminary permits "shall not be transferable" (fetched: https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/16/798). The term is 4 years plus one extension of up to 4 years, and a third permit needs "extraordinary circumstances".
- Each application is noticed for competing filings under 18 CFR 4.36 (https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2024-06-13/html/2024-12983.htm).
- Value can move only through a sale of the SPV holding the permit or a joint venture.
- No owner who can't read the data is selling anything cheaply. The only exception is private land, where a 1-2 GW pair needs many parcels and the public permit filings tip off the owners.
- **Share of uplift kept:** from the Tent Mountain structure, about 30-50% arrives upfront and the rest is contingent on milestones.

## Capitalised buyer
TransAlta, AGL, CIP and Rye, Brookfield (BPUS), NextEra, and utilities with IRP storage needs (PacifiCorp, NV Energy, APS). They are real and well capitalised, but they buy through small upfront options plus milestones and they walk away (Kanmantoo).

## Integration ladder above
1. FERC licence (5-7 years)
2. Offtake or IRP contract
3. EPC (about $2-5B per GW, estimate)
4. Owning and operating the plant
5. A multi-site long-duration storage IPP

The ladder is very large, but each rung is driven by capital and regulation, not discovery.

## Who else does this
- **Serial filers:** Premium Energy Holdings (7+ California sites), rPlus Hydro, Nature and People First, Gravity Storage, Nightfall, HGE (https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/energy-business/policy-and-regulation/ferc-issues-two-preliminary-permits-to-premium-energy-holdings/).
- **Buyers who originate in-house:** Brookfield BPUS, Invenergy and Rye.
- FERC actively polices site banking (the San Vicente third-permit denial).

## Smallest feasibility test (2 weeks)
1. Pull every PSH preliminary-permit docket from FERC eLibrary that ended in surrender, cancellation or denial (about 200 dockets, 2005-2025, estimate).
2. OCR the surrender letters and final progress reports and code the cause of death: geology, water, transmission, commercial or diligence.
3. **Kill criterion:** under 25% site-technical causes, or less than half of the technical deaths sitting in NREL's top-ranked sites. If either holds, ML has no edge over the NREL screen.
4. In parallel, ask 4 buyers (TransAlta, Rye, rPlus, BPUS) for an indicative cash price on a greenfield US site at the permit-plus-geotech-plus-PAD stage.
5. **Kill criterion:** less than $15k/MW upfront.
