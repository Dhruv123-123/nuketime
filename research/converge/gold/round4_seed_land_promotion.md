## Land promotion, Round 4 re-dive (UK first, US and Australia checked)

### Frame of reference
The rung is "unconsented farmland to land with outline consent", sold to a housebuilder. A capitalised buyer exists and pays when consent is granted. But discovery is not 80% of this rung. Most of the cost and time goes into proving: a planning application, then an appeal, over 2-5 years, decided by political and legal judgement. Picking the site is the smaller part. The relaxed C3 does not change that.

I tested the variant the brief suggested: **buy Green Belt or "grey belt" farmland outright at agricultural value, then promote it under the Dec 2024 NPPF grey-belt policy.** Grey belt is a new policy, its tests are judgement calls, and the evidence sits in council Green Belt assessment PDFs. That made it the best candidate for land where the hope value is not yet priced in.

### Step-up ratio
- **Unproven price.** Farmland is about £20-25k/ha. Green Belt farmland trades at £8-15k/acre, towards the top of that range near London, because hope value is already priced in ([urbanistarchitecture](https://urbanistarchitecture.co.uk/green-belt-land-planning-gain-uplift/)). The Upper Tribunal has held that even remote Green Belt prospects carry hope value ([DKLM](https://www.dklm.co.uk/site/library/latest/hope_value_boosts_compulsory_purchase_compensation_award.html)).
- **Proven price.** Real outline-consented sales came to about £400-490k per gross acre (prior verifier: Sudbury and Hereford). That is about 45-55x farmland before s106, infrastructure and affordable housing.
  - Grey belt is worse. The golden rules require 50% affordable housing by default, or the existing requirement plus 15 points ([LGA webinar](https://lb2.local.gov.uk/sites/default/files/documents/Dec%2024%20NPPF%20Webinar%2019%20March%20Golden%20Rules.pdf)). Viability assessments cannot be used to reduce this ([DHA](https://www.dhaplanning.co.uk/knowledge/the-golden-rule-for-viability)).
  - Councils' benchmark land values for greenfield run at about 14-24x existing use value, or about £370-620k/ha ([Lichfields](https://lichfields.uk/blog/2024/september/20/fool-s-gold-how-a-rigid-approach-to-affordable-housing-and-benchmark-land-values-for-green-belt-could-undermine-housing-delivery); [Rossendale viability](https://www.rossendale.gov.uk/downloads/file/16410/el8-020-2-action-20-2-viability-study-update)).
  - Government floated cutting that to 3x ([BCLP](https://bclplaw.com/en-US/events-insights-news/nppf-consultation-green-belt-reform.html)).
- **Net to the company (estimate).**
  - Freehold variant: buy at about £12k/acre, spend about £30k/acre on promotion, sell at about £250-350k per gross acre after the golden rules. That is about 6-8x on capital, in 10-100x territory only before costs.
  - Promoter variant (prior evidence): the promoter keeps 10-25% of the uplift, or about 3-10x on promotion spend.
- **Transactions per year.** Gladman consented about 2,796 plots/yr (prior evidence). Grey-belt appeals produced about 1,600 homes in 8.5 months ([HBD/Marrons](https://hbdonline.co.uk/?p=363028)). The total pool is £10-20B/yr (estimate).
- **US.** Texas cropland is about $2,710/acre against about $10,200/acre for development-stage land ([Neuhaus](https://neuhausre.com/raw-land-investing/)). Generic guidance puts entitled land at about 2x raw ([RealWealth](https://realwealth.com/?p=101492)). That is a 2-6x step-up, so worse than the UK.

### Hit rate and proving capex, today and with ML
- **Proving capex.** £250k-1M per site, or £25-40k per gross acre, over 2-5 years (prior evidence). This passes the "expensive" leg.
- **Hit rates.**
  - Marrons, to Aug 2025: major residential grey-belt appeals were allowed 80% of the time (16/20); all residential, 54% ([HBD](https://hbdonline.co.uk/?p=363028)).
  - Marrons, to Feb 2026: majors 75% (18/24), all residential 43%, and 2-9 dwelling schemes about 30% ([GB Chambers](https://www.greaterbirminghamchambers.com/resource/grey-belt-policy-continues-to-increase-housing-planning-permissions-research.html)).
  - Promoters win about 67-76% on the sites they choose (Gladman, Mac Mic; prior evidence).
- **With ML (estimate).** On sites already filtered, ML can cut the cost per consented site by about 1.2-1.35x at most, because failures are only 25-33% of attempts. Choosing sites before an option is signed, across all farmland, could in theory do better. But the filters that matter are legible fields, which LandTech already scores (next section). So the extra lift over existing tools is probably below 1.5x.

### Data source
- **Inputs that are partly illegible.** PINS appeal decision letters (public PDFs), council Green Belt assessments and grey-belt reviews (PDFs that score each parcel against purposes a, b and d), HELAA call-for-sites submissions, and local plan examination reports.
- **The outcome is mostly already legible.**
  - Each council's 5-year supply position, its Housing Delivery Test score and appeal outcomes are all structured fields.
  - LandTech already sells a parcel-level "Grey Belt favourability" score built on purposes a, b and d plus the footnote-7 exclusions ([LandTech support](https://support.land.tech/en/articles/9915221-understand-grey-belt-favourability); [LandTech blog](https://land.tech/blog/find-grey-belt-sites-fast-with-landinsight)).
- **Result.** The new illegible signal was turned into a product within months. C4 fails again.

### How the right is bought
- **Seller.** Farm owners, nearly always through an agent. Bruton Knowles markets a 37.8-acre Cheltenham site as "grey belt", on option or promotion terms only ([landsale](https://www.landsale.co.uk/properties/129539)).
- **Terms.** Under a promotion agreement the promoter keeps 10-25% of net proceeds. An option is exercised at a 10-20% discount to market value. Outright sales carry 30-50% overage for 20-30 years (prior evidence).
- **Freehold variant.** I found no evidence of unencumbered freehold sales at agricultural value next to settlements. Owners and agents already flag grey-belt potential. The company keeps about 100% of the uplift only if it buys outright with no overage, and the market does not offer that on edge-of-settlement land.

### Capitalised buyer
Volume housebuilders pay cash when consent is granted: Barratt Redrow, Persimmon, Taylor Wimpey and Vistry. Barratt also bought Gladman outright for £250M (prior evidence).

### Integration ladder above
Promoter, then housebuilder (land plus construction margin), then build-to-rent or a rental platform. The ladder is real but bounded: UK housebuilders are worth single-digit billions each (estimate). It is not a trillion-dollar chain.

### Who else does this
- **Promoters:** Gladman (owned by Barratt), Catesby, Richborough, Hallam, Rosconn, Mac Mic and Wallace.
- **Data tools:** LandTech, Searchland and Nimbus.
- **Advisers:** Marrons and LPDF track the appeals.
- **AI-native entrant:** Tract shut in March 2025 with no revenue (prior evidence).

### Smallest feasibility test (2 weeks)
1. Take the 224 grey-belt appeals Marrons reviewed (pull them from PINS).
2. Extract features with ML from the matching council Green Belt assessment PDFs and from earlier inspector letters.
3. Test whether this beats a baseline of LandTech favourability plus 5-year-supply and HDT status. Kill if the AUC gain is under 0.05.
4. In the same two weeks, ask 10 land agents for freehold prices with no overage on 5 candidate grey-belt parcels. Kill if the asks are above 2x agricultural value.

### Verdict: still refuted (12/25)

| Criterion | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| C1 Frame | 2 | A buyer exists, but proving, not discovery, is most of the work |
| C2 Step-up | 3 | Gross 20-55x on land, but only about 6-8x net for a freehold owner and 3-10x for a promoter |
| C3 Hit-rate lever | 3 | Proving is expensive, but with 67-80% base rates on chosen sites the lever is about 1.2-1.35x |
| C4 Data + right | 1 | The outcome sits in legible fields that LandTech scores, hope value is already priced in, and rights are sold through agents with overage |
| C5 Ladder | 3 | Real, but bounded at UK housebuilder scale |

The relaxed C3 does not rescue this candidate. The old C4 problems still bind, and so does the promoter's small share of the uplift. Grey belt was the best variant to test, but LandTech had already turned it into a product within months, and the golden rules (50% affordable housing) cut what consented land is worth.
