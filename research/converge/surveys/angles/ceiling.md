## Airborne geophysics market ceiling, 2010-2026

### Findings

1. **Total yearly spend is about US$1.0-1.5B, and reported figures above that include other work.**
   - DataHorizzon puts the airborne geophysical service market at about US$1.5B in 2024, forecast at US$3.2B by 2033 (8.1% CAGR).
   - Another trade report puts it at more than US$1.35B in 2026.
   - SkyQuest says US$2.56B (2024) and US$2.73B (2025), but its segments include LiDAR mapping, which is not geophysics.
   - My bottom-up count (item 6) gives about US$0.9-1.5B (estimate). For comparison, the broad geophysical services market, which is mostly seismic, was about US$19.4B in 2021. Airborne is roughly 5-8% of that.

2. **Airborne is only about 3-6% of the US$12.4B nonferrous exploration budget (estimate), and that share is shrinking.**
   - S&P Global nonferrous budgets by year:
     - 2012: about US$21.5B (all-metals record)
     - 2016: US$6.89B (fourth straight annual fall)
     - 2017: US$7.95-8.5B
     - 2019: US$9.8B
     - 2022: US$13.1B (recent peak)
     - 2024: US$12.5B
     - 2025: US$12.4B (third straight nominal decline)
   - S&P says grassroots exploration reached a record-low share of budgets in 2025, with money moving to minesite and near-mine work. Airborne surveys are mostly a grassroots cost, so their share of company budgets is falling. Gold is now about half of all budgets (US$6.15B, up 11%).
   - Implied corporate spend on airborne surveys: about US$370-740M/yr (estimate).
   - The only published method split is old. In 1980 airborne was 51% of worldwide mining geophysics spend (43% ground, 6% borehole). In 1967 it was about US$10.1M of US$32M.

3. **Airborne revenue swings hard with the exploration cycle.**
   - When CGG bought Fugro's airborne business in 2013, its Land & Airborne revenue fell 37% year on year in Q3 2013, which CGG blamed on "weak demand in the mining market."
   - CGG sold its Multi-Physics unit (airborne EM and gravity gradiometry) to Xcalibur in July 2021. Neither the price nor the unit's revenue was disclosed.
   - Some of the main contractors and their size:

| Contractor | Size | Revenue |
|---|---|---|
| Xcalibur Multiphysics | 20-40 aircraft, 250-400 staff, claims more than 50M line-km flown over its history | Undisclosed; my guess is US$150-300M |
| Sander Geophysics | 8 Cessna Caravans, 2 Twin Otters, 2 helicopters, more than 160 staff | Undisclosed |
| Geotech (VTEM/ZTEM) | More than 20 aircraft | Database estimates of US$7-50M |
| Others | Bell Geospace (more than 2M line-km of gravity gradiometry), SkyTEM, EON, MPX, Expert Geophysics, TerraQuest, MagSpec, Thomson | — |

   - The market is fragmented and privately held. The top 3-4 firms probably hold about half of it (estimate).

4. **Government regional surveys are the growing part of the market, at roughly US$200-400M/yr worldwide (estimate).**

| Programme | Budget | Airborne-specific data |
|---|---|---|
| **USGS Earth MRI** | FY19 $9.6M; FY20 $10.6M; FY22-26 $64M/yr from the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act ($320M total); more than $74M/yr of new mapping including regular appropriations | $129M spent on data acquisition in 2019-23. Typical survey is $4-5M for 100-200k line-km. Example surveys: MD/PA/WV 212,312 km; W-central Utah 144,600 km; Cuyuna (MN) 162,000 km; FL/GA 121,182 km; E. Nevada about 126,000 km for about $4.7M. 5. **Unit prices: helicopter EM costs 5-10 times more per kilometre than government fixed-wing magnetics.**
   - Government fixed-wing magnetics/radiometrics: about US$13-40/line-km (Nigeria $23, E. Nevada about $37, Namibia 1994 about $13). A Russian vendor lists aircraft surveys "from 24" and UAV "from 32" per km (currency not stated).
   - Helicopter time-domain EM plus magnetics for a junior (2000s BC invoice): $125/line-km, $17,000 mobilisation and $2,000/day standby. That job was 1,070 km and cost $173,400.
   - OpenEI gives an airborne EM range of $48 / $317 / $1,609 per mile (low / median / high).
   - Typical Athabasca VTEM surveys are 539-3,350 line-km at 100 m spacing (Aero Energy 3,350; Alpha 1,892; F4 1,100).
   - At today's estimated $150-250/km plus $20-50k mobilisation (estimate), a survey costs about US$100-900k. That matches the founder's "about $100k per survey" for 400-600 km blocks.

6. **Bottom-up breakdown (all figures are estimates)**

| Segment | US$/yr |
|---|---|
| Corporate mineral exploration | 400-700M |
| Government regional surveys | 200-400M |
| Oil and gas (gravity gradiometry, aeromagnetics for frontier basins) | 100-250M |
| Groundwater / environmental airborne EM | 30-80M |
| UXO, engineering, other | 20-60M |
| **Total** | **0.9-1.5B** |

   - Line-km flown per year worldwide: probably about 5-10M (estimate). No source gives a global total; government programmes alone run at 1-3M+ per year.
   - The number of company surveys per year is probably in the low thousands (estimate).

7. **What this means for a new venture**
   - Selling surveys sits in a market of about US$1-1.5B that moves with the exploration cycle.
   - A 20-30% share of the helicopter-EM and junior segment is only US$100-300M of revenue.
   - Reaching venture scale probably means either taking government programmes away from incumbents (about US$300M/yr and growing, paid for by the public) or changing the economics: drones or autonomous systems at about $30/km, or survey-as-data. That second route also takes spend from the much larger US$12.4B budget by replacing ground geophysics and some early drilling.
