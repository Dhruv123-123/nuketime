## Airborne survey economics for a junior explorer: what a survey adds to property value

**Read this first.** The shared search budget (200 WebSearch calls per turn, across all agents) was already used up when I started, so I got no search results. I then fetched 7 pages directly: 4 returned 404, 1 didn't load, and 2 Wikipedia pages had none of the numbers asked for. **None of the figures below come from a source I checked in this session.** They are from background knowledge, marked "(estimate)" where they are guesses or "(unverified)" where I recall a specific fact but couldn't confirm it. Treat every number as a hypothesis to check. A follow-up message would start a new search budget, and a verified rerun of this angle is worthwhile.

### Findings (all unsourced, ranked by how much they matter)

1. **The value step-up happens when a drill-ready target exists, not when the airborne survey is flown.**
   - Raw staked ground in the Athabasca Basin is valued roughly at its staking cost plus a small premium: about C$0.60–C$15/ha all-in (estimate).
   - Once a property has *ground-confirmed EM conductors plus a structural or geochemical vector*, it typically gets optioned for C$0.5–5M of staged spend plus shares (estimate).
   - An airborne survey on its own mainly turns "raw ground" into "ground with ranked conductor corridors". Markets and partners pay most of the premium only after ground follow-up, which is ground EM (moving-loop TEM or DC resistivity) and sometimes gravity. That follow-up typically costs C$100–400k (estimate).
   - Rough ladder of land value at each stage (estimate):

| Stage | Indicative value of a 5–20k ha claim block |
|---|---|
| Raw staked ground | ~C$50k–300k |
| Airborne survey done, conductors identified | ~C$0.3–1.5M |
| Ground-confirmed, drill-ready target | ~C$1–5M |
| Mineralized intercept | ~C$10–100M+ |

2. **Option deals are priced on conductors and work commitments.**
   - The typical Athabasca option structure is an earn-in of 51–80% for C$3–15M of exploration spend over 3–5 years, plus C$0.1–1M in cash and shares (estimate).
   - Optionors are often project generators, which hold the ground and farm it out for partners to fund the exploration.
   - Press releases headline "XX km of EM conductors" as the key selling point. So the survey output is effectively the sales collateral for the option.
   - Project generators run this as a business: stake, fly or compile geophysics, then option out. In uranium that means companies such as CanAlaska, Skyharbour, ALX and Fortune Bay (all unverified). This is the "company owning the ground captures the step-up" model the brief asks about. It already exists and is a proven way for small caps to raise money, but no project generator I know of is venture-scale. Most have market caps of C$20–300M (estimate).

3. **Market reaction to survey news is small; reaction to drill results is large.**
   - Airborne survey news ("survey complete", "conductors identified") typically moves a junior's stock 0–15% for a day or two, and often not at all (estimate).
   - A first mineralized intercept can move it 50% to over 1000%. The well-known uranium examples are NexGen Arrow in 2014 and Fission Patterson Lake South in 2012–13. Both were found by drilling EM conductors; PLS was found together with boulder-train work (unverified).
   - Paladin acquired Fission for roughly C$1.1B in 2024 (unverified). That is the scale of value a discovery creates. Survey-stage value is about three orders of magnitude smaller.

4. **Hit rates: how often an anomaly leads to drilling, and drilling to a discovery.**
   - Airborne conductor to drill program: maybe 10–30% of airborne conductor packages ever get drilled (estimate).
   - Drilled conductor to "significant mineralization": roughly 1–5% in the Athabasca (estimate).
   - Grassroots program to economic discovery: commonly quoted as roughly 1 in 100 to 1 in 1000 projects, in the style of MinEx Consulting / Schodde (unverified; I couldn't load their publications page).
   - Many conductors are graphite with no uranium, which is why ground follow-up and drilling are needed. The airborne survey is a necessary filter but has very low specificity.

5. **What a single survey is worth to a client (estimate).**
   - A ~US$100k survey that produces one drill-ready target raises the option value of the ground by about C$0.5–2M. That is a 5–20x return on survey spend, but only for the minority of properties that yield good conductors.
   - The expected value per survey is driven by the probability that the resulting target is optionable or drillable.

6. **Implications for a new venture (my analysis).**
   - The step-up the survey stage creates is real but modest. Most of the land-value capture already happens through the project-generator model.
   - A venture that lowers the cost of a "drill-ready target" (fewer helicopter hours, or skipping the airborne step through ML on legacy or public data) mainly raises the hit rate per survey dollar. That is valuable, but it is a service or property business, not a step-function value capture.
   - The big value is at discovery. A company that owns the ground captures meaningful value only if it is willing to keep equity through to drilling.
   - Venture-scale returns therefore need a portfolio of many cheaply targeted properties optioned for carried interests, not survey-stage resale alone.
