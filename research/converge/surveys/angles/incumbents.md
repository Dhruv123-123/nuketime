## Airborne geophysics: market structure and incumbents

### Key findings
1. **This is a small, cyclical, low-margin contracting business that big companies keep leaving.** The same airborne assets went from CGG to Fugro (1999/2000), back from Fugro to CGG (2013), and then from CGG to Xcalibur (2020–21). Each time the seller treated the unit as non-core. CGG cut its airborne fleet from 16 to 10 airplanes in 2017, flagged Multi-Physics as "market for sale" in Nov 2018, and sold it with the multi-client data library held back. A 2016 attempt to sell Multi-Physics to NEOS was announced, but I couldn't confirm it closed. No price was disclosed for any of these airborne sales.
2. **Margins are thin and swing with the junior-mining cycle.** Aeroquest is the one pure-play that published financials (TSX: AQL). In Q1 FY2012 (Oct–Dec 2011) its revenue was C$7.9M (down 26% YoY), airborne gross margin was 11.6% (27.1% a year earlier), helicopter gross margin was 14.9%, and EBITDA was –C$0.4M. Management blamed "increased competition and pricing pressure" and trouble mobilising aircraft into some countries, which hurt fleet utilisation. Aeroquest had a ~US$57M market cap in Aug 2007. Geotech bought it in 2012 for C$5.6M (C$0.15/share), roughly a 90% drop.
3. **One consolidator is pulling ahead: Xcalibur (Madrid; now "Xcalibur Smart Mapping" / Xcalibur Multiphysics).** It reports 40+ owned aircraft, 400+ staff, 50M line-km flown and 1,400+ projects. Its roll-up so far:
   - Aerial Sign, Colombia (2019)
   - CGG Multi-Physics (closed July 2021)
   - McPhar (2022)
   - MPX Geophysics, Canada (Oct 2023, partly financed by COFIDES)
   - PGW's interpretation division (2025)
   - EMerald Geomodelling, Norway (Mar 2026)
   - A 25.5% stake built in Getech plc (Jul 2026)

   Financing: €40M of asset-backed debt for fleet expansion including helicopters (Kartesia/Flexam and Miralta, Jan 2025), then a minority growth-equity stake from MCH Private Equity (SOPEF fund) and Moira Capital (23 Jun 2026). Its stated goal is to become an "Earth Intelligence platform", which means moving from flying hours to data and interpretation. It is also testing drones and airships with Dronamics and Cloudline.
4. **Everyone else is small and private, and most are founder- or family-owned.**
   - SkyTEM, the leading helicopter TDEM brand, files Danish accounts:
     - Gross profit DKK 47.8M in 2025 (about US$7M), up from 19.1M in 2021.
     - EBIT DKK 7.0M in 2025, after –11.4M in 2024 and –0.6M in 2023.
     - 49–50 employees.
   - Geotech (VTEM/ZTEM, the main Athabasca contractor) is private. It reports 20+ aircraft, 30 VTEM and 10 ZTEM systems (2012 figure), and more than $2M a year of R&D. Its revenue is unknown; ZoomInfo's US$6.9M is clearly too low for 20+ aircraft, and a realistic figure is US$40–80M (estimate).
5. **The market is about US$1–1.4B a year, with mining only part of it.** Verified Market Research puts airborne geophysical services at US$1.06B (2024); another figure is >US$1.35B (2026). Global nonferrous exploration budgets were US$12.4B in 2025, the third straight nominal decline. Uranium exploration budgets were US$331M in 2024 (+33%). If 5–10% of exploration spend goes to airborne work, that is US$0.6–1.2B (estimate). The rest of the market is government regional mapping, groundwater and oil and gas.
6. **Why the large players left.** Airborne survey is capital-heavy (owned aircraft or chartered helicopters, custom EM systems) and lumpy. Utilisation collapses when junior financing dries up (2013–2016). CGG's combined Land and Multi-Physics acquisition revenue fell from US$133M to US$88M (9M 2015 vs 9M 2016, –33%). Mining activity "remained low for most of 2017", with 2017 at about US$102M. The exits are also part of a wider move to asset-light models: CGG shut down most acquisition in 2018, and Fugro left seismic and geoscience in 2012–13. The value they kept was the data. CGG held onto the multi-client library, Bentley paid about US$1.05B for Seequent (geoscience software, roughly US$80M ARR, about 13x) in 2021, and Xcalibur is now buying interpretation and ML assets.

### Consolidation timeline
- **1990s:** High Sense buys Aerodat; CGG owns Geoterrex-Dighem.
- **1999–2001:** Fugro buys Geoterrex-Dighem from CGG, plus High-Sense, Questor, World Geoscience, Geodass and AOC, then Sial, Spectra, Tesla 10, Kevron and parts of Scintrex. The result is Fugro Airborne Surveys (effective 1 Jan 2000).
- **2012:** Geotech buys Aeroquest (and UTS) for C$5.6M after a margin collapse.
- **2012–13:** Fugro sells its Geoscience division to CGG for €1.2B (total purchase price US$1.57B). The airborne tranche closes separately on 2 Sep 2013 after licensing approvals. No airborne-only price was disclosed.
- **2016:** CGG announces a binding agreement to sell Multi-Physics to NEOS; I couldn't confirm it closed.
- **2017:** CGG cuts its fleet from 16 to 10 airplanes. Its helicopter jobs use subcontracted or chartered helicopters, so CGG didn't own rotary assets.
- **2018:** CGG puts Multi-Physics up for sale and books acquisition as a discontinued operation (–US$600M 2018 loss across all acquisition).
- **2020–21:** SPA with Xcalibur (6 Aug 2020); sale closes 30 Jun / 1 Jul 2021.
- **2023–26:** Xcalibur adds MPX, PGW interpretation, EMerald and a 25.5% Getech stake, plus PE and debt funding.

### Pricing and structure
- Contracts are usually a fixed fee per line-km. Mobilisation/demobilisation is quoted as a separate line item and standby is charged per day. CGG's 20-F confirms the per-line-km model.
- A BC assessment report (AeroTEM helicopter, roughly 2005–2008) gives the shape of a bill: 1,070 line-km at C$125/line-km, plus C$17,000 mob/demob, plus C$2,000/day standby, totalling C$173,400.
- A second report (Aeroquest) shows C$194,437 for 1,206 line-km (about C$161/line-km all-in).
- Today, helicopter TDEM is likely C$150–300/line-km plus C$15–40k mobilisation (estimate). That fits the founder's figure of about US$100k for a 300–600 line-km block. The larger Athabasca surveys of 2,500–3,350 line-km would come to roughly C$0.4–0.9M (estimate).
- **Seasonality:** Canadian and northern flying is limited by weather and daylight. The USGS describes winter breaks and November cutoffs. The Oct–Dec quarter was Aeroquest's weak one.

### What this means for a new venture (inference)
- **Weak points in the incumbents:**
  - Fixed per-line-km pricing.
  - Mobilisation costs that dominate small surveys.
  - Utilisation risk the contractor carries itself.
  - Little software or ML layer; even the leader is buying interpretation firms only now.
- **Precedents that argue against an asset-heavy contractor:** Aeroquest's margins, CGG's write-downs and repeated exits. Value builds up in data and libraries (CGG kept its library) and in software (Seequent at about 13x revenue).
- **Main threat:** Xcalibur is putting PE money into fleet, drones and data. A new entrant would compete with a consolidator that has 40+ aircraft and new capital.
