## Helicopter airborne geophysical surveys: what they cost and why (cost angle)

### Findings
1. **The ~US$100k figure holds up as the typical bill for one small survey on one property.** It is not the typical bill for a full program.
   - At sourced rates of about C$120–280 per line-km for helicopter time-domain EM, US$100k buys roughly 500–900 line-km.
   - That is the size of a single-property Athabasca VTEM survey: Stallion flew 676 line-km, and Xcite flew 697 line-km split over 6 projects.
   - Multi-property programs flown at tight line spacing cost US$200k–600k or more. Examples: Cosa 932 km, CanAlaska Frontier 916 km EM plus 3,651 km mag/rad, Forum 1,650 km, CanAlaska Key Lake 2,481 km EM plus 8,861 km mag/rad, Aero Energy 3,350 km.
2. **Electromagnetics (EM) is the expensive part.** It costs about 2–4x more per line-km than magnetics or radiometrics, and about 5x more than large government mag/rad surveys.
3. **Small surveys pay about twice as much per km.** Fixed costs (mobilisation, ferry, system set-up, minimum crew days) are spread over few km. Sourced data:
   - 83 km VTEM: C$279/km
   - 304 km AeroTEM: C$263/km
   - 754 km AeroTEM: C$117/km for acquisition, C$132/km all-in
   - 1,765 km VTEM: about C$170/km
   - 10,000 km VTEM: about US$140/km
4. **Biggest cost component: helicopter time** (aircraft, pilot, fuel, ferry), about 40–50% of the bill (estimate). It is expensive because the work is slow and low. Systems fly at 80–90 km/h with the sensor at 30–80 m above ground. At 100–200 m line spacing, each km² needs 5–10 line-km. An AS350B3 or H125 rents for C$2,850–3,130 per hour.
   - **On small surveys, the biggest component is mobilisation plus standby.** These are fixed costs. They are what push the per-km rate from about C$120–170 up to C$260–280.

- **Per-km rate in AeroTEM/VTEM invoices.** The Berg cost statement books all of the EM spend (C$88,394, 754.35 line-km) as one line, with no separate mobilisation charge. That suggests mobilisation is often folded into the per-km rate on small jobs (inference). Geoscience BC tender guidance says to quote a firm price per line-km, with mobilisation/demobilisation as an optional separate item and standby included in the per-km rate.
- **Contract terms (SkyTEM/MCWD 2017 contract).** Billing has four parts: a line-km rate, fixed mobilisation/demobilisation charges, standby charges, and pro-rated re-mobilisation after weather pull-backs. Standby is charged on any day the contractor collects fewer than 100 km for reasons outside its control. Rates are set in the proposal; the dollar figures are not public.
- **Minimums (Geotech FAQ).** There is no minimum survey size. The minimum line length is 3 km for heli mag, TDEM and FDEM, and 10 km for ZTEM, fixed-wing and gravity. Standby is billed for days lost to weather, magnetic (diurnal) activity or local issues.

**Why it is expensive, structurally:**
- The aircraft is the cost. Heli rates are C$2–3k+/hr, and the system has to fly slowly at low altitude.
- Tight line spacing multiplies km per km²: 100–200 m spacing means 5–10 line-km per km².
- Remote northern logistics: fuel caches, ferry flights and camps. Yukon assessment reports say the same thing.
- Weather and seasonal standby risk.
- Low-level flying with a sling load raises insurance and safety overhead.
- Few contractors own the proprietary EM systems:
  - Geotech: 30+ VTEM systems, more than 20 aircraft, privately held.
  - CGG/Xcalibur: HeliTEM.
  - SkyTEM.
  - NRG: Xcite.
  - Expert Geophysics: MobileMT and Target EM.
  - Aeroquest: AeroTEM.
- Removing either the helicopter or the mobilisation cost is the obvious lever. Two related figures:
  - Drone magnetics already costs US$150–600/km all-in (US$200–400 on most projects), with US$5–25k mobilisation and viable surveys from about 50–100 km. That source is vendor-reported.
  - Wyoming's survey office estimated a statewide mag/rad survey at about US$20M, and adding EM plus gravity gradiometry at about 10x that (about US$200M).

### Context
- **Market size:** estimates for the global airborne geophysical services market range from US$1.06B (Verified Market Research, 2024) to US$2.56B (SkyQuest).
- **Exploration spending:** Canadian exploration spend was C$4.1B in 2024. Saskatchewan was C$561.6M in 2024 and C$663.1M in 2025 intentions (NRCan).
- **Cameco:** spent more than C$12M on regional target-generation geophysics and drilling.
- **No subsidy for airborne work in Saskatchewan:** its TMEI 25% rebate covers ground and borehole geophysics, not airborne (cap C$50k/yr for uranium). Western Australia's EIS co-funds 50% of airborne surveys, up to A$250k per project.

### Gaps
- I found no public itemised invoice that splits out helicopter hours, fuel and crew, so that breakdown is modelled.
- Uranium-specific Saskatchewan assessment cost statements were not reachable online; the Saskatchewan Mineral Assessment Database is the next place to look.
- No published rate cards for HeliTEM, SkyTEM or Xcite.
- Mobilisation fees are only available from an unverified vendor blog (US$50k–150k for crewed surveys).
- Most per-km points are from 2008–2014 BC filings. Recent anchors are Medallion 2026 (about A$217/km) and Precision's 2017 brochure. Inflation and 2026 fuel prices probably push current rates up 20–40% (estimate).
