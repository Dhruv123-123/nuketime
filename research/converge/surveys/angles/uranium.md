## Uranium airborne surveys: findings (Athabasca focus)

**Research limit:** the shared WebSearch budget for this turn (200 calls across all agents) was used up before this agent's first search ran. Everything below comes from directly fetching company news pages and press-release PDFs. **No primary source I could reach gave a dollar cost for an airborne survey.** Juniors almost never publish survey cost. Every $/line-km and budget-share figure below is therefore marked (estimate). A follow-up turn with search budget should target SMAD assessment-report expenditure statements and NI 43-101 budget tables, which do list costs.

### Bottom line
- **In uranium, the airborne survey is a small, early line item, not the binding cost.** In the Athabasca the ore sits at or below the sandstone–basement unconformity, roughly 100–1,000 m deep (sandstone cover 100–1,000 m thick; Wikipedia).
  - Airborne EM and mag are used to map basement graphitic conductors and magnetic-low corridors at regional scale.
  - The money then goes to **ground EM**, mainly stepwise moving-loop TDEM (SWML-TDEM), to pin conductors precisely enough to drill, and then to **drilling**.
- **Evidence that drilling dominates:**
  - Denison spent about C$10M on exploration in H1 2026 and drilled more than 50,000 m in 140 holes on 10 properties. It also ran geophysical surveys on 14 properties, method not stated.
  - CanAlaska's C$15M 2026 West McArthur budget (Cameco JV) is three drill rigs plus one ground SWML-TDEM survey. No airborne work is in it.
- **Ground EM costs more per km than airborne.** CanAlaska's 2025 West McArthur SWML-TDEM covered about 193 line-km in 55 loops (Dias Geophysical, SQUID sensor). Its 2026 Waterbury South program is about 90 km (SJ Geophysics, Volterra-EM).
  - At about C$3–6k per line-km (estimate), 193 km is roughly C$0.6–1.2M (estimate) for one corridor.
  - That is roughly 20–40x airborne TDEM per km (estimate).
- **Where airborne is big:** grassroots, land-bank-scale portfolios.
  - Atha Energy flew what it called the "largest ever multiplatform EM survey" in the basin over 16 projects covering 2.1M acres (of a 3.4M-acre package), using VTEM, Xcite, MobileMT, ZTEM and QMAGt.
  - Its North Terra / North Pinnacle block alone was 7,166 line-km of Axiom Xcite heli-TDEM. At about C$150–250/line-km (estimate) that is roughly C$1.1–1.8M (estimate), and the whole program was likely C$5–15M (estimate).
  - This is the one buyer profile for whom airborne is material.
- **The founder's ~US$100k per survey fits a small block:** about 400–700 line-km of heli TDEM/mag at about C$150–250/line-km plus mobilisation (estimate). Against Athabasca holes costing about C$250–700k each (estimate), one survey costs less than one drill hole.

### Which system is chosen depends on depth to the unconformity (from Atha's 2023 release)
| Depth to unconformity | Systems used | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Shallow to moderate (basin margins, East Rim near McArthur: surface to >500 m) | Heli TDEM: Geotech VTEM, Axiom Xcite | Atha East Rim; CanAlaska Key Lake-area projects (modern VTEM filling gaps in historical VTEM) |
| Deep (>800 m, e.g. Cable Bay) | MobileMT (Expert Geophysics, heli AFMAG-type passive EM), ZTEM | Atha Cable Bay; Purepoint 2026 MobileMT at Dorado and Henday Lake (IsoEnergy JVs), plus Celeste East, Russell South and Tabbernor |
| Alteration / structure | Airborne gravity gradiometry (Xcalibur Falcon Plus); gravity lows that coincide with conductors mark alteration | Skyharbour South Falcon Point: 2,843 line-km at 200 m spacing, 80 m height, 44,470 ha (2022) |
| Cheap structural mapping | Fixed-wing mag/VLF, heli mag, UAV-mag | Valor (Hook Lake): 5,100 line-km fixed-wing mag/VLF at 75 m (Precision GeoSurveys, 2021); Medaro (Yurchison): 1,424 line-km heli mag at 50 m (Geodata Solutions, 2022); Skyharbour Moore: UAV-MAG |
| Radiometrics | Minor in the basin under thick sandstone; used outside it (e.g. Western Athabasca Syndicate VTEM plus radiometrics) | — |

GEOTEM and MEGATEM are fixed-wing TDEM systems. I found no recent Athabasca use of them; they appear to have been replaced by heli TDEM and MobileMT (estimate).

- Mobilisation to northern Saskatchewan: about C$15–50k per survey (estimate).

### Budget split for a typical Athabasca junior (estimate)
- Airborne: about 3–10%, concentrated in year 1 of a new land package.
- Ground EM and resistivity: about 10–20%.
- Drilling, including camp and helicopter support: about 60–80%.
- Earn-in deals show the scale: Medaro's option required C$5M of exploration over 3 years, and Cosa committed C$6.5M on three Denison properties. One airborne survey is a few percent of those.
- Saskatchewan assessment requirements (about C$15/ha per year in years 2–10, rising later; estimate, not verified) make airborne a cheap way to file credits across large land packages. That supports demand for the survey even when its exploration value is marginal.

### Is airborne a binding pain point?
- **Mostly no, for uranium.** Problems that are bigger than the survey invoice:
  1. **Resolution at depth:** airborne cannot locate conductors at 500–1,000 m precisely enough to drill. That forces C$0.5–1M+ ground SWML-TDEM per corridor (estimate). This is the costlier and slower step.
  2. **Seasonality and contractor scheduling:** heli surveys are flown in summer; ground EM usually runs in winter; drilling runs in both. Missing a window loses a season.
  3. **Interpretation:** plate modelling and 3D inversion are done by small consultancies (Convolutions Geoscience, Earthfield Technology, Expert Geophysics' own modelling). That is a services opportunity, but a small one.
- **A venture-scale angle exists only if airborne replaces ground EM or drill holes**, for example deep-penetrating passive EM (MobileMT-type) or drone TDEM at a fraction of heli cost, with drill-ready conductor locations at 800 m+. Cutting survey cost alone saves a few percent of a junior's budget.
- **Market size:** Saskatchewan uranium exploration is about C$300–400M a year at the recent peak (estimate). Airborne is roughly C$15–40M of that (estimate), which is small for a uranium-only company.
