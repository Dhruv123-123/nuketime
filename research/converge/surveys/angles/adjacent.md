## Adjacent buyers of airborne EM and magnetics outside mineral exploration

**Caveat on sourcing.** Web search was unavailable for this run: the shared budget of 200 searches per turn was already used up before my first query. Direct fetches mostly returned 403 or 404. Only the figures tagged **[sourced]** come from a page I actually read. Every other number is from memory and is marked **(estimate)**. Check them before any deck or model relies on them. A follow-up run with search budget could confirm them.

### Findings first

1. **Governments are the largest non-mining buyer, and they are spending more.** They buy for two reasons: national critical-minerals programmes and groundwater mapping.
   - **USGS Earth MRI** cumulative data-acquisition spend: $7M (FY19), $14M (FY20), $21M (FY21), $77M (FY22), $129M (FY23), $199M (FY24), $266M (FY25) **[sourced]**. The jump came from the IIJA, which adds $320M over 5 years from FY2021 **[sourced]**.
   - US coverage is still thin. Magnetic/radiometric coverage went from 8% to 26% of the US between FY19 and FY25. EM coverage went from 1% to 2% between FY22 and FY25 **[sourced]**. About 98% of the US has no modern AEM.
   - USGS issued low-level flight alerts for new surveys in New Mexico (Sep 2026) and eastern California (Oct 2026) **[sourced]**, so the programme is still flying in 2026.
   - Similar programmes elsewhere (all estimates):
     - Saudi Arabia: national regional geophysical programme, hundreds of thousands of line-km.
     - Canada: critical-minerals geoscience funding, about C$80M.
     - Australia: Exploring for the Future, about A$225M over 2016–2024. In 2024 it was followed by "Resourcing Australia's Prosperity", a national mapping commitment of about A$3.4B over decades.
     - Africa: Botswana, Zambia, Morocco and Namibia national airborne programmes.
   - **Assessment: large and growing.** Spending is tied to critical-minerals policy and supply-chain security.

2. **Groundwater is the most proven adjacent market for AEM specifically.** The SkyTEM system was developed at Aarhus University for Danish groundwater mapping.
   - SkyTEM lists its markets as **water, minerals and geotechnical** **[sourced]**.
   - It reports **over 2 million helicopter line-km flown** across 7 continents **[sourced]**.
   - Example jobs **[sourced]**:
     - Botswana regional aquifer survey (Tshukudu, Ghanzi): 18,970 line-km.
     - Kemcore survey near Maun: about 6,100 line-km.
     - BRGM (French geological survey): repeat work over 15+ years, on the mainland and in overseas territories.
     - Greenland: permafrost work.
   - **Denmark:** national groundwater mapping ran from about 1999 to 2015 at about DKK 2B (roughly US$300M) (estimate), funded by a levy on water bills (estimate). It is now largely complete, with only update and infill work left.
   - **AusAEM (Geoscience Australia):** regional flying at **20 km line spacing, starting 2017** **[sourced]**. AusAEM1 was about 60,000 line-km over about 1.1M km² (estimate). The cumulative total is probably over 200,000 line-km (estimate). The output is used for both minerals and groundwater.
   - **California SGMA:** DWR's statewide AEM programme ran from 2021 to about 2023. It flew roughly 10,000 line-miles over high- and medium-priority basins, with SkyTEM as the operator (estimate). Funding was about US$12–15M (estimate). The DWR page returned 403.
   - **India:** CSIR-NGRI and CGWB ran heliborne aquifer-mapping pilots under NAQUIM from about 2017, using SkyTEM. A larger heliborne phase over arid regions (Rajasthan, Gujarat, Haryana, Punjab) followed in the 2020s, worth tens of crore rupees, roughly US$5–15M per tranche (estimate).
   - **Assessment: growing.** Drought, aquifer depletion and SGMA-style regulation are spreading to other US states, India, MENA and the Sahel. The buyers are government agencies with lumpy, tender-driven budgets.

3. **Geotechnical and infrastructure corridors are a small but real niche that is growing, mostly in Europe.**
   - Uses: rail, highway and tunnel route screening for depth to bedrock, quick clay (Norway) and karst.
   - Buyers: Norwegian road and rail agencies, Danish and Dutch dike and coastal work, Swedish tunnelling.
   - A typical job is 500–3,000 line-km, worth about $100k–$600k (estimate).
   - Transmission-line and pipeline routing and HS2-type megaprojects are the upside.
   - Market size: about $20–60M a year globally (estimate).

4. **UXO is a separate submarket that buys mostly magnetometry.**
   - It is flown as low-altitude helicopter magnetometry (e.g. HeliMag/boom systems). Offshore UXO work is mostly marine.
   - Buyers: US DoD (FUDS and BRAC sites), European munitions sites, Ukraine demining, offshore-wind cable routes.
   - Market for airborne UXO: about $30–100M a year (estimate).
   - **Assessment: rising sharply.** Ukraine has an estimated 140,000+ km² of potentially contaminated land, and demining donors are funding drone- and helicopter-based magnetics there. This is the most demand-elastic adjacent market (estimate).

5. **Defence magnetic navigation (MagNav) is an emerging adjacent market.**
   - The USAF/MIT work on GPS-denied navigation needs high-resolution magnetic anomaly maps. Continental-scale aeromagnetic data becomes strategic infrastructure.
   - Spending today is R&D: single-digit $M a year (estimate). It could become a large government mapping customer if MagNav is fielded (estimate).
   - **Assessment: growing, but early.**

6. **Smaller markets** (all estimates; each is likely under $20M a year of airborne spend):
   - **Permafrost:** Alaska, Greenland and Canadian Arctic infrastructure. Growing with climate-driven ground instability.
   - **Soil and dryland salinity:** Australia's Murray–Darling was the biggest historical use. Flat to declining.
   - **Geothermal:** mostly magnetotellurics (MT) and gravity; airborne magnetics and gravity are useful for regional structure. Growing slowly with next-generation geothermal (Fervo-style developers).
   - **Oil sands:** muskeg and aggregate mapping and shallow channel delineation. Flat.
   - **Carbon storage siting:** AEM is too shallow for the reservoir. It helps with near-surface monitoring baselines and fault screening, and gravity and magnetics help regionally. Nascent.
   - **Archaeology:** negligible spend.

### Market sizing summary (all rows are estimates unless marked)

| Segment | Airborne spend/yr (global) | Trend | Main buyers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mineral exploration, private (baseline) | $600M–1B | Cyclical, rising with critical minerals | Juniors and majors |
| National geoscience programmes | $200–400M | **Growing** (US Earth MRI to $266M cumulative **[sourced]**; AU, Saudi Arabia, Africa) | Geological surveys |
| Groundwater | $50–150M | **Growing** | Water agencies, development banks |
| UXO and demining | $30–100M | **Growing fast** (Ukraine, offshore wind) | DoD, NATO, donors |
| Geotechnical and infrastructure | $20–60M | Growing | Road and rail agencies, engineering firms |
| Defence MagNav mapping | <$10M now | Emerging | USAF, DARPA-type programmes |
| Permafrost, salinity, geothermal, CCS, oil sands, archaeology | <$20M each | Mixed | Utilities, operators |

Total airborne geophysical services market: about **$1–2B a year** (estimate). Main players:
- Xcalibur Multiphysics, which absorbed CGG's airborne unit.
- SkyTEM, Geotech (VTEM) and New Resolution.
- Expert Geophysics (MobileMT), Sander Geophysics and EON.
- A long tail of drone-magnetics operators.

### Unit economics context (all estimates)

- Helicopter TDEM: about **$150–300 per line-km**, plus **$20–60k mobilisation**. A 300–500 line-km junior-scale block therefore lands near the founder's **~$100k** figure.
- Fixed-wing magnetics and radiometrics: about $5–20 per line-km.
- National programmes keep costs down with wide spacing (AusAEM uses 20 km **[sourced]**) and very large contracts of 10k–100k+ line-km.
- Drone magnetics: about $20–60 per line-km, but limited to small areas.

### Implications for a new venture

- **Larger total market.** Government groundwater, critical-minerals, UXO and infrastructure buyers roughly double the market beyond mining exploration (estimate). They are less tied to the commodity cycle than junior explorers.
- **The best adjacent market is UXO and demining.** Demand is growing fastest there, and it is a natural fit for cheaper drone magnetics.
- **The biggest single buyer pool is national programmes.** Earth MRI, Geoscience Australia and Saudi Arabia buy at huge volume and wide spacing. They reward whoever has the lowest cost per line-km.
- **Groundwater is the proven EM market.** It is now spreading from Denmark to California, India and Africa.
- **Business-model risk.** These buyers procure through tenders, sales cycles are slow, and the incumbents (SkyTEM, Xcalibur) are entrenched.
