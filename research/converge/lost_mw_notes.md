# R7: Lost megawatts at operating nuclear plants (DVR + ML diagnostics, gain-share)
Research date: 2026-10-03. [est] = my estimate.

## 2. DVR in nuclear (done first)

- **EPRI TR 3002018337** ("Use of Data Validation and Reconciliation Methods for Measurement Uncertainty Recapture"): NRC final SE issued; approval dated **Aug 11, 2023** (per Salem LAR, ML26002A031). Final SE: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2319/ML23193A782.pdf ; approved -A version ML23285A311 https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2328/ML23285A311.pdf . The SE's illustrative example: feedwater flow uncertainty from ±2.0% to ±0.6% lets an RTP move 3500 -> 3549 MWt (+49 MWt, +1.4%). Staff called DVR a "new technology" with conditions.
- **Bruce Power (BTB Jansky PROCESSPLUS)**: selected 2020 (https://btbjansky.com/news/2020-processplus-bruce-power). Mechanism: "reconciled correction factors for feed water flow and temperature" applied to plant instrumentation (i.e., the plant was conservatively under-reading its power due to biased feedwater flow/temperature measurement, so was running below licensed thermal power). Result per WNN (Dec 5, 2024): **39.7 MWe total across 8 CANDU units, CAD 15.9M/yr**, expected **>50 MWe** after Units 3 and 4 MCR returns in 2027-28. https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/bruce-power-signs-long-term-master-services-agreement . BTB site separately: "9 MWe (approx. 1%) Power Recovery at Unit A3" (https://www.btbjansky.com/, accessed 2026-10-03). => ~5 MWe/unit average, up to ~9 MWe/unit (~1%). Implied value CAD ~400k per MWe-yr (~CAD 46/MWh at ~100% CF) [est from the two numbers]. (The "42 MW" figure in the brief could not be found in a primary source; 39.7 MWe -> >50 MWe is the sourced number.)
- **BTB Jansky footprint** (company site, accessed 2026-10-03): 70+ nuclear+fossil units; Canada 8, USA 7, Europe 20+ sites (BE, DE, HU, NL, SI, ES, CH, SE), UAE Barakah 1-4, Japan 11 units; claims 190 MWe recovery potential identified, "$68.4M/yr"; "up to 40 MWe" per NPP unit; Gösgen (CH) 10+ yrs. https://www.btbjansky.com/
- **GSE + Belsim VALI** (US leader): NEI Magazine (Dec 17, 2021): >30 US units have implemented DVR software via GSE TrueNorth/Belsim; three stations achieved power recovery from DVR feedwater correction, **combined 20 MWe**. https://www.neimagazine.com/advanced-reactorsfusion/how-data-validation-and-reconciliation-improves-nuclear-plant-performance-9329611/ . GSE DVR page (accessed 2026-10-03): "utilities are recovering ~15-20 MWe"; "many plants will have recoverable losses up to 5 MWe, ~$1M/yr"; "power recoveries up to ~15 MWe" on multiple units; uses $25/MWh -> $200k/MWe-yr. https://gses.com/power-generation/dvr/
- **PSEG Salem LAR S25-03** (submitted Dec 31, 2025; ML26002A031 https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2600/ML26002A031.pdf): adds DVR (GSE + Belsim, VALI, VDI-2048 compliant, per EPRI TR) as the **primary** MUR method, Crossflow UFM retained as backup. **Not an uprate**: CLTP stays 3,459 MWt; DVR reconciled total FW flow uncertainty <0.54%, which supports the existing 1.4% MUR from 2001. Pre-sub meetings Mar 5, 2025 (ML25078A217) and Sep 23, 2025 (ML25272A016). Key read: the first US DVR licensing use is replacing an ultrasonic meter that drifts/fails, i.e., protecting existing MW, not adding new ones. **Verified: ML26037A270 = PSEG supplement LR-N26-0007, Feb 6, 2026** (https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML2603/ML26037A270.pdf): DVR uses ~250 plant instruments per unit; addresses all **11 conditions/limitations** in the Aug 2023 NRC SE (linear constraints, instrument error accounting, validation data showing reconciled error < measurement error).
- **Europe**: DVR codified as VDI 2048 (Germany); Belsim VALI and BTB used at German/Swiss/Belgian plants; Angra 2 (Brazil, Siemens/KWU PWR) DVR paper https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/22126270 . BTB lists BE/DE/CH/NL/SE/ES/SI/HU nuclear+fossil sites.

## 1. How big are lost MW at operating LWRs

**Feedwater venturi fouling / calorimetric error (the biggest single, best-documented bucket)**
- Caldon (2005, ML072700849 https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ml0727/ml072700849.pdf): measured venturi fouling bias **0.1%-2.6% across 21 plants, average 1.0%**; dynamic fouling 1-2%; one case 1.2% during power ascension. External LEFM test installations identified **435 MW** of recovery; permanent installs recovered **307 MW**, **7-30 MW per plant**. Appendix K (MUR) uprates via LEFM: **25 plants, +368 MW as of Jan 1, 2005**. Chordal LEFM in 32 plants; external LEFM in 53.
- Mechanism: fouled venturi reads HIGH -> calorimetric says power is higher than real -> plant runs below licensed thermal power. Typical 1% of a 1,000 MWe unit = ~10 MWe [est].
- The cautionary tale: power-recovery via Crossflow UFM correction factors (under 10 CFR 50.59, no NRC review) led to **Byron 1 running ~2.7% over licensed thermal power for several years** (found Aug 2003); Byron 1/2 and Braidwood 1/2 all overpowered. NRC UFM allegation task group report, 2004: https://www.nrc.gov/docs/ML0417/ML041760370.pdf . Consequence: NRC and utilities are now very wary of software/correction-factor "power recovery"; this is the regulatory scar tissue any new entrant inherits.

**Cycle isolation / valve leakage**
- Curtiss-Wright (Valve Magazine, Fall 2019): cycle isolation losses can exceed 400 Btu/kWh in some cases; "typical savings ... 1 MW per unit (~$250k/yr at $29/MWh)"; cites 1.3% generation improvement case. https://www.cw-connect.com/resources/article-cycle-isolation-monitoring
- NEI Magazine (Sep 5, 2018, True North): ~**150 high-energy valves per plant** are monitoring candidates; Columbia (1,207 MWe BWR) leaking evaporator relief valve cost **$330k over 6.5 weeks** (~4-5 MWe at ~$30-35/MWh [est]). https://www.neimagazine.com/analysis/finding-the-lost-megawatts-6759767/

**Condenser fouling / air in-leakage / cooling**
- EPRI 2004 NPPI paper (via Intek): San Onofre unit 1,107 MWe rated, 1,080 actual; air binding loss **8.7-13.2 MWe (1.2%)**; TMI-1 averaged 2.9% below design capacity; projected 12 MWe recovery at one site, B/C 26.8:1 over 5 yrs. https://intekflow.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/EPRI-2004-NPPI-Paper.pdf
- EPRI TR-107422-V2 (Oct 1998): condenser improvements can "recover up to 25 MW" at a nuclear plant; EPRI survey: cooling towers average 85% of rated capability (= +2-2.5°F CW temp); Watts Bar 22.3 MWe per °F wet bulb when backpressure-limited; TVA cooling tower deficiency 34,000 MWh/yr (~3.9 MW avg). https://restservice.epri.com/publicdownload/TR-107422-V2/0/Product

**Feedwater heaters**: EPRI TR-107422-V2: +1°F top heater TTD = +0.016% heat rate; other stages 0.013%; 3°F final FW temp = ~0.05%. => a 10°F FW temp shortfall on a 1,100 MWe unit ~ 1.5-2 MWe [est]. Small individually, chronic.

**Turbine degradation**: no clean fleet number found; steam-path degradation typically 0.5-1.5% over a cycle on HP sections [est, needs confirmation]. Fix is a capital turbine retrofit, not software.

**IAEA TECDOC-1971 (2021)**: plants typically set "action" thresholds of **1-3 MWe** of unexplained loss before launching a recovery search. https://www-pub.iaea.org/MTCD/Publications/PDF/TE-1971web.pdf (Implies that loss below 1-3 MWe often goes uninvestigated.)

**Sizing synthesis [est]**: For a typical 1,000-1,200 MWe US LWR, recoverable chronic losses look like: FW flow/temperature measurement bias 0-15 MWe (median ~3-5 since most US units already run LEFM/Crossflow); cycle isolation 1-5 MWe; condenser/air in-leakage 2-13 MWe (seasonal); FW heaters 1-3 MWe. Realistic software-identified, operator-actionable recovery: **~3-8 MWe/unit average**, with fat tail of 15-20 MWe units. US fleet 94 units x 5 MWe = **~470 MWe** [est] ~ 4 TWh/yr. Cross-check: Bruce 39.7 MWe / 8 units = 5 MWe/unit; GSE "~15-20 MWe" utility-wide / "up to 5 MWe" typical; BTB 190 MWe potential across 70+ units = ~2.7 MWe/unit.

## 3. MUR uprates

- NRC approved-uprates table (fetched 2026-10-03): **57 MUR uprates totaling 2,013 MWt** (~670 MWe at 33% [est]); 63 stretch, 28 EPU in that table (148 total). https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/licensing/power-uprates/status-power-apps/approved-applications.html . NRC backgrounder (updated Sep 14, 2026): 171 uprates approved as of Jan 2022, ~24,089 MWt / 8,030 MWe total. https://www.nrc.gov/reading-rm/doc-collections/fact-sheets/power-uprates
- Recent MURs: Catawba 1/2 (2016, 1.7%, 58 MWt each), Columbia (2017, 1.66%), Peach Bottom 2/3 (2017, 65 MWt each), Hope Creek (2018), Farley 1/2 (2020, 46 MWt each), Watts Bar 2 (2020, 1.4%), Oconee 1-3 (2021, 42 MWt each), Millstone 3 (2021, 59 MWt). Typical MUR = 1.4-1.7% = 40-65 MWt = **~13-22 MWe/unit** [est at 33-34% efficiency].
- **Remaining candidates**: NRC expected-applications page (updated Sep 8, 2026): **10 MUR applications, 754 MWt** expected (incl. Brunswick 1/2 Q1 2027, Wolf Creek Q2 2027), plus 2 SPU (Salem 1/2 Q2 2027) and **19 EPU, 6,582 MWt** (McGuire 1/2, Hatch 1/2, Columbia, Catawba 1, Vogtle 1/2, Perry, Beaver Valley 1/2, Davis-Besse, plus proprietary Constellation). Total 31 applications / 7,336 MWt. https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/operating-reactors/operating-reactor-licensing/power-uprates/status-of-power-uprate-applications/expected-applications-for-power-uprates . Pending (under review) page shows none as of Sep 15, 2026. RIS 2025-02 (Feb 7, 2025) surveyed licensees' uprate plans (Morgan Lewis: https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/upandatom/2025/02/planning-a-reactor-power-uprate-time-to-get-in-line-at-the-nrc).
- => Of ~94 US units, ~55-60 already took an MUR (some units more than one) [est from NRC table]; ~10 more planned; the residual "never-MUR" pool is roughly **25-30 units** [est], many of which are skipping straight to SPU/EPU. MUR itself is a largely harvested market.
- **UFM incumbents**: Sensia (Cameron/Caldon) LEFM CheckPlus on **90+ PWR/BWR reactors worldwide**, typical 0.3% uncertainty, NRC-approved for MUR up to 1.7% since 1999. https://www.sensiaglobal.com/measurement/ultrasonic-flow-meters-nuclear . AMAG (Westinghouse/Crossflow) is the other; Crossflow was implicated in the Byron/Braidwood overpower (2003) and later root-cause work (ML081630410).
- **How DVR changes the MUR path**: (a) no new spool-piece hardware or outage installation; uses existing instrumentation (200-300 measured variables, NEI Mag 2021); (b) EPRI TR + NRC SE (Aug 2023) gives a generic licensing path; GSE claims DVR supports MUR "up to 1.5%" (https://gses.com/power-generation/mur/); EPRI SE example ±2.0% -> ±0.6% FW flow; BTB claims 0.4% (2σ); (c) first US LAR (Salem, Dec 2025) uses DVR to **replace/back up** the UFM for an existing 1.4% MUR, not to add MW. So DVR in US licensing is currently a resilience/maintenance play (UFMs go out of service -> plant must derate to pre-MUR power), and an MUR enabler for the remaining ~25-30 units [est]. Note DVR uncertainty (~0.5%) is worse than LEFM (~0.3%), so DVR cannot beat an LEFM on recapture size.

**Pricing / gain-share evidence**
- No public pricing for PEPSE/EtaPRO/VALI. Typical model is perpetual or annual license + engineering services T&M [est; industry norm].

## 6. Balance-of-plant limits in uprates

- **INL review (July 2026)**: Kim, Al Dawood, Williams, Mikkelson, Westover, "A review of limitations, upgrade strategies, and performance projections for nuclear power generation system uprates," *Nuclear Engineering and Technology* 58(12) 104580, online **Jul 24, 2026**. Finding: "uprates above ~20% increasingly become a balance-of-plant throughput problem." Limiters: steam-path choking, turbine exhaust and moisture limits, condenser backpressure, FW/condensate pump and valve capacity margins. Three architectures: parallel steam conversion (supplemental TG), heat-balance optimization (regenerative FW heating changes), steam diversion/cogeneration. Calls for "integrated, constraint-based evaluation" and validated models. https://inl.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/a-review-of-limitations-upgrade-strategies-and-performance-projec/
- **INL cost table (INL/RPT-25-89185, Dec 2025)**: MUR $24M for 17 MWe (**$1,412/kW**); PWR SPU/EPU $635M/80 MWe ($7,938/kW); BWR EPU $660M/220 MWe ($3,000/kW); PWR EPU+ $1.48B/190 MWe ($7,789/kW); BWR EPU+ $880M/330 MWe ($2,667/kW). Target: EO 14302 goal of **5 GW of uprates by 2030**. https://lwrs.inl.gov/content/uploads/11/2026/03/Preliminary-Projections-to-Incentivize-NP.pdf
- **Typical BOP mods** (NRC/Morgan Lewis/ANS): HP turbine replacement, condensate and FW pumps + motors, main generator (rewind/upgrade, H2 cooling), main transformers, MSR, FW heaters, condenser, plus cooling water/thermal discharge permits. Calvert Cliffs (Sep 2026): 190 MWe (~10%) EPU, ">$3B" Maryland investment (likely bundles SLR; [est]). Bruce Power (Nov 24, 2025): Siemens Energy new steam turbines, **+30 MW per unit, 125 MW total, 2028-2031**. https://www.brucepower.com/2025/11/24/bruce-power-signs-exclusive-agreement-with-siemens-energy-canada-for-new-steam-turbines-to-increase-output-power-ontarios-clean-energy-future/
- **Who does BOP uprate engineering**: NSSS OEMs (Westinghouse, Framatome, GE Vernova-Hitachi) own the reactor-side analysis and usually prime; turbine OEMs (Siemens Energy, GE Vernova, Mitsubishi/Toshiba) own the steam-path redesign; A/E firms (Sargent & Lundy https://www.sargentlundy.com/services/nuclear-power/ , Enercon https://www.enercon.com/markets/nuclear-power/ , Bechtel, Zachry/MPR) do BOP scoping, heat balances (mostly in PEPSE), mods and 50.59s. 
- **Engineering study costs**: no public figure found. [est] BOP feasibility/scoping study $1-5M per site; full EPU engineering + licensing $50-150M, i.e., ~5-15% of the INL capex numbers. The PSEG Salem, Duke McGuire/Catawba, Southern Hatch/Vogtle, Vistra Perry/DB/BV, Constellation programs (sec. 3 list) are ~20-30 units of near-term demand for BOP studies.
- Wedge for a software entrant: a calibrated, DVR-reconciled "digital heat balance" of the as-operated plant is exactly the input an uprate BOP study needs (true margins on pumps, condenser, turbine flow capacity). The incumbents build these models by hand in PEPSE from design data + test runs.

## 7. Strongest arguments against this company

1. **The big bucket is already harvested.** Venturi fouling averaged ~1.0% (Caldon, 21 plants), but since 1999 UFMs have been installed on 90+ reactors and 57+ MURs taken (2,013 MWt). Most US units already run on LEFM/Crossflow, so the "free" feedwater-bias megawatts are largely captured. What remains is 1-5 MWe/unit of diffuse losses (cycle isolation, condenser, heaters) that plants consciously tolerate below their 1-3 MWe action thresholds (IAEA TECDOC-1971).
2. **Competent, entrenched, cheap incumbents.** GSE+Belsim (>30 US units, and the Salem LAR vendor), BTB Jansky (70+ units incl. Bruce, Barakah, Japan), Curtiss-Wright PEPSE/FAMOS (the standard heat-balance tool), EPRI (free to members). DVR is a 20+ yr-old method codified in VDI 2048; physics-informed reconciliation is not novel. The "ML diagnostics" layer has to show something DVR + a thermal performance engineer can't.
3. **Regulatory scar tissue.** Byron 1 ran ~2.7% over licensed power for years after correction-factor "power recovery" (found Aug 2003). The NRC SE on DVR carries 11 conditions; the first US DVR LAR (Salem, Dec 2025/Feb 2026) still isn't approved, and it is used to *protect* an existing MUR, not add MW. A startup's software that touches calorimetric power is safety-related or near it: 10 CFR 50 App. B QA, software V&V, NRC review, 1-2 yr licensing. Utilities will want an established vendor of record.
4. **Gain-share is unproven and awkward.** I found zero public examples of a nuclear thermal-performance vendor paid on recovered MWh. Utilities' supply-chain and rate-regulated accounting prefer fixed-price; merchant owners (Constellation, Vistra, Talen) have in-house fleet performance groups and M&D centers and would rather keep 100% of the MWh. Attribution disputes (was it your model or our maintenance?) and baselining against weather/seasonal condenser swings make gain-share contracts hard to write.
5. **Small TAM for the core product.** ~5 MWe/unit x ~$550k/MWe-yr = ~$2.75M/yr of value per unit; at 15-25% share, ~$0.4-0.7M/unit/yr. US fleet 94 units -> ~$25-50M ARR ceiling even at high penetration [est]. Global adds maybe 2-3x. Customer count is tiny (~25 US operators, ~10 dominant; Constellation alone ~25 units).
6. **Data access and cyber.** Plant data lives behind NEI 08-09/10 CFR 73.54 boundaries; most thermal data is in plant PI historians that do flow out to corporate networks one-way, which is workable, but cloud/SaaS in nuclear faces long security reviews. DOE/INL already funds advanced remote monitoring with the Utilities Service Alliance (https://inl.gov/nuclear-energy/u-s-energy-department-invests-in-advanced-remote-monitoring-project/), so the labs offer free alternatives.
7. **Uprate BOP engineering is a services business with entrenched A/Es and OEMs.** Westinghouse/Framatome/GEH prime the NSSS work, turbine OEMs own the steam path, S&L/Enercon/Bechtel own BOP. Software margins collapse when you sell engineering hours; the EPU wave (19 EPUs expected, ~6.6 GWt) is real but is a ~5-yr window and the buyers are conservative.
8. **Founder-market fit is required.** Selling to plant engineering requires credibility (ex-thermal performance engineers, ASME PTC 6/46 knowledge, PEPSE fluency). Without it, sales cycles are 12-24 months.

**Counter-arguments / where the opening is [est]**: (a) MWh value roughly tripled vs the $25/MWh that vendors still quote (now $51 energy + $14 capacity in PJM, $70-115 PPAs), so marginal MW and uprate margin are worth chasing again; (b) UFMs age and fail, and when they do plants derate to pre-MUR power, so DVR as a backup has real value (Salem is doing exactly this); (c) the uprate wave (EO 14302 5 GW by 2030; 31 expected applications, 7,336 MWt; hyperscalers funding Calvert Cliffs, Vogtle, Clinton, Vistra 433 MW) creates demand for a reconciled as-operated plant model to find true BOP margins quickly; (d) incumbents are small and services-heavy (GSE is a micro-cap; BTB is a German Mittelstand firm), so a software-first entrant could win on speed.
