# R9: Big-ceiling energy startup opportunities (research date 2026-10-03)

Filter order: (1) ceiling >= $1B revenue path, discard if buyer pool caps < ~$300M/yr; (2) openness vs funded players; (3) provable in 8 weeks.
Exclusions: prospecting/mining/drilling/geothermal drilling; generic AI-for-grid.

## Candidate checked and REJECTED as not open: Nuclear uprate developer/financier
- Idea: finance + execute extended power uprates at existing reactors, sell incremental firm MWh/ZECs to hyperscalers.
- Size: big. NRC expects 31 uprate applications through 2032, ~2,421 MWe (https://www.nrc.gov/reactors/operating/licensing/power-uprates/status-power-apps/expected-applications); DOE UPRISE targets 5 GW by 2029 via uprates/restarts (POWER 2026-03-12: https://www.powermag.com/doe-unveils-initiative-to-add-5-gw-of-nuclear-capacity-through-uprates-and-restarts/). Google + Georgia Power NU-1 tariff for ~96 MW of uprates, 2026-09-22 (https://www.world-nuclear-news.org/articles/google-agreement-to-support-Georgia-plant-uprates).
- Why rejected: exactly this model is Alva Energy ($33M Series A, Playground Global, 2026-02-12, targets 10 GWe, 200-300 MWe per reactor via second turbine-generator + steam generator replacement, financed with hyperscalers): https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260212304625/en/Alva-Energy-Launches-with-$33-Million-to-Unlock-10-GWe-of-Nuclear-Power . Plus utilities are doing direct deals (Google/Georgia Power) with no intermediary. Not "missed".
- Adjacent gap that may remain open: the uprate bottleneck list (POWER) is "engineering bandwidth, long-lead equipment (turbines, condensate pumps, main generators, transformers), staffing". See candidate on power-plant equipment recovery below.

## CANDIDATE 1: Recertified power-equipment bank — harvest, remanufacture, warranty and lease long-lead HV gear (GSUs 50-150 MVA, substation transformers 5-100 MVA, HV breakers, MV switchgear, generators) from retiring plants and cancelled projects; plus a spares pool for non-utility large loads
- One line: "Back Market + AerCap for long-lead grid hardware": buy units out of retiring coal/gas/peaker plants, cancelled data-centre/IPP projects and utility change-outs; test, rebuild to IEEE/ANSI, warranty; sell or lease to data-centre developers, IPPs, uprate/restart projects; run a subscription spares pool for the buyers Grid Assurance does not serve.
- Who pays / how many: data-centre developers and hyperscalers building their own HV substations (ERCOT large-load queue alone 226 GW, 77% data centres, Nov 2025 — Latitude Media https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/ercots-large-load-queue-has-nearly-quadrupled-in-a-single-year/ ; reported 410 GW by Apr 2026 — RTO Insider https://www.rtoinsider.com/129421-ercot-large-load-requests-soar-again/), IPPs/gas plant builders (100.3 GW of turbines ordered globally in 2025, Utility Dive 2026-03-23 https://www.utilitydive.com/news/5-year-waits-and-rising-costs-how-demand-is-redefining-the-gas-turbine-mar/813385/ — every one needs a GSU), BESS/solar developers, industrial loads, nuclear uprate projects (Alva's second turbine-generator model needs a second GSU each). Thousands of buyers.
- Market size: US transformer market $7.90B (2025) -> $12.09B (2031), power transformers 56.7% share (Mordor Intelligence, https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-transformer-market). Add MV switchgear/breakers and generators (not sized here). Supply: ~11 GW of US retirements planned in 2026 (6.4 GW coal, 4.6 GW gas steam/CT) — EIA 2026-02-23 https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=67206 — each retiring unit carries a GSU, station-service transformers, breakers and a generator.
- Why big: lead times are 2-3 years (GSU 50-150 MVA 100-150+ weeks; 25-50 MVA substation 85-110 weeks; 38 kV switchgear 78-104 weeks — Terrapin, 2026-06-10 https://terrapincg.com/news/switchgear-transformer-generator-lead-times-2026); 30% power-transformer supply deficit and +77% price since 2019 (WoodMac). A unit delivered in weeks is worth a large premium over list because it pulls a $1B+ data centre or a $300M/GW gas plant forward by a year (GE Vernova cites ~$300M revenue per GW served for DC). Path to $1B: ~8-10% of a ~$10B US transformer+switchgear market at premium pricing, or a leasing book (aircraft-lessor economics) of a few $B of equipment. Generators from retiring units can also be sold as synchronous-condenser conversions (global sync-condenser market $1.6B 2025 -> $3.2B 2035, GMI https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/synchronous-condenser-market) — a second product line using the same harvested asset.
- Incumbents / funded: Maddox Industrial Transformer (reman + build-to-stock; claims 1-4 week lead time vs 60-80 weeks and 10-40% cheaper, but focus is pad-mount/distribution, e.g. 1-2 MVA examples — POWER partner content https://www.powermag.com/partner-content/beating-the-transformer-bottleneck-remanufacturing-build-to-stock-and-smart-procurement/ ; private, 400+ staff, revenue approx $54M per Kona Equity estimate https://www.konaequity.com/company/maddox-industrial-transformer-llc-4393770959/ — approximate/unverified). OEM service shops (Hitachi Energy, Siemens Energy, SPX Transformer Solutions) repair customers' own units, they do not run a recertified inventory. Grid Assurance pools EHV transformers and breakers for UTILITIES ONLY (https://gridassurance.com/grid-assurance-ceo-dave-rupert-responds-to-transformer-shortage-article/, 2025-03-26). MegaGrid Supply (dealer/slot reservation marketing, funding unknown, https://megagridsupply.com/about) and SecondWatt (procurement intelligence + RFQs; explicitly says "cancelled projects, repowering programs, and site consolidations release turbines, transformers, switchgear lineups... continuously" and that it is under-used — https://secondwatt.com/resources/ge-vernova-backlog-176b-turbine-slot-buyer-guide). New-build startups are funded but solve a different problem: Heron Power $140M (SST, 2026-02-18, TechCrunch), Ayr Energy $25M (Indian-partner iron-core build, $500M order book, TechCrunch 2026-04-15 https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/this-energy-startups-bet-on-100-year-old-grid-tech-is-paying-off/), Hyperscale Power, DG Matrix, Amperesand.
- Why still open: VC money has gone to NEW transformer designs (SST, new factories) which add capacity in 2028+; reman is seen as an unglamorous services business and the only scaled reman player sits at the distribution end. Nobody is systematically mining EIA-860 retirements + cancelled interconnection-queue projects for the 25-150 MVA class, which is where data centres and gas plants are stuck. Retirement-equipment value is still priced at scrap-plus by plant owners' decommissioning contractors.
- 8-week proof: (1) build a database joining EIA-860 retiring/retired generators (nameplate -> GSU size), interconnection-queue withdrawals and utility IRP retirements; estimate MVA of harvestable GSUs/breakers in 2026-28; (2) call 15 plant owners/decom contractors to get 3 units under option; (3) get 3-5 data-centre/IPP buyers to sign LOIs at a stated price/lead time; (4) line up one OEM-independent test/rebuild shop. Proof = signed LOIs on optioned units with a spread.
- Biggest risk: condition and liability. Old GSUs have unknown insulation life (DGA, furan, PD history), buyers' lenders/insurers may refuse used gear on a $1B campus, and DOE 202(c) emergency orders are delaying retirements (only 4.6 GW of 12.3 GW planned actually retired in 2025, EIA), shrinking supply. Also impedance/voltage mismatch limits fungibility of large units.

## CANDIDATE 2: "Power-build collateral desk" — surety-backed credit for the collateral the AI power build-out now demands (large-load tariff security, turbine slot deposits, interconnection deposits), with a secondary book for transferring slots/positions
- One line: replace the cash and bank LCs that non-investment-grade data-centre developers, neoclouds and IPPs must post to utilities and OEMs with surety-backed LCs / credit wraps, priced off proprietary project-viability data; then use that position to broker transfers of stranded turbine slots and interconnection positions when projects slip.
- Who pays / how many: (a) large-load customers under new tariffs — collateral provisions in 43 of 55 utilities' large-load tariffs (78%); Dominion GS-5 requires $1.5M per MW of contracted capacity; AEP Ohio 50% of total minimum charges over the term; FPL the full incremental generation charge over the term; reduced collateral only for A-/A3 credits (LBNL technical brief, Aug 2026: https://eta-publications.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2026-08/rate_designs_large_loads_2026.08.10.pdf). A 300 MW Dominion data centre = $450M of collateral (Latitude Media 2026-09-02: https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/how-much-will-utilities-actually-earn-from-large-load-tariffs/). (b) turbine buyers — GE requires ~20% deposit to label a slot a reservation, non-refundable, price not yet set; ~$7.6B implied across GE's 63 GW reservation book; LG&E/KU paid $25M to hold one slot (Business Model Analyst, https://businessmodelanalyst.com/gas-turbine-oligopoly-pricing-power/). (c) interconnection deposits (Texas PUCT large-load rule; NRG asks for >=$10,000/MW and that surety be accepted: https://www.nrg.com/assets/documents/energy-policy/puct-58481-large-load-interconnection-standards-nrg-comments-101025.pdf). Buyers: hundreds of non-IG developers (neoclouds, colo developers, crypto-to-AI converts, IPPs).
- Market size (derived, approximate): even 20 GW of non-IG large load at an average $0.5-1.5M/MW = $10-30B of required collateral; at 1.5-3%/yr credit-wrap fee = $150-900M/yr in fees, before turbine deposits ($7.6B at GE alone) and interconnection security. Derived from the sourced per-MW figures above; the 20 GW and fee rates are my assumptions.
- Why big: collateral scales with GW, not with headcount; every non-IG GW that wants to connect needs it; Dominion-style per-MW security is the template other utilities are copying. A specialty insurer/MGA with a proprietary loss model is a multi-billion company if losses stay low.
- Incumbents / funded: Segue Sustainable Infrastructure already does this for RENEWABLE interconnection deposits (surety-backed LCs, ~200 projects, ~9 GW; https://segueinfra.com/articles/deposits-deposits-deposits, 2025-01-10). Big sureties/brokers (WTW, Aon) focus on construction performance bonds, not tariff collateral (WTW https://www.wtwco.com/en-us/insights/2025/06/surety-bonds-for-data-center-development ; Janus Re piece does not cover tariff collateral). Procurement intelligence players (SecondWatt, MegaGrid Supply) do not take credit risk. No venture-backed player found targeting large-load tariff collateral specifically.
- Why still open: large-load tariffs with per-MW collateral are new (2025-26), so the instrument does not exist yet; utilities default to LC/cash/parental guarantee and have not been asked to accept surety; non-IG developers are currently tying up equity in cash collateral. Requires cross-domain knowledge (utility tariffs + surety + power project risk) that neither insurers nor energy startups hold alone.
- 8-week proof: (1) tabulate the 43 collateral tariffs (amount, form accepted, step-downs); (2) interview 10 non-IG developers to size collateral posted/needed and their cost of LC; (3) get one surety carrier or fronting reinsurer to issue a term sheet for an SBLC programme; (4) get one utility (or the Texas PUCT docket) to confirm surety acceptability — a filed comment in an open tariff docket is a cheap, public proof step.
- Biggest risk: correlated default. If the AI capex cycle turns, many neocloud developers walk at once (exactly when utilities draw the collateral) — this is a concentrated, cyclical book, and carriers will demand reinsurance. Second risk: utilities/commissions refuse surety in lieu of LC (form-of-security fights); large banks can enter with LC capacity.

## CANDIDATE 3: Large-load grid-compliance product (NERC Level 3): validated EMT/RMS models, ride-through settings audit, commissioning tests and post-event forensics for computational loads
- One line: "UL for data-centre grid behaviour": a standard package that turns a data centre's UPS/VFD/IT-load configuration into TP-accepted PSCAD/PSS/E models, verifies ride-through at commissioning, and reads the TO's new dynamic fault recorders after events.
- Who pays / how many: data-centre developers in queues (ERCOT 226 GW Nov 2025, 77% DCs; Latitude Media) and transmission planners/owners who must now publish modelling-data lists, study stability annually and install dynamic fault recorders (NERC Level 3, 2026-05-04, Utility Dive https://www.utilitydive.com/news/data-center-load-disruptions-nerc-alert-recommendations/818036/).
- Market size: no sourced figure. Approximate: a few thousand large-load projects x $0.2-1M of study/commissioning work = low hundreds of $M/yr. Likely CAPS BELOW the ~$300M/yr filter as a standalone services/software business.
- Incumbents: ERCOT + Texas A&M published an OPEN-SOURCE PSCAD AI-data-centre model (2026-04-24, https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/04/24/AIDataCenter_PSCAD-Manual.pdf); consultancies (Keentel, Electranix, EPC engineering firms); OEMs (Schneider, Eaton, Vertiv) ship models for their own gear; ON.energy etc. sell the hardware fix.
- Why open: nobody yet sells a productised, OEM-neutral model+commissioning certificate; but the free ERCOT model and consultants compress price.
- 8-week proof: take the open ERCOT model, parameterise it for 3 real facility designs, and get one TP to accept the output in a study.
- Biggest risk / verdict: size. It is a good wedge into the developer, but the ceiling is the problem; only reaches $1B if it becomes the gate to hardware (crowded) or to the collateral/equipment businesses above. Kept for ranking but scored low on ceiling.

## CANDIDATE 4: Private-grid spares pool + parametric outage cover for non-utility HV assets (data-centre campuses, IPP/BTM plants, large industrials)
- One line: Grid Assurance for everyone who is not a utility: a subscription pool of pre-positioned GSUs/HV transformers/breakers in standard ratings plus parametric cover that pays per day of transformer-caused outage.
- Who pays / how many: every data-centre campus or BTM plant that owns its HV substation (thousands of sites as US DC demand goes 31 GW 2025 -> 66 GW 2027, Utility Dive 2026-06-26 https://www.utilitydive.com/news/data-centers-flexibility-utilities-speed-to-power/822588/), plus thousands of IPP plants with GSUs.
- Market size: not directly sourced. The economic hook is sourced: GSU replacement lead time 100-150+ weeks (Terrapin 2026-06-10), and Grid Assurance's pool is restricted to utilities (https://gridassurance.com/grid-assurance-ceo-dave-rupert-responds-to-transformer-shortage-article/). Approximate: if a 300 MW campus earns its operator ~$300M+/yr (Latitude: utility bill alone $145-300M/yr for 300 MW), a 2-year outage is a $0.5B+ loss; a 1% annual premium on even $50B of insured exposure is $500M/yr (my assumption).
- Incumbents: Grid Assurance (utilities only); OEM spares programmes (per-customer); insurers write property/BI but cannot shorten a 2-3 year replacement. CNBC reports data centres as a coming cat-bond market (2026-09-12, page 403, unverified detail).
- Why open: the pool needs physical inventory + engineering standardisation + insurance wrapper; utilities solved it for themselves in 2016, private HV owners are new at this scale.
- 8-week proof: survey 10 DC/IPP owners on their HV single-points-of-failure and spare policy; standardise 3 "pool ratings" that cover most of them; get a carrier MGA term sheet; secure 1-2 units (links to Candidate 1).
- Biggest risk: heterogeneity (voltages, impedances, footprints) kills fungibility, and big hyperscalers self-insure and buy their own spares. Best run as a product line of Candidate 1 rather than alone.

## CANDIDATE 5 (nuclear-touching): Nuclear-qualified downstream manufacturing cell (machining, welding, NDE, QA records under NQA-1/ASME III) for SMR/advanced-reactor vendors and fleet uprates
- One line: a software-first, automated job shop that holds NQA-1/N-stamp and sells qualified machined/welded/inspected parts on short lead times.
- Who pays: ~10-20 advanced reactor developers, 3-4 LWR OEMs, the US operating fleet (uprates, restarts). Small buyer count; spend tied to SMR orders.
- Market size: not sourced as a $ figure. Sourced bottleneck: the 2026 US advanced-nuclear supply-chain landscape says "the most acute manufacturing bottlenecks increasingly lie downstream... Machining, welding, finishing, inspection, and NDE frequently sit on the critical path," with a "market paralysis" where suppliers won't invest without firm orders (Nuclear Scaling Initiative, Mar 2026: https://www.nuclearscaling.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2026-Landscape-of-U.S.-Domestic-Advanced-Nuclear-Energy-Supply-Chain.pdf).
- Incumbents / funded: BWXT, Curtiss-Wright, Holtec, ATB, many NQA-1 fab shops; Hadrian raised $1.37B at ~$8B (2026-08-06, TechCrunch https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/06/defense-tech-hadrian-raises-1-37b-at-8b-valuation/) and can add nuclear any time; Gecko Robotics ($1.25B valuation, 2025-06-12) does robotic inspection in power.
- Why open: VC-backed automated manufacturing has gone to defense; nobody has built the nuclear-QA-native version.
- 8-week proof: hard. NQA-1 programme and N-stamp take far longer than 8 weeks; best 8-week proof is signed purchase intent from 2-3 reactor developers for a defined part family.
- Biggest risk: demand timing. The order book depends on SMR FIDs that are stalled ("market paralysis"); ceiling only >$1B in the bull case for SMR deployment. Ranked low.
