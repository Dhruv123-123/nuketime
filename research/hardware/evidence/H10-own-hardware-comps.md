Research note: the session's web search and fetch budgets ran out partway through. Figures taken only from search-result headlines are marked (headline). Figures from memory are marked (unverified).

## Summary

- The winners own the asset. KoBold builds its own sensors (Hyperpod, Korecam360, MetaLab). It is pre-revenue and valued at about $2.96B. That value rests on Mingomba copper (80% of the non-state interest, about 300 kt/yr design) and not on the sensors.
- Sensor companies that sell surveys get valued like services businesses. Fleet Space raised $150M at an $800M+ valuation selling ExoSphere surveys to Rio and Barrick. It owns no ground, so its upside stops at survey fees.
- Survey contractors are the trap. CGG left seismic acquisition in 2020 and kept its multi-client data library plus Sercel (equipment). Xcalibur bought CGG Multiphysics in 2021 *excluding* the multi-client library. The library and the ground hold the value. The crews and aircraft do not.
- Data-as-a-product works only at scale. Planet earned $307.7M in FY26 at a 56% GAAP gross margin and was just EBITDA positive ($15.5M), about 10 years after launch. Spire sold its maritime unit for about $239M net and is guiding to only $75-85M of revenue.
- In geothermal, the capitalised buyers already build hardware for *drilling and production* (Fervo fiber/DAS, Quaise gyrotrons, Eavor loops, Ormat turbines). Nobody owns a proprietary *exploration sensor network* plus the rights. Zanskar comes closest, using public data and its own wells. That is the open slot.

**(b) Geothermal: who sells sensors, and who builds hardware**

- Exploration sensor vendors are mature and sell to anyone. MT systems come from Phoenix Geophysics (MTU-5C), Quantec, Zonge and Metronix. DAS fiber comes from Silixa and Luna. Nodal seismic comes from STRYDE and SmartSolo. Satellite-linked ambient noise comes from Fleet Space (all unverified as current product lists). Vendor sensor data belongs to the client, so no vendor builds a data network that compounds.
- Fervo: IPO in May 2026 priced at $27, raised about $1.89B, and debuted valued at about $10B (headline: [Axios](https://www.axios.com/pro/climate-deals/2026/05/13/fervo-prices-27-upsized), [S-1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1853868/000162828026025821/fervoenergy-sx1.htm)). Its own hardware is in-well fiber/DAS for frac and flow monitoring and a tuned drilling program (unverified detail). It builds nothing for *exploration*. It is a proven buyer of sites.
- Zanskar: about $180M raised, including a $115M Series C (Spring Lane) and a $40M development-capital facility ([Latitude](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/armed-with-115-million-geothermal-startup-zanskar-gets-ready-to-build/), [ThinkGeoEnergy](https://www.thinkgeoenergy.com/zanskar-closes-40m-in-development-capital-financing-to-support-greenfield-geothermal-sites/)). It controls 700+ MW of potential sites, plans six 20 MW plants, found Big Blind (about 250 F at about 2,700 ft), and tripled output at Lightning Dock. No proprietary sensor was found. Its edge is public data plus its own wells. **It is the most dangerous copier, and also a possible buyer.**
- Ormat: vertically integrated, making its own OEC turbines and running its own drilling (unverified). It self-sources resources.
- Quaise: $134M first close of its Series B (Jul 2026, headline: [EnergyGlobal](https://www.energyglobal.com/other-renewables/10072026/quaise-energy-raises-us134-million-in-first-close-of-series-b/)). Its hardware is a millimeter-wave gyrotron drill, used for access, not exploration.
- Mazama: raised $135M (Sep 2026, headline: [Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/geothermal/mazama-energy-raises-135m-oregon-geothermal)) for superhot EGS at Newberry.
- Eavor (own closed-loop downhole tools), Sage (pressure-geothermal storage) and XGS (conductive closed loop) all build *production* hardware (unverified).
- **Conclusion:** across more than $2.5B raised in geothermal in 2025-26, no one owns a proprietary *shallow thermal or geophysical sensing network* tied to a rights bank.

**(c) The services trap, in numbers**

- CGG wound down land and marine acquisition in 2020 to go "asset-light." It kept the multi-client library and Sercel equipment ([World Oil](https://www.worldoil.com/news/2020/2/18/cgg-officially-exits-the-seismic-acquisition-business)). TGS (the library owner) then absorbed PGS (the vessel owner) in 2024 ([MTN](https://www.marinetechnologynews.com/amp/news/norwegian-seismic-specialists-merge-631168)). The asset owner bought the operator, not the reverse.
- Dawson Geophysical, the last big US land seismic crew, has had revenue under about $100M a year with repeated net losses ([SEC 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/799165/000155837025004024/dwsn-20250328xex99d1.htm); exact numbers unverified).
- Contractor margins sit at single-digit EBITDA through cycles. A multi-client library earns 40-60%+ cash margins on resale (unverified, industry rule of thumb).

## Hardware design sketch (lesson applied)

Build the Earth AI move for geothermal. Use our own cheap shallow probes and a light rig for 30-150 m temperature-gradient holes, plus passive nodes (ambient noise and MT) built from commodity parts. The probe and rig matter only because they cut the cost of the proving step. Target under $15k per TG hole versus an assumed $50-150k for a contractor (unverified). BOM for a prototype probe string plus logger is about $5-20k. A rented or used light rig costs about $150-400k (unverified). Prototype in 3-6 months.

## Data it unlocks, and why incumbents lack it

Each hole we drill on ground we already hold produces gradient and heat-flow data that is ours, sits under our rights, and is never filed until the law requires it. Vendors give their data to clients. Fervo, Ormat and Zanskar drill only where they already plan to build. Nobody runs dense, cheap gradient drilling across rights they do not intend to develop.

## How it compounds into owned assets

The sequence is: model ranks parcels, then we file or lease cheaply ($1-10/ac), then cheap holes give a gradient, then we sell confirmed prospects to Fervo (about $10B market cap and $1.89B of fresh cash), Ormat or Zanskar. Every hole retrains the model, and only we hold the labels. KoBold shows that one hit carries the whole company.

## Risks and kill conditions

- We become Fleet Space: selling surveys because owning ground is slow. Kill condition: if more than 50% of revenue is fees by year 2.
- Shallow gradients mislead in hydrothermal outflow plumes. Kill condition: if hole-to-deep-well correlation is below about 0.5 on public pairs.
- Buyers self-source. Zanskar already holds 700+ MW. Kill condition: if no buyer will sign a right of first offer (ROFO) or a $/MW term sheet.
- Hardware can be copied. Rigs and probes are commodity, so the moat has to be the labels plus the rights.

## Cheapest first test

Spend about $25k over 30 days. Have a contractor drill, or log by renting a probe, 3 or 4 existing open holes or new 50 m holes on parcels the model scores high and low. Pass if the gradient ranks match the model at Spearman 0.6 or higher, and one buyer gives a written $/MW indication.
