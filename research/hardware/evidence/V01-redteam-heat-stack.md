# V01: Red team of "HEAT TRUTH STACK"

Method: 13 searches and fetches. The fetch budget ran out, so some points are (unverified).

## Verdict

The stack really does improve the plain bank in one way: it creates labels nobody else has, including cold holes. But the headline economics ($7.5M to $0.6M per discovery against $3M of value) compare the wrong things on both sides. The regulatory facts mostly check out. Revised score: **18/25** (c1 4, c2 4, c3 4, c4 3, c5 3). That is below the 20/25 earlier given to the plain bank, because the hardware makes the company heavier without raising the price a buyer pays or the ceiling.

## (b) Strongest reasons it fails

**1. The economics compare the wrong numbers (strongest).**
- **The cost side.** The $7.5M "conventional" cost per discovery assumes $0.5M holes. Our own memo says DOE play fairway work got 2 of 2 blind hits for $1.5M of shallow drilling (MEMO s5, L01). That is about $0.75M per discovery with contract rigs today.
- **The cost gain.** Contract mineral-exploration coring in Nevada runs about $150 to $300/m all-in (unverified). So the honest gain from owning a rig is about 2 to 5x, not 12x.
- **The value side.** The $3M is the value of a *confirmed* 30 MW resource, at $0.1M/MW. Confirmation needs a deeper well and a flow test. That costs millions and sits outside our 150 to 300 m holes. A shallow gradient anomaly is a land-stage asset, worth the land step-up. It is not worth $0.1M/MW.
- **The result.** The 5x margin vanishes unless we also fund confirmation. That pushes the bank into Zanskar's developer shape, the first kill in MEMO s8.
- **Fixable?** Partly. Restate the math with contract slim holes as the baseline, and price at the anomaly stage. Then sell many anomaly packages under an override.

**2. Depth blindness at both screening tiers.**
- **2 m probes.** They only see systems whose heat reaches the near surface. Range-front water tables are often 50 to 150 m deep, and cold lateral groundwater sweeps the heat away (unverified). Systems like that read blank at 2 m. The blank-valley rate in Boom and Bust shows recall is low.
- **150 to 300 m holes.** Our own memo says to budget 500 to 900 m holes, because Argenta Rise missed at 244 m (MEMO s5, L04). NBMG then permitted further Argenta wells (https://minerals.nv.gov/Programs/Geo/GeoPermits/Geo_1623_10-21-2024_NBMG_Argenta_Rise_Lander_34-41).
- **The result.** A light rig built for 300 m is designed for exactly the depth our own evidence calls too shallow.
- **Fixable?** Partly. First measure recall: carpet 5 known producing fields and check that at least half show a 2 m anomaly. Then size the rig for 500 m. That raises cost per hole, probably to $60 to 120k (unverified).

**3. Copying and buyers who re-drill.**
- **Zanskar already has this shape.** It closed a $115M Series C in January 2026 (https://www.utahbusiness.com/press-releases/2026/01/23/zanskar-raises-115-million-series-c-following-year-geothermal-discoveries/). It is hiring a Head of Drilling and drilling engineers (https://jobs.lever.co/Zanskar/a534611c-8cc2-4ae5-b381-330b02d49d3d). Its private label set from its own wells has a years-long head start. Probes, telluric loggers and slim rigs are all public methods.
- **Buyers re-drill.** Ormat and Fervo will drill their own confirmation wells before committing capital, whatever our holes show. So "cheap proof" lowers our cost but barely raises their price.
- **Selling leaks the label set.** A neutral seller must open data rooms to several buyers.
- **Fixable?** Only in part, and not by hardware. The defenses are speed, rights locked first, and staying on private and state land, where Zanskar's federal-lease habits matter less.

**4. Secrecy, hard rock and ceiling (secondary).**
- **Secrecy.** Probes stay private, holes do not. Every BLM hole needs an NOI and a CX decision on the NEPA register. Every Nevada gradient well gets a public NDOM permit (H13). Once we hold the lease, 3253.10(a) makes us file the data.
- **Hard rock.** Borobotics is a soft-ground robot and Earth AI's $100/m is a target. Silicified volcanics, lost circulation and shallow artesian hot water push holes toward H11's own $100k kill line. The prototype rig costs $1.1 to 1.5M before the first hole.
- **Ceiling.** The 51,200-acre federal cap still limits hydrothermal to about $0.3 to 1B (MEMO s6). The EGS tier is unproven, since a small seismic patch over 300 m holes says little about stress at 3 to 5 km (unverified). EGS land is already auctioned at $111/acre average and $1,207 top (H12).
