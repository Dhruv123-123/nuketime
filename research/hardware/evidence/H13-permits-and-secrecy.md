# H13: Permits, access and data secrecy for our own sensors

Scope: deploying our own shallow sensors at scale on western US land (NV, UT, NM, ID, OR, plus CA notes). Research was cut short when the shared web search and fetch budgets ran out. Facts I could check in a primary source are cited. The rest is marked (unverified) and needs a lawyer or agency call before we rely on it.

## Summary

- **Shallow sensors on BLM land need no permit and no notice.** BLM IM 2026-024 (effective Aug 31, 2026) lists as casual use: hand soil and gas sampling, direct-push probes under 2 in diameter, battery sensors placed in small holes, and near-surface installs under 10 ft deep. No notice is needed. https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2026-024
- **Data from unleased federal land does not have to be filed.** 43 CFR 3253.10(b): on unleased land, or a lease you do not hold, "you are not required to submit data". You only file if you want the work to count toward diligence spending on a lease you hold. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/43/3253.10
- **The leaks are the drill permits, the NOIs and the nominations, not the sensors.** Nevada's Division of Minerals posts every geothermal permit online with operator name, project area and date (for example "Geo 1751, 7-17-2026, Ignis H2 Energy, Esmeralda, Weepah Hills"). BLM tracks every NOI on an internal dashboard and must publish each decision that relies on the new GEO categorical exclusion. Lease nominations name the parcels.
- **When we do need to drill gradient holes, federal permitting is now fast.** A BLM NOI gets a response within 15 days (plus up to 10 more). The GEO CE (Apr 28, 2026) covers exploration with 10 acres or less of disturbance, which makes NEPA review short.
- **Stealthiest legal path:** use casual-use sensors (under 10 ft, no drill rig) on unleased BLM land and on private ranches under NDA. File nothing. Take the cheap right first (private lease, state first-file, BLM nomination spread across decoy parcels). Drill permitted gradient holes only on land we already control.

## Key facts with numbers

**BLM casual use (IM 2026-024, Aug 31, 2026 to Sep 30, 2029)** https://www.blm.gov/policy/im-2026-024
- Casual use covers: field mapping; hand sampling of rock, soil, spring water or gas; ground geophysics (gravity, magnetics, EM, seismic, MT) and near-surface installs; drone and airborne surveys; sampling existing wells without changing them; small portable direct-push probes; battery sensors placed in small holes (geophones, MT).
- Limits: installs under 10 ft deep, push rods under 2 in diameter. No earth-moving equipment, no truck-mounted or mechanized drill rig, no heavy equipment such as vibroseis.
- Vehicles: existing roads and trails, or off-road where the travel plan allows.
- "Casual use activities require no notice or application." If you file an NOI just to be safe, BLM must answer within 15 calendar days. If it confirms casual use, you can start the same day.
- Exception: a Field Office can still pull an activity out of casual use if it expects significant disturbance. Sage-grouse habitat, wilderness study areas and cultural sites are where this is most likely (unverified).

**BLM notice level (NOI, Form 3200-9, 43 CFR 3250)** https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-43/subtitle-B/chapter-II/subchapter-C/part-3200/subpart-3250
- An NOI is needed for anything beyond casual use, for example gradient holes drilled with a rig. You can file it on unleased land.
- Response comes in 15 + 10 days under the IM. The GEO CE took effect Apr 28, 2026. It applies when total disturbance is 10 acres or less, there is no flow testing, and the site is reclaimed. BLM must "publish documentation" of each CE it uses on its NEPA register. https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-08235.pdf
- Bond: a $5,000 statewide or $25,000 nationwide geothermal exploration bond is typical (unverified, check 43 CFR 3251.15).
- Cost: about $10-30k per NOI for consultant surveys (cultural and biological clearances), plus bond (unverified).

**Data filing and disclosure (federal)**
- 3253.10(a): on your own lease, you must submit "all data you obtain" with the notice of completion. 3253.10(b): on unleased land, no submission is needed. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/43/3253.10
- 3266.10: data you submit falls under the FOIA rules in 43 CFR Part 2. BLM "will not treat surface location, surface elevation, or well status information as confidential". So the location of any hole on a lease becomes public, even if its temperatures stay protected. https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/43/3266.10

**State drilling permits and confidentiality**
- Nevada: drilling a geothermal well needs a Division of Minerals permit (NRS 534A, NAC 534A). Thermal gradient wells and "project area" permits are posted publicly. Examples: Ormat New York Canyon (Mar 2025), Ormat Diamond Flat (Mar 2026), Raser Desert Queen (Apr 2026), NBMG Argenta Rise (Oct 2024). https://minerals.nv.gov/Programs/Geo/GeoPermits/Geo_1582_04-12-2024_Ormat_Lone_Mountain_Project_Area and https://www.leg.state.nv.us/nrs/NRS-534A.html
  - Logs and records stay confidential for about 5 years, then become public (NRS 534A.031). The brief gives 5 years. I could not fetch the exact wording (unverified).
  - The permit itself (operator, location, date) is public from day one. Only the data is confidential.
- Oregon: DOGAMI permits geothermal and prospect wells under OAR 632-020. Logs are released through DOGAMI's geothermal logs page after a confidential period (length unverified, possibly 2-4 years). https://oregon.public.law/rules/oar_632-020-0060 and https://www.oregon.gov/dogami/mlrr/Pages/geothermal-logs.aspx
- Utah: the Division of Water Rights permits geothermal wells. Gradient holes under about 100 ft may be exempt (unverified). Confidentiality period unverified.
- Idaho (IDWR) and New Mexico (EMNRD): both require geothermal well permits. Confidentiality periods are unverified.
- California (CalGEM): exploratory well records are confidential (period unverified).
- A casual-use probe under 10 ft is very unlikely to count as a "well" under any state statute (unverified). That is the key secrecy point: no state filing means no public record and no confidentiality clock.

**State trust land access**
- Utah SITLA: a Right of Entry (ROE) permit is needed for any survey work. It has an application fee and a per-day or per-site fee, and the handbook is public. Terms expect the data to be shared with the trust or kept confidential by agreement (unverified). https://TrustLANDS.utah.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ROE-Handbook-FORMATTED.pdf
- NV: little state trust land is left (about 3,000 ac), so it rarely matters (unverified).
- NM State Land Office, Idaho IDL, Oregon DSL: each issues a right-of-entry or temporary use permit, typically $100-$500 plus a per-acre or per-day fee, in 2-8 weeks (all unverified). Each application names the parcel, which is a leak inside a small office.

**Private land**
- Access needs only a signed access agreement. There is no public filing. Typical cost is $0-$2,000 per ranch, or folded into an option on the geothermal rights (unverified). Combining access, NDA and a geothermal lease option in one document is the stealthiest route.

## How each step leaks our interest

| Step | Public? | Who can watch | Lag |
|---|---|---|---|
| Casual-use sensor on BLM | No filing | Rancher or field staff who see the gear | None |
| NOI filed | Internal BLM dashboard; FOIA-able; CE decision posted on the NEPA register | Anyone checking ePlanning, FOIA requesters | Weeks |
| NV gradient well permit | Posted online with operator name | Ormat, Zanskar, Fervo staff who check NDOM | Days |
| BLM lease nomination | Parcels listed in the sale notice | Everyone | 90+ days before sale |
| State first-file | Public lease record | Anyone | Immediate |
| Lease data submission | Locations public; data under FOIA rules | FOIA requesters | Years |

Watching NOIs and NDOM permits is standard practice for competitors. That is how operators followed the Zanskar and Fervo plays (unverified). So our sensors themselves are nearly invisible, and our first public filing is the moment we tip our hand.

## Recommended stealth path, with time and cost

1. **Private ranch land, about 2 weeks, $0-2k per ranch.** Sign access, NDA and a geothermal lease option together. Deploy probes, soil gas sensors and nodes. Nothing is filed.
2. **Unleased BLM land, casual use, 0 days, $0.** Use 2 m probe nodes and soil CO2 chambers. Install under 10 ft with hand tools or a portable push rig, and use existing roads. Do not file an NOI. Keep a memo that maps our methods to the IM 2026-024 list. Optional: file one NOI on a dull decoy area to get a written casual-use ruling for our method within 15 days. This leaks only one location.
3. **State trust land, 2-8 weeks, $100s-$1k+.** Avoid it unless the anomaly sits there. If it does, ask for confidentiality in the ROE terms.
4. **Take the right before drilling.** Use a private lease, a state first-file (about $1/ac/yr), or a BLM nomination padded with decoy parcels. Nominate in counties or blocks wide enough to hide the target.
5. **Drill gradient holes only on rights we hold.** Under the BLM NOI and GEO CE this takes 3-8 weeks and about $10-30k plus bond. A Nevada NDOM permit takes weeks (unverified). The permit is public, but the data stays confidential for about 5 years in NV. That is long enough to get confirmation financed and sold.

## Risks and kill conditions

- A Field Office reads "significant disturbance" broadly, or a new IM replaces 2026-024 (it expires Sep 2029).
- A state counts any probe hole as a "well" and requires a public permit. Check NV, UT and OR rules first.
- Sensors left out for months may become "occupancy", which needs a permit (unverified). Plan deployments of 2-8 weeks.
- Theft, or ranchers talking about the gear. Use low-profile buried gear and keep an NDA with every landowner.
- Kill condition: legal review finds that unattended sensors over 30 days, or any hole, need an NOI in our 3 core states.

## Cheapest first test

Cost: about $5-10k for a land-use attorney memo plus one decoy NOI. Time: 30 days. Pass if (a) BLM gives a written casual-use ruling for 2 m temperature probes plus soil gas left for 30 days, and (b) NV, UT and OR confirm in writing that holes under 10 ft are not "wells". Fail if any of them requires a public permit.
