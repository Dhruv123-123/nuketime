# Pair sweep: drop L2 (outcome in a legible field) AND R-ML (ML must be what makes the pain solvable)

Written 2026-10-19. Every other law and standing rule is kept: L1, L3, L4, L5, L6, no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, and a credible path to $1B+.

## Verdict

**Nothing survives as a venture-scale company Dhruv could own.** Dropping both laws turns every candidate into the same company: a capital-and-speed developer that acts on a public list, with ML as a ranking and back-office tool. The best idea scores 14/25 before the checks and 11 after. It is a "permit-stack" site assembler for behind-the-meter data-centre power, combining land, gas tap, air permit, offsets and turbines. Three web checks show what kills it and its neighbours. **L3 still binds even for distressed sellers:** Plug Power, cash-strapped, sold a 164 MW powered hydrogen site straight to Stream Data Centers at $305-466k/MW, against a primary-market average of $584k/MW. **L6 binds:** Joule, TerraVolt, VoltaGrid, Crusoe, Cloverleaf, Tract and FTAI already hold every rung. **The proof is a bespoke build (L1/C1):** air permits, pipelines and turbines are the bottleneck, and only 2.2% of 90 GW of announced behind-the-meter capacity is running. A new pattern also emerges, proposed as **L8: when the signal is legible, the capitalised end buyer buys directly from the seller**, so a discovery middle rung never forms (Plug→Stream, Century→TeraWulf, Auction 108→T-Mobile).

## How the rubric is rescored with both laws dropped

- **C4 (data and right):** a legible signal costs nothing. The edge may be a cross-dataset join, speed, or a seller who is forced (distress, a regulatory deadline, fees) rather than ignorant. L3 is still charged in full: if the seller can read the field and the field prices the asset, C4 is at most 3.
- **C3 (lever):** there is no penalty when ML is only a ranker, join or speed tool. C3 now scores how much our *process* (ML plus deal ops) cuts the cost per proven asset compared with an ordinary developer. C3 stays capped by L1: if the proof usually succeeds or is a bespoke build anyone can do, the lever is 1-2x.
- **C5 (ladder):** L6 is kept, so a rung held by a funded or PE player costs 1-2 points.
- **C1 and C2:** unchanged.

## 3. Top 3 adversarial web checks

### 3.1 Permit-stack site assembler (N2) and stranded-load recycler (N1): die on L3 + L6 + L1/C1, and expose L8

**Market size is real**
- Data-centre land sales were about $3.3B in Q1 2026, up 141% year on year, and about $6B in H1 2026 ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-development/data-center-sites-are-warping-the-market-for-developable-land-134771); [DCK](https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/ai-data-center-land-buying-hits-6b-as-power-premiums-rise)).
- Cushman & Wakefield puts primary-market powered land at **$584k/MW** in 2026 YTD, up 51% year on year ([Finviz/C&W cost guide](https://finviz.com/news/388803/cushman-wakefield-releases-2026-data-center-development-cost-guide-citing-21-rise-in-per-mw-construction-costs)).

**The bottleneck is not discovery**
- Cleanview identifies **~90 GW of behind-the-meter projects, 59 projects in all**, 82 GW of it announced since 2025.
- Only **2.2% is operating** and 36% is permitted.
- The binding constraints are air permits (Nebius, a 400 MW plant in New Jersey), pipelines (New Mexico blocked Stargate's 2.45 GW fuel line) and turbines ([Cleanview](https://www.cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers)).
- These are bespoke, slow proofs (L1/C1), and pipelines and OEMs own them.

**The rung is held (L6)**
- Joule Capital: 4,000 acres and 4 GW in Utah, a Caterpillar supply deal, rezoning won ([DCF](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/site-selection/article/55313620/joule-capital-caterpillar-team-on-ai-hpc-in-utah)).
- TerraVolt/CalEthos packages behind-the-meter gas with pre-permitted land ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/terravolt-inks-natural-gas-supply-deal-for-onsite-power-plant-at-planned-data-center-campus-in-idaho/)).
- VoltaGrid, Crusoe, Cloverleaf and Tract are covered in S-L6 §3.2.

**Distressed sellers do not sell cheap (L3) and buyers go direct (L8)**
- Plug Power, in a liquidity push, sold its Graham, TX hydrogen site (66 acres, **164 MW of grid interconnection**) **directly to Stream Data Centers for $50M plus up to $26.5M** tied to confirmed load ([SEC 8-K ex99.1](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001093691/000110465926082854/tm2620282d1_ex99-1.htm); [DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/plug-power-sells-texas-site-to-stream-data-centers/)).
- That is **$305-466k/MW** for a non-primary Texas site, against $584k/MW for primary markets: a near-market price, with no 10x discount for a middleman to capture.

**Verdict:** N2 falls to **11** and N1 to **10**. This is a fundable developer business (capital, permits, OEM relationships), not a discovery rung.

### 3.2 Nonattainment offset bank (N3): dies on "too small"

- **San Joaquin Valley:** the 2026 cost log lists **8 trades in total**, NOx at **$9,000-20,000/ton** (three trades, about 49 tons/yr combined) and VOC at $4,200/ton ([SJVAPCD ERCCost2026](https://apps.valleyair.org/busind/pto/erc/ERCCost2026.pdf), pdftotext of the 19 Oct 2026 version).
- **Houston-Galveston-Brazoria:** TCEQ's discrete-credit trade report shows NOx at **$7,000-7,250/ton** in Aug 2025 and Jan 2026 ([TCEQ DERC trade report](https://www.tceq.texas.gov/assets/public/permitting/air/reports/banking/dectradereport.html)). HGB VOC has spiked in the past (from $5k to $300k/ton per AIChE), but that was a thin, episodic squeeze ([AIChE](https://www.aiche.org/conferences/videos/conference-presentations/emission-reduction-solutions-houston-ozone-nonattainment-permitting)).
- **Massachusetts:** about 582 tpy of NOx ERCs are on the registry, down from 889 ([MassDEP May 2026](https://www.mass.gov/doc/massdep-emission-reduction-credit-erc-registry-may-22-2026/download)). Supply is tightening, but in tons, not dollars.
- **Why it cannot scale:** at about $10k/ton, offsets for a 400 MW plant with SCR are a rounding error on a $1B+ project. ERCs are not the bottleneck and the market is in the tens of millions of dollars. **8/25.**

### 3.3 Used turbine inventory (N4) and engines to ground power: dies on L3 + L6

- **New-unit prices:** Wood Mackenzie projects $600/kW by end-2027, up 195% since 2019. Orders reached about 100-110 GW against 60-70 GW/yr of capacity ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-01/gas-turbine-prices-surge-crimping-efforts-to-power-data-centers); [WoodMac](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/gas-turbine-prices-soar-195-as-market-faces-supply-demand-crisis/)). GE Vernova slot reservations rose from 43 to 56 GW ([Power Eng](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/turbines/data-centers-drive-record-surge-in-ge-vernova-power-equipment-orders-as-turbine-slots-tighten-through-2030/)).
- **Used units already ask near new prices (L3):** a used 100 MW Westinghouse W501D-5 is listed at **$39.36M, about $394/kW** ([USPE listing](https://uspeglobal.com/inventory/natural-gas-turbines/)). Sellers read the same headlines.
- **The rung is held (L6):** FTAI's JV J&F Power signed a **$1.465B** initial order with a cloud provider for 25 MW CFM56 "Mod-1" units, targeting 100 units in 2027 ([Pulse 2](https://pulse2.com/ftai-secures-1-465-billion-gas-turbine-generator-order-through-jf-power-systems/amp/); [Distribution Strategy](https://distributionstrategy.com/2026/01/ftai-aviation-moves-into-data-center-power-leveraging-engine-distribution-and-mro-platform/)). ProEnergy and the brokers are also present.
- **Verdict:** the step-up is about 1.5-2x and disappears when OEM capacity catches up around 2029-30. **9/25.**

## 5. Answers to the brief

1. **Does anything survive?** No. The best is 14 before the checks (powered land and its permit-stack combination) and 11 after.
2. **Which law now kills?** **L3 first, then L6.** With L2 and R-ML gone, the business that remains is "act faster on public data with more capital". The L6 sweep already showed that business is won by capital and relationships (Cloverleaf with $300M). The 2026 deals show it pays the seller close to market, so there is no discovery step-up to own.
3. **Most interesting finding:** dropping R-ML does not unlock anything new, because the step-up was never in the ML. It is in **who controls the physical bottleneck**: air permits, pipeline taps and turbine slots. Those are held by regulators, midstream companies and OEMs, none of whom are uninformed sellers. Distress does not produce cheap sellers either: Plug sold a 164 MW site at 52-80% of the primary-market $/MW, directly to the end buyer. That suggests a new law, **L8: a legible signal plus a capitalised end buyer means no middle discovery rung.**
4. **Suggested next pair:** L3 + L6. Allow an informed seller and an occupied rung, and test whether a capital-heavy powered-land or behind-the-meter developer is acceptable. That is a question of what Dhruv wants to build, not a rubric gap.
