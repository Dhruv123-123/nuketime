# First principles: relaxing the laws (19 Oct 2026)

**Verdict: nothing survives.** Dhruv asked for each law that killed the last ~570 ideas to be relaxed on its own, then in combinations, sweeping new ideas and re-sweeping old ones each time. That ran as 19 sweeps in four passes: 6 single laws, 7 pairs, 4 triples and 2 quads, plus one deep check. Together they generated about 350 new ideas and rescored about 300 old ones. The best idea after web checks scored 16/25. The highest score before checks was 20/25, for failed-drug rescue, which fell to 13 once checked. No pass produced a venture-scale company that Dhruv's team could own and that still looks like Terranox.

The useful result is the map of *why*, and the one decision it leaves.

## What first principles say

1. **L2 and the ML rule are one law, and that law is Terranox.** Drop the ML rule and every survivor turns into an ordinary developer or manufacturer whose edge is money (Cloverleaf, Ayr, Frontier). Drop L2 and the ML becomes a tool anyone can rent (LandGate, Paces, Patlytics, Emerald). The edge exists only where an archive is too large for any person to read *and* nobody has summarised the answer. Without ML, reading an illegible record is a one-off job for an expert, and the owner can hire that expert once they suspect value (L13).
2. **The rubric's other legs are facts about markets, not choices.** Sellers price in the hope (L3) unless they are orphans, and orphaned hardware goes to insiders (L10). Gatekeepers make the decisive prediction themselves (L4, L11). End buyers go direct or fund the proof (L8). Relaxing these on paper changes nothing, because the web checks find them again.
3. **Where ML does make the decision on an owned asset, the payoff is a yield, not a jump** (L12). The congestion-rights book is the only in-sector case in ten hunts where ML is the decider and the asset is owned. It pays like a trading desk.
4. **Free-entry mineral tenure is still the only place every leg holds at once.** That is the "no prospecting" rule's cost, stated plainly: the rule excludes the one structure the rubric describes.

## Seven new laws found this round (L7-L13)
- **L7:** a throughput or prover ML edge is sold by vendors to incumbents, so it is not ownable.
- **L8:** the capitalised end buyer buys or funds the proof directly, so no middle rung forms.
- **L9:** a policy-shock premium has a half-life. Transformer lead times fell from over 100 weeks to about 30, and the gallium/germanium export ban pause runs out on 27 Nov 2026.
- **L10:** orphaned hardware goes to insiders (creditors, JV partners, licensors) in 9 of 12 estates.
- **L11:** a regulator-accepted ML proof is a method open to every applicant, so it creates no scarcity.
- **L12:** where ML is the private decider, the asset is a priced claim, so the edge shows up as a yield.
- **L13:** without ML, illegible data is a one-off expert job, so L2 is an edge only together with the ML rule.
