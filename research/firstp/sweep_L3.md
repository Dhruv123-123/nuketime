# Sweep L3: drop "the seller already prices in the hope" (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
Nothing survives as a real, venture-scale company Dhruv could own. Dropping L3 mostly cancels itself out. C4 gains about 1 point because a hope-priced or optioned right is now allowed. C2 loses 0-1 point because the step-up is measured against the hope price actually paid. Net totals move between -1 and +1, so the best score rises only from 15 to 15-16. The only structure where the hope price does not eat the step-up is an option, JV or NewCo in which the owner keeps a share (Pfizer kept 25% of Telavant, and Roivant turned about $45M into about $5.2B net). That structure is already the core business of funded incumbents in each domain: Cloverleaf for powered land, Earthrise for surplus interconnection, and Roivant, Formation Bio and Biossil for shelved drugs. So **L6 (the ladder is held) kills it, with L1 (cheap proofs pass) and L2 (outcomes legible) close behind.**

## How the relaxation changes the rubric
- **C4 (data and right):** a right bought at 2-10x its use value, or held through an option or JV, now counts as "buyable". It gains about 1 point wherever illegibility was not the real problem. Where the data is legible (Poland GML, FEMA BLE layers, LandGate headroom), C4 stays at 1-2.
- **C2 (step-up):** the denominator is now the hope price or option strike, not farm value. In German Bauerwartung land (16-40% of the Bauland price) and data-centre farmland (5-10x farm value) this removes most of the multiple. Options and JVs restore it as leverage on the option fee or equity contribution.
- **L1, L2, L4, L5 and L6 are untouched.** Most old land ideas also died on L1, L2 and L6, so they barely move.

## 3. Adversarial checks on the top 3
Chosen: powered-land options/JV (15), the shelved-drug NewCo (15, which also has the largest step-up seen in any hunt), and the surplus-interconnection JV (14, the newest frame). UK promotion (16) was skipped because it was already refuted 3/3 twice. N4 (15) was not checked and is flagged as untested.

### A. Powered-land options / JV: dies on L6
- **Real prices:** Texas power-ready sites about $800k/acre against $2.5-10k/acre unstudied. Northern Virginia entitled land about $4M/acre without power against $6M+ with power, only 1.5x. Columbus farmland at $30k/acre goes to over $150k/acre when rezoned. Committed utility load was the strongest predictor of price (Altus). Source: [Data Center Knowledge](https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/ai-data-center-land-buying-hits-6b-as-power-premiums-rise).
- **Step-up against the hope price:** about 8-30x in Texas on a strike at 5-10x farm value, and much higher on the option fee. That is real leverage, so C2 = 3.
- **Who already does it:** Cloverleaf Infrastructure has raised about $300M (NGP, Sandbrook), took an Nvidia minority stake in Aug 2026 reported at several hundred million dollars, and has sold over 7 GW of sites, including for Oracle and OpenAI, with a 10 GW+ pipeline ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/nvidia-backs-data-center-powered-land-company-cloverleaf/), [Latitude](https://latitudemedia.com/news/investors-embrace-cloverleafs-energy-first-approach-to-data-centers)). LandGate sells headroom for every substation.
- **Base rate:** the land deal market was $3.3B in Q1 2026, up 141% ([Bisnow](https://www.bisnow.com/national/news/data-center-development/data-center-sites-are-warping-the-market-for-developable-land-134771)). Local opposition killed Cloverleaf's Wisconsin campus, so proofs fail on politics, not on discovery.
- **Verdict:** the business is real and venture-scale, but the rung is held. The edge is collateral and utility relationships, not ML. L6 kills it, with C1 (discovery is about 30-40% of the work) behind.

### B. Shelved-drug NewCo (owner keeps equity): dies on L6 and the standing rule that ML must be decisive
- **Real step-up:** Roivant put about $45M plus development funding into 75% of Telavant (RVT-3101, in-licensed from Pfizer, which kept 25%). Roche bought it for $7.1B cash plus a $150M milestone, and Roivant netted about $5.2B, a gain of about $5.3B ([Roivant 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1635088/000114036124007260/ef20015327_10q.htm), [BioCentury](https://www.biocentury.com/article/650358/roche-to-challenge-merck-in-ibd-via-7-1b-telavant-takeout)). That is about 100x on the upfront, the textbook case of "the owner keeps the hope, you take the proving margin". C2 = 5.
- **Who already does it:** Roivant (a vant per asset); Formation Bio ($372M, in-licenses from Sanofi and China) ([Pharm Tech](https://www.pharmaceutical-technology.com/newsletters/formation-bio-secures-372m-funds)); Biossil ($70M from OpenAI and Founders Fund, using AI to pick failed or shelved molecules to buy or license) ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/biossil-exits-stealth-with-70-million-usd-to-give-failed-medicines-a-second-chance/)). China out-licensing hit $135.7B headline value and about $7B upfront across 157 deals in 2025, one third of global licensing spend, with nine NewCos ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2026-03-17-key-trends-and-considerations-in-cross-border-life-sciences-partnering-licensing), [DIA](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/chinas-out-licensing-deals-bear-fruit-in-2025/)).
- **Base rate:** Phase 2-to-approval odds are typically about 15-30% (general knowledge, not checked here). Licensors are the most sophisticated sellers in any market and price hope through upfronts, milestones and retained equity. Asset data (ClinicalTrials.gov, publications) is legible to every BD team.
- **Verdict:** this is the strongest step-up in any hunt, and it only works with L3 relaxed. But the ladder is crowded with $70-370M funded players, and it is outside Dhruv's energy and deeptech edge. The proof is a $50-300M clinical program, not an 80%-discovery rung (C1 = 2), and ML's lift on choosing assets is unproven: BenevolentAI and others did not show it. L6 and C1 kill it.

### C. Surplus-interconnection JV: dies on L2, L1 and L6
- **Size:** a UC Berkeley working paper finds 102 GW of solar plus 24 GW of storage and 28 GW of wind could share existing PJM POIs. It treats fossil plants under a 15% capacity factor as hosts, and the connection takes 1-2 years against 5-7 for a new POI ([pv magazine](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/?p=18626)). FERC accepted PJM's expanded SIS rules in Feb 2025 ([McGuireWoods](https://www.mcguirewoods.com/client-resources/alerts/2025/2/ferc-approves-expansion-of-pjms-surplus-interconnection-service-to-address-near-term-resource-adequacy-concerns)). MISO is studying 72 SIS projects totalling about 8.96 GW, mostly batteries proposed by utilities themselves (Entergy, Xcel, Duke) ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/virginia-indiana-surplus-interconnection-pjm-miso-spp/813442/)).
- **Who already does it:** Earthrise Energy buys gas peakers (since 2021) and adds solar through SIS. Its 270 MWac Gibson City project reached commercial operation on 9 June 2026 with about $630M raised, and it has about 1.5 GW more in Illinois ([Solar Power World](https://www.solarpowerworldonline.com/2026/06/illinois-solar-project-shortens-interconnection-by-tying-into-natural-gas-infrastructure/), [pv-tech](https://pv-tech.org/earthrise-raises-us630-million-for-270mw-illinois-solar-project)). D.E. Shaw added 300 MW of batteries to its own Hornshadow solar site.
- **Why it dies:** the screen is a public dashboard (L2). SIS is allowed only where no network upgrades are needed, so the study usually passes (L1, lever about 1.2x). The host owner must agree and can read its own capacity factor, and utilities and IPPs self-develop (L4/L6). Earthrise shows the "pay the hope price for the plant" version already exists.

## 4. Which law kills it instead
- **L6 (the ladder is held)** is the binding law once L3 goes. Every option, JV or NewCo structure that makes a hope-priced right worth buying is already a funded company's whole strategy: Cloverleaf, Earthrise, Roivant, Formation, Biossil, the UK promoters and Millrose.
- **L1 and L2** sit right behind. The surviving land and grid ideas have proofs that pass 40-90% of the time and signals already sold as data products (LandGate, Paces, Shovels, LandTech, the Berkeley SIS dashboard).
- **C2 measured against the hope price** wipes out most of the land multiples (German edge fields 1.5-4x, Australian growth corridors under 3x, data-centre zoning 1-3x). Only option and JV leverage restores it, and that is the structure the incumbents already use.
- **The one place L3 was really the only blocker** is shelved drug assets, and there C1, the standing rule that ML must be decisive, and the sector fit fail instead.
