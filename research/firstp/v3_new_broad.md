# v3 broad sweep: new Terranox-shaped ideas outside energy and machines (19 Oct 2026)

**Verdict: no pass.** I generated 30 new ideas across 12 sectors and scored them under the relaxed v3 tests in `V3_SHAPE.md`. Five scored 16-18 before web checks. I web-checked the top four, and none reached 17 afterwards. The best after checks was **15/25**, for unused interment rights in sold-out cemeteries, and that market is too thin to become a large company. The other three checked ideas failed on the same three laws as before:
- **L8 (end buyer or owner does the proof):** backlist owners now make AI audiobooks themselves through Audible and ElevenLabs.
- **L7 (the edge is sold as a service):** Troveo, Protege and Calliope broker AI-training footage for owners, so owners don't need to sell cheap.
- **L6 (rung held):** Spotter, with $755M raised, already holds creator catalogs.

Relaxing the numbers did not help, for the reason VERDICT.md gives. Where the step-up is real, the seller can see it. Where the seller can't see it, there is no honest proof that ML picks better than a simple baseline.

What is new here: AI-training-data licensing is the one current, sector-wide repricing of a physical or archival asset I found ($1-4 per minute of footage; Shutterstock reported $104M of AI licensing revenue). But intermediaries let owners capture that repricing themselves, so a buyer of archives keeps only orphaned estates. That is L10 territory again.

## Web checks

### #1 Archive-for-AI vault: 18 -> 14
- **Price gap is real but small and visible.** Bloomberg (Jan 2025) reported that OpenAI, Google and others pay **$1-4 per minute** of unused footage, $1-2 for ordinary YouTube-style footage; deals "often add up to thousands of dollars". Later write-ups cite up to $6/min for professional or niche footage, and buyers want libraries of 1,000+ hours ([the-decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-and-google-are-buying-youtubers-unpublished-videos-for-up-to-4-per-minute/), [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/ai-giants-pay-thousands-for-creators-unused-footage-to-train-models/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/artificial-intelligence/youtubers-are-selling-their-unused-video-footage-to-ai-companies)). So 1,000 hours yields about $60-240k per non-exclusive licence. That is a real step-up over the storage-cost value of an orphaned archive, but each licence is small.
- **Incumbents are intermediaries, which kills S1.** Troveo raised a $4.5M seed (Seven Seven Six), has paid content owners more than $20M and licenses more than 8M hours. Protege raised a $25M Series A (Aug 2025) after a $10M seed, holds 300k+ hours of video and 500k+ hours of audio, and has 100+ media partners ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.nz/story/troveo-expands-ai-data-platform-into-five-categories), [Pulse 2.0](https://pulse2.com/protege-25-million-series-a-raised-for-expanding-ai-training-data-platform/)). Even a lingerie-fighting league signed an AI data deal for 75 TB in Jan 2026 ([StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Lingerie+Fighting+Championships+Sign+AI+Deal/25818020.html)). Once any owner can get a revenue share without selling, the seller no longer prices in ignorance. That leaves only orphaned estates, which go to insiders (L10).
- **Market size is unclear and capped.** Luminate (Dec 2025) says the market is hard to quantify and that "licensing is far outstripped by scraping" ([Luminate](https://luminatedata.com/blog/protege-media-is-licensing-video-to-train-ai-what-it-means-for-film-tv/)). Shutterstock reported $104M of AI licensing revenue (with a $250M projection for 2027) on about $935M total; the year is ambiguous between sources ([Business Insider NL](https://businessinsider.nl/2-of-the-largest-stock-photo-platforms-are-merging-sending-their-shares-up/)). The largest owners deal directly with labs: Lionsgate gave Runway its 20,000-title library ([RightsTech](https://rightstech.substack.com/p/lionsgate-reaches-a-deal-with-runway)). That is L8.
- **ML lift vs baseline: weak.** Data-valuation research says novelty and de-duplication matter (Asymmetric Data Shapley, [arXiv 2511.12863](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.12863); TinyStories duplication study, [arXiv 2411.15821](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15821)). But I found no evidence that labs pay more for data scored that way than a simple hours × resolution × category baseline would predict. Prices are set deal by deal.
- **Rescore:** S1 3, S2 3, S3 2, S4 4, S5 2 = **14**.

### #2 Backlist format rights: 17 -> 13
- **The owner does the proof (L8).** In May 2025, Audible launched AI narration for publishing partners, with 100+ voices in four languages and AI translation in beta ([Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250519/97756-audible-expands-catalog-with-ai-narration-and-translation-services.html)). Virtual Voice had produced 40,000+ KDP titles by mid-2024 ([Travsonic](https://www.travsonic.com/audible-virtual-voice-audiobook-narration/)). HarperCollins converts its foreign-language backlist with ElevenLabs. Elredo (€1M+ raised) and Spines ($22.5M raised) sell bulk backlist conversion as a service ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/160679-elredo-voice-raises-1m-to-bring-ai-audiobook-production-to-us-publishers/), [PW on Spines](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/99198-spines-adds-ai-audiobook-voice-cloning-translations.html)). Producing the audio is now nearly free for the rights holder, so there is no gap for a buyer to capture.
- **Rights are mostly placed already.** A publisher quoted by PW said most backlist titles "have already either been exploited directly by us, or ... sub-licensed to the many audio-only publishing houses" ([PW](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/content-and-e-books/article/97387-the-audiobook-business-learns-to-embrace-ai.html)). That cuts S1.
- **ML:** predicting title demand from Goodreads ratings and used-book prices is plausible, but I found no evidence it beats a sales-rank baseline. AI flooding also lowers revenue per title.
- **Rescore:** S1 2, S2 2, S3 3, S4 4, S5 2 = **13**.

### #3 Unused interment rights: 17 -> 15 (best after checks; too small)
- **Price gap exists at the top end.** Gravesites range from several hundred dollars to "upwards of $100,000" in prestige cemeteries, and many plots go unused because families move or split ([Deeds.com](https://www.deeds.com/articles/a-matter-of-grave-importance-heres-how-to-transfer-a-cemetery-plot/)). In Hong Kong, a 2016 report said a resale broker made up to 10x on niches bought a decade earlier, and quoted a Po Fook Hill niche at over HK$500k ([The Star via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/malaysia/the-star-malaysia/20161010/282063391477237)). The Consumer Council (2022) found private double niches up to about HK$5.2M ([Consumer Council](https://www.consumer.org.hk/en/press-release/p-545-private-columbarium-services)). By contrast, Catholic-cemetery public niches cost HK$7,490-16,040 ([Catholic Cemeteries HK](https://cemeteries.catholic.org.hk/en-GB/burial-services/35)).
- **But the market is thin and gated.** Kiplinger says there is "not really an established resale market", many cemeteries won't buy back plots, and every co-owner must sign ([Kiplinger](https://kiplinger.com/article/retirement/t021-c000-s001-selling-a-burial-plot-is-a-grave-decision.html)). The biggest US listing site charges $49/yr and has about 100 clients ([KFOX](https://www.kfoxtv.com/news/nation-world/online-platform-helps-people-buy-sell-unwanted-cemetery-plots-nationwide-united-states-owner-jeff-solomons-north-carolina-website)). Since the Private Columbaria Ordinance (30 Jun 2017), only licensed columbaria may sell or let niches, and buyers from non-licensees can cancel; some tenure ends in 2047 ([HK Gov](https://www.isd.gov.hk/eng/tvapi/20_fh70.html), [LegCo 2022](https://www.info.gov.hk/gia/general/202205/04/P2022050400446.htm)). Resale by a trader may be legally risky; I could not confirm the statute text.
- **ML lift:** the edge is matching cemetery deed books to probate records and obituaries. That is ordinary entity resolution with a modest lift over a skip-tracer, and cemeteries don't publish occupancy data, so access to records is the real constraint.
- **Rescore:** S1 4, S2 3, S3 2, S4 4, S5 2 = **15**. No path to a large company.

### #4 Creator catalog + AI dubbing: 16 -> 13
- **Incumbent:** Spotter raised $200M led by SoftBank at a $1.7B valuation (Feb 2022), $755M in total, with a stated $1B to deploy on creator catalogs ([Tubefilter](https://tubefilter.com/2022/02/16/spotter-raises-200-million-softbank-1-7-billion-valuation/), [Concurrent Media](https://concurrentmedia.com/2022/02/18/spotter-says-it-has-1-billion-war-chest-to-buy-youtube-creators-video-catalogs/)). MrBeast used Spotter money to fund dubbing, so the dubbing step-up is already bought ([dot.LA](https://dot.la/spotter-raise-2656693955.html)).
- **Step-up erodes:** YouTube's auto-dubbing (memory, not re-checked) and reported algorithmic deprioritisation of back catalogs (an unverified aggregator claim) both compress what a buyer can earn.
- **Rescore:** S1 3, S2 2, S3 2, S4 3, S5 3 = **13**.

## What this sweep adds to the map
1. **A new instance of L7/L8 (call it L14 if kept): when a revaluation is caused by a new buyer class (AI labs), marketplaces form within about 12 months and let owners keep the gain.** That happened with footage (Troveo, Protege), audio (Audible, ElevenLabs) and creator catalogs (Spotter). An owner-buyer only wins on assets with no reachable owner, and those go to insiders (L10).
2. **Collectible-style record-linkage arbitrages (cemeteries, watches, fashion) do pass S1 and S4**, but they are dealer-scale. The relaxed rubric dropped the $1B gate at the first rung, but none of them has a second rung.
3. **The idea with the largest asset base, small water systems, has its step-up written into statute for the seller** (fair-market-value laws). That is L12 from the other side.
