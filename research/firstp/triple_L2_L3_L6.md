# Triple sweep: drop L2 (legible outcome), L3 (seller prices the hope) and L6 (rung held), 19 Oct 2026

## Verdict

**No survivor.** The flex-powered land idea from pair_L2_L3 does not get through with all three laws dropped:

- **Owner variant fails.** A landholder with no tenant cannot hold a flexible-load allocation as an ownable, assignable asset in any of the seven jurisdictions tested.
- **Best energy idea:** 14/25.
- **Best idea overall is still off-sector:** the failed-drug rescue, now combined with an FDA-accepted ML companion diagnostic, at 18/25 conditional.

**What kills flex-powered land once L2, L3 and L6 are gone:**

1. **L4, the allocation is a contract and not property.** Utility capacity rights "arise from contracts and tariffs, not recorded real-property interests", so a sale of the parcel does not carry them, and any transfer "typically requires the utility's consent or a new agreement" ([Kidder Mathews](https://kidder.com/trend-articles/when-power-becomes-the-property-the-real-estate-behind-the-ai-data-center-boom/)).
2. **The flex proof lives in the tenant's workload, not the land.** SVP requires utility control of the load-side breaker, and the allocation is given for "verified and dispatchable flexibility" ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/emerald-ai-150m-funding-data-centre-grid-flexibility)). A landholder with no tenant has nothing to verify. The only way round this is to supply the flex itself with an owned battery or gensets (as GridCARE did with PGE), which makes it a build (C1), not an ML proof.
3. **Proposed new law L9: an accepted proof becomes a standard tariff term.** Once regulators accept that curtailment works, they write it into the default service available to every applicant:
   - Texas SB6 makes curtailment mandatory at 75 MW and above.
   - PJM's Board (27 Jul 2026) puts loads that bring no generation on an "Interim Resource Adequacy Service" and curtails them first ([PJM](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/cifp-rbp/2026/20260731/20260731-board-decisional-letter-on-critical-issue-fast-path.pdf)).
   - SPP's CHILLS (FERC-approved June 2026) gives any large load non-firm service for up to 7 years ([Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2026/07/FERC-Show-Cause-Orders-Signal-Broad-Reform-to-Large-Load-Interconnection-Policies)).
   - FERC's six show-cause orders (18 Jun 2026) push flexible-load transmission services across all six RTOs ([Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/06/ferc-issues-six-tailored-show-cause-orders-to-accelerate-large-load-interconnection/)).
   - Ofgem is weighing mandatory curtailment ([Gowling](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2026/ofgem-demand-connection-queue-reform)).

   So "flex" stops being scarce, and the scarce thing becomes **collateral**: $50k/MW in Texas, £237.5-712.5k/MW proposed in the UK.
4. **L8 and L7, as pass 2 proposed.** The end buyer (the tenant) is the party that must sign the flex, and the controller is rented from Emerald or GridCARE.

**The law that now kills the whole "ML is the proof" family is L9.** Where a regulator accepts an ML proof, it accepts it as a *method open to everyone*: PEMS under EPA PS-16, flexible load in tariffs, SAS/AFC for spectrum. Where the proof stays bespoke and scarce (FAA life limits, water courts, FDA labels), the regulator does not yet accept ML. In nine hunts, no case has had both an accepted ML proof and a scarce asset it unlocks.

## Rubric with all three dropped

- **C4** can reach 4 where the signal is public (L2 gone) and the right is bought at the hope price (L3 gone), if the right is actually transferable. Transferability is not something these three laws relax. L4 still applies, and it is the binding term.
- **C5** may reach 5 even when Cloverleaf, Emerald, Formation or Biossil hold the rung, because L6 is gone.
- **C3** is still charged for R-ML and for L7 (an ML prover anyone can buy is not ownable). C1 still requires the physical step to be trivial.
- **Kept:** L1, L4, L5, R-ML, own the asset, no prospecting or drilling, not Terranox, $1B+. The proposed laws L7 and L8 are noted wherever they bind.

### B. T9 PEMS-certified emission offsets: the cleanest case of a regulator accepting ML, but too small. Revised 9.

- EPA PS-16 allows a PEMS, meaning a statistical or ML model, to demonstrate compliance under 40 CFR 60/61/63. It can replace a CEMS through an alternative-monitoring petition ([EPA PS-16](https://epa.gov/sites/default/files/2017-08/documents/performance_specification_16.pdf)). So this is real regulatory acceptance of ML.
- But a PEMS *measures* emissions. It does not create reductions. ERCs only come from real control or shutdown of a source.
- ERC prices are modest. San Joaquin Valley NOx traded at **$9.5-20k/ton** in 2025-26 ([SJVAPCD 2026](https://apps.valleyair.org/busind/pto/erc/ERCCost2026.pdf)). A turbine plant with SCR needs only tens of tons a year of offsets, so the ERCs are worth about $0.5-2M against a $1B+ project.
- PEMS is sold by ABB, CMC and others (L7), and the method is open to every source (L9).
- Scored 3-1-1-3-1 = **9**.

### C. T10 recertified large power transformers (machines): real shortage, but ML is not the proof. Revised 12.

- **The shortage is real.** Lead times are up to 4-5 years and US prices are up about 79% (FT via [aiweekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ft-ai-data-center-boom-blows-out-power-transformer-lead-times-from-months-to-4)). Generator step-up units take more than 160 weeks (WoodMac via [Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-07-09/us-power-companies-scramble-secure-equipment-surging-data-center)). Data-centre transformer demand is about $8.5B in 2026.
- **No used-unit price series exists publicly.** No resale prices were found (this is a gap), so the step-up from scrap value to resale is not sourced.
- **The acceptance proof is IEEE C57 factory and site testing** (DGA, power factor, FRA, and heat-run on rewind). That is test-stand physics. ML only ranks which retired units are worth testing. That is R-ML, and L1 applies: units that were healthy at retirement pass.
- Rebuild shops and brokers already run this trade (Sunbelt Solomon and others, from memory). L6 is dropped here, but they win on test bays and copper, not ML.
- Scored 3-2-1-3-3 = **12**.

## 4. What kills what with L2, L3 and L6 gone

| Killer | Share of top 20 | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **L4: the right is a consent-bound contract, or the asset is public** | ~55% | Flex allocations, queue positions, closed-plant service, SATOA, FIRO, FAA life limits |
| **R-ML / L7: ML ranks, or the prover is a vendor tool** | ~55% | Transformers, pipelines, PEMS, curtailment forecasts, Emerald and GridCARE |
| **C1: the proof is a build or a trial** | ~45% | BESS-backed flex, the energise bundle, drug trials |
| **L9 (new): an accepted proof becomes an open standard** | ~30% | SB6, PJM IRAS, SPP CHILLS, FERC show-cause orders, PS-16, SAS/AFC |
| L1: cheap proofs pass | ~20% | SIS, grey belt, rezoning |
| Too small | ~15% | ERCs, verified-flex compute, patents |

**Proposed L9: when a regulator accepts an ML proof, it accepts it as a method, not as a grant.** A method is open to every applicant, so the proof can no longer be what makes an asset scarce. Scarcity moves to whatever the method does not cover: collateral ($50k/MW, £237.5-712.5k/MW), owned generation (BYOG priority in PJM and Alberta), or the end buyer's signature (L8).

## 5. Feasibility test for the only open thread (under $25k)

**Kill-test for T1:** send a written question, about $15-20k of regulatory counsel plus direct calls, to SPP transmission owners (Evergy, OG&E, Xcel SPS) and to SVP:

> Can a CHILLS or flexible-load applicant that is a landholder with no tenant hold non-firm service, and assign it to a later end user with its priority intact?

- If **two of them say yes in writing**, T1 goes back to about 15 (C4 to 3, C1 to 3), and the play is an SPP-only flex-site holdco. The step-up would be Altus's $28k/acre to $0.46-2.8M/acre, discounted for non-firm.
- If none says yes, T1 is dead on L4/L9.

Expected outcome, given the evidence above: no. The same counsel time would be better spent on the drug-rescue replication test in pair_L3_L6.md (Vivli/YODA), which is still the highest-scoring thread in pass 3.
