# v3 re-score of the strongest old ideas (19 Oct 2026)

## Verdict

**Nothing passes v3 either.** We re-scored 44 of the strongest old ideas on the five qualitative legs in `V3_SHAPE.md`. Six scored 16-18/25 before checks. We web-checked the top five, and all of them fell to 13-16. The bar is 17+ with no leg at 0-1, so nothing passes.

| Top 5 under v3 | v3 before check | After check | Leg that fell | Why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ML crop actives as a CoM prospect generator | 18 | **16** | S3 4 -> 3, S4 3 -> 2 | No published hit-rate lift from Moa, Enko or AgPlenus. Partners register the active, but on a 10+ year clock with thin terms |
| Orphan patents with evidence of use (EoU) | 17 | 14 | S3, S5 | Patlytics ($65M raised) sells EoU search to everyone, so the lift is rented, not owned. The brokered market is flat at $158M a year |
| ML materials composition patents | 17 | 14 | S2, S3 | No AI-found material has been deployed at scale. There is no royalty precedent. Discovered Materials (YC, $9M, Aug 2026) and Periodic Labs hold the rung |
| Alberta end-of-life well liability transfer ("ClosureCo") | 17 | 13 | S2, S3, S4 | The rung is nearly empty (Skye holds <20 wells). But the fee equals the estimated closure cost, so the gain is a cost margin, not a step-up. No ML cost-prediction lift has been published, and the physical closure is most of the work |
| Brightfield: buy distressed C&I and community solar | 16 | 14 | S2, S3 | Healthy DG portfolios trade at ~$0.86/W, so buying distressed and fixing gives roughly 1.5-3x on equity. Fault-detection ML is sold by vendors (Raptor Maps, Omnidian) |

**What v3 actually changed.** Dropping the numbers revived the ideas that had died on numeric overfit: step-up under 10x, a 1.3-3x lever, discovery under 80% of the rung, slow repricing, and "too small vs $1B". About 15 of the 44 kills were mainly numeric, and those ideas rose 2-5 points. Even so, every one stalls at 13-16 on a structural leg:

- **S3: the lift is unmeasured or rented.** The honest measured lifts are zero (powered land: AUC 0.721 vs 0.727 for a lookup) or negative (trial subgroups: Chekroud 2024). Where a lift plausibly exists, a vendor sells it to everyone (Patlytics, LandTech, Raptor Maps). v3 accepts "any honest lift", but none of these has a demonstrated *owned* lift.
- **S1: sellers price the hope.** This is unchanged from v2 for every land, power, queue and licensor idea.
- **S5: private outcome labels exist only in screens.** Greenhouse and synthesis screens, confidential settlements and closure-cost records have them. Everything else has public outcomes.

**The best non-pass is ML crop actives (16/25).** It is the only idea with no leg below 2 that has a clear private flywheel, a 3x+ step-up and a capitalised partner who funds the physical step. It misses on S3, because no lift has been published, and on time to value. Bayer's icafolin is the first new broadacre post-emergent mode of action (MoA) in 30 years, with €750M expected peak sales and launch from 2028. That shows the prize is real and the clock is long. Its cheapest kill test is a hindcast: train on public HRAC/patent actives with known MoA and measure top-decile enrichment versus a similarity baseline. That costs roughly $20-40k.

## Web checks on the top 5

### 1. ML crop actives as a CoM prospect generator: 18 -> 16 (fail, closest)
- **Real step-up.** The prize is large but slow.
  - Bayer's icafolin is the first new broadacre post-emergent MoA in 30+ years. Bayer expects ~€750M peak sales, with launch from 2028 in Brazil and registrations filed in the US, Canada, Brazil and the EU ([AgTechNavigator](https://www.agtechnavigator.com/Article/2025/08/04/bayer-unveils-first-new-herbicide-mode-of-action-in-over-30-years); [Bayer](https://www.bayer.com/media/en-us/bayer-submits-registration-applications-for-novel-herbicide-in-four-major-markets/)).
  - A filed CoM on a confirmed new MoA is worth well over 3x its filing cost to a major. S2 = 4 holds.
- **ML lift evidence.** None has been published.
  - Moa says it has screened >900k compounds and found ">80 potential novel MoAs". Enko/Syngenta claim lower "time and cost". Neither gives a hit-rate number against a baseline ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/moa-technology-raises-22-2-million-series-c-to-advance-next-generation-herbicides/); [CropLife](https://www.croplife.com/crop-inputs/herbicides/syngenta-and-enko-use-ai-to-advance-safe-sustainable-weed-control-solutions/)).
  - Academic work (cropCSM) notes that "little has been done" on computational hit rates for herbicides.
  - S3 4 -> 3. The lift is plausible, but unproven.
- **Who holds the rung.**
  - Moa: £22.2M Series C (Jul 2026); partners Nufarm, Gowan, Certis Belchim and Corteva.
  - Enko: last round a $70M Series C (2022), with no 2026 round found ([Seedtable](https://seedtable.com/companies/enko-chem)).
  - AgPlenus (Bayer licence) inside Evogene, worth $7-10M.
  - The majors run AI in-house.
  - The rung is held but thin: no one has shown a royalty arriving.
- **Realistic size.** The licensor captures a single-digit royalty on a ~€750M-peak active, about ten years after licensing. That is a **$100-500M company** unless it ladders into owning registrations.
- **Rescore.** S1 4, S2 4, S3 3, S4 2 (registration takes 10 years and $300M even with the partner paying), S5 4 = **16/25**.

### 2. Orphan patents with evidence of use: 17 -> 14
- **Real step-up.** The 2024 brokered market was **$158M**, flat since 2022 ([IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/smarter-sharper-patent-market); [Richardson Oliver](https://www.richardsonoliver.com/publications/the-2025-richardson-oliver-patent-market-report)). The average asking price is ~$100k (2022). A chart adds 32-108% (hunt 9). A 3x+ return needs assertion, which a funder runs. S2 3.
- **ML lift evidence.** Patlytics claims an 80% cut in project time and $30k saved per chart. It has raised ~$65M (Series B $40M, Apr 2026) and sells this to 40% of the AmLaw 100 ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/patlytics-40-million-raised-for-ai-patent-lifecycle-platform-expansion/amp/)). The lift is real but rented to every buyer (L7). S3 3 -> 2, S5 3 -> 2.
- **Who holds the rung.** Acacia: ~$450M market cap and $215M TTM revenue, much of it from non-patent businesses ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/42250-78)). Also IV, RPX and Fortress.
- **Realistic size.** Sub-$0.5B. The largest licensing pure-plays are worth about that after 20+ years.
- **Rescore.** 4, 3, 2, 3 (litigation is the proof), 2 = **14**.

### 3. ML materials composition patents: 17 -> 14
- **Real step-up.** No AI-discovered material has been deployed at scale, and manufacturability is the stated risk ([TechNews/Discovered Materials](https://technews.tw/2026/08/16/discovered-materials-is-playing-ai-whack-a-mole-to-hunt-cooler-chips/)). There is no disclosed licensing or royalty deal between an AI-materials company and a chipmaker. S2 3 -> 2.
- **ML lift evidence.** Simulation-verified candidates only. The lift from candidate to qualified material is unmeasured. S3 3 -> 2.
- **Who holds the rung.**
  - Discovered Materials: $9M seed (Lightspeed, Peak XV, Paul Graham), Aug 2026, with exactly this model: patent materials and license to chipmakers ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/144130-discovered-materials-raises-9m-seed-to-hunt-cooler-ai-chips/)).
  - Periodic Labs: $300M seed, reportedly seeking a ~$7B valuation ([Implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/periodic-labs-raises-300-million-to-automate-science/)).
- **Realistic size.** Large if a licensing model works, but that is unproven, and the capital-heavy incumbent is ahead.
- **Rescore.** 4, 2, 2, 3, 3 = **14**.

### 4. Alberta end-of-life well liability transfer: 17 -> 13
- **Real step-up.**
  - Skye Asset Retirement takes well licences and receives "cash consideration equal to the estimated cost to obtain full regulatory closure". The return is the gap between that fee and its own cost ([BOE Report](https://boereport.com/2024/09/26/skye-asset-retirement-expands-alberta-portfolio-with-new-liability-acquisition/amp/); [Globe and Mail, Mar 2025](https://www.theglobeandmail.com/business/article-can-closurecos-solve-albertas-oil-and-gas-liability-problem)).
  - That is a cost margin (plausibly 10-40%), not a re-rating. S2 3 -> 2.
  - In the US, Diversified (via Next LVL) states $20-25k per well against state averages of ~$157k for orphans. That shows the cost gap exists, but it is captured as a service margin ([WV Gazette-Mail](https://wvgazettemail.com/news/energy_and_environment/disappointing-wvs-diversified-well-plugging-deal-viewed-as-missed-opportunity/article_fe713b87-17f0-43f2-9e7d-b0a7c486adfb.html); [Post-Gazette 2018](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/pittsburgh-post-gazette/20180924/281951723739349)).
- **ML lift evidence.** No published model predicts closure cost from Petrinex/AER data. XI Technologies argues that the deemed (LLR) costs are biased in both directions ([EnergyNow](https://energynow.ca/2019/07/xi-technologies-why-using-llr-can-cause-inaccurate-abandonment-cost-estimates/)). A lift is plausible but undemonstrated. S3 3 -> 2.
- **Who holds the rung.** Almost nobody. Skye holds **<20 wells** (Globe). Alberta has 274,215 inactive, marginal or decommissioned wells and a C$33B+ liability. The OWA took 4,000 Long Run wells in Apr 2026, and the levy is C$154M a year ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/edmonton/article/albertas-abandoned-oil-and-gas-wells-have-skyrocketed-in-number-who-pays-to-close-them/)). This is the only top-5 idea with an open rung.
- **The catches.**
  - The licensee must pass AER's capability assessment (formerly LMR 2.0) to hold liabilities. Sequoia and Redwater show the regulator scrutinises these transfers.
  - The physical closure is most of the work. S4 3 -> 2.
- **Realistic size.** Closure spend runs about C$0.7-0.9B a year (Directive 088), so a large share at a 20% margin is a **$100-300M-a-year business**. That is a real company, but it is a contractor with an underwriting edge, not a Terranox shape.
- **Rescore.** 3, 2, 2, 2, 4 = **13**.

### 5. Brightfield distressed DG/community solar: 16 -> 14
- **Real step-up.**
  - A healthy contracted DG portfolio sold at ~$0.86/Wdc (Ecofin, 63.7 MWdc for $54.5M EV, Mar 2025; our arithmetic) ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en/news/a56631c5/norton-rose-fulbright-represents-ecofin-us-renewables-infrastructure)).
  - No distressed $/W price is disclosed. Buying at $0.4-0.7/W and restoring output gives about **1.5-3x on equity**. S2 3 -> 2.
- **ML lift evidence.** Vendor claims only. Raptor Maps finds anomalies affect 2.6% of power, but it publishes no measured recovery lift, and it sells the tool to everyone ([Greentown Labs](https://greentownlabs.com/raptor-maps-uses-drones-and-artificial-intelligence-to-identify-and-predict-solar-farm-outages/)). S3 3 -> 2.
- **Who holds the rung.** CleanCapital: a $250M CarVal vehicle that targets distressed and credit-heavy assets, plus a Manulife $145M commitment ([Solar Builder](https://solarbuildermag.com/financing/cleancapital-carval-investors-form-250-million-equity-partnership-to-invest-in-solar/); [SolarQuarter](https://solarquarter.com/2024/12/18/cleancapital-boosts-solar-portfolio-with-23-mw-acquisition-from-kendall-sustainable-infrastructure/)). Also Spruce, SunStrong and Altus/TPG.
- **Realistic size.** A yieldco of ~$1-2B, decided by the cost of capital.
- **Rescore.** 3, 2, 2, 4, 3 = **14**.

## What this says

1. **The Terranox numbers were partly overfit, but not the reason nothing passes.** Removing them lifted about a third of the old ideas by 2-5 points. The ceiling after checks moved from ~13-16 (gold/v2) to 16 (v3).
2. **The binding leg under v3 is S3 plus S5 together: an *owned*, *measured* ML lift.** Every idea either has a measured zero/negative lift, an unmeasured lift, or a lift a vendor rents to all buyers. That is L7 and L13 again, now visible without any number thresholds.
3. **The cheapest way to move the best near-miss to 17+ is to measure S3.** A crop-actives MoA hindcast (~$20-40k, 4-6 weeks) would do it. If the top decile shows a clear enrichment over a Tanimoto-similarity baseline on held-out post-2010 actives, S3 goes to 4 and the idea reaches 17. Its size ceiling (a $100-500M royalty company) would still need a ladder into owning registrations.
4. **Alberta ClosureCo is the only open rung found.** It is a sound business, but not this shape: S2 and S4 are capped by its being a closure contractor.

## Caveats
- Several old scores are ranges across sweeps. "Old" shows the best checked figure.
- Some figures are the earlier sweep files' own memory figures, marked there.
- Each check is one agent's judgement with standard web search. No primary filing was read for the agchem royalty rates or for distressed solar $/W, because neither is disclosed.
