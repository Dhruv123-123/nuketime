# Triple sweep: drop L1 (the proof is the expensive build), L6 (rung one is held) and R-ML (ML must make it solvable) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict

**One shape survives as a business, and none survives as a Terranox-shaped company.** With all three laws dropped, the only policy-shock or shortage manufacturing rung a pre-seed team can actually win and grow to venture scale is the asset-light one: **own the design, the brand and the customer, and contract out the factory**. The live example is grid equipment for data centres. Ayr Energy was founded in 2024. It came out of stealth in September 2025 with more than $250M of contracted orders, had an order book above $500M by April 2026 and has raised only about $25M (EIP, General Catalyst, 3one4). Its iron-core transformers are built to its specs by Indian contract manufacturers. Every rung that needs the company to own a melt, a casting line, a reactor or an NRC licence is out of reach before Series B, whatever the shock: germanium/gallium refining, rare-earth-free magnets, GOES or amorphous ribbon, HALEU deconversion or metallisation, nuclear graphite. Niron needed $150M of tribal loans plus a conditional $150M OSC loan for a 1,500 t/yr plant, and DOE gave the HALEU deconversion IDIQs to six incumbents. The cheap-to-enter rungs (Ga/Ge scrap recovery, antimony substitutes) are too small or the shock has already faded. US gallium metal imports were about $15M in 2025, and antimony fell from about $59,750/t to $18,300-19,000/t.

**Where it stops being Terranox-shaped:** at the moment R-ML is dropped. Terranox reads illegible data, buys an unproven right cheaply and proves a 10^3x step-up. The survivor has none of those parts: no discovery rung, no right to buy and no step-up. It earns a **scarcity margin** on a commodity product, and that margin decays as capacity lands. On the gold rubric it scores **11/25**. On a plain "is this a good hardware venture" lens it scores well. The rule that kills it inside the gold frame is C1/C2 (no discovery rung, no step-up), plus a proposed law **L9: a policy-shock premium has a half-life**. Antimony took about 15 months to fall from peak to a third of peak, distribution-transformer lead times fell from 100+ weeks (2023) to about 30 weeks, and the Chinese Ga/Ge/Sb ban suspension expires on 27 Nov 2026.

**Recommendation:** do not take this to YC as a new company for the Terranox thesis. If Dhruv wants a "make it and own it" company anyway, the only version a pre-seed team wins is a **third entrant after Ayr**: a data-centre medium-voltage power package (pad-mount transformer + switchgear, contract-built) with a <$100k feasibility test (below). It is a sales and supply-chain company, not an ML company.

## How the triple changes scoring

- **L1 dropped:** C1 may count the build (scale-up, qualification, plant, contract manufacture) as the proof. C3 credits a lever that cuts the cost or time per *qualified unit shipped*, not only per discovery.
- **L6 dropped:** an occupied rung costs nothing on C5 unless the occupant blocks the ladder above.
- **R-ML dropped:** C3 may credit any lever: capital, speed, contract-manufacturing access, design modularity, relationships. ML is optional.
- **Kept:** L2, L3, L4, L5, no prospecting/drilling/mining (recovery from scrap and industrial by-products counts as manufacturing; ore does not), own the asset, not Terranox, $1B+ path.
- **Added lens (because the rubric stops describing these companies):** **PS** = winnable by a pre-seed team (capital to first revenue under ~$5M?), **Dur** = shock durability (does the premium outlast a 5-7 year build-out?), **T** = Terranox-shaped (illegible signal + cheap right + step-up: Y / partly / N).
- With all three dropped, C1's "discovery is 80%+ of the work" clause is the only place the original frame survives. Most ideas below score C1 = 1-2 for that reason, which is the point of this sweep.

## 3. Adversarial checks

### Check 1: N1, contract-built data-centre MV power package (the survivor shape)

- **A pre-seed team has already won this rung, which is the strongest evidence in any sweep so far.** Ayr Energy was founded in 2024 and came out of stealth in Sep 2025 with more than $250M of contracted orders. By April 2026 its order book was above $500M on about $25M raised across two rounds (EIP led the April round, with General Catalyst and 3one4). Indian manufacturers build to its specs, and it plans solid-state transformers later ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/15/this-energy-startups-bet-on-100-year-old-grid-tech-is-paying-off/), [Renewable Energy World](https://www.renewableenergyworld.com/power-grid/new-power-grid-equipment-manufacturer-launches-with-more-than-250m-in-contracted-orders), [Munich Startup](https://insights.munich-startup.de/news/feed/ayr-energy-raises-25m-to-build-century-old-transformers-for-ai-data-centres)). That is about 20x order book per dollar raised.
- **Demand is real but easing at the average:** distribution-transformer prices are up 78-95% since 2019 and power-transformer prices about 77%. WoodMac puts the 2025 supply deficit at about 10% for distribution and about 30% for power transformers, with imports filling ~50% and ~80%. Average DT lead time is ~30 weeks, down from 100+ in 2023, **but pad-mount 3-phase (the DC unit) still runs 2-3x pre-pandemic** and is expected to worsen. Power transformers average 128 weeks and GSUs 144 weeks ([Power Magazine](https://powermag.com/transformers-in-2026-shortage-scramble-or-self-inflicted-crisis/), [pv magazine USA](https://pv-magazine-usa.com/2026/05/11/u-s-transformer-market-faces-severe-supply-constraints-as-lead-times-extend-to-four-years/), [Giga Energy blog, secondary](https://www.gigaenergy.com/blog/lead-time-delays-for-data-centers)).
- **Market size:** the US distribution-transformer market alone is ~$3.4B (2025), growing to ~$5.6B by 2031 (single vendor, [Mordor](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/united-states-distribution-transformer-market)). Power transformers and switchgear add several times that (memory). Incumbent backlogs: Končar ~$2.72B, Astor ~$1.85B ([Transformer Technology](https://transformer-technology.com/koncar-plans-texas-factory-as-part-of-2-7-billion-order-backlog/)). DOE expects installations to triple by 2050 ([ECMag/DOE](https://www.ecmag.com/magazine/articles/article-detail/doe-finalizes-new-rule-on-energy-efficiency-standards-for-transformers)). A $1B company needs ~$1B/yr revenue at hardware multiples, so roughly 5-10% of the US DT + MV switchgear market. Ayr shows the path but nobody has walked it yet.
- **What breaks (adversarial):**
  1. **The margin is a shortage margin (L9).** Cliffs is adding ~25% GOES (Butler, ~2028) and won a $400M DLA GOES contract. Hitachi, Eaton and others are adding capacity, and Proterial is adding 30 kt/yr of amorphous ribbon in India. When lead times normalise, a contract-built brand competes on price against the factories that build it.
  2. **Tariff and trade exposure.** Indian-built transformers pay 1.6% MFN with no 232 duty on assembled units (forum commentary only), but country-level US tariffs on India have swung widely since 2025 (memory). India has also opened an anti-dumping probe on CRGO and amorphous imports, which raises its contract makers' input costs ([Hitavada](https://www.thehitavada.com/Encyc/2026/6/27/anti-dumping-duty-on-electrical-steel-may-push-transformer-costs-impact-grid-expansion-gtri.html)).
  3. **A third entrant is late.** Ayr has the Indian factory relationships and the DC logos. A new team would need a different factory geography (Turkey, Mexico, Vietnam, Korea) or a different product (MV switchgear, GSU, SST bridge).
  4. **No ML and no discovery.** ML-optimised transformer design (loss vs cost) is a commodity design tool. The edge is sales, factory QA and working capital. R-ML is truly dropped, not just bent.
- **Rescore:** C1 2 (buyer pays for a discrete unit, but it is a product sale, not a proven right), C2 2 (gross margin 20-35% assumed, memory, not a step-up), C3 3 (contract manufacture cuts capex to first revenue by ~10-50x versus a factory; speed lever), C4 1 (no illegible signal; inputs at market), C5 3 (transformer → switchgear → SST/power island is a real ladder) = **11/25**. Hardware-venture lens: PS yes, Dur medium, T no.

### Check 2: N5, Ga/Ge recovery from scrap (the cheapest policy-shock rung)

- **Shock is real but on/off:** China has 99% of primary low-purity gallium. Global output was ~990 t in 2025 against ~1,700 t capacity. China lifted its US ban for one year in Nov 2025; the suspension runs to **27 Nov 2026**, with a licence still required per shipment ([USGS MCS 2026](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-gallium.pdf), [Fastmarkets](https://fastmarkets.com/insights/china-suspends-export-prohibition-on-superhard-materials-us)). Prices conflict: ex-China 6N ~$2,350/kg (May 2026), versus SMM China benchmark ~$235/kg and FOB ~$433/kg (Sep 2026, unverified site) ([SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103927025-high-purity-gallium-prices-surge-in-europe-spot-trading-reaches-2350kg-amid-supply-shortage), [rare-earth-mining.com](https://rare-earth-mining.com/gallium-price/)).
- **Market too small:** US gallium metal imports were **~$15M** in 2025 (GaAs wafers ~$120M) and US consumption averaged ~16 t/yr (2016-20) (USGS). Even at the ex-China price, US demand is ~$40M/yr.
- **Rung occupied and grant-funded:** Indium Corp ($3.2M DOE TRACE-Ga, one of five), Metallium (DLA SBIR ~A$100k, Phase II up to $1M planned) ([Semiconductor Today](https://semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2026/apr/indium-270426.shtml), [Semiconductor Today](https://www.semiconductor-today.com/news_items/2025/sep/metallium-030925.shtml)).
- **Rescore:** C1 2, C2 2, C3 2, C4 2, C5 1 = **9**. Killed by the $1B bar and L9 (the shock can be switched off by MOFCOM in one notice; antimony shows it).

### Check 3: N8 rare-earth-free magnets and N12/N11 HALEU-adjacent steps (capital-gated rungs)

- **Magnets:** Niron's Sartell plant (1,500 t/yr, start 2027) is funded by a $150M SMSC loan (Aug 2026), a conditional OSC commitment of up to $150M that requires an equity raise, a $10M Minnesota grant and ~$52.2M of tax credit, with a 10,000 t/yr second plant to break ground in 2028 ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/rare-earth-free-magnet-maker-niron-secures-150m-loan-for-first-plant-2026-08-19), [MPR](https://mprnews.org/story/2026/08/18/niron-magnetics-150-million-tribal-loan-advances-plan)). Adamas sees ~80-100 kt/yr of ex-China NdFeB capacity targeted for 2030, with price floors (MP-DoW) and a 25% US tariff on Chinese magnets from Jan 2026 ([Adamas](https://www.adamasintel.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Adamas-Intelligence-Rare-Earth-Magnet-Market-Outlook-Top-Predictions-for-2026.pdf)). The rung is winnable only with $300M of patient debt; a pre-seed team cannot get there. Rescore 12.
- **HALEU deconversion:** DOE's deconversion IDIQs went to six incumbents (Framatome, Orano, Westinghouse, GNF, NFS, ACO per DOE's list), with a $2M minimum each and no guaranteed work. TerraPower is funding Framatome's metallisation pilot. No commercial US deconversion plant exists yet ([DOE](https://www.energy.gov/node/4830468), [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/framatome-terrapower-team-up-for-haleu-metallization-pilot-line/)). TRISO: Standard Nuclear raised a $140M Series A in Jan 2026 and is already fabricating ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260126420607/en/Standard-Nuclear-Begins-Production-of-Advanced-HALEU-Fuel-and-Raises-140-Million-in-Series-A-Funding)). **L4 kills this:** the public buyer picks its incumbents, and an NRC Cat II licence takes years. Rescore 10.
- **Li-7 (best nuclear-adjacent idea):** the US has had no domestic production since 1963 and relies on Russia and China; PWR demand is ~1 t/yr, and WNA projects ~250 t/yr if MSRs commercialise ([GAO-13-716](https://gao.gov/products/gao-13-716), [Power Technology](https://www.power-technology.com/features/enriched-lithium-and-the-race-for-advanced-nuclear-technologies/)). A mercury-free lab process is a cheap pre-seed proof, and DOE's Isotope Program is a capitalised buyer. But today's market is ~1 t/yr, worth a few tens of $M at most (memory), and the venture case needs MSRs that are a decade out. It fails the $1B bar on today's demand. It is the only idea here with a real discovery-ish rung (process chemistry), and it is still not Terranox-shaped. Rescore 12.

## 5. Answer to the brief

1. **Which policy-shock manufacturing rungs can a pre-seed team win at venture scale?** Only the ones where the factory can be rented. Of the named families, that is **transformers and grid gear** (N1/N4), and Ayr proves it: 2024 founding, $25M raised, $500M+ order book. Everything else fails one of three tests:
   - It needs owned process capital: germanium/gallium refining, RE-free magnets, GOES/amorphous casting, HALEU deconversion and metallisation, TRISO, graphite.
   - It is too small: Ga/Ge scrap recovery (~$15M of US Ga metal imports), Li-7 today.
   - The shock has faded: antimony is back to $18-19k/t, and EU ITRE is moving to exempt HFO refrigerants from the PFAS restriction.
   PFAS-free is the one family where demand is policy-durable, but the open problems are physics, not manufacturing (coolant, fab chemicals, elastomers), and so belong to the discovery frame Dhruv already rejected in P-L1L6.
2. **Survivor?** As a business, conditionally yes (N1). Against the gold frame, no (**11/25**). The killer is C1/C4 plus proposed **L9: a policy-shock premium has a half-life shorter than the build-out**, so the step-up is a decaying margin, not a one-time revaluation.
3. **Where it stopped being Terranox-shaped:** at R-ML. Dropping L1 and L6 together still left "ML discovers, we build" (P-L1L6), which was Terranox's shape with an expensive proof. Dropping R-ML removes the discovery rung, and what is left is the ordinary industrial question of who can ship scarce hardware fastest. No illegible data, no cheap right, no 10^3x step-up. Any pitch for N1 should say plainly that it is a new and different company, not a variant of Terranox.
