# v2 principles: new analogs (19 Oct 2026)

**Verdict: none of the 23 ideas clears 16/25 after web checks.** The best is a *pre-standard SEP generator*: own patent families on technical proposals before a standard freezes, and let pools or licensing partners pay for essentiality checks and enforcement while you keep a share. It scored 19 before the checks and **~15 after**. The other two checked ideas fell harder: ML materials composition patents went 17 -> ~13 (a YC company launched this exact model in Aug 2026, and royalties take 15+ years to arrive), and the SBIR Phase I index went 16 -> ~12 (founders will not sell at a schedule price, and it is a fund, not a company).

The pattern behind this: outside minerals, almost the only rights issued by schedule or first-come (P1) are **patent filings** and a few permits (FERC preliminary permits, ITU filings, plant variety rights). Patents almost never get a *standard proof that reprices at once* (P2). Their value appears slowly through licensing or litigation over 5-15 years. The two exceptions are drug trial readouts, which VERDICT.md already killed, and freezes of a standard's spec (SEPs). Permits with first-come priority mostly cannot be transferred, or else the regulator keeps the upside (L4). **The no-prospecting rule costs about 5-7 points.** Free-entry mineral tenure (a non-uranium prospect generator) scores 22, versus ~15 for the best idea that respects the rule.

Recommendation: there is no new survivor. The SEP generator is worth one cheap hindcast (below) only if Dhruv wants an IP-shaped option that is not Terranox. It does not beat option A in VERDICT.md, the FTR book.

Scoring: P1 option before anyone knows; P2 heavy tail with fast repricing; P3 private outcome flywheel; P4 a partner pays for confirmation; P5 hindcastable. Each is scored 0-5. Figures marked (memory) are unverified.

## Web checks of the top 3

### 1. Pre-standard SEP generator: 19 -> ~15
- **Market and tail.** The aggregate cellular royalty is about $3-16 per device across court decisions, averaging $9.25 ([CRA](https://www.crai.com/insights-events/publications/aggregate-royalty-for-cellular-seps-in-recent-court-decisions/)). Across ~1.2B handsets plus IoT and autos, the pool is roughly $10-15B a year (memory-based estimate). The tail is real: Optis won a $300M jury verdict against Apple ([EWCA 2025](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/ewca/civ/2025/552)), and InterDigital earns ~$0.8-0.9B a year (memory).
- **Concentration (adversarial).** Six entities hold 71% of declared 5G families, and the other 29% is spread across ~65 entities ([GreyB](https://info.greyb.com/5g-standard-essential-patents-whitepaper)). The top 10 hold ~78% of the identified 5G universe ([PA Consulting](https://assets.paconsulting.com/assets/uploads/5G_SEP_IPR_Report.pdf)). Only ~22% of declared families are "core" ([Clarivate](https://clarivate.com/?p=92694)), and 20-30% are essential in most studies ([IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/who-leading-the-5g-patent-race-patent-landscape-analysis-declared-seps-and-standards-contributions)). This is L6 in its plainest form: essentiality is decided by whoever writes the spec text in the meetings.
- **Base rate for an outsider's proposal.** Even Huawei, Ericsson and Nokia get only ~37-39% of 3GPP contributions approved ([ABI Research](https://abiresearch.com/news-resources/chart-data/top-5g-3ggp-contributors)). An outsider filing on *predicted* outcomes must also keep claims broad enough to read on text that other people write. Continuation practice makes that possible, but it is exactly what courts and pools discount.
- **Partner-funded confirmation terms.** Licensing firms of the Acacia type take patents for little or nothing upfront, with contingency counsel paying for enforcement, and the inventor usually keeps ~50%, sometimes as little as ~15% ([EDN](https://www.edn.com/?p=4059218), older source). Pools pay out by patent count or by a pre-agreed weighting ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1547940/the-rise-of-patent-pools-in-the-5g-and-iot-sectors)). That flattens the tail for a small licensor, so per-patent P2 is weaker than the court verdicts suggest. P4 does hold: someone else pays for the essentiality check and for enforcement.
- **P3 and P5.** Contribution, declaration and approval data are public and large, so a hindcast is feasible: score 3GPP Rel-15/16 proposals, then check them against frozen spec text and later declarations. But ABI, IPlytics and GreyB already sell contribution analytics (L7). The only private data you would generate is your own essentiality and negotiation results, which is thin.
- **Regulatory.** The EU withdrew the SEP Regulation in Feb 2025, formally in Oct 2025, and Parliament is challenging the withdrawal at the CJEU ([EP tracker](https://www.europarl.europa.eu/legislative-train/theme-a-europe-fit-for-the-digital-age/file-patent-licensing-package-1)). So SEP enforcement stays strong for now, but this is a policy-risk tail (L9).
- **Adjusted score:** P1 3, P2 3, P3 2, P4 4, P5 3 = **15**. This is a privateer business (IPCom, Optis/PanOptis, Sisvel). Privateers mostly *buy declared families at prices that already reflect what is known*. The pre-declaration option version is unproven and depends on rooms that outsiders do not control.
- **Cheapest kill test (~$20-40k, 4-6 weeks):** train on the text of Rel-15/16 contributions against "approved and later declared essential". Kill the idea if the top decile's lift over the baseline (contributor identity plus approval history) is under 2x, or if outsider-authored proposals never reach the top decile.

### 2. ML materials composition patents: 17 -> ~13
- **Incumbent, same model, same accelerator.** Discovered Materials (YC) raised a $9M seed in Aug 2026 to "patent promising material candidates and license" thermal and semiconductor materials to chipmakers ([Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/144130-discovered-materials-raises-9m-seed-to-hunt-cooler-ai-chips/), [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=3151410)). Periodic Labs, Lila, CuspAI, Orbital Materials and Radical AI are funded at scale (memory). L6.
- **Royalty base rate.** Investors note that pharma-style royalties for materials are "unproven", that customers resist paying in perpetuity, and that value accrues in scale-up and deployment ([Pangaea](https://www.pangaeaventures.com/blog/ai-and-materials-discovery-part-1)). L5/C1 again.
- **Repricing speed (fails P2).** The canonical win is Argonne's NMC cathode. It was filed in 2000, and value arrived only through an ITC win in 2016 and the Umicore licence in 2017 ([BASF](https://www.basf.com/ca/en/media/News-Releases/2017/04/P-US-17-042)). That took 17 years, needed a capitalised co-owner (BASF) to fund the fight, and the terms were never disclosed.
- **Adjusted:** P1 3, P2 1, P3 4, P4 2, P5 3 = **13**.

### 3. SBIR Phase I index: 16 -> ~12
- **Base rates.** ~13% of applicants win Phase I, and ~40% of Phase I winners never apply for Phase II. VC-backed firms are *less* likely to apply ([NAS](https://sites.nationalacademies.org/cs/groups/pgasite/documents/webpage/pga_167189.pdf), [Springer 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11187-023-00740-y)). An illustrative model puts NIH Phase II commercialisation at ~26% ([NAP](https://www.nap.edu/read/12543/chapter/4)). The good firms are the ones that do not need you: adverse selection. "SBIR mills" make up much of the pool ([Acquisition Talk](https://acquisitiontalk.com/2022/08/sbir-mills-dual-use-tech-and-the-case-for-reform-with-ben-van-roo/)).
- **Incumbents.** No SBIR-only fund turned up, but AFWERX/Air Force Ventures, Shield Capital and the Navy's FY27 "shared third-party investment" push are all moving into the same gap ([AFWERX](https://afwerx.com/wp-content/uploads/20241227_Ventures_Program_Overview_v2.pdf), [Navy 2026](https://www.navysbir.com/docs/Transitions_Summer-2026.pdf)).
- **Structure.** P4 is the strongest of any idea here, because the government pays for Phase II. But P1 needs founders to accept a schedule price (the YC trick), the business is a fund, and the right is a minority stake, not an owned asset.
- **Adjusted:** P1 2, P2 3, P3 3, P4 4, P5 3 = **15** as a fund, **~12** as a YC-fit company.

**Cost of the rule:** best inside the rule ~15, best outside 22 (X1). The 7-point gap matches VERDICT.md's finding 4: free-entry mineral tenure is the only place where P1-P5 all hold. X1 overlaps Terranox's playbook almost completely (it is Terranox minus uranium), so lifting the rule largely amounts to "build Terranox's model in a second commodity."

## Caveats
- This was one scorer with one round of web checks. Figures marked (memory) are unverified: InterDigital revenue, Kindred Bio price, Benson Hill bankruptcy, Honeycrisp royalties, and the size of the cellular royalty pool.
- SEP essentiality and concentration figures differ by method (declared vs granted vs core families) and cannot be compared directly.
- The SBIR base rates are mostly NIH data from 1992-2002; DoD may differ.
