# Pair sweep L1 + L3: drop "cheap proofs usually succeed / the failing proof is the bespoke build" AND "the seller prices in the hope" (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
Nothing survives as a real, venture-scale company that Dhruv could own. Dropping both laws allows one structure: pay the owner its hope price, or give it equity, then fund the proof yourself, whether that proof is a cheap high-pass filing or an expensive bespoke build. The best scores rise to 17/25: the shelved-drug NewCo and UK grey-belt promotion, which are the only two ideas whose binding killers were exactly this pair. Both then die on the laws that are still in force. L6 says the rung is held (Roivant, Formation Bio, Biossil; Gladman and the other promoters), and R-ML says ML is not what makes the pain solvable, because capital, the trial or the planning campaign is. The new failure mode from the web checks is **the end buyer finances the proof itself**. Google funds Constellation's 890 MW of uprates through a 20-year PPA, Nvidia took a stake in Cloverleaf, and Pfizer and Roche fund the drug NewCos. Once the hope price is paid and the proof is a build, the company becomes a specialty financier. The best-capitalised financier is always the hyperscaler, pharma major or PE buyer at the top of the ladder. The binding law is L6, with R-ML right behind it.

## How the pair changes the rubric
- **C1 (frame):** a bespoke-build proof (trial, restart, uprate, pilot line) no longer costs points. Discovery need not be 80% of the work, and C1 rises by 1 wherever that was the only penalty.
- **C3 (lever):** a 1.1-3x hit-rate lever is acceptable, and so is a lever from throughput or from ML cutting the cost of the proof itself.
- **C4 (data and right):** a right bought at its hope price, or held through an option, JV, royalty or retained equity, counts as buyable.
- **C2 (step-up):** measured against the hope price or the equity actually paid. Option and JV leverage counts.
- **Still in force:** L2, L4, L5, L6, R-ML, own-the-asset, no prospecting or drilling, not Terranox, venture scale.

## 3. Adversarial web checks on the top 3

### A. Nuclear uprate royalty (N1, new frame): 14 -> about 9
- **Real prices:** Constellation and Google signed a 20-year PPA tied to about $4.3B of upgrades at 11 PJM units for 890 MW by 2032, with the first online in 2028. That is about $4,800/kW (my arithmetic). Braidwood and Byron cost about $800M for about 135 MW (about $5,900/kW). Amazon backs about 190 MW at Calvert Cliffs ([POWER](https://www.powermag.com/google-deal-backs-890-mw-of-constellation-nuclear-uprates-at-11-pjm-reactors/), [Power Eng.](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/constellation-plans-800m-upgrades-at-two-nuclear-plants/)).
- **Who pays:** Constellation invests from its own balance sheet, and Google provides revenue certainty as the anchor offtaker ([Power Eng.](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/google-constellation-doe-nuclear-plant-investment/)). Nobody needs a third-party financier.
- **Pool:** the NRC expects applications adding about 2.4 GW through 2032 against about 98 GW of fleet. Filers are Constellation, Duke, Southern and Vistra ([NRC expected uprates](https://www.nrc.gov/facilities-safety/operating-reactors/operating-reactor-licensing/power-uprates/status-of-power-uprate-applications/expected-applications-for-power-uprates), [MIT TR Oct 2026](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/10/15/1146577/nuclear-power-upgrade/amp/)).
- **Dies on:** the owner keeps the best (L4/L6 pattern; four investor-owned utilities hold the whole pool). The end buyer funds the proof directly. A royalty on someone else's plant is a financing product, not an owned asset. ML is not what solves it (R-ML): the uprate is margin engineering plus a turbine and generator swap. The step-up is about 1.1-1.2x on MW at the plant level.

### B. Closed-mill / idle large-load JV (N2, S-L3 N4, untested in pass 1): 16 -> about 11
- **Who does it:** Amp Z (Amp Energy, which got $374M from Carlyle in 2021) holds an option on about 1,041 acres of the former Southland paper mill at Lufkin, TX. The site has a 55 MW connection, with plans for 175 MW in 2026, 1.1 GW grid by 2028 and 1 GW of self-generation, and Amp Z plans more than 5 GW of campuses. Verdant Industries bought that mill at auction in 2010 for $11M ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/multi-gigawatt-data-center-campus-planned-in-east-texas/), [Lufkin Daily News](https://lufkindailynews.com/news/local/ai-data-center-planned-on-1-000-acres-at-former-southland-paper-mill-site/article_ff9e97bf-361d-5649-835b-68a75818bd94.html)). Digital Power Optimization bought the Wisconsin Rapids mill together with its power supply ([WPR](https://wpr.org/news/new-data-center-kenosha-wisconsin-rapids)). EcoDataCenter paid SEK 400M (about $39M) for 20 ha of a Borlange mill with 250 MW ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/ecodatacenter-breaks-ground-on-borl%C3%A4nge-sweden-data-center/)).
- **Step-up:** the $11M auction price in 2010 against a $1.05B campus under negotiation is real, but that leap is the campus build, and the land step is perhaps 5-30x. Hope is already priced: a 2026 Kouvola mill parcel was listed at about €48M for 21 ha ([search summary; preliminary deal](https://ondemand.tappi.org/?p=9260)).
- **Base rate (L1 runs the other way):** Sentinel dropped its $550M plan at the Jay, ME mill in June 2026 ([Baxtel](https://baxtel.com/news/sentinel-dc-withdraws-maine-paper-mill-data-center-proposal)). Proofs fail on politics and power, not on discovery.
- **Dies on:** L6 (Amp Z, Cloverleaf, DPO and every powered-land broker market "powered shells"). R-ML: closed mills are a list of about 100-200 named sites that brokers already know, with no illegible signal. Utilities reclaim unused service (L4-like).

### C. Shelved-drug NewCo / China-asset NewCo with ML indication selection (row 1 + N9): 17 -> about 12
- **Step-up (unchanged and still the best of any hunt):** Roivant put about $45M into Telavant and netted about $5.2B on Roche's $7.1B purchase (see sweep_L3.md, sourced to the Roivant 10-Q).
- **Who does it, updated:** Biossil announced a $43M round led by Founders Fund and OpenAI on 21 Apr 2026, to revive failed drugs ([Osler](https://www.osler.com/en/about-us/representative-work/biossil-inc/)). Formation Bio in-licensed BLKR201 (TYK2) from Lynk and an anti-CD226 antibody from IMIDomics in 2025-26 ([Formation blog](https://www.formation.bio/blog/category/licensing-and-pipeline)). China out-licensing ran to 157 deals and about $7B upfront in 2025 (sweep_L3.md).
- **Dies on:** L6 (at least three funded AI-native players plus Roivant). R-ML: no public evidence that ML selection beats BD teams at picking shelved winners. The edge is capital and clinical execution. The licensor is the most sophisticated seller in any market. Outside Dhruv's energy and deeptech edge. Venture-scale, yes, but not ownable by a newcomer.

## 5. Most interesting finding
**Proposed L8: once you pay the hope price and the proof is a build, the end buyer finances the proof.** Dropping L1 and L3 together turns every idea into "fund someone else's proof for equity or a royalty". In every domain checked, the best-capitalised funder sits at the top of the ladder and does this itself. Google underwrites Constellation's $4.3B of uprates through a PPA. Nvidia takes equity in Cloverleaf. Roche and Pfizer pay for the NewCo's trial through the exit and retained equity. Carlyle-backed Amp Energy buys the dormant mill. A startup in the middle is a higher-cost-of-capital intermediary. This is the pair-level form of the pass-1 lesson (late winners won with capital, not ML). It suggests the next pass should pair **L6 + R-ML**, because those are now the only laws standing between the 17s and a survivor.

Feasibility test if someone insists on the shelved-drug or powered-land 17/16: backtest whether an ML ranker picks the 2019-2023 in-licensed or shelved assets that later exited (Telavant-type) better than a lookup on modality, phase and licensor. If it shows less than a 0.05 AUC gain, as in our queue backtest, R-ML is confirmed dead.
