# Pair sweep: drop L3 (the seller prices in the hope) AND R-ML (ML must be what makes the pain solvable) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
Nothing survives as a venture-scale company Dhruv could own. Dropping these two laws together turns almost every idea into the same business: a capital-and-relationships developer that pays the owner's hope price, or gives the owner equity, and earns the proving margin by executing faster or with more capital. Pass 1 showed this model works (Cloverleaf, Roivant/Telavant, Invenergy/Grain Belt, Pattern/SunZia, Earthrise). But once ML is not the edge, discovery is no longer 80% of the work, so C1 falls to 1-2. Every rung where this model pays at venture scale is already held by a funded developer, so L6 kills it. The best idea is 16/25 before checks (powered-land JV and shelved-drug NewCo, both old) and 15 for new ideas (Alberta powered-land JV, stalled merchant transmission). After checks the best is about 13-14. **The binding law is L6, and the gold frame's own C1 ("discovery is 80%+ of the work") is a close second.** With L3 and R-ML gone, C1 is effectively the last place the ML requirement survives.

## How the pair changes the rubric
- **C2 (step-up):** measured against the hope price or option strike actually paid. Options, JVs and retained-equity NewCos restore it as leverage on the fee or equity cheque, as in sweep_L3.
- **C3 (lever):** credits any discovery lever, including capital, relationships, speed and ML used as a ranking tool. It is still capped at 1/base hit rate, and it still only counts if it lowers the cost of *finding* winners, not of building them.
- **C4 (data and right):** a hope-priced, optioned or JV right counts as buyable (+1 where L3 was the issue). L2 is kept, so the signal must still be absent from any legible field. "Illegible" may now mean private knowledge held through relationships, not only data that ML has to read.
- **C1 is unchanged and becomes the hidden killer.** Without ML reading illegible data, what is left is diligence, permitting, financing and building, so discovery is rarely more than 30-40% of the work.
- **Kept:** L1, L2, L4, L5, L6, no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, $1B+ path.

## 3. Adversarial checks on the top three

### A. Alberta powered-land JV (N1, 15): dies on L4/L1-shaped rationing and L6
- **Demand is real, but the grid is rationed.** Large-load requests passed 16 GW against a peak load of ~12 GW. The AESO set a one-time 1,200 MW interim cap for projects of 75 MW+ already in studies, and it went to **2 of 29** proposed projects after the rest withdrew ([Mondaq/McCarthy](https://www.mccarthy.ca/en/insights/blogs/canadian-energy-perspectives/alberta-faces-a-surge-in-ai-data-centre-power-demand-aeso-responds-with-phased-connection-plan), [calgary.tech](https://calgary.tech/2025/06/05/alberta-caps-data-centre-grid-access-amid-ai-fueled-power-surge/), [Ivey Business Review](https://www.iveybusinessreview.ca/magazine/articles/ai-data-centres)). Phase 2 ("bring your own generation") was still in engagement in Q2 2026 ([AESO](https://aesoengage.aeso.ca/pre-engagement-phase-ii-large-load-integration)). The proof is a regulator's allocation, not discovery (L4, C1).
- **The rung is held.** Beacon AI Centers is majority-owned by Nadia Partners, led by an ex-Blackstone CEO, and plans six campuses, 2,000+ acres, ~C$10B and up to 4.5 GW ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/beacon-ai-canada-artificial-intelligence-data-centres/), [Latitude](https://latitudemedia.com/news/this-data-center-developer-is-betting-on-alberta-to-solve-the-power-backlog/), [ARC](https://www.arcenergyinstitute.com/inside-the-coming-power-surge-beacon-ai-centers-bet-on-alberta/)). Pembina/Kineticor and the O'Leary Wonder Valley project are also active (memory). This search found no Beacon funding round for 2026.
- **Step-up:** land is not the scarce input. With BYOG, the scarce inputs are gas turbines and AESO sign-off, so the land-holder JV captures only a site premium (C2 about 2-3).
- **Verdict: dead.** It is Cloverleaf in a smaller, rationed market that already has a funded local Cloverleaf.

### B. Stalled merchant transmission buyer (N2, 15): dies on C1 and L6
- **The step-up is real.** Invenergy bought Grain Belt Express's development assets from Clean Line in Dec 2018 (terms undisclosed; Missouri PSC approved 5-0 in 2019; sole owner in 2020) ([North American Windpower](https://nawindpower.com/invenergy-to-acquire-clean-lines-grain-belt-express), [Canary](https://www.canarymedia.com/articles/enn/sale-of-grain-belt-express-transmission-project-boosts-hopes-for-its-completion)). The project now carries ~$1.7B of Quanta/Kiewit contracts and an Illinois CPCN upheld 6-0 by the state Supreme Court, and it proceeds on private finance after DOE cancelled the $4.9B guarantee in Jul 2025 ([ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/62416-illinois-supreme-court-give-key-backing-to-11b-grain-belt-express-project), [ABC7](https://abc7chicago.com/amp/post/president-donald-trump-administration-cancels-49b-loan-guarantee-line-deliver-green-power-midwest-eastern-us/17262522/)). Pattern bought SunZia (3 GW, 550 mi HVDC) from SouthWestern Power Group in Jul 2022, terms undisclosed, for an $8B+ combined line and wind build ([Pattern](https://patternenergy.com/pattern-energy-acquires-sunzia-transmission-project/), [NS Energy](https://www.nsenergybusiness.com/news/pattern-energy-acquires-sunzia-transmission/)). Clean Line sold its Plains & Eastern Oklahoma assets to NextEra in Dec 2017, also undisclosed, "to get something concrete back for investors" ([Talk Business](https://talkbusiness.net/2018/03/clean-line-doe-terminate-controversial-2-5-billion-wind-project-across-arkansas/)). Orphaned rights do change hands cheaply relative to the build.
- **Why it dies:**
  - **C1 = 1.** The proving step is a decade of state CPCNs, eminent domain (40+ Missouri petitions) and offtake. That is development, not discovery.
  - **L6:** every buyer was a multi-GW IPP (Invenergy, Pattern, NextEra), and Grid United (founded 2021 by Clean Line's ex-CEO, "patient capital") is developing North Plains Connector with a $700M GRIP award and BHE, NorthWestern and PSE as partners ([RTO Insider](https://www.rtoinsider.com/?p=84745), [Energy Central](https://www.energycentral.com/energy-management/post/berkshire-hathaway-energy-joins-grid-united-advance-32-billion-us-XPHBNLEiJ4v1dGD)).
  - **L2:** project status sits in public dockets.
  - **The pool is a handful of assets a decade, and a seed company cannot carry one.** Too lumpy for a YC company.

### C. Shelved deep-tech hardware NewCo (N3, 12): no evidence the step-up exists
- **Structure exists:** Unilever-to-Own Products and Aventis-to-Accovion are the generic carve-out pattern, with the parent keeping a minority stake ([CFO.com](https://www.cfo.com/news/capitalizing-on-rd/666089/), [Outsourcing-Pharma](https://www.outsourcing-pharma.com/Article/2011/11/24/Forming-CRO-spin-outs-can-help-big-pharma-research)). No 2025-26 energy or hardware example of the Telavant pattern turned up.
- **Best hardware analogue:** Malta spun out of Alphabet X in Dec 2018 with a $26M Series A led by Breakthrough Energy Ventures and later raised ~$50M. In 2025 it was still at its first commercial-scale deployment, with no exit ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2018/12/24/alphabet-spins-off-moonshot-project-malta-with-backing-from-gatess-bev-fund), [Energy-Storage.news](https://energy-storage.news/long-duration-pumped-heat-energy-storage-startup-malta-raises-us50-million)). Unlike a shelved drug, a shelved hardware program has no regulator-defined proof that a buyer pays 100x for. Proof is the full build plus a market (C1 1, C2 about 3).
- **Verdict: dead.** The drug version works because Phase 2 data is a discrete, sellable rung bought by pharma. Hardware has no such rung.

### D. Brief note on N9 (14): defense Phase II to program of record
DoD publishes no standard Phase III or program-of-record transition rate. A 2026 NPS paper says SBIR "repeatedly fails at transition", and only SOCOM's PIA authority reports ~50% ([FAI](https://www.thefai.org/posts/recommendations-for-the-small-business-innovation-research-program), [NPS](https://dair.nps.edu/bitstream/123456789/5549/1/SYM-AM-26-107.pdf), [ExecutiveGov](https://www.executivegov.com/?p=269447)). So L1 holds: the proof usually fails and it is expensive. But the gate is a program office's demand, which relationships cannot manufacture, and Arlington and the defense primes already buy winners (L6). It is not checked further.

## 5. Answers to the brief
1. **Does anything survive?** No. Best before checks: 16 (powered-land JV, shelved-drug NewCo, both old). Best new: 15 (Alberta powered-land JV, stalled merchant transmission). After checks the best is about 13-14.
2. **The law that now kills it:** **L6.** "Hope-priced right + capital/relationship edge" is the Cloverleaf, Roivant, Invenergy and Beacon business model, so every rung where it pays at venture scale is held. Behind it, **C1** kills anything L6 misses: once ML is not the reason the pain is solvable, the proving step becomes the work.
3. **Most interesting finding:** relaxing R-ML does not just loosen C3; it hollows out C1. The gold frame (discovery is 80%, the physical step is trivial) only fits businesses whose edge is reading something others cannot. Remove that, and every survivor turns into a developer whose edge is the cheque. That makes "L3 + R-ML + L6" the natural triple for pass 3: the Alberta JV and powered land would then be judged on capital access alone, which is not an edge a pre-seed YC founder has.
4. **For a combination test, if Dhruv wants one:** the only structure with a proven 100x step-up is the retained-equity NewCo (Telavant: about $45M into about $5.2B net). A pass-3 triple that also drops L6 should look for a *non-drug* domain with a Phase-2-like discrete, sellable proof. Transmission CPCN and AESO allocation are the nearest candidates in energy, and both fail C1 here.
