# Pair sweep: drop L1 (the proof is the bespoke build / cheap proofs pass) and L6 (rung one is held) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict

**Nothing survives as a venture-scale company Dhruv could own that also meets R-ML and the remaining laws.** Dropping L1 and L6 together opens exactly one new shape: **ML discovers a new molecule or material, and the company owns the expensive build that proves it**, such as scale-up, qualification or registration, even on a rung that Orbital, Osmo, SES AI or Enko already work. That shape clears L2-L5 more easily than any earlier frame, because an invented composition has no seller pricing hope (L3), no regulator recording the outcome (L2) and is patentable (L5). It then dies on what L1 had been standing in for. In two of the three checks ML is not the bottleneck: refrigerant chemical space is small and was already enumerated by NIST, and electrolyte additives are a ~$1.5B commodity pool. In the third, crop-protection actives (best score, 17 before and 13 after the check), the build costs $307M and takes 11.4 years per active ingredient, which is pharma, not a YC company. The most telling evidence is that the funded holders of these rungs are moving off the molecule and onto the system. Orbital now sells modular data centres built with SLB, and SES AI has formed a toll-manufacturing JV. A discovered molecule on its own does not make a company.

## How the pair changes scoring

- **L1 dropped:** C1 no longer requires "the physical step is trivial". A rung counts if a capitalised buyer pays for a discrete output, even when the proof is scale-up, qualification or registration. C3 now credits ML for cutting the cost per *qualified* product, including failed builds, and a throughput lever on cheap proofs that usually pass also counts.
- **L6 dropped:** C5 no longer penalises an occupied rung. A funded startup or PE roll-up only lowers C5 if it blocks the ladder above.
- **Kept:** L2, L3, L4 and L5, R-ML (ML must make the pain solvable), no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, and the $1B bar.

## 3. Adversarial checks on the top 3

### Check 1: N1, ML-found new-mode-of-action crop actives (17 to 13)

- **The build is pharma-sized.** AgbioInvestor (May 2026) puts the cost of discovering, developing and registering one new conventional active at **$307M, over an average 11.4 years**. Discovery alone was about $133M in 2020-23, and roughly 140,000 compounds are screened per registered active ([FertilizerDaily](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260529-crop-protection-rd-costs-climb-to-307m-as-regulatory-burden-lengthens-development-timelines/), [SeedToday](https://www.seedtoday.com/article/1142519/crop-protection-product-development-averages-11-4-years-study-finds), [AgriBusiness Global, 2024 edition $301M](https://www.agribusinessglobal.com/agrochemicals/agbioinvestor-publishes-cost-of-new-agrochemical-product-discovery-development-and-registration-study-results)). With L1 dropped this is allowed, but no seed-to-Series-B company can carry it.
- **The step-up is real.** FMC's tetflupyrolimet (Dodhylex) is the first new herbicide mode of action in 30+ years. Reported peak estimates are $300-400M a year, and an analyst on a 2026 call cited a ramp to $1B by 2030. That figure is unverified, and the sources conflict ([echemi](https://www.echemi.com/cms/2361099.html), [FMC](https://www.fmc.com/en/articles/fmc-corporation-announces-dodhylextm-active-global-trademark-novel-grass-herbicide), [FMC call](https://financialfilings.com/filings/fmc-corp/call-transcript/2026/48468414/)). A proven new mode of action is worth $1B+ against near-zero for a hit compound, so C2 = 5 holds.
- **Occupants are funded but not winning fast.** Enko has raised ~$150M since 2022 (Nufarm-led Series C), changed CEO in March 2025 to a Corteva licensing executive, and in May 2025 reported field trials that beat standards, with no registration yet ([Global AgInvesting](https://globalaginvesting.com/enko-expands-series-c-to-80m/), [BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250303332434/en/AgTech-Innovator-Enko-Chem-Announces-CEO-Transition), [AgTechNavigator](https://www.agtechnavigator.com/Article/2025/05/21/enko-chem-reports-breakthrough-field-trial-success-for-next-generation-graminicide-portfolio)). Moa has screened 900k+ compounds and found 80+ novel mode-of-action areas, but raised only **£22.2M** in its Series C (Jul 2026). Its partners (Corteva, Nufarm, Gowan, Certis Belchim) disclose no deal terms ([Pulse 2](https://pulse2.com/moa-technology-raises-22-2-million-series-c-to-advance-next-generation-herbicides/)). Small late rounds and undisclosed partner economics suggest the majors pay little before registration.
- **R-ML:** ML helps choose which compounds to make, but most of the cost is development, tox and environmental fate, and field efficacy. ML predicts these only partly. Enko is 8+ years in with DEL plus ML and has no product, so the 10x capex cut has not been shown.
- **Rescore:** C1 2 (no rung sells cheaply before field proof, and terms are undisclosed), C2 5, C3 3, C4 1 (the "right" is a filing on computed chemistry, with no illegible public data and no cheap owner to buy from), C5 2 (the buyers are five majors, a monopsony) = **13**. It also falls outside Dhruv's energy and deeptech edge, and it is pharma-shaped, not a YC company.

### Check 2: N2, non-fluorinated low-GWP refrigerant (16 to 9)

- **The search space is already enumerated.** NIST (McLinden et al., 2014-17) screened about **56,000** small molecules (15 atoms or fewer), cut them to about 1,200 on GWP, flammability, stability, toxicity and critical temperature, and ended with a handful of candidates, all with trade-offs. They concluded the A1 (non-flammable, low-toxicity) requirements may need relaxing ([STLE](https://www.stle.org/files/TLTArchives/2017/06_June/Tech_Beat_I.aspx), [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/publications/search/topic/248561?page=9)). There is no hidden region for ML to find. This is close to an L2 kill: the outcome is already published.
- **The demand shock is fading.** A study commissioned by the European Parliament's ITRE committee (Apr 2026) recommends taking fluorinated refrigerants, including R-1234yf, out of the universal PFAS restriction, regulating them under the F-gas Regulation, and says substitution is not currently feasible ([VASA](https://vasa.org.au/europe-makes-progress-on-conflicting-pfas-ban-and-f-gas-phase-out-regulations/), [IIR](https://iifiir.org/en/news/reach-regulation-the-restrictions-proposed-by-five-european-states-concern-many-alternative-refrigerants)). The non-fluorinated alternatives that do exist are old: propane, CO2, ammonia and isobutane.
- **No startup was found** working on this rung, which fits a space that is closed by physics, not one that is under-explored.
- **Rescore:** C1 2, C2 2, C3 1, C4 1, C5 3 = **9**.

### Check 3: N3 / old DropIn, ML electrolyte additives with toll manufacture (13 to 10)

- **SES AI already runs exactly this frame.** In Oct 2025 it signed a term sheet for a 90%-owned JV with Hisun New Energy Materials, a Texas electrolyte toll manufacturer, to supply four Molecular Universe-discovered electrolytes and stay capex-light. Its 2025 revenue was $21M, of which $13.6M was one-time services, and about six customers have passed phase-2 testing ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251014634801/en/SES-AI-Signs-Term-Sheet-to-Establish-Joint-Venture-to-Commercially-Supply-Materials-Discovered-by-Molecular-Universe), [Yahoo/Q4 call](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ses-ai-q4-earnings-call-014828059.html)). No 2026 confirmation that the JV closed was found.
- **The pool is a commodity.** The whole battery electrolyte additive market is about **$1.5B** (2024). VC trades at about 220-235k yuan/t (~$30/kg), down from 475k at the 2021 peak, and the largest supplier has a 15.2% share ([GII/Grand View](https://giikorea.co.kr/report/grvi1869821-battery-electrolyte-additives-market-size-share.html), [China Galaxy via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/13999982742455296), [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3914909117011846), [HKEX prospectus](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/app/sehk/2026/108444/a132061/sehk26041601978.pdf)). A novel additive earns a premium only during a 2-3 year qualification at a cell maker that keeps the process know-how.
- **Rescore:** C1 2, C2 2, C3 2, C4 2, C5 2 = **10**. The pool is too small and the ML is sold as SaaS (Molecular Universe), which brings back pass-1's proposed L7 (a throughput edge is not ownable).

### Side note: the coolant holder pivoted

Orbital Industries' $50M Series B (Plural, NVentures, mid-2026) funds **modular data centres built with SLB** and a multi-year AWS partnership. The PFAS-free two-phase fluid is now one component, and no coolant customer or plant has been announced ([IT Brief](https://itbrief.co.uk/story/orbital-raises-usd-50-million-to-scale-data-centres), [DataCentreNews](https://datacentrenews.uk/story/orbital-industries-raises-usd-50-million-in-series-b)). The best-funded team on the best old idea in this family decided the molecule was not a company.

## 5. Answer to the brief

1. **Does anything survive?** No. The best score before checks was 17 (crop actives) and after checks 13. The best old idea is the PFAS-free coolant at 15, unchecked again here because its holder has pivoted away from it.
2. **What kills it now:** **R-ML first**, with the $1B bar and pharma-scale time second. Once the build can be the proof, the value moves to the build, and ML only does the cheap part.
3. **The closest call, for completeness:** *an ML-first new-mode-of-action herbicide company.* Why now: resistant weeds, a 30-year gap in new modes of action, and the first new one (Group 28, Dodhylex) launching in 2026 with a possible $1B ramp. Rung one is a field-validated new-MoA lead licensed to one of five majors. The step-up runs from near zero to $300M-1B+ a year of peak sales, but $307M and 11.4 years sit in between. A feasibility test would be a retrospective benchmark: train on pre-2010 public herbicide SAR and HRAC data, and test whether the model ranks the post-2010 new-MoA leads (tetflupyrolimet, cinmethylin's revival, bixlozone) in its top 0.1% of an enumerated library at 10x the hit rate of the majors' screens. Even if it passes, Enko and Moa show that the 10x discovery saving does not shorten the 11-year build, so this is not a YC-shaped company.
4. **The most interesting finding:** dropping L1 and L6 together gives the first frame with a truly tiny base rate and a truly huge step-up (crop actives: 1 in 140,000, from near zero to $1B), which is Terranox's shape. But the proof is the most expensive part, not the cheapest. The funded holders' moves point the same way: Orbital went from coolant to modular DCs, SES AI from a discovery platform to a toll JV, and Enko hired a licensing CEO. All three moved off the molecule and toward the system or the channel. L1 was right in spirit: when nature hides a thing that you make rather than find, the build is the moat, and ML does not own the build.
