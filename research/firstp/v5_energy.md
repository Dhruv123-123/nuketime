# V5 energy sweep: AI as the lever for $10B-ceiling energy companies (19 Oct 2026)

Frame: `V5_OPTIMIST.md` (tech optimism; the numbers and the clock decide; every vertical killer gets an AI counter-move with a stated effect; prefer $10B+ ceilings). Scored N/T/C/A/D, 1-5 each, pass at 19+ with no leg below 3. Dead ideas from `VERDICT.md` (powered-land JV, closed-plant sites, flex micro-sites, PSD netting, FTR book, recertified transformers) are only re-entered where a new AI counter-move or a new market fact changes the numbers, and that is said in the row.

**Result: 1 pass (20/25), 4 at 18, the rest 13-17.** The pass is a distributed capacity utility in PJM and MISO: it owns batteries at commercial and industrial (C&I) sites and sells accredited capacity to data centres under long-term contracts. It works because of three 2025-26 market facts, not because of the AI: PJM capacity prices are fixed inside a $175-325/MW-day band through May 2030, PJM is short about 6.6-6.8 GW, and data centres may now buy capacity from behind-the-meter resources bilaterally. The AI's job is to cut the soft costs that killed the last C&I storage wave (Stem, Advanced Microgrid Solutions). The near-misses all fail on D (defensibility), because a capitalised player already holds the rung: Base Power, VoltaGrid/Crusoe, Intersect/Google, Hunt.

## 1-page model: #1 Distributed capacity utility, PJM then MISO (20/25)

**One line.** We own batteries at C&I hosts (factories, cold storage, hospitals, big-box retail; ≥1 MW peak load), and the host pays nothing. We register the fleet as PJM capacity (DR now, DER aggregation when Order 2222 lands) and sell that capacity forward to data-centre LSEs under 5-10-yr bilateral contracts, which the Reliability Backstop Procurement is opening up. Hosts get a share of demand-charge savings and backup power. We own the asset, the site lease and the capacity contract. This is not a service shop.

**Why now (all sourced).** PJM cleared at the cap twice ($329, $333/MW-day) and is about 6.6-6.8 GW short. The collar ($175 floor, ~$325 cap) runs through May 2030, so the first 4 years of capacity revenue have a known floor, which lenders can bank. PJM's 15 GW backstop Phase I would let BTM and distribution batteries contract directly with data centres. Google, three states, Constellation and Vistra back BTM eligibility. BTM batteries avoid the >5-year transmission queue because they use the host's existing service. US turnkey BESS is $219/kWh and falling (global $117).

**Market.** PJM capacity spend is $16.4B/yr at the cap. DR is 7.3 GW (~5% of cleared capacity). MISO adds ~$120/MW-day annualised, with summer zones at $384-424. NYISO and ISO-NE come later. Target: 3-4 GW of owned BTM capacity in 10 years, ≈ 50% growth of the current PJM DR pool, aimed at the shortfall.

**Unit economics (per 1 MW / 4 MWh site, host peak ≥1.5 MW).**

The AI effect is the whole margin of safety. At the capacity floor, the incumbent cost stack falls below its cost of capital and the AI stack does not. That gap explains why Stem/AMS-era fleets failed and why this one can scale at the floor.

**Capital path.**
- Seed: $4-6M. AI origination + design engine; 10 sites (~8 MW) through partner EPCs, funded by a $10M tax-credit-transfer + mezz warehouse.
- Series A: $30-40M (yr 2). 100-150 MW. $120M construction/term debt. First 5-10-yr DC capacity contract (the backstop bilateral window).
- Series B: $100-150M (yr 3-4). 400-600 MW. $400-500M project finance + tax-credit transfer. First ABS once 24 months of telemetry exist.
- Comparables for raisability: Base Power ($1B Series C at ~$3-4B; ~$12-13B talks), VoltaGrid ($1B from Blackstone), Hunt Energy Network (Manulife-backed, 420 MW of ~10 MW sites in 5 yrs).
- Total equity to $100M revenue: ~$180-220M. Total project capital: ~$550M net.

**Timeline to $100M revenue.**
- Yr 1: 8-20 MW live; host savings revenue from day 1; DR registration for the next delivery year (PJM DR can enter via CSP or incremental auctions).
- Yr 3: ~150 MW, ~$25M.
- Yr 5-6: ~600 MW across ~700-900 sites, ~$100M revenue, asset base ~$0.8B.
- Yr 8-10: 2-3 GW across PJM, MISO and NY; $350-500M revenue; adds a retail-supply book for hosts and the #4 curtailable-campus product.
- Base Power's ramp (1.5k→7k homes in 6 months; 500 MWh in ~2 yrs) shows the deployment pace is achievable with VC + debt.

**Ceiling.** At 3 GW × ~$165k, revenue is ~$500M/yr of long-contracted cash flow plus the platform (origination engine, host network, DC capacity desk). Contracted distributed-energy owners trade at 12-15x EBITDA, so a $5-8B outcome is realistic. $10B+ needs MISO/NY/NE plus retail supply, i.e. a Base/Octopus-like gentailer for C&I. C=4.

**Competitors.**
- Voltus: aggregator, no owned assets; Google bilateral capacity deal in PJM.
- CPower: Constellation-owned DR aggregator.
- Calibrant Energy and Convergent Energy & Power: own C&I/BTM storage; they commissioned the BTM-in-backstop deck, so they are the closest direct rivals. Their funding was not checked here.
- Enchanted Rock: gas microgrids.
- Base Power, and Sunrun/Tesla/Renew Home: residential, selling capacity to DCs.
- Stem: pivoted to software.
- Edge: own the assets (vs Voltus/CPower), and a lower soft-cost stack plus DC contract desk (vs Calibrant/Convergent). D=4 rests on 12-15-yr host site leases (sticky, scarce ≥1 MW hosts), fleet performance data that lowers financing cost, and contracted DC offtake. Incumbent aggregators would need a balance sheet; incumbent owners would need to rebuild their origination.

**Kill risks (ranked).**
1. Capacity prices after May 2030. The collar expires. New gas (EIT 10/yr), the 15 GW backstop and DR growth could push prices toward or below the $175 floor. Mitigation: 10-yr DC bilaterals signed before 2030. Kill test: no DC bilateral signed at ≥$200/MW-day within 18 months.
2. PJM DR rules. ELCC falls from 92%, stricter penalties, or BTM is excluded from the backstop (the IMM, Buckeye and Invenergy oppose it). The upside case, DER aggregation under Order 2222, keeps slipping.
3. FEOC cell costs. Non-Chinese cells could add 30-60% to hardware. At $400/kWh all-in, net capex rises ~$0.3M/MW and AI-stack IRR drops to ~10%.
4. Host and site risk: building sales, fire codes (NFPA 855, FDNY-style AHJs), utility non-export interconnection delays.
5. Competition compresses host shares, e.g. Base-style pricing at $95 per unit moving into C&I.

**Cheapest test (~$40-80k, 8-10 weeks).**
- Build the AI origination model on public data (EIA-861 tariffs, PJM zonal PLCs, CBECS building types, county parcel data) for one zone (ComEd or PECO).
- Generate 300 auto-proposals and send them through 2 brokers.
- Kill if fewer than 10 signed LOIs, or if measured soft cost per MW stays above $180k.
- Run in parallel: ask 3 DC developers or LSEs for indicative bilateral capacity pricing for 2028-2033.
