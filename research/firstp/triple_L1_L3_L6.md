# Triple sweep L1 + L3 + L6, focused on orphaned assets outside drugs (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
**Nothing outside drugs survives.** The orphaned-asset inversion is real in hardware: owners do dump the hope. Examples:
- Half of Petra Nova, a $1B carbon-capture plant, sold for $3.6M.
- GE-Hitachi's 76% of laser enrichment (GLE) went for $20M, deferred until GLE earns revenue. Silex is now worth about A$1.1B.
- Iridium, built for about $5B, sold for $25M.
- Volocopter's assets sold for €10M against a €42M book value.
- Britishvolt sold for £8.5M; its site then went to Blackstone for up to £110M.

So dropping L1, L3 and L6 does unlock prices of 10^2-10^3x below the completed value. Two things still kill every non-drug idea:

1. **Insiders capture the orphan. This is proposed as law L9.** In 9 of the 12 hardware estates checked, the buyer was already inside the deal:
   - a secured creditor or lender (Ambri, Li-Cycle/Glencore, Volta/Luxor);
   - a JV partner or licensor (Petra Nova/JX, GLE/Silex and Cameco);
   - the IP owner keeping control (mPower/BWXT keeps ownership, manufacturing and royalties);
   - a strategic or competitor (Lilium IP to Archer in a bidding war, Volocopter to Wanfeng, Nikola's plant to Lucid).

   Drug licensors are pivoting biotechs with no secured lender, so an outsider can buy. Hardware estates carry debt and insiders who also hold the data.
2. **R-ML: pooling does not create an ML edge in hardware.** It works for drugs because every package shares one unit (the patient) and one schema (endpoint and covariates). Hardware packages are n-of-1 designs with test regimes that differ from estate to estate. Voltaiq's own study found that a widely cited battery-lifetime model "is unable to extrapolate to different datasets", with severe overfitting ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2022.1059154/full)). There are about 10 orphaned reactor designs, which is too few to pool. Where the value is real, it comes from capital, a grid connection or a licence, not from a model.

The best non-drug idea is **N6, bankrupt-estate grid-site capture, at 16/25**. It is powered land in disguise and dies on L2 (WARN notices, court dockets and grid maps are legible; Blackstone saw Cambois) and on R-ML.

**Side finding:** inside this triple the **failed-Phase-3 pooled-data drug rescue rises to about 20/25**. L1 was its C1 penalty, and L3 and L6 were already soft. Only R-ML is left, and that can be tested for under $100k (see section 5). It remains off-sector.

## How the triple changes the rubric
- **L1 dropped:** an expensive bespoke proof (licensing, restart, recertification, a trial, FOAK) no longer costs C1 points. A cheap proof that passes is also allowed.
- **L3 dropped:** a right bought at the seller's hope price, through an option or JV, or with retained equity counts as buyable. C2 is measured against the price actually paid.
- **L6 dropped:** an occupied rung (Applied Atomics, Lyten, Biossil, Cloverleaf, Applied Intuition) costs no C5 points.
- **Kept:** L2, L4, L5, R-ML, the ban on prospecting and drilling, own the asset, not Terranox and venture scale. Proposed L7 (vendor ML) and L8 (end buyer funds the proof) are noted where they bite.

## 3. Combination factors
- **Laws that killed most often:**
  - R-ML hit 15 of 17 new ideas.
  - L9, insiders capture the orphan, hit 7.
  - L2 hit 4. L4 hit 3.
  - L8 (the end buyer buys directly) and L7 (vendor ML) hit 2 each.

| Combination | Effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Orphan price (L3 inverted) x pooled data (ML) | Works only when packages share a unit and a schema. Drugs have patients and endpoints. Hardware has n-of-1 designs | Adds +2 in drugs, 0 in hardware |
| Orphan price x grid connection | The highest real step-up outside drugs (Cambois 13-27x) | The value is legible powered land, so L2 |
| Orphan price x licence or regulatory history (NRC, FERC, FAA) | The licence carries the value | The history is public (ADAMS, eLibrary), and the owner keeps or recycles the design |
| Orphan price x outsider access | Needed for any edge | Hardware estates go to creditors and partners (L9). Drugs do not |
| Relaxing R-ML on top of the triple | A pure capital roll-up of orphans | Fundable as a PE strategy, but ML is not the edge, so it fails the brief |

## 4. Adversarial checks on the top 3

### A. N6: grid-site capture from bankrupt estates (16)
- Britishvolt bought the Cambois site for £4M, and the council kept a £4M buyback option. Recharge bought the estate for £8.5M. Blackstone/QTS then agreed about £20M upfront plus phased payments of up to £110M once planning and power were secured, and won consent for 720 MW ([IREI](https://irei.com/news/blackstones-qts-to-buy-britishvolt-site-for-new-data-center/), [Prolific North](https://www.prolificnorth.co.uk/?p=74230), [DCD](https://datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/blackstone-gets-green-light-for-10bn-qts-data-center-in-northumberland-uk)).
  - The step-up is real, but the outsider who bought the estate (Recharge) failed. The site owner with the grid connection was the one who captured the value.
- Nikola's Coolidge plant went to Lucid for $30M after several auction rounds ([TT News](https://www.ttnews.com/articles/lucid-nikola-arizona-plant)). Li-Cycle Rochester got a $40M Glencore stalking horse and closed as a credit bid ([ESG Dive](https://www.esgdive.com/news/glencore-completes-takeover-of-li-cycle-battery-recycling-assets/757412/)).
- **Kill:** every case is public in court dockets and WARN notices, and the bidders were strategics or creditors. The edge is reading grid headroom, which the pass-1 backtest already showed a lookup table matches (L2). ML is not needed (R-ML).

### B. N1: orphaned SMR designs with NRC history (14)
- mPower cost about $500M, including $111M from DOE, and was shelved in 2014 and ended in 2017. Applied Atomics (ex-SpaceX founders; about $12M raised, with an $8.3M seed in Mar 2026) took an exclusive land-based licence on 17 Jun 2026. BWXT **keeps the IP, the manufacturing rights and the royalties** ([GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/17/3313477/0/en/applied-atomics-secures-exclusive-land-based-license-for-mpower-small-modular-reactor-technology-from-bwx-technologies.html), [funding](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/25/3262324/0/en/Applied-Atomics-Raises-8-Million-to-Advance-Full-Stack-Nuclear-Power-Plant-Deployment.html)).
- mPower never filed a design certification; it reached pre-application only ([Watkins summary of the NRC archive](https://www.jaredwatkins.com/research/energy/nuclear/applied-atomics/)). The "review history" is thinner than its reputation.
- NuScale spent over $500M and 2M labour hours on its DCA, and the NRC spent 115,000 hours on Phase 1 ([Power Eng](https://www.power-eng.com/nuclear/smrs/nuscale-smr-becomes-first-to-complete-nrc-phase-one-review/)). The orphaned package saves part of that $500M, and the FOAK build is still everything.
- Westinghouse shelved its 225 MW SMR in 2014, revived it in 2015 and turned it into AP300 ([Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2015/03/21/westinghouse-revives-smr-efforts/)). Owners recycle their orphans.
- Atomic Canyon has trained on all 52M pages of ADAMS and has Nvidia backing ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/03/12/artificial-intelligence-nuclear-power), [Neutron Bytes](https://neutronbytes.com/2026/08/20/atomic-canyon-launches-ai-virtual-assistant-for-nuclear-reactor-operations/)). The case against pooled data:
- **R-ML (pooling):** Voltaiq tested the widely cited early-cycle lifetime model on open data and found it "unable to extrapolate to different datasets", with severe overfitting ([Frontiers 2022](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fenrg.2022.1059154/full)). - **What the orphaned package costs:** salvage to tens of millions of dollars.
- **What the proven version is worth:** a qualified cell line. That costs billions and is a build, not a discovery.

**Kill:** L9, R-ML and the build.

## 5. Answer to the brief
1. **Survivor outside drugs? No.** The best is N6 at 16, and it dies on L2 and R-ML. N1, N2 and N10 reach 14.
2. **Does the orphaned-asset inversion exist outside drugs?** On price, yes, strongly: 10^2-10^3x below the completed value (Petra Nova, GLE, Iridium, Volocopter). On access, no. 9 of the 12 hardware estates checked went to creditors, partners, licensors or strategics. Proposed **L9: orphaned hardware is captured by insiders who hold the security interest and the data.**
3. **The R-ML answer:** pooling gives an ML edge only when many packages share one unit of observation and one outcome schema. Failed drug trials do (patients and endpoints). Hardware orphans do not:
   - reactors: about 10 designs;
   - batteries: models fail across datasets;
   - eVTOL and satellites: n of a few.
4. **What the triple does to the drug idea:** the failed-Phase-3 pooled-data rescue reaches about 20/25, and **R-ML is now its only killer**. The feasibility test still costs under $100k:
   - Take drugs with two independent RCTs in Vivli, YODA or Project Data Sphere.
   - Train on trial 1 and pre-register the subgroups.
   - Test the interaction in trial 2. The bar is at least 30% of predictions replicating, against Wallach's 0 of 5.

   If it passes, it is the only live company from three passes. It is off-sector, and Biossil is about 18 months and $150M+ ahead (L6, dropped here).
5. **Next pass:** treat L9 and R-ML-pooling as laws. Any remaining non-drug idea needs an orphaned asset that outsiders can buy (no secured creditor) **and** a shared outcome schema across many packages. Candidates worth one look: wind-farm SCADA from distressed IPPs (shared turbine schema; L7 risk) and solar-module field-failure data.
