# Adversarial deep check: ML crop actives (new-MoA composition-of-matter, license, then own registrations) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
**The idea scores 15/25 after the check, down from 18. It fails v4 by 2 points. It is real science, but the economics of the first rung are thin, and the climb to owning registrations is pharma-sized.**

Score: **S1 4, S2 3, S3 3, S4 2, S5 3 = 15/25.** No leg falls to 0-1, but the idea misses the 17 pass line.

The prize holds up. Icafolin has an expected peak of about €750M, and FMC's new actives earned about $200M in 2025, against guidance of $300-400M for 2026. Resistance keeps rising: 548 unique cases, and 21 of 31 sites of action are already compromised. Four findings pull the score down:

1. **A licensor captures little at the greenhouse or lead stage, and this is now measured.** AgPlenus holds herbicide licences from both Bayer (2024: upfront, research funding, milestones, royalty) and Corteva (2020). Evogene's whole Agriculture segment booked only **$1.1M in 2023, $3.0M in 2024 and $1.4M in 2025**. Evogene had $13M of cash at end-2025 and a going-concern warning, and in 2025 it cut AgPlenus staff by 40% ([Evogene 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001574565/000117891326001767/zk2634882.htm); [Q2 2025 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1574565/000117891325002976/exhibit_99-1.htm)). The large money arrives only near launch. Corteva paid FMC a **$200M prepurchase** for co-exclusive rights to rimisoxafen, and FMC, the owner of the molecule, kept ownership and supply ([Indian Chemical News, Jun 2026](https://www.indianchemicalnews.com/chemical/lite/fmc-and-corteva-strike-herbicide-deal-to-expand-access-to-next-gen-weed-control-tech-in-americas-30834)). The value sits with whoever carries the asset through field trials and registration.
2. **The ML lever is plausible but data-starved, and the closest analogue is struggling.**
   - Public herbicide SAR is small: cropCSM trained on about 4,500 compounds ([cropCSM](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9155605/)).
   - Only about six new herbicide MoAs have appeared since 2010.
   - The majors hold decades of private greenhouse data.
   - Recursion, the flagship ML-native discovery platform, trades near $3 after pipeline cuts and a 20% layoff ([Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/recursion-lays-20-staff-wake-pipeline-cutbacks); [TipRanks Q1 2026](https://www.tipranks.com/news/company-announcements/recursion-highlights-q1-2026-results-and-pipeline-progress)).
3. **The funded leaders are 5-8 years ahead, and none has shown a lift or a licensing windfall.**
   - Moa: 830k+ compounds screened, 80+ MoA areas and four partners, but its Series C was only £22.2M (Jul 2026).
   - Enko: a Syngenta fungicide entered field testing in Dec 2025, and the company has raised nothing new since 2022.
   - Agtech VC deal count collapsed in H1 2026.
4. **The climb to owning registrations costs $307M and 11.4 years per active.** Even a company that owns a blockbuster is valued below $1B in Japan. Kumiai's pyroxasulfone sells about ¥75bn a year, yet Kumiai's market cap is about ¥90bn (~$0.6B) ([stockanalysis](https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tyo/4996/statistics/); [JM Financial note](https://bsmedia.business-standard.com/_media/bs/data/market-reports/equity-brokertips/2025-12/17657901860.10081900.pdf)).

**What would revive it:**
- A hindcast (Section 6) showing at least 3x top-1% enrichment over a same-patent-family similarity baseline.
- Then a wet screen of 100 ML-picked purchasable compounds with a hit rate at least 3x a random-diverse control.
- Both pass → S3 back to 4 and S5 back to 4 → 17, a pass, but still with the size and time caveat. Our prior that both pass: about 20-25%.

**Read-across.** Ag deals almost never disclose terms, and we found no published ag royalty rate. We judge that pharma preclinical norms of low-to-mid single digits, with tiers, are the right prior. The one hard number for an ML licensor (AgPlenus) is about $1-3M a year in total. The old assumption of "milestones plus royalty" is right in form, but before field data it is worth roughly a research grant.

## 2. How often a greenhouse hit becomes a registered active
- **Industry base rate: about 1 in 140,000-160,000 compounds evaluated per launched product.** Kumiai claims about 1 in 7,500 for itself ([Kumiai corporate PDF](https://www.kumiai-chem.co.jp/english/company/pdf/EN_kumika_corp_CC2022.pdf); [FertilizerDaily on AgbioInvestor 2026](https://www.fertilizerdaily.com/20260529-crop-protection-rd-costs-climb-to-307m-as-regulatory-burden-lengthens-development-timelines/)).
- **About 12 years from development candidate to market** (Sumitomo); Corteva says 10-12 years ([Sumitomo](https://www.sumitomo.gr.jp/english/act/visits/35/); [Corteva pipeline](https://www.corteva.com/us/Resources/crop-protection/rice/product-pipeline.html)).
- **$307M per active**, of which $133M is discovery, mostly research chemistry ([AgriBusiness Global](https://www.agribusinessglobal.com/agrochemicals/innovation-delayed-is-progress-denied-new-study-reveals-307m-cost-and-11-year-path-to-crop-protection-innovation/)).
- **No published stage-by-stage probability exists.** Our estimate from the ratios above: a confirmed greenhouse hit (active at field-relevant rates, with crop selectivity) reaches registration perhaps **1-3% of the time**, and a field-validated development candidate perhaps **15-30%**. Most attrition after the greenhouse is from toxicology, environmental fate, crop safety and cost of goods. These are the screens where ML property models help least on novel scaffolds.
- **Implication.** Licensing at the greenhouse stage sells a lottery ticket with a 1-3% chance of paying out, so its price is about $1M. Licensing at the field-validated candidate stage sells a ticket with a 15-30% chance, priced at roughly $10-50M plus royalty (our judgement). The step-up comes from field data, which a two-person team cannot generate.

## 3. Prize size and the resistance tailwind
- **Icafolin (Bayer):** the first new broadacre post-emergent MoA in about 30 years. Expected peak is about **€750M**. Filed in the EU, US, Brazil and Canada (EPA dockets 2026) and Australia (Aug 2026). **Launch from 2028**, with no approval yet anywhere ([AgTechNavigator](https://www.agtechnavigator.com/Article/2025/08/04/bayer-unveils-first-new-herbicide-mode-of-action-in-over-30-years); [Bayer AU](https://www.crop.bayer.com.au/news-events/news/2026/bayer-submits-application-for-approval-of-icafolin-methyl-in-australia)).
- **Tetflupyrolimet (FMC, HRAC Group 28, DHODH):** emergency registrations only in 2025, so 2025 sales were "modest". FMC's new actives together (fluindapyr, Isoflex, Dodhylex) made about **$200M in 2025, against $250M expected**, with 2026 guidance of $300-400M. A call transcript cites about $1B by 2030 for the group, not for Dodhylex alone ([FMC 4Q25](https://s21.q4cdn.com/968238644/files/doc_financials/2025/q4/4Q25-Earnings-Release-vF.pdf); [FMC 10-Q Q1 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000037785/000003778526000096/fmc-20260331.htm)). The echemi "$3B breakthrough" headline is not supported.
- **The licensee pool is shrinking.**
  - FMC posted a 2025 GAAP loss of $2.24B, has opened a sale process, and plans $1B of debt paydown through asset sales and **licensing deals**.
  - FMC is now an out-licensor looking for cash, not a buyer of early leads.
  - That leaves Bayer, Syngenta, Corteva and BASF, plus Nufarm, Gowan and Japanese mid-caps who pay small sums. This is a monopsony.
- **The resistance tailwind is real and steady.** As of Jun 2026 there are 548 unique resistance cases in 275 species, and **21 of the 31 known sites of action** have resistance ([weedscience.org](https://www.weedscience.org/Home.aspx)). It builds demand, but it does not raise what a lead licensor gets paid. It raises the value of field-proven assets.
- **Size:** the herbicide market is about $40B. A new MoA at peak is worth $300M-1B a year. A licensor's 5% royalty on a €750M peak is about $35-40M a year, more than 10 years out.

## 5. ML plausibility (judgement) and the smallest $1B+ ladder
**The case for (keeps S3 at 3, not lower):**
- **Strong chemistry priors.** Agrochemical-likeness rules (Tice/Hao-style physchem windows) are tight. Uptake, phloem mobility and soil fate follow physics. Toxicology and environmental-fate predictors are mature enough to triage.
- **Public data exists.** HRAC and FRAC MoA tables, PPDB (about 2,000+ actives with properties), the patent record (SureChEMBL exemplified compounds with "herbicidal" claims) and cropCSM's 4,500 labelled compounds. Open MoA classifiers already exist (naive Bayes, SVM, XGBoost; [IRB](https://data.fulir.irb.hr/islandora/object/irb:141); [Cambridge MoA prediction](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/handle/1810/261929)).
- **Prospectivity analogy.** Ranking a huge enumerated library for weak multi-signal activity is virtual screening, where 5-50x enrichment over random is routine in pharma.

**The case against (why not 4):**
- **The target is out of distribution.** A *new* MoA is by definition unlike past labels, so similarity-to-known-actives models will tend to rediscover old MoAs. The real lift has to come from phenotypic data (Moa) or target biology (AgPlenus), and both need wet labs and years.
- **The positive set is tiny.** Only about 6 new herbicide MoAs since 2010 (tetflupyrolimet G28, cyclopyrimorate G33, cinmethylin, aclonifen reclassified, icafolin, possibly rimisoxafen), and about 6-8 fungicide new-target actives (fenpicoxamid, florylpicoxamid, metyltetraprole, ipflufenoquin, fluoxapiprolin, aminopyrifen). That is too few labels to validate "finds new MoAs". It is enough only to validate "finds herbicidal chemistry".
- **Analogues cut both ways.**
  - Jayatunga et al. (Drug Discov Today 2024): AI-discovered drugs pass Phase I 80-90% of the time against a 40-65% norm, but Phase II at about 40% is **no better than the norm** ([HT World](https://www.htworld.co.uk/news/ai/ai-discovered-drugs-achieved-higher-success-rate-than-those-by-humans-study/)). ML helps with properties and safety, and helps less with whether the molecule works in the real setting.
  - Recursion and Insitro-style platforms have not yet turned that into value.
  - In ag, the "works in the field" step is the field trial.

**Smallest ladder to $1B+ (judgement, each rung with its gate):**
1. **Hindcast plus wet screen** ($50-100k, 6 months). Gate: at least 3x enrichment. See Section 6.
2. **3-5 greenhouse-confirmed scaffolds with CoM filed** (~$3-5M, 2 years; contract synthesis at about $1-3k per compound, contract greenhouse screening). Exit: option deals at about $0.5-2M each (the AgPlenus level). This rung does not pay for itself.
3. **Own one asset through multi-site field trials and a 90-day tox package** (~$20-40M, 3-4 years). Exit: licence at the field-validated stage. Judgement: $10-50M upfront, $50-150M in milestones, 5-10% royalty.
4. **Become a supply-model originator, as Kumiai did for pyroxasulfone.** Keep manufacturing and supply of the active, and license regional formulation rights for a prepurchase (the FMC-to-Corteva template). This needs $150-300M and 10-14 years in total.
5. **$1B+ requires two actives at more than $300M each in peak sales, or one at more than $750M, owned or held at more than 10% economics.** Kumiai's market cap of about $0.6B on a ~$500M active is the sobering comparable.

**Ladder total: roughly $200-350M of capital and 12-15 years to reach $1B, with perhaps a 5-10% chance.** This is a pharma-biotech shape, not a seed-stage one.

## 6. Can a 2-person mining-ML team start it? The cheapest test
**Credible for rung 1 only.** Mining prospectivity skills (imbalanced positives, spatial-style leakage control, ranking under a tiny base rate) map well onto a hindcast.

From rung 2 on, the team needs three hires: a synthetic or agro chemist with a patent track record, a weed scientist or plant pathologist to run contract greenhouse work, and a licensing executive. Enko hired a Corteva licensing executive as CEO for this reason. Majors license from people they trust on field data. Without these hires, the team is about 5 years and £50M+ behind Moa.

**Cheapest feasibility test: a post-2010 hindcast (about $5-15k of compute and data, 4-6 weeks):**
- **Train** on actives commercialised up to 2010:
  - PPDB, HRAC and FRAC lists, and cropCSM data.
  - Patent-exemplified compounds as labelled negatives and positives: the compound developed versus its siblings in the same patent.
- **Test** on about 40-60 actives first registered 2011-2026, the new MoAs listed above plus new same-MoA actives.
- **Decoys:** each test active ranked against **its own patent family's never-developed exemplified compounds**, plus a diverse 1M-compound purchasable set. The same-family decoys are the honest benchmark.
- **Baselines:** max-Tanimoto to known actives, and physchem-window rules.
- **Pass:**
  - Median rank of the test active within its patent family in the top 10%.
  - Top-1% enrichment against the diverse set at least 3x the Tanimoto baseline.
  - At least 2 of the about 12 new-MoA actives in the top 1%.
- **Kill:** enrichment below 1.5x baseline, or new-MoA actives ranking no better than random.
- **Follow-on wet test (~$30-60k):**
  - Buy 100 ML-ranked plus 100 diverse-random purchasable compounds.
  - Run a contract pre- and post-emergence screen on 4 weeds and 2 crops.
  - Pass at a hit rate at least 3x random.

**Caveat:** the hindcast can only show that the model "recognises developable agro chemistry". It cannot show that it "finds new MoAs", because there are too few labels. Only the wet screen, plus MoA deconvolution, tests the second claim.
