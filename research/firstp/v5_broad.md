Frame: `V5_OPTIMIST.md`. Tech risk is not a kill; the numbers and the clock are. Pattern tested: **a startup owns the valuable thing (balance sheet, regulated licence, rate base, acquired operating companies, land) because AI collapses the labour, origination or licensing cost that used to need hundreds of people or PE-scale capital.**

Scoring: N numbers, T time, C ceiling, A AI answers the killers (quantified), D defensibility/ownership. Pass = 19+ and no leg below 3.

Tags: "(web)" = checked this session (sources at the end); "(memory)" = from prior knowledge, verify before external use; "(est.)" = my derivation.

**Headline:** 7 of 34 pass at 19-20; the top 2 score 20. All seven pass on numbers and time, not on a unique edge. Their weakest leg is D (3 for most). The AI operating tools they rely on are mostly sold by vendors to everyone (L7), so the ownable things are the balance sheet, licence, rate base or landfill, plus how fast they can execute. ## One-page models (19+)

### 1. AI-native contract-surety carrier: 20 (N4 T4 C4 A5 D3)

**Thing owned:** the balance sheet (a Treasury-listed surety carrier). Later also a contractor working-capital loan book underwritten from the same WIP data.

**Market:** US surety direct premium ~$11-12B (top-100 writers $8.84B through Q3 2025, annualised; web). Contract surety is the majority, about two-thirds (memory). Wedge: small and mid contractors with single bonds under $5-10M. These are underwriting-labour-heavy and under-served, roughly $3B of premium (est.). Industry loss ratio was 26.4% in 2024 and ~20.7% in 2025 (SFAA via secondary; web). Underwriting profit has been >$2B a year for 3 straight years (AM Best; web). RLI's surety combined ratio is 82 (web), which implies an expense ratio of ~55-70% (est.). That expense is the target.

**Price and unit economics:** contract bond rates ~1-3% of contract value; an average small-contractor account pays ~$15k/yr (est.).

| Per $100 premium | Incumbent (est.) | AI-native (est.) |
|---|---|---|
| Losses + LAE | 25 | 21 (continuous WIP monitoring catches fades early) |
| Agent commission | 27 | 27 (keep agents whole; they own the contractor) |
| Internal underwriting/ops | 22 | 8 |
| Fronting (MGA phase only) | 0 | 6 |
| **Combined** | **~74-80** | **~56-62** |

**AI counter-moves:**
- **Underwriting labour:** a human underwriter reads CPA-reviewed statements, WIP schedules, bank lines and job-cost reports, and carries ~150-250 accounts. AI does the read plus monthly monitoring through QuickBooks/Procore/bank feeds, so one underwriter carries 1,000+. That is the -14 pt expense swing.
- **Agent channel:** AI-generated bond-ready packages turn a 1-2 week quote into same-day, and the agent keeps full commission. That makes the carrier the agent's first call for small accounts.
- **Treasury listing and capacity:** start as an MGA on fronted T-listed paper, then form or buy a carrier. Treasury caps each bond at 10% of surplus unless reinsured (web: per-bond limitation, excess reinsured). Use 50-70% quota share with surety reinsurers.

**Capital path:**
- Seed $5M and Series A $30M (MGA). MGA revenue = ~27% override + profit commission.
- Series B $120-180M to capitalise the carrier ($100M surplus writes ~$100-150M premium with reinsurance).
- Corgi shows the market funds this: >$370M raised in ~6 months for a full-stack AI carrier (web).

**Timeline:**
- First revenue: month 9-12 (MGA).
- $100M gross premium: year 5-6 (about 1% share).
- $100M **revenue** as carrier, net of quota share: year 6-7.
- $1B premium (~8% share): year 10-12.

**Ceiling:**
- Surety alone: $1.5B premium at a 30% underwriting margin plus float is ~$500M pre-tax, worth ~$5-8B.
- Adjacencies push toward $10B: contractor working-capital lending (the surety already sees WIP and receivables better than any bank), subcontractor default insurance, fully automatable commercial and licence bonds, and international markets.

**Competitors:**
- Incumbent carriers: Travelers, Liberty Mutual, CNA, Chubb, Zurich; specialists Merchants and IFIC.
- AI startups so far are tools, not carriers: InRev ($3.8M, profitable MGA, backed by Accelerant and Greenlight Re; web), axiTrust ($2.7M seed; web), SuretyOS (agency tooling).

**Kill risks:**
- (a) Surety losses lag construction downturns by 2-3 years; a 2009-12-type cycle hits before the book seasons.
- (b) Adverse selection: being fast attracts contractors other sureties declined.
- (c) Travelers deploys the same AI internally (D is 3, not 4).
- (d) Treasury and AM Best rating timelines delay federal work. Verify the 31 CFR 223 seasoning requirements.

**Cheapest test:** back-test an AI underwriter on ~500 historical bond files from a partner MGA or carrier with known claims. Kill if it ranks claims no better than the carrier's own scores (AUC lift < 0.05) or cannot cut file-review time ≥70%. Cost ~$50-100k, 3-4 months.

### 2. AI-run fire and life-safety inspection roll-up: 20 (N4 T5 C4 A4 D3)

**Thing owned:** the operating companies and their code-mandated recurring inspection contracts (NFPA 25 sprinklers, 72 alarms, 10 extinguishers, 96 kitchen hoods). Inspections are the annuity; repair and retrofit pull-through is the profit.

**Market:**
- US fire and life-safety services $27-32B, 3,000+ firms, no player >5% (web).
- Pye-Barker grew from 80 to 250+ branches; ~$350M EBITDA, valued at $6B+ (17-20x; web).
- Sector platforms trade at 16-20x EBITDA (web). Small shops change hands at ~5-7x (est.).

**Unit economics, per acquired shop (est.):**

| | Before | After AI ops (18-24 months) |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | $5.0M | $6.0M (+20% tech capacity, higher repair conversion) |
| EBITDA margin | 15% | 25% |
| EBITDA | $0.75M | $1.5M |
| Price paid / value | $4.5M (6x) | $22M at 15x platform multiple |

**AI counter-moves:**
- **Report writing:** AI drafts the report from voice and photos and files it to the AHJ portal. Tech admin time falls from ~25% to ~5%.
- **Repair conversion:** deficiency-to-quote-to-approval is automated, raising conversion from ~40% to ~60%.
- **Back office:** scheduling, dispatch and billing fall from ~13% to ~5% of revenue.
- **Labour:** AI-guided inspection lets junior techs do more under one certified lead, easing the NICET bottleneck.

**Capital path:**
- $60-100M Series A/B equity plus 4-5x unitranche debt buys ~20 shops ($100M revenue) in 3-4 years.
- The model is fundable now: Long Lake raised $670M, made 18 acquisitions and reached ~$100M EBITDA in under 2 years (web, secondary); General Catalyst earmarked $750M-1.5B for AI roll-ups (web, conflicting).

**Timeline:** first revenue at the first close, month 4-6. $100M revenue in year 3-4. $1B revenue around year 8-9, at 25% EBITDA ($250M).

**Ceiling:** ~$4-5B on the Pye-Barker comp. $10B+ requires becoming an APi-scale consolidator (memory: APi Group is a high-teens-$B company), so C is 4.

**Competitors:** Pye-Barker (LGP/Altas/ADIA/GIC), APi Group, Summit Fire & Security, many PE platforms.

**Kill risks:**
- (a) Certified-technician supply caps growth.
- (b) PE bids add-on multiples up toward 8-10x.
- (c) LPs warn about integration risk in AI roll-ups (web).
- (d) The AI tools are sold to everyone, so the edge is execution speed (L7).
- (e) It is closer to a services business than the "own the asset" rule prefers. What it owns is a recurring legal mandate.

**Cheapest test:** buy or partner one $3-8M shop, run the AI stack for 6 months, and measure tech billable hours and repair conversion. Kill if EBITDA margin does not rise ≥5 pts.

### 3. AI-native small water/wastewater utility consolidator: 19 (N3 T3 C4 A4 D5)

**Thing owned:** regulated monopoly systems and their rate base. This is the strongest ownership of the seven.

**Market:**
- ~49,000 community water systems, most tiny and many in violation (web).
- Fair-market-value (FMV) acquisition laws in 13 states let buyers pay appraised value above book and put it in rate base (web).
- Only 9% of ~1,531 deals since 2015 used FMV (web), so most of the room is still open.
- CSWR, the PE-backed incumbent consolidator, added 50k+ connections since 2016 by buying distressed systems (web).

**Unit economics (est.):**
- Combined water+sewer revenue ~$1,200 per connection per year (memory). Post-catch-up rate base ~$6-8k per connection.
- Allowed ROE ~9.5-10.5% on ~50% equity (memory).
- Equity value comes from building rate base at ~1x book and holding it in a vehicle valued like water IOUs, ~2x book (memory).

**AI counter-moves:**
- **Origination:** AI ranks all 49k systems on SDWIS violations, operator vacancies, municipal budget stress and council minutes, then sends tailored offers. Pipeline cost per closed deal is ~1/5 of a broker-led approach (est.).
- **Operations:** AI on SCADA, sensors and remote alarms lets one licensed operator cover ~3x more systems. O&M per connection falls ~30%.
- **Regulatory:** AI drafts rate cases, FMV appraisal responses and PUC data requests, cutting a small rate case from ~$300k to ~$75k. That makes consolidated multi-system tariffs and annual filings economic.
- **Capital planning:** pipe-failure prediction cuts unplanned capex ~15%.

**Capital path:**
- $20-40M equity for the first ~20k connections, then infrastructure-fund equity plus utility first-mortgage bonds. This is not a venture-shaped return.
- CSWR's backers show infra money funds the model.

**Timeline:**
- First revenue: month 9-18 (PUC approval of the first deal).
- $100M revenue: ~85k connections, year 7.
- $10B equity: ~$5B rate base, 15-20 years.

**Competitors:** CSWR, Nexus Water Group, American Water, Essential Utilities, SJW/H2O America, state-level IOUs.

**Kill risks:**
- (a) Consumer advocates are pushing back on FMV (NASUCA explainer, 2026; web).
- (b) PUC deal timelines.
- (c) PFAS and lead capex, rate shock and political risk.
- (d) Returns are capped, so venture investors will not fund it; N is 3.

**Cheapest test:** build the distress-ranking model on public SDWIS and state data and pitch the top 50 systems in one FMV state. Kill if fewer than 5 sign LOIs within 6 months. Cost ~$100k.

## Honest read across the passes

1. **The passes are execution plays, not step-ups.** Each owns a balance sheet, licence, rate base or permitted asset, but the AI levers (report-writing, quoting, routing, back office) are increasingly sold by vendors to every incumbent (L7). In the roll-ups, the durable gains are multiple arbitrage (buy at 4-7x, re-rate at 12-20x) and speed. Surety and water are the exceptions, because the AI is the underwriting or origination decision on an owned balance sheet or regulated asset.
2. **Surety is the cleanest V5 fit.**
   - Big, profitable, labour-heavy underwriting.
   - Capital-light enough for a venture-backed carrier, with Corgi as proof.
   - No AI-native carrier exists yet, only tools (InRev, axiTrust, SuretyOS).
   - It extends naturally into lending against the same WIP data.
3. **The water consolidator has the best ownership but the worst clock and return shape.** It is infrastructure money, not venture money.
4. **Verify before using externally:** surety expense-ratio split, small-shop entry multiples, the packaged-gas market size, the Rollins and APi multiples, and Treasury listing seasoning rules (all memory or estimate).
