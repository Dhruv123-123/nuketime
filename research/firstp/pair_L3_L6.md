# Pair sweep L3 + L6: drop "the seller prices in the hope" and "the first rung is already held" (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
Nothing survives cleanly, but one idea comes closer than anything in pass 1. It is a **failed-Phase-3 rescue company that pools patient-level data**: buy drugs that failed late-stage trials, take their patient-level datasets with them, train one responder model across all of them, and re-run only the assets where that model finds a subgroup. It scores **18/25** with both laws dropped (the pass-1 best was 16). Both relaxed laws turn out to be weak here even in fact. L3 is empirically false: Summit sold the Phase 3 asset ridinilazole for $0.5M upfront, and Galera sold two Phase 3 assets for $3.5M. L6 is beatable: the occupant, Biossil, is only 18 months out of stealth with 12 molecules. It still dies on two things that the pair does not relax:
- **C1:** the proof is a new $50-300M trial, not a cheap step. Even a biomarker Phase 2b is $15-40M.
- **R-ML:** there is no evidence that ML-found subgroups replicate. In Wallach 2017, none of the 5 subgroup claims that were retested held up in a later meta-analysis or trial.

Every energy, land and grid idea still dies on L2 (the signal is sold) and R-ML (late winners won with capital, not ML), so for those dropping L3 and L6 together buys only about 1 point.

## How the pair changes the rubric
- **C4 (+1 where L3 bit):** a right bought at the seller's hope price, or through an option, JV or retained-equity NewCo, counts as buyable. C2 is then measured against the price actually paid.
- **C5 (+1 where L6 bit):** a rung held by a funded startup or a PE roll-up counts as winnable if a second entrant has won one like it before (Cloverleaf, AerSale, GoodFinch, Sortera, Lyten).
- **Kept:** L1, L2, L4, L5, R-ML, the bans on prospecting and drilling, own-the-asset, not-Terranox and venture scale.
- **Sources:** facts marked (memory) are carried from sweep_L3.md and sweep_L6.md; URLs are from this sweep.

## 3. Combination factors
The brief asked us to combine the factors that kill most often. When we pair the things that survive the laws, only one combination adds a point.

| Combination | Effect | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Assets nobody wants (L3 false) **x** data that comes with the asset (C4) **x** cross-asset pooling (ML scale) | Each asset bought adds training data, so the edge compounds even though the occupant has more capital | **N2, +1 over N1.** This is the only combination that answers R-ML structurally |
| Retained-equity NewCo (L3) **x** powered land (L6) | The farmer keeps equity and we prove load | Still L2: the headroom signal is sold |
| Bankruptcy estate (L3 false) **x** hardware test logs (C4) | ML reads failed-plant data | The restart is the build (L1/C1) |
| Throughput (S-L1) **x** occupied rung (L6) | Run 100x more cheap proofs than the incumbent | The throughput tool is sold as SaaS to the incumbent (proposed L7) |

## 4. Adversarial web checks on the top 3

### A. Failed-Phase-3 rescue with pooled data (N2/N1, 17-18): the closest call, but not a pass
- **L3 is empirically false here, and that is the core finding.** Summit sold the Phase 3 antibiotic ridinilazole to Biossil on 14 Jul 2026 for **$0.5M upfront**, plus up to $104.5M in milestones and tiered royalties. The asset had beaten vancomycin on observed sustained response but missed superiority, and Summit had failed to find any buyer since 2022 ([BioSpace/Summit PR](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/summit-therapeutics-signs-agreement-to-sell-phase-iii-asset-ridinilazole-to-biossil-inc), [Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/summit-sells-failed-antibiotic-openai-backed-startup-105m-deal)). Galera sold GC4419 and GC4711 for **$3.5M upfront** plus up to $105M, after the FDA asked for another Phase 3 ([Gazelle Parent S-4](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0002130606/000119312526170834/d117787ds4.htm), search snippet). Sellers who have pivoted dump the hope rather than price it.
- **Step-up:** the most Biossil is committed to pay is $0.5-3.5M upfront, against approved-asset values of $1-7B. Telavant sold for $7.1B; it was an in-license, not a failure (memory, Roivant 10-Q). That is about 10^3x on upfront, so C2 = 5. The ceiling is paid in milestones, though, so the seller still takes about 10% of the upside.
- **Supply:** of 640 drugs entering Phase 3 between 1998 and 2008, 54% failed, 57% of those for efficacy, and only 40% of the failed trials were ever published ([Hwang et al., JAMA IM 2016](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamainternalmedicine/fullarticle/2565686)). That is a large, illegible pool, which supports C4.
- **Occupant (L6, now relaxed):** Biossil (Toronto) came out of stealth in Apr 2026 with about $70M from OpenAI and Founders Fund. It reportedly raised **$153M at a $1B valuation**, led by the OpenAI Startup Fund, around 28 Sep 2026, and holds 12 molecules ([The Logic](https://thelogic.co/briefing/openai-backs-biossil-in-us153m-funding-round-report/), [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/157312-openai-leads-150m-round-for-drug-reviving-startup-biossil/)). That proves venture scale and investor appetite. Its having 12 molecules and no readout yet means the rung is not locked.
- **R-ML, adversarial:** Wallach et al. (JAMA IM 2017) found 117 subgroup claims in 64 RCTs. Only 39% had a significant interaction test. Of those 46, 5 were ever retested, **0 of 5 replicated**, and every effect shrank toward the null ([Wallach 2017 PDF](https://www.Gwern.net/doc/statistics/meta-analysis/2017-wallach.pdf)). The record of AI-led repurposing is poor:
  - BenevolentAI delisted in Mar 2025, and its lead asset is at Phase 1 ([Business Wire via DutchNews](https://www.dutchnews.nl/businesswire/result-of-extraordinary-general-meeting-and-delisting-from-euronext-amsterdam/)).
  - Lantern Pharma (RADR) is worth about $15.6M as of Sep 2026 ([Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/quote/LTRN/report)).
  - Repronovo raised $65M to revive nolasiban by re-running Phase 2, with no ML subgroup ([BioWorld](https://www.bioworld.com/newsletters/1/issues/17715)).
  So "ML finds the responders" is a hypothesis with a negative prior. The ML is required, but it is not shown to work.
- **C1:** the proving step is a confirmatory trial. The cheapest sellable rung is a biomarker-enriched Phase 2b at about $15-40M (general knowledge, not checked here), sold to pharma. Discovery is about 20% of the work, so C1 = 2.
- **Fit:** it breaks no standing rule. It is not prospecting, the company owns the asset and it is venture-scale. But it is far outside Dhruv's energy and deeptech base, and against Biossil's $153M he would start roughly 18 months and about $200M behind.

### B. Powered land and closed-plant capture, paying the hope price against Cloverleaf (N9/N10, 15-16): dead
- Hawesville shows the hope price in action. Century sold about 750 acres with **480 MW** of on-site substation capacity to a TeraWulf affiliate on 2 Feb 2026 and **kept a 6.8%** stake ([Spectrum News](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2026/02/07/hawesville-ai-database), [Yieh](https://yieh.com/en/News/century-aluminum-sells-hawesville-site-to-terawulf-for-ai-data-campus/159163)). The price was about $200M (memory, S-L2). The owner captured the value and the buyer was a funded incumbent, so with both laws dropped, a new entrant could only pay the same.
- Cloverleaf raised over $300M from NGP and Sandbrook in Jul 2024 ([Proximo](https://proximoinfra.com/news/65098/Cloverleaf-secures-300m-from-NGP-and-Sandbrook-Capital)). Nvidia invested in Aug 2026 and 7 GW has been sold (memory, S-L6). The land market was $3.3B in Q1 2026, up 141% (memory, S-L3).
- **Kill:** dropping L3 and L6 only lets us pay what TeraWulf paid and compete with Cloverleaf. The edge left is ML reading sites, but L2 (LandGate and Paces sell headroom, and our backtest found no lift) and R-ML (the winners won with capital and utility relationships) both remain. A capital-and-relationships developer could be fundable, but it is not this brief's company.

### C. Engine green-time and part-out against FTAI and AerSale (N12/old part-out, 14): dead
- **Values** (broker data, so only indicative): a CFM56-5B core or teardown unit costs **$0.5-1.0M**, a serviceable engine $2.5-4.0M, and a green-time engine $4.5-6.5M. A teardown on $0.8-1.2M of input recovers **$1.6-2.2M** of USM ([Safe Fly 2026](https://safefly.aero/cfm56-engine-market-report-2026/)). That is about 1.5-2.5x, so C2 = 2.
- **Market:** USM is $4.8-9.0B a year globally, with estimates varying 4x by scope ([Fortune BI via Notion summary](https://grizzly-hydrogen-b07.notion.site/Air-Transport-USM-Market-Size-Share-Growth-Forecast-Report-2025-2034-39e44dad392d80ac8332c63e0d04967e)). Asset managers already part out early because engine values exceed lease income ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/mro/aircraft-propulsion/fast-5-engine-values-drive-earlier-aircraft-teardowns)).
- **Kill:** the step-up is under 3x and values are published by mba and Ascend (L2). Records are private to the operator, so ML has no public, illegible signal. AerSale won its rung on inventory capital, not ML (R-ML).

## 5. Answer to the brief
1. **Does anything survive as a real, venture-scale, ownable company?** **No clean survivor. The best is N2 at 18/25, conditional.**
2. **The company (N2):** buy failed late-stage drugs for $0.5-5M upfront plus milestones, always with their patient-level trial data. Pool that data across every asset into one responder model. Run a cheap biomarker-enriched Phase 2b only where the model predicts a replicable subgroup, then sell or partner each positive readout to pharma.
3. **Why now:**
   - Pivoting biotechs are dumping Phase 3 assets for under $5M (Summit, Galera).
   - Capital is rewarding the model: Biossil reached a $1B valuation on 12 molecules and no readout.
   - Foundation models can now read heterogeneous patient-level data and trial PDFs at scale.
4. **First rung:** one failed-Phase-3 asset bought for under $5M, with its data.
5. **Step-up:** about 10^3x on upfront if the asset is approved (a $1-7B approved value against a $0.5-3.5M upfront). Of that, about $105M is owed to the seller in milestones.
6. **Feasibility test (cheap, before buying anything):**
   - Take drugs that have **two** independent randomised trials in Vivli, YODA or Project Data Sphere.
   - Train the subgroup model on trial 1 only.
   - Check whether the predicted subgroup's interaction effect replicates in trial 2, against a pre-registered bar of at least 30% of predictions replicating. Wallach's base rate is 0 of 5.
   - If the model clears the bar, R-ML is answered and N2 is a real company. If it does not, R-ML kills it.
7. **What still kills it today:**
   - **C1:** the proof is a $15-300M trial, not a cheap step.
   - **R-ML:** there is no evidence yet that ML subgroups replicate.
   - **Sector fit:** it is outside Dhruv's energy and deeptech base. That is not a law.
   - **For every energy idea:** L2 (the signal is sold) and R-ML. Dropping L3 and L6 together moves them about 1 point, to a ceiling of 16.
8. **Most interesting finding:** L3 does not just relax for failed late-stage drugs. It is **inverted**. Sellers who have pivoted pay to be rid of the hope, which is why a Phase 3 asset went for $0.5M. That is the Terranox pattern (buy a right whose owner cannot or will not read its data) outside mining. The occupant is new enough that L6 is soft too. The binding question has moved entirely to whether the ML works, and that can be tested on open trial data for under $100k.
