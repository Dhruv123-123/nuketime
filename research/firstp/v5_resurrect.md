Ideas that died on physics or on a real negative test (failed-trial subgroups, Chekroud 2024; powered-land AUC 0.721 vs 0.727 for a lookup; refrigerant space already enumerated; second-life LCOS vs new LFP) keep their negative on N or A here. Optimism covers the tech. It does not cover evidence that already exists.

## Verdict

**One thesis passes: an AI-native reverse-engineering parts house that owns a growing portfolio of qualified part approvals. The approvals are DLA source approvals, power-gen gas-turbine replacement parts and, later, FAA PMAs. Score 21/25.** Under the old frames it died as four separate small ideas: DLA surplus lots (17, failed the size rule), gas-turbine hot-section parts (17 on paper; PSM, EthosEnergy and MD&A hold the rung), Tracebird engine LLPs (16) and orphan sole-source defence parts (13-15; "requalification dominates"). Each was killed by the same thing: *engineering and qualifying one part costs too much for the demand behind it, and incumbents hold the parts that do pay*. AI reverse engineering attacks exactly that number, the engineering cost and time per qualified part. If it falls 3-5x, the long tail of parts with $0.5-3M a year of demand, which HEICO, TransDigm and the OEMs ignore, becomes a profitable portfolio of owned rights. HEICO and TransDigm show the comparables: HEICO's Flight Support Group did $3.12B of sales at 24% operating margin in FY2025, and TransDigm did $8.83B at 53.9% EBITDA margin.

Its three wedges also clear 19 on their own: defence obsolescence 20, power-gen turbines 20, aviation PMA 19. They are one company with three markets, not three separate passes.

**Nothing else reaches 19.** Four ideas reach 18 and fail on one leg or the threshold:
- Distributed inference data centres at distribution substations (flex micro-sites, scope expanded to owning and operating the sites).
- The nuclear-qualified contract manufacturer (PICK3).
- A third-entrant medium-voltage power package after Ayr (D = 2).
- The congestion-node book, i.e. FTRs plus a flexible-load IPP. Merchant battery revenue in ERCOT has collapsed to about $30/kW-yr trailing, so the owned-asset half does not close.

The honest pattern is that **AI counter-moves work best where the killer is engineering or paperwork labour per unit**: reverse engineering, certification packages, quality records, origination. They work worst where the killer is a gatekeeper's discretion (utility allocation, FDA/EPA clocks), capital intensity (power plants, drugs) or someone else's balance sheet (life settlements, FTR collateral).

## 1-page model: AI reverse-engineering parts house (21/25)

**What it owns.** Every part it qualifies becomes a durable, income-earning right:
- a DLA approved-source status or a TDP it holds data rights to;
- an FAA PMA;
- a utility or IPP qualification on a power-gen part.

The portfolio compounds the way HEICO's PMA catalogue and TransDigm's sole-source catalogue do. Its manufacturing is contract-machined at first, then it buys the small shops (the HEICO roll-up playbook). The scan, material and test outcomes of every part feed the next part's model.

**Market (sourced).**
- **Defence.** DLA did $51.8B of goods and services in FY2025, and DLA Aviation alone obligated $10.3B ([DLA FY2025 report](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/Headquarters/AnnualReport/DLAFY2025AnnualReport.pdf); [DLA About](https://www.dla.mil/AboutDLA/)). DLA Aviation manages ~1.8M NSNs ([DLA Aviation trifold](https://www.dla.mil/Portals/104/Documents/Aviation/DLA%20Aviation%20Trifold%20Web-AUG-2024.pdf)). DLA itself ran 1,600+ reverse-engineering projects in FY2015-18 to break sole-source pricing ([GAO-19-586](https://gao.gov/products/GAO-19-586)), so the demand for second sources is institutional. On 1 Oct 2025 DLA merged Aviation and Land & Maritime into DLA Weapons Support.
- **Power-gen.** Gas-turbine services are $10-48B in 2026 depending on scope. OEMs take ~62% of service revenue, and that share is eroding to ISPs ([summary](https://antonios-newsletter-aff62c.beehiiv.com/p/the-aftermarket-empire-why-service-not-sales-rules-the-gas-turbine-industry); [Future Market Report](https://www.futuremarketreport.com/industry-report/gas-turbine-services-market)). Scarcity of new units extends old ones: GE Vernova's backlog plus slot reservations reached 116 GW in Q2 2026 against 20 GW a year of output ([GEV Q2 2026](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001996810/000199681026000147/gevpressrelease2q26.htm)).
- **Aviation.** The PMA market is ~$11.4B (2024) rising to $14.9B (2030); this is a single market-report figure, so treat it as low confidence.
- **Comparables.** HEICO FSG FY2025: $3,117M of sales, $750M of operating income (24.1%) ([HEICO 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/46619/000004661925000076/a10312025ex991earningsrele.htm)). TransDigm FY2025: $8,831M of sales, $4,760M EBITDA As Defined (53.9%) ([TDG release](https://transdigmgroupinc.gcs-web.com/news-releases/news-release-details/transdigm-group-reports-fiscal-2025-fourth-quarter-and-year-end)). PSM had ~$70M of sales when Alstom paid $242M for it in 2007; it is now Hanwha Power ([Alstom](https://www.alstom.com/press-releases-news/2007/3/alstom-to-acquire-usa-gas-turbine-service-provider)).

**Price.** Second-source and PMA parts sell 25-40% below OEM or sole-source price; this is the industry norm, and the vendor claim of 40-60% is unverified. The gross margin target is 50-55%, between HEICO's operating economics and a contract-machining cost base.

**Unit economics per part (derived; the core assumption to test).**

| | Traditional RE/PMA (est.) | AI pipeline (est., tech assumed) |
|---|---|---|
| Engineering + substantiation per part | $250-600k | $60-150k |
| Time to qualified | 12-24 months | 4-8 months |
| Long-tail part demand / our share | $1.5M/yr / 20% | same |
| Revenue / gross profit per part (steady) | $300k / ~$160k | same |
| Payback | 1.5-3.7 yrs | 0.4-0.9 yrs |

AI does not create demand. It makes parts with $0.5-3M a year of demand worth qualifying. Those are exactly the parts incumbents skip, and they are where DLA's sole-source pain sits.

**Revenue path (derived).** Assumptions: parts qualified per year of 40, 80, 140, 190, 200; first-year revenue per part is 40% of steady state; plus repairs and spot buys.

| Year | Y1 | Y2 | Y3 | Y4 | Y5 | Y6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ~$1-2M | ~$9M | ~$22M | ~$53M | ~$100M | ~$150M |

Engineering spend at 190 parts a year × $100k is ~$19M/yr, and working capital is ~25% of revenue. Tuck-in acquisitions of small PMA, repair and machine shops (HEICO's model) could pull $100M forward by 1-2 years.

**Capital path.**
- Seed $4-6M: scan and metrology lab, 4-6 engineers, first 30-40 DLA NSNs, plus 7E/7F combustion hardware with one IPP.
- Series A $25-35M: 150+ parts, AS9100 and first DER repairs.
- Series B $80-120M plus acquisition debt: the shop roll-up and the first FAA PMAs.

Total equity to $100M of revenue is ~$110-160M. That is well within reach of a venture plus private-credit stack, and acquired shops carry their own EBITDA for leverage.

**Timeline.**
- First revenue: 6-12 months, from DLA small-business awards on DIBBS and power-gen spot parts.
- $100M revenue: year 5-6.
- $1B+ outcome: at ~$150M revenue × 30% EBITDA × 25x ≈ $1.1B. Aftermarket-parts portfolios trade higher (HEICO and TDG are $40-100B companies; that range is from memory, not checked).
- $10B ceiling: the TDG and HEICO precedent says parts portfolios scale to that size. It needs aviation PMA plus defence at HEICO-like breadth.

**Who is already there.**
- HEICO (PMA leader).
- TransDigm (OEM sole-source; the price target).
- Chromalloy, Jet Parts Engineering (bought Mitchell PMA), Av8 PMA.
- Power-gen ISPs: Hanwha Power/PSM, EthosEnergy, MD&A, Sulzer.
- DLA second-sourcers and small PMA shops (pair_L1_L3).
- AI-native: Novineer pitches AI reverse engineering for defence sustainment ([Defense Post, Jun 2026](https://thedefensepost.com/2026/06/30/novineer-ai-reverse-engineering-sustainment/)), with no funding found. PhysicsX ($300M at ~$2.4B, Jun 2026) does AI design for OEMs, not aftermarket rights. Hadrian does machining, not approvals.

No funded AI-native company owning a parts-approval portfolio was found. That rung is open, but Novineer and PhysicsX could step onto it.

**AI counter-moves, per killer.**
- **Rung held (HEICO, TDG, OEMs).** Go where they don't: low-demand parts are below their engineering cost-hurdle, and AI lowers ours ~4x.
- **Slow proof (SAR or PMA review).** A complete, AI-built package cuts rework cycles. Government review time is not ours to compress, so we pipeline 100+ parts in parallel and start in power-gen, which has no regulator.
- **Labour cost.** Engineer throughput rises ~3-4x (est.).
- **Size ceiling per part.** Portfolio breadth replaces per-part size.

**What would kill it.**
1. Substantiation is dominated by physical testing (fatigue, engine or rig tests) that AI cannot shorten, so the cost per part stays above $250k. **Kill test (~$50-100k, 8-10 weeks):** take 20 DLA sole-source NSNs with open DIBBS solicitations, run the scan-to-TDP pipeline, and measure cost and time per complete TDP+SAR package. Kill if the median is above $150k or 6 months.
2. Second-source price drops are too small to win awards. Test this from FPDS award history (sole-source vs post-SAR prices); kill if under 20%.
3. DLA SAR review backlog throttles approvals to a few dozen a year per vendor.
4. OEM trade-secret litigation, or DFARS data-rights limits on reverse engineering of some parts.
5. On power-gen, long-term OEM service agreements (CSAs) lock much of the F-class fleet. The addressable fleet is units off CSA, which is large on 7E/7F legacy units but needs counting.
6. HEICO buys or builds the same AI pipeline. It is a fast follower with distribution, which is why D is 4, not 5.

**Wedge notes.**
- **1a defence (20).** Fastest revenue. Its moat is approvals held per NSN and the DIBBS award history. Its risk is government review cadence.
- **1b power-gen (20).** T = 5 because there is no regulator and the customer is an IPP or utility under turbine scarcity. D = 3 because Hanwha/PSM and EthosEnergy can match on common frames. Start with combustion and repair, not single-crystal blades; casting capacity at PCC and Howmet is a real constraint.
- **1c aviation PMA (19).** The biggest ceiling, but the FAA and airline/lessor acceptance clock makes it the year-3+ expansion, not the wedge.

## Near misses (18) and what would lift them to 19
- **#2 Distributed inference DCs at substations.** The numbers are good: a ~$12M/MW build earns ~$2M/MW-yr at $170-200/kW-mo and is worth ~$20-30M/MW at a 6-7% cap. A lift needs proof that 5-20 MW inference tenants sign leases; EPRI's ~25-site pilot is the market test ([IEEE Spectrum](https://spectrum.ieee.org/distributed-inference-data-centers)). It also needs a defensible origination lead that EPRI/Prologis/NVIDIA (L8) cannot match.
- **#3 Nuclear contract manufacturer.** A lift needs firm multi-unit orders (the US-Korea $120B framework) to land before 2029 so that T reaches 4.
- **#4 MV power package and #6 FTR book.** Both fail on D. Nothing they build compounds, and AI does not change that.
- **#5 Congestion-node IPP.** This fails on merchant storage revenue (ERCOT ~$30/kW-yr trailing, spreads down ~50% y/y by Jun 2026, [Modo](https://modoenergy.com/research/en/ercot-battery-storage-2026-things-to-watch)). Pairing the model with inference load instead saves too small a share of TCO.
