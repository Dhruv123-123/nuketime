## Web checks (all legs except S3)

### #8 Tracebird: 17 -> 16 (S1 3, S2 4, S3 3, S4 3, S5 3)
- **Step-up (S2 holds at 4).** A broken chain means an LLP is treated as having zero life left, so a disc worth about $250k becomes scrap ([Oxmaint, vendor](https://oxmaint.com/industries/aviation-management/life-limited-parts-llp-tracking-back-to-birth-cmms)). GE's MBA Aviation survey: over half of respondents discount non-TCH CFM56/CF6/GE90 cores by more than 20%, about 50% treat serviceable PMA as scrap at teardown, and restoration costs more than ~$500k ([GE TRUEngine](https://www.geaerospace.com/commercial/services/truengine), [presentation](https://www.geaerospace.com/sites/default/files/trueengine-presentation.pdf)). Records restored to a clean state move a part from scrap to full value, so the step-up is many times over.
- **Rules are softer than assumed.** The FAA has repeatedly said US law does not require back-to-birth records, even for LLPs. The requirement is a market norm, so documentary reconstruction that buyers accept is enough ([SASSofia paper](https://sassofia.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Considerations-Related-to-EASA-Life-Limited-Parts-Hard-Life-and-Back-to-Birth-Traceability-Requirements.pdf)). After the AOG Technics forged-documents scandal, though, buyers distrust any reconstructed paper ([Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/mro/supply-chain/usm-specialists-mull-options-improve-parts-traceability)). That keeps S4 at 3 rather than 4.
- **Who holds the rung and how sellers behave (S1 4 -> 3).** Part-out houses such as AerFin review records, trace critical parts back to birth and set aside parts with discrepancies ([ATW/Aviation Week](https://aviationweek.com/mro/supply-chain/usm-specialists-mull-options-improve-parts-traceability)). GE's materials business sells "TRUEngine LLP" with records reviewed. So the big sellers already sort traceable from untraceable. The cheap supply is their discrepancy bin, which they have already tried to trace. I found no dedicated records-recovery company.
- **Size.** The global USM market is about $7.6B (2025), and engines are the largest segment ([SNS Insider](https://www.snsinsider.com/reports/used-serviceable-material-market-8691/segmentation)). The share lost to traceability gaps is not published. There is a path to an AerSale-scale company, but not obviously beyond it.
- **Cheapest test.** Buy 20-50 scrap-priced discrepancy LLPs from a part-out house and try to recover records. Kill the idea if fewer than 1 in 5 can be documented to buyer standard.

### #1 Orphan patent evidence-of-use: 17 -> 15 (S1 4, S2 3, S3 4, S4 2, S5 2)
- **Size and step-up.** The brokered secondary patent market was about **$158M in 2024**, flat with 2022-23, with prices stabilising and buyers "quietly cleaning up" ([IAM on Richardson Oliver 2025](https://www.iam-media.com/article/smarter-sharper-patent-market), [ROI](https://www.richardsonoliver.com/publications/the-2025-richardson-oliver-patent-market-report)). The last per-patent asking figure I found is $277k (2014-15) ([IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/the-rules-of-the-game-may-have-changed-the-brokered-patent-market-still-alive-and-kicking)). Distressed portfolios clear far below their owners' marks: Kodak's sold for about $525M against its own $2.4B ([The Middle Market](https://www.themiddlemarket.com/news/todays-transactions-kodak-sells-patents-for-525m-to-nathan-myhrvolds-intellectual-ventures-and-others)). Cheap entry is real, but a 3-30x exit relies on licensing or litigation, not on a sale.
- **Who holds the rung.** IV, Acacia, RPX and NPE aggregators, plus litigation funders, who back about 30% of US patent suits ([Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2023/02/litigation-disclosure-executive.html)). Funders say fundable patent cases are hard to find and recoveries are slow ([Above the Law](https://abovethelaw.com/2025/09/texas-holds-a-litigation-funding-conference-flush-in-2026)). That is demand for good charts, but it also shows the value sits in litigation (S4 2).
- **Seller behaviour.** Insolvency practitioners now use data tools to set reserve prices (Intangio, [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/alibaba-deploys-tool-provide-price-guidance-patents-going-under-the-hammer)), which narrows the S1 gap at the large-estate end. Small estates and lapse-bound owners stay cheap.
- **S5 at 2.** Litigation outcomes are public in dockets and decisions, so your own outcomes compound only slowly.

### #37 Floodway flip: 17 -> 12 (S1 3, S2 2, S3 4, S4 2, S5 1)
- **Step-up is weak.** A regression-discontinuity study in Texas found no SFHA boundary effect on coastal homes and a +2% effect inland. The one vacant-land hedonic study I found (La Plata, Argentina) gives a 17% discount ([FHFA working paper](https://fhfa.gov/media/52041), [UCA repository](https://repositorio.uca.edu.ar/handle/123456789/2325)). A Fort Collins review found no discount for SFHA proximity in the arid West ([CWCB](https://dnrweblink.state.co.us/CWCB/0/doc/115457/Page2.aspx)). The LOMA version (SFHA to Zone X) is therefore a small step-up. Lenders can also keep requiring insurance after a LOMA.
- **Floodway version.** Removing a lot from the floodway needs a LOMR with engineered no-rise modelling, and no construction is allowed that raises the base flood elevation ([LOMR-FW determinations](https://sjcounty.net/home/showpublisheddocument/4381/637703468977230000)). LiDAR LOMAs cannot be used in the floodway, in coastal high-hazard zones or on fill ([Region V LiDAR-LOMA factsheet](https://dlnreng.hawaii.gov/nfip/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2019/02/Region_V_LiDAR_LOMA_FS_v3_012219_FINAL.pdf)). So the big step-up case needs bespoke engineering (S4 2).
- **Who holds the rung.** FEMA and its contractors already run LiDAR LOMAs in bulk: a Dewberry pilot issued 1,491 determinations in Denton and Bexar counties without owners applying ([Dewberry](https://www.dewberry.com/projects/lidar-for-lomas-pilot-study)). Whatever data advantage exists is being handed out for free, which kills S5.
