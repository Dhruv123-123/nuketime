# Sweep L1: drop "cheap proofs usually succeed" (2026-10-19)

**Relaxation:** law L1 is dropped. An idea may have a proving step that usually succeeds (p 0.5-0.9) and an ML hit-rate lever of only 1.1-3x, provided the value comes from elsewhere. That can be throughput (ML lets one team run 100x more cheap proofs than anyone else) or a large step-up per unit. C3 is rescored so that a high-throughput engine can score 3-4 when ML is what makes the throughput possible. Every other law and standing rule still applies.

## Verdict

Nothing survives. The best score with L1 dropped is 14/25, a small-site entitlement engine (UK garden and infill plots, US middle-housing lots). That is the same ceiling as the earlier hunts, and its three checks killed it. Relaxing C3 lifts about 10 old ideas by 1-2 points each, but none crosses 15, because they were also failing C2 (step-up), C4 (seller prices in the hope, L3) and C5 (the rung is held, L6). A new failure mode appears once throughput is the value: **the throughput edge is not ownable**. The ML that triages every parcel or patent is already sold as SaaS to the incumbents: LandTech, Searchland and Plota in UK land, and Patlytics, Solve Intelligence and XLSCOUT in patents. So the funded promoter or patent aggregator gets the same 100x volume without buying the start-up. Dropping L1 also exposed a factual weakness in L1 itself. The cheap proofs in the best "volume" pools do not reliably succeed. LA approved 0 of 28 SB 9 lot splits in 2022, London schemes of 5-9 homes pass about 54% of the time, and small-site appeals win 21%.

## 3. Adversarial checks on the top 3

### Check 1: Small-site entitlement engine (N1 + N2, UK and US), 14 -> about 10

- **Who already does it:** In the US, BuildCasa pays homeowners for excess lot space, runs the SB 9 permits and sells the plot to a builder ([TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/26/housing-supply-startups/)). It reports about 100 units facilitated since 2022 and about $3.5-10.8M raised (sources conflict) ([Techleap/CZI](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/buildcasa-receives-725k-from-czi), [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/buildcasa)). Yardsworth does the same in SoCal and fronts about $75k of split costs per deal ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/yardsworth-matt-lucido-profile/)). In the UK, Searchland, LandTech and Plota already sell parcel and planning screens to every SME builder ([Searchland](https://searchland.co.uk/guides/small-residential-sites-market-trend-data)).
- **Base rate (L1 fails here, in the wrong direction):** LA approved 0 of 28 SB 9 lot-split applications in 2022. By May 2024 fewer than 500 homeowners statewide had split or added units ([Terner Center](https://ternercenter.berkeley.edu/research-and-policy/sb-9-turns-one-applications/), [Planetizen](https://planetizen.com/node/121105)). In London, 5-9 unit schemes have the lowest success rate at 54% (2019-24), and small sites win only about 21% of appeals ([Construction Index](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/study-reveals-london-planning-authority-bias-against-small-builders), [Searchland](https://searchland.co.uk/blog/uk-planning-application-approval-rates)). The headline England grant rate of 87% is for all applications, including householder extensions ([MHCLG Q4 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/planning-applications-in-england-october-to-december-2025/planning-applications-in-england-october-to-december-2025-statistical-release)).
- **Real step-up:** A UK self-build plot with consent sells for about £100-200k, and a promoter cites £100-400k per serviced plot ([homebuilding.co.uk](https://www.homebuilding.co.uk/advice/how-to-build-a-home-on-a-garden-plot)). Garden land without consent has no clean comparable, but the homeowner knows the plot is in their garden and will ask for a share. The net uplift is about 3-10x the option cost, not 10^3x.
- **Pool:** UK small-site applications are down 55% since 2018, and only about 17k homes were approved on 3-9 unit sites in 2024 against a historical 35k ([Searchland](https://searchland.co.uk/guides/small-residential-sites-market-trend-data), [Housing Today/HBF](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/approvals-for-smallest-sites-halve-hbf-report-shows/5138641.article)). The shrinkage comes from SME build economics, not undiscovered plots, so finding more plots does not create more buyers.
- **Ownability:** SB 9 needs a 3-year owner-occupancy affidavit for lot splits, so an investor cannot hold the right; it must partner with the homeowner ([Seal Beach SB 9 form](https://sealbeachca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/SB9-3.pdf)).
- **Dies on:** L3 (the homeowner owns the hope), C2 (3-10x), and an ML throughput edge that is already sold as SaaS. A venture-scale outcome would need tens of thousands of plots a year in a market that approves about 17k small-site homes a year.

### Check 2: Patent volume engine (continuation forge plus evidence-of-use chart factory), 13 -> about 10

- **Who already does it:** Patlytics sells evidence-of-use discovery and claim charting ($14M Series A in Feb 2025, then a reported $40M Series B) ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250224053849/en/Patlytics-Secures-$14-Million-Series-A-Funding-Led-by-Next47), [TAmradar](https://www.tamradar.com/funding-rounds/patlytics-series-b-40m)). Solve Intelligence raised about $40M in Dec 2025 and offers claim charting ([CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/patlytics-vs-solve-intelligence)). XLSCOUT markets continuation claims aimed at competitor design-arounds ([XLSCOUT case study](https://xlscout.ai/case_study/ai-powered-post-notice-of-allowance-noa-analysis-for-continuation-strategy/)). The 100x throughput is a tool that every NPE and law firm can rent.
- **Costs rising against volume:** Since 19 Jan 2025, continuing applications filed 6 or more years after the earliest benefit date pay $2,700 ($4,000 at 9 or more years) ([Norton Rose Fulbright](https://nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/e912e104/uspto-fee-increases-set-for-january-19-2025-key-changes-and-strategies-to-consider)).
- **Step-up and pool:** Unchanged from the deep dive. The median sold asset is $33-95k, evidence of use adds a 32-108% premium, and the whole brokered market is about $158M/yr (Richardson Oliver 2025, in `research/converge/gold/deep_orphan_patent_evidence-of-use.md`).
- **Dies on:** throughput is not ownable, value is realised through litigation (the proof is an opinion), and the pool is too small. The idea remains a solid small NPE.

### Check 3: Settlement-claim buyer (N6), 12 -> about 8

- **Who already does it:** Claims Compensation Bureau (later part of PRA Group) has bought class-action claims outright since at least 2011, including in the $7.25B Visa/MC interchange case ([PRA Group 2012](https://ir.pragroup.com/2012-07-24-CCB-Offers-Services-to-Maximize-Merchant-Claims-in-7-25-Billion-Credit-Card-Settlement)). Certificate Clearing Corporation was buying Visa/MC claims in 2024 ([listing](https://www.marylandrestaurants.com/buyers-guide/certificate-clearing-corporation)). Litigation funders also source sellers through lead brokers, as alleged in CoreStream v. Managed Care Advisory Group ([RGRD 2024](https://dailynews.rgrdlaw.com/news/20241025-10.25.24media.pdf)).
- **Step-up:** No buy price is published. Claims are bought at a discount to an expected payout that is itself capped by face value, so the realistic return is about 2-5x on capital over years, with settlement-administrator haircut risk. Investor class settlements median about 2-3% of losses ([InvestmentNews](https://www.investmentnews.com/?p=170344)).
- **Dies on:** C2 (bounded by face value), L2 (class lists and settlement notices are public and legible), L6 (PRA Group and funders), and C5 (it is financial collections with no ladder into a physical industry).

## 5. The useful finding

**Proposed L7: throughput edges are not ownable.** Once value comes from volume rather than hit rate, the ML that creates the volume is a general triage tool. In every domain checked, a venture-funded SaaS company already sells it to the incumbents who hold the rung. Those are LandTech, Searchland and Plota in land, Patlytics, Solve and XLSCOUT in patents, and LexisNexis in claims and assets. The incumbent then gets the same 100x throughput plus its existing capital, relationships and buyer book. A hit-rate edge on a hidden natural signal (Terranox) is proprietary in a way a throughput edge on public filings is not.

A corollary is that L1 is too generous in the volume pools. The cheapest residential proofs (lot splits, small schemes) pass well below 87% in exactly the markets where volume would matter: 54% in London and 0/28 in LA. Small-site approvals are shrinking for economic reasons, not discovery reasons.
