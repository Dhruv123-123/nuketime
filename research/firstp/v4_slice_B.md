Each is scored on v4 (`V4_PLAUSIBLE.md`): S1 buy before it's known, S2 step-up 3x+, S3 ML plausibility, S4 discovery-heavy frame, S5 compounding edge. Each leg is 0-5, for /25. A pass is 17+ with no leg at 0-1. Standing rules still apply: no prospecting or drilling, not Terranox, own the asset, and a path to a large company.

**What changed vs gold.** Almost nothing in this slice died *because* its ML lift was unproven. The gold reasons are overwhelmingly structural:
- the outcome is a legible regulator field;
- the right is non-exclusive or can't be bought cheaply;
- the physical, legal or build step dominates (the geothermal-in-coal-mines pattern);
- the step-up is 1-2x.

v4 rescues only the handful where (a) a large, messy, multi-source record decides the outcome, (b) the gold verdict hit the 10^3 step-up bar rather than the 3x bar, and (c) choosing the asset really is most of the work. Two ideas pass and two more come within a point or two.

## Web checks on the top 3 (everything except the ML leg)

### 1. Lapse-Rescue Life Settlements (v4 18 -> 17)
- **Step-up.** On LISA member data, sellers got about 9x the insurer's cash surrender value in 2025: an average of $212,066 against $24,360 surrender, with $626.6M paid over 2,955 deals. In 2024 the figure was about 6.5-7x. That gap goes to the *seller*. The buyer's own step-up is from purchase price plus premiums to the face value at maturity, and the market discount rate fell from 22% to 10% between 2023 and 2026. A lapsing term policy bought near zero gives a larger multiple; a brokered UL buy is roughly 2-4x over years. **S2 lowered from 4 to 3.**
- **Who holds the rung.** Abacus bought 1,310 policies for $580.8M and traded 1,059 (about $1.8B face) in 2025. Coventry and the LISA broker auctions also sit here, so S5 stays at 3: incumbents hold decades of maturity data.
- **Seller behaviour.** Seniors who use brokers get competitive auctions. The cheap end is the large pool that lapses without ever being shopped. Braun, Cohen, Malloy and Xu estimate about $26.6B a year of forgone settlement value. The older LISA estimate of $112B a year of face lapsed by over-65s uses 2008 data and comes from an interested party. S1 stays at 3, provided origination reaches policies *before* they are brokered.
- **Size.** The LISA-member market is under $1B paid a year, but the lapse pool is tens of billions. A path to a large company exists: Abacus is public, Coventry is a large private.
- Sources: [rethinking65 / LISA 2025](https://rethinking65.com/?p=31591), [beinsure $626M](https://beinsure.com/news/life-settlement-payouts-reached-626mn/), [Coverager: Abacus 2025](https://coverager.com/abacus-posts-record-life-settlement-activity-in-2025/), [France Epargne: discount rate](https://www.france-epargne.fr/news/life-settlements-abacus-titrise-plus-de-400-millions-de-dollars-de-polices-dassurance-vie), [Braun et al.](https://www.vwrm.rw.fau.de/files/2018/08/E1_1-Braun-Cohen-Malloy-Xu.pdf), [ThinkAdvisor $112B](https://thinkadvisor.com/2015/02/24/forfeited-life-insurance-benefits-pegged-at-112-billion-2), [Fool caveat](https://www.fool.com/personal-finance/2015/04/03/seniors-lose-life-insurance-every-year.aspx)

### 2. Unenforced commercial judgments (v4 17, unchanged)
- **Step-up.** Judgment buyers aim to buy below 5c on the dollar and recover 20-30% of face, which is about 4-6x. Near-miss verifiers found that a judgment with assets already located trades at 50-73c, against 5.6c for the Zeek portfolio, so about 10-15x per hit. S2 = 3.
- **Who holds the rung.** At the top, Omni Bridgeway runs enforcement and buys awards outright: Ares paid more than 3x invested for 70% of its Fund 9. At the bottom are small recovery shops, contingency firms taking 40-60%, and JudgmentMarketplace listings. I found no ML-first judgment *buyer*; the AI debt-recovery startups (e.g. Rezolv) serve lenders. LexisNexis sells AI asset search, which lowers ownability, not plausibility.
- **Seller behaviour.** Creditors with uncollected judgments have no organised market, which supports S1 = 4. Champerty and licensing rules (NY Jud. Law 489, WA RCW 19.16.260) restrict some states.
- **Size.** No source gave a market size. The near-miss brief's $97B of unpaid federal judgments is mostly asset-less restitution debtors, per GAO. **The commercial pool size is the open question.**
- Sources: [Omni Bridgeway enforcement](https://omnibridgeway.com/litigation-funding/judgment-enforcement), [Bloomberg Law: Ares/Omni deal](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/business-and-practice/ares-omni-bridgeway-close-a-320-million-litigation-finance-deal), [Small Biz Trends: Judgment Marketplace](https://smallbiztrends.com/judgment-marketplace/), [HRW debt buying](https://www.hrw.org/report/2016/01/20/rubber-stamp-justice/us-courts-debt-buying-corporations-and-poor), [Rezolv](https://raising.fi/news/rezolv-series-a-august-2026); near-miss verifier sources are in `nearmiss_verify.json`.

### 3. Retired-Iron Grid Bank (v4 16 -> 15)
- **Step-up.** Large power transformers cost up to $10M new, and lead times are about 120-128+ weeks (Wood Mackenzie; GSUs about 144 weeks). Power-transformer prices are up about 77% since 2019. Remanufactured units sell 10-40% below new and ship in 1-6 weeks (Maddox, distribution class). So scrap at $0.1-0.4M to serviceable at several $M holds up: S2 = 4.
- **Who holds the rung.** For distribution class: Maddox and similar remanufacturers. For spare large power transformers: Grid Assurance stockpiles, and DOE has studied a federal reserve. Large-unit refurbishment is less of a market, and rewind shops are partner-able.
- **Seller behaviour.** GAO reports utilities avoid holding spares because of cost. But with the shortage now public, retired units are less likely to go to scrap at metal value. I found no evidence of cheap retired-unit sales. **S1 lowered from 3 to 2.**
- **Size.** No used-unit price index was found. The US substation market is about $1.7B a year (GMI), and 80% of power transformers are imported.
