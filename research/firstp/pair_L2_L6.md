# Pair sweep: drop L2 (legible outcome) and L6 (rung held), 19 Oct 2026

## Verdict

**Nothing survives as a venture-scale company that Dhruv could own and that still obeys R-ML.** Dropping L2 and L6 together lets a company compete on a public signal that funded players already sell or act on. So the ideas that improve most are the "late entrant wins a crowded, legible rung" plays: powered land and queue positions, and the China-sourced drug NewCo. The best of them, the China drug NewCo, scores 15/25 after checks. Its step-up is real and sourced: Hengrui took about $100-110M upfront plus 19.9% of Kailera, and Kailera IPO'd in April 2026 at about $2B and traded at $2.4-3.1B. But three things kill it, all still in force:
- **L3:** Chinese licensors now collect $7.0B a year in upfronts and a third of global licensing spend, so they price in the hope.
- **R-ML:** picking assets is business-development judgement plus capital, and the language edge is a feature an LLM gives everyone, not a reason the pain becomes solvable.
- **C1:** the proof is a $50-300M clinical program, not a trivial physical step.

Once L2 and L6 are both gone, **R-ML is the binding killer, with L3 right behind it.** Every surviving idea is a capital-and-relationships business where ML is a tool.

## Rubric adjustments for this pair

- **C4 (data and right):** the signal may be legible, and may already be sold by LandGate, Paces, Patlytics or PharmCube. C4 then scores whether the right is still cheap (L3 is kept) and whether a third party can capture the step-up.
- **C5 (ladder):** a funded occupant no longer lowers C5. Only the shape of the chain counts.
- **C3 (lever):** this is where R-ML is charged. When the edge is a join, a lookup table, or speed plus capital, C3 is at most 2. L1 is kept, so C3 also needs a proof that is expensive and usually fails.
- **Still in force:** L1, L3, L4, L5, R-ML, no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, and the $1B+ bar.

## 3. Top three, checked adversarially

### 3.1 China-asset NewCo (N1): step-up confirmed, killed by L3 and R-ML

- **Step-up (sourced):**
  - Kailera: Hengrui licensed its GLP-1s for about $100-110M upfront plus a 19.9% stake ([Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2024/10/understanding-the-newco-model-a-trending-approach-of-chinese-pharmaceutical-companies); [36Kr](https://eu.36kr.com/en/p/3333137400457477)).
  - Kailera launched with a $400M Series A in Oct 2024 (Bain, Atlas, RTW). Its April 2026 IPO priced at $16 for about $1.98B and raised $625M ([IPOScoop](https://www.iposcoop.com/?p=38182)).
  - It closed day one at about $3.1B ([Bloomberg Gov](https://news.bgov.com/private-equity/obesity-drugmaker-kailera-soars-63-after-625-million-ipo-1)) and stood at about $2.4B by July ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/quote/NASDAQ/KLRA)).
  - That is about 20x on the upfront, but only about 2-3x on the ~$1B of total equity raised.
  - Keymed's Ouro Medicines (15% retained) sold to Gilead for up to $2.175B, and Candid exited to UCB for about $2B ([Bamboo Works](https://thebambooworks.com/drug-spin-off-trend-gains-traction-with-ipo-by-hengrui-offshoot/); [A&O Shearman](https://www.aoshearman.com/en/insights/life-sciences-and-healthcare-insights/chinas-pharma-innovators-pursue-a-range-of-deal-structures-to-support-global-expansion)).
- **L3 (adversarial):**
  - China outbound licensing reached **$135.7B of headline value, $7.0B of upfronts and 157 deals in 2025**, up from 94 deals and $51.9B ([DIA Global Forum](https://globalforum.diaglobal.org/issue/march-2026/chinas-out-licensing-deals-bear-fruit-in-2025/)).
  - That is a third of global licensing spend ([Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2025/2026-03-17-key-trends-and-considerations-in-cross-border-life-sciences-partnering-licensing)).
  - The sellers are Hengrui-class companies with BD teams and bankers. The "cheap right" closed in about 2023-24.
- **L6 (dropped, but the size of the crowd matters):** Endpoints/Patsnap count at least 18 NewCos with at least $2.3B raised since 2025, and Forbion says 9 in 10 management-plus-asset NewCos source from China ([Patsnap Synapse](https://synapse.patsnap.com/organization/f55daf6e2c483b2deb1364f24018c57d)). The rung is winnable only with a $100-400M Series A, which means crossover-fund relationships, not a YC seed.
- **R-ML:**
  - Reading Chinese registries is now done by PharmCube, Citeline, Patsnap Synapse and every big pharma's Shanghai BD office.
  - An LLM translation layer is a commodity.
  - Choosing which asset will survive a US Phase 2 is the open problem, and nobody has shown ML beats BD judgement there (BenevolentAI, per S-L3).
- **C1:** the proof is the US trial, $50-300M per asset, so discovery is under 30% of the work.
- **Revised score: 15/25 (2,4,2,3,4).**

### 3.2 Powered land / queue positions (N2, old #1-2): killed by L3 (collateral) and R-ML

- **No market price for queue positions exists.**
  - Berkeley Lab data give only upgrade-cost estimates. PJM's mean was $240/kW for 2020-22 requests (up from $29/kW), and withdrawn projects averaged $563/kW ([APPA](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/study-finds-steeply-rising-interconnection-costs-pjm)).
  - No sale prices in $/kW were found in PJM, ERCOT or anywhere else.
- **Texas:** the PUCT large-load rule (Sept 2026) requires **$50k/MW of financial security** from the customer ([mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/09/21/texas-cuts-the-data-center-grid-deposit-from-100000-to-50000-per-mw-during-its-own-queue-pause/), secondary source). Priority goes to whoever posts capital, not to the best-informed party.
- **Queue waits:** PJM queues average 40 months ([Carbon Direct](https://www.carbon-direct.com/press/carbon-direct-releases-new-analysis-of-power-grid-interconnection-queues-pjm-ercot)), so the asset is time plus collateral.
- **Market size (from S-L6):** about $3.3B of data-centre land sales in Q1 2026, up 141%. Power-ready Texas sites sell for about $800k/acre against $2.5-10k for unstudied land.
- **Verdict:** this is a big, winnable rung once L6 is dropped, but our own backtest shows no ML edge, and the step-up goes to collateral. **13-15/25.** It is fundable as a developer, and that breaks R-ML.

### 3.3 Distressed deeptech plants (N3): the step-down is real, but the restart is the bespoke build

- **Li-Cycle:** after raising more than $1B including Glencore's $327M of converts, Li-Cycle's spokes, the Rochester hub and its IP went to Glencore for a **$46.4M stalking-horse credit bid** ([Torys](https://www.torys.com/work/2025/05/deb56f52-ba11-4698-961a-200a37477d14); [Waste Dive](https://www.wastedive.com/news/glencore-completes-takeover-of-li-cycle-battery-recycling-assets/757199/)).
- **Northvolt:**
  - Assets once valued at about $5B went to Lyten at an undisclosed "substantial discount". The trustee said the sale would not repay creditors.
  - The Swedish part closed only around Feb 2026, and the restart was promised for H1 2026 with no confirmed output ([The Local](https://www.thelocal.se/20260108/whats-happening-with-the-american-takeover-of-swedens-northvolt); [ESG Today](https://www.esgtoday.com/lyten-acquires-bankrupt-battery-giant-northvolt/)).
- **Adversarial points:**
  - The cheap price goes to the **secured creditor** (Glencore credit-bid its own debt) or to a buyer with existing capital (Lyten). A seed company cannot outbid a credit bid.
  - Proving value means restarting a plant that already failed once: L1's "proof is the bespoke build", and it usually fails.
  - ML has no role in finding these assets, because the dockets are front-page news.
- **Revised score: 11/25 (1,4,1,2,3).**

## 5. Answer to the brief

1. **Survivor?** No. The best idea is the China-asset NewCo at 15/25 after checks, tied with powered land at 15.
2. **What now kills it:** **R-ML**, with **L3** close behind. With the legible-outcome and occupied-rung laws both gone, the remaining big step-ups (biotech NewCos at about 20x on upfront, distressed deeptech plants at about 20-100x on asset value, powered land at about 100x on acreage) are all captured by capital, BD access or secured credit. ML reading data is not what unlocks them.
3. **Most interesting finding:** China-sourced NewCos are the cleanest modern example of "buy an unproven right from an owner who cannot capture its Western value". Kailera turned a roughly $100M upfront into a $2-3B listed company in 18 months. But the window closed in about 2023-24: upfronts now run $7B a year and at least 18 funded NewCos compete. The arbitrage was real, it was legible, and it was won by Bain/RTW capital, not models.
4. **Suggested next pair:** R-ML with L3. That would test whether Dhruv will accept a capital-led developer (powered land or a NewCo) with ML as a speed tool. The feasibility test would be whether he can raise a $100M+ first close, which no YC-stage founder without crossover relationships can.
