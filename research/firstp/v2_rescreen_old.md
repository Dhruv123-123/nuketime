# v2 re-screen of the old near-misses (19 Oct 2026)

## Verdict

**v2 does not revive any old idea into a venture-scale company.** Each idea was restructured the way v2 suggests: hold the right, let a partner fund the confirmation, keep a royalty, and build a private-outcome flywheel. On that basis three old ideas now score 16-18/25, up from 11-15 under the gold rubric:

| # | Idea, restructured | v2 score | Status after web check |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | ML crop actives as a composition-of-matter (CoM) prospect generator: file CoM claims on new modes of action, a major licenses and develops them, we keep milestones and a royalty | **18** -> 15 | **Exists already** (AgPlenus, Moa, Enko). Partners do fund it. The companies that do it are small or shrinking |
| 2 | Orphan or lapse-bound patents with a funder: buy at the maintenance-fee price, a funder or aggregator pays for assertion, we keep a share of net proceeds | **17** -> 14 | **Exists already** (Acacia, Fortress/VLSI with NXP keeping a share). Repricing takes years and verdicts can be reversed |
| 3 | Failed or shelved drugs held as rights: buy orphaned Phase 3 assets, a royalty financier or pharma pays for the new trial, we keep a royalty | **16** -> 13 | **Exists already** (Biossil, Pathos). Partners fund trials for the owner who has conviction. P5 fails |

**The principle that kills most of the rest is P1 (option before anyone knows).** In 17 of 25 old ideas the seller already knows enough to price the hope: power land, queue positions, licensors, plant owners, promoters' landowners. Or insiders take the asset (L10). Under v2, L3 does not go away. It turns into P1 = 1-2.

**P4 caps the size of the three that survive.** A prospect generator that keeps a royalty is a **sub-$0.5B business even in mining, where the model was invented**:
- EMX Royalty was worth about **$454M after ~20 years** and was absorbed into Elemental Altus in Nov 2025.
- Altius is worth C$3.2B, but most of that comes from royalties it bought, not ones it generated. Its project-generation equity book is about **C$80M**.

The crop-actives version of the model already shows this ceiling. Evogene (AgPlenus) is worth **$7-10M** after cutting AgPlenus staff by 40%. Moa raised only £22M in its Series C (Jul 2026). Enko's last mark was $320M (2022).

**One positive finding.** The patent system is the free-entry tenure that the no-prospecting rule took away. A CoM filing is a staked claim, priced by fee schedule before anyone knows its value. That is a true P1 = 4-5, and it is why molecules (crop, odorant, drug) and patents float to the top under v2. But in every such field, confirmation is slow: 10+ years for an agchem active, 2-6 years for a patent verdict or a trial. So P2's "fast repricing" fails, and so does the venture clock.

Pattern:
- **High P1 (>=3):** only the IP-shaped ideas and the true orphans: #1-5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 16, 22.
- **Every land/power idea gets P1 <= 2.** v2 punishes them harder than the gold rubric did, because their sellers know.
- **P3 is high only where proofs are confidential by nature:** greenhouse screens, licensing settlements, trial data rooms.
- **P5 is low wherever the outcome label is a rare event,** like drugs and approvals. The FTR book is the opposite: perfect labels, but nothing to option.

## 2. Web checks on the top 3

### 2.1 ML crop actives as a CoM prospect generator (18 -> 15)
- **Does the restructured version already exist? Yes, several times over.**
  - **AgPlenus (Evogene) and Bayer, Feb 2024:** Bayer takes an exclusive licence to molecules on AgPlenus's AI-found APTH1 mode of action. AgPlenus gets "an upfront payment, ongoing research funding, milestone payments and royalties". The CEO declined to give amounts ([Evogene PR](https://evogene.com/press_release/agplenus-announces-licensing-collaboration-agreement-with-bayer-to-develop-a-new-sustainable-weed-control-solution/); [Q1 2024 results](https://www.biospace.com/evogene-reports-first-quarter-2024-financial-results)). AgPlenus also had an earlier Corteva co-development deal (2020) with Corteva holding exclusive rights.
  - **Moa Technology:** R&D deals with Nufarm (exclusive access to one new MoA product), Gowan, Certis Belchim and Corteva. Its £22.2M Series C (Jul 2026) is "supplemented by upfront and milestone payments from industry partners" ([AgNavigator](https://www.agnavigator.com/Article/2026/07/23/moa-wins-backing-for-ambition-to-become-leading-source-of-herbicide-innovation/)).
  - **Enko:** partnerships with Syngenta and Bayer. Nufarm led a $70M Series C at a $320M valuation, for $140M raised in total ([AgriBusiness Global](https://www.agribusinessglobal.com/agtech/enko-raises-70m-to-commercialize-safe-crop-protection-through-machine-learning-based-discovery-technology/)).
- **Do partners fund confirmation on third-party rights? Yes.** Every major in-licenses: Bayer, Corteva, Syngenta, Nufarm. **Terms are never disclosed.** Agchem licence economics are generally believed to be low single-digit to low-teens royalties (estimate, unverified).
- **Is the private flywheel real? Yes, more than anywhere else checked.** Your own greenhouse and target-validation outcomes are seen by nobody else. Partners' field-trial results flow back under the collaboration. But AgPlenus had that flywheel and still shrank.
- **Is it venture scale? No.**
  - Evogene's market cap is $7-10M (Nov 2025 to 2026). It cut AgPlenus's workforce by 40% and folded it into ChemPass ([Evogene 20-F FY2025](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001574565/000117891326001767/zk2634882.htm)).
  - The confirmation takes ~10 years even with AI. That is Syngenta's own estimate: 15 years falls to 10 ([AgriMarketing/WSJ](https://www.agrimarketing.com/s/150448)).
  - The royalty arrives after year 10, and the buyer side is 4-5 majors.
- **Revised scores:** P2 3 -> 2 (repricing isn't fast), P4 4 -> 3 (the partner is a monopsony and terms are thin). **15.**

### 2.2 Orphan patents with a funded assertion carry (17 -> 14)
- **Does the restructured version already exist? Yes; it is the NPE industry.**
  - **Acacia** pays all costs and shares net licensing revenue with patent owners on negotiated terms, commonly cited as 50/50. It says it has returned $727M+ to "patent partners" ([Acacia](https://acaciaresearch.com/patent-licensing); [OCBJ](https://www.ocbj.com/news/acacia-racks-up-revenue-share-price-gains/)).
  - **Fortress/VLSI** bought NXP patents, and NXP keeps a share of damages ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/intel-convinces-jury-fortress-controls-vlsi-in-3-billion-fight)). That is exactly the prospect-generator structure, with a seller who keeps a royalty.
- **Do partners fund confirmation on third-party rights? Yes.**
  - Funders pay costs non-recourse and target 100-300% of what they put in, through a waterfall ([Wiggin](https://www.wiggin.com/publication/assertion-of-patent-rights-and-third-party-funding-a-litigators-perspective/)).
  - **A disclosed example** (SITO Mobile 8-K, 2017): $1.5M up front, repaid first, plus 10% of the balance up to $7.5M, then 2.5% forever ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1285543/000147793217004407/sfor_8k.htm)).
- **Is the private flywheel real? Partly.** Settlement and licence terms are confidential, so an asserter learns which claim-chart patterns pay. Incumbents already hold that data: Acacia, IV and RPX, which has seen the defence side for 15 years.
- **Heavy tail:** real but slow and reversible.
  - VLSI won $2.18B (2021). The verdict was vacated.
  - In May 2025 a jury found Fortress controls VLSI, which may trigger a Finjan licence defence.
  - Repricing takes 3-6 years of litigation, not a standard proof.
- **Revised scores:** P2 3 -> 2, P4 4 -> 3 (the funder takes most of the upside), P5 stays 2 (outcome labels are sealed). **14.** The brokered market is still ~$158M/yr.

### 2.3 Failed or shelved drug, partner-funded trial (16 -> 13)
- **Does the restructured version already exist? Yes.** Biossil bought ridinilazole for $0.5M upfront plus up to $104.5M in milestones, and Galera's assets for $3.5M upfront ([Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/summit-sells-failed-antibiotic-openai-backed-startup-105m-deal)). Pathos is another.
- **Do partners fund confirmation? Yes, at very large scale, but to the holder with conviction.**
  - **Blackstone and Apogee (May 2026):** up to $1.3B, including up to $800M of synthetic royalty. Tiers are low to mid single digits for 15 years, capped above $8B in sales. The money comes in tranches: $100M at signing, $100M at full enrolment, $200M on positive Phase 3 data, then post-approval money ([Fierce](https://www.fiercebiotech.com/biotech/apogee-bags-13b-royalty-funding-deal-push-eczema-challenger-phase-3)).
  - **Blackstone and Merck (Nov 2025):** $700M for low to mid single-digit royalties on sac-TMT ([MarketScreener](https://www.marketscreener.com/news/merck-gets-700-million-from-blackstone-life-sciences-for-development-of-cancer-drug-ce7d5cded08df627)).
  - In these deals the financier takes the royalty and the asset holder keeps the asset. That is the reverse of v2's P4 shape. A holder who wants v2's shape out-licenses to pharma, and pharma pays little for failed-trial assets (the $0.5M price above).
- **Is the private flywheel real? Weak.** Every buyer sees the same data room. Chekroud 2024 (Science) found that pooled trial models perform at chance on other trials, so P5 = 1 stands.
- **Score: 13**, the same as the earlier deep check.

## 3. Which principle kills what

| Principle | Ideas it holds at <=2 | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **P1 option before anyone knows** | 14 of 25 (all land, power, queue, licensor and estate ideas) | The decisive filter. Only fee-schedule rights (patents, CoM filings) and true orphans pass |
| P2 heavy tail plus fast repricing | 18 of 25 | Even the IP survivors fail the *fast* half: confirmation is a 3-15 yr trial, suit or registration, not a drill result |
| P3 private flywheel | 12 of 25 | Real only in screens and confidential settlements |
| P4 partner funds, you keep carry | 3 of 25 | Almost always available. **But it caps scale:** the royalty is 1-5% (agchem, drugs) or a 20-50% net share after the funder's 100-300% return (patents) |
| P5 hindcastable | 18 of 25 | Rare-event outcomes (approvals, verdicts) have thin or sealed labels |

**Answer to "which principle kills the rest": P1 for most of the list, then P2's need for fast repricing for the IP survivors.** P4 is not a killer but a ceiling: prospect generators that keep royalties are sub-$0.5B businesses (EMX ~$454M after two decades; Altius's generated-project book is ~C$80M of its C$3.2B). Terranox's v2 thesis works only because a uranium drill result reprices a claim within months. No old idea has a confirmation that is both cheap for the partner and fast.

## 4. What, if anything, to do
- **No old idea warrants a build.** Crop actives is the best v2 fit and is already a crowded, shrinking niche: Evogene is worth $7-10M.
- **Carry this lesson into new hunts:** look for free-entry or fee-schedule rights (P1 = 5) where confirmation takes months and is paid for by a capitalised partner. In the old set, only mineral tenure had that. The IP analogues fail on time to confirmation, not on structure.
- **Caveats:**
  - Agchem and patent royalty rates are generally undisclosed; the ranges above are estimates.
  - Scores are one scorer's judgement.
  - The EMX market cap is a stale aggregator figure from before the acquisition.
