# Pair sweep: drop L2 (outcome recorded in a legible field) and L3 (seller prices in the hope) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict

**Nothing survives as a real, venture-scale company Dhruv could own.** The best idea after checks scores 13/25: flex-powered land, meaning land optioned at the hope price and powered years early by an ML-run flexible (curtailable) grid connection. Once L2 and L3 are both gone, the right and its value are public and fairly priced, so the edge can only come from **proving faster or more cheaply than anyone else**. That kills discovery as the business (C1 falls to about 2). It also turns the company into a developer that competes on capital, utility relationships and execution. That is exactly where **L6 plus pass 1's "throughput edge is not ownable" (proposed L7)** take over: Cloverleaf ($300M plus an Nvidia stake) holds powered land. Emerald AI ($1.05B valuation, Aug 2026) and GridCARE ($64M Series A, May 2026) sell the ML prover to any landholder, so a site owner gets no lasting edge from it. Behind those, the binding killers are utility control (L4: no standard flexible-connection contract exists, and the utility insists on holding the breaker) and R-ML, because ML is the edge in only 3 of 18 new ideas.

## How the rubric changes with both laws dropped

- **C4 (data and right):** may now reach 3 even when the signal sits in a public field (headroom maps, the EIA, queues, the Orange Book) and the right is bought at hope price or held through an option, JV or royalty. It does not reach 4. Nothing about the right is hidden, and the seller is informed, so no part of the edge comes from the purchase.
- **C2 (step-up):** measured from the hope price paid, or from the option fee. A legible, priced asset only steps up when the company itself performs a hard proof. A step-up from simply buying cheap no longer exists.
- **C3 (lever):** the only lever left is on the proving step: how much ML cuts its cost or time, or raises its odds. Discovery lift scores 0.
- **C1 (frame):** this is where the pair bites. With nothing to discover, "discovery is 80% of the work" fails by definition, so C1 is at most 2-3. It reaches 3 only when ML *is* the proof, not discovery.
- **Kept:** L1, L4, L5, L6, R-ML, own the asset, no prospecting or drilling, not Terranox, $1B+.

## 3. Adversarial checks on the top 3

### A. N1 flex-powered land (16 raw): the strongest frame in pass 2, and it dies on L6/L7 and L4. Revised 13.

**The step-up is real and sourced:**
- Altus Group, 80 transactions: committed data-centre load is the strongest price variable. Median $2.8M/acre in Northern Virginia and $100-460k/acre in Central Ohio, Phoenix and Atlanta, against **$28k/acre in markets with no committed load**, so about 100x. Sites within half a mile of a high-voltage substation trade at about 2x sites 2-5 miles away ([Altus](https://www.altusgroup.com/insights/where-value-is-created-in-data-center-land/); [DCK](https://datacenterknowledge.com/data-center-construction/ai-data-center-land-buying-hits-6b-as-power-premiums-rise)).
- Texas: about $800k/acre power-ready against $2.5-10k/acre unstudied.
- Land purchases for future data centres reached about $6B in H1 2026, up 79%.
- Buying at a hope price of 5-10x farm value still leaves a 5-30x step-up once committed load is proven.

**The headroom exists and is published (this is L2 dropped):**
- Duke's *Rethinking Load Growth*: **76 GW at 0.25% curtailment, 98 GW at 0.5% and 126 GW at 1.0%**, with average events of 1.7-2.5 h. PJM has 18 GW at 0.5% ([Duke PDF](https://nicholasinstitute.duke.edu/sites/default/files/publications/rethinking-load-growth.pdf); [S&P](https://www.spglobal.com/market-intelligence/en/news-insights/articles/2025/2/us-grid-could-serve-100-gw-of-added-load-through-modest-curtailments-study-87513622)).
- Critique: system-wide headroom is not deliverability at a specific node ([Handshy](https://medium.com/@brianwhandshy/dukes-rethinking-load-growth-study-is-being-misused-in-the-interconnection-debate-b7d3d981e8ca)).
- Camus, encoord and Princeton modelled 500 MW sites in PJM connecting in 2 years with 20% non-firm load, against 5-7 years for firm ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/flexible-grid-connections-byoc-data-centers-utilities/807047/)).

**Who holds the prover (L6/L7):**
- **Emerald AI** raised a $150M Series A at a **$1.05B valuation** (Aug 2026, DCVC/Energize, with Nvidia and GE Vernova), about $215-220M in total. It runs the first Flexible Load Interconnection Program, with Silicon Valley Power, and a 96-100 MW Manassas site with Digital Realty and Nvidia, tested with Dominion and PJM ([TNW](https://thenextweb.com/news/emerald-ai-150m-funding-data-centre-grid-flexibility); [DCVC](https://www.dcvc.com/news-insights/dcvc-co-leads-emerald-ais-150-million-series-a-round-to-transform-data-centers-into-intelligent-grid-responsive-assets)).
- **GridCARE** raised a $64M Series A (Sutter Hill, May 2026). It unlocked 400 MW for five data centres at Portland General and supports about 2 GW across a dozen markets. It is software and owns no sites ([SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/05/15/gridcare-raises-64m-speed-ai-data-center-projects/)).
- **Verrus** (Sidewalk Infrastructure Partners) is the owner-operator version, with campuses in Salem, Oregon and in Michigan ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/alphabet-spin-out-verrus-plans-three-building-data-center-campus-outside-portland-oregon/)). Camus sells FlexConnect to Duke, Edison and PG&E.
- **Cloverleaf** holds the firm powered-land rung.
- Emerald and GridCARE are vendors, so every landholder, Cloverleaf included, can buy the prover. Owning the site gives no ownable ML edge, which is exactly pass 1's L7.

**Utility control (L4/L1):**
- SVP's COO calls utility control of the breaker "non-negotiable".
- As of 26 June 2026 there was no standard binding flexible-connection agreement. The only field result is Emerald's own 256-GPU Phoenix test (25% cut for 3 h) ([implicator](https://www.implicator.ai/emerald-ai-150-million-flexible-data-center-grid-loads/)).
- Texas SB6 already makes curtailment and remote disconnect mandatory for new loads of 75 MW and up, and the PUCT requires $50k/MW security, so in ERCOT "flex" is the price of entry, not an edge ([Utility Dive](https://utilitydive.com/news/texas-law-gives-grid-operator-power-to-disconnect-data-centers-during-crisi/751587); [mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/09/21/texas-cuts-the-data-center-grid-deposit-from-100000-to-50000-per-mw-during-its-own-queue-pause/)).
- The flexible allocation goes to the **load customer**, so a landholder without a tenant cannot prove it (C1).

**Result:** C1 3→2, C3 3→2 (the prover is rented, not owned) and C5 4→4. Revised: 2-3-2-3-3 = **13**.

### B. N5 / old #2 queue positions and closed-plant capture (14): dies on L4. Revised 11.

- **No public price for a position:** none exists per MW. Whole powered campuses trade at about $12-18M/MW (DC Hub, unverified), up from about $5M/MW in 2019 ([DC Hub](https://dchub.cloud/state-of-the-data-center)).
- **Transfer is blocked:** about **1 in 5 signed data-centre LOIs now fails, mostly on interconnection consent clauses, not valuation**, and Dominion, Georgia Power and Oncor have tightened assignment ([Datacentres.com](https://www.datacentres.com/news/why-data-centre-deals-die-at-exclusivity-the-ground-lease-and-interconnection-cl-slot3-2026-09-06)). The right does not travel cleanly to a buyer (L4).
- **Closed plants:** at the hope price, a closed plant costs what Hawesville did: $200M plus 6.8% equity for 480 MW, about $0.42M/MW (S-L2). The only remaining step-up is the $10-12M/MW build, which is TeraWulf's rung.
- **ML:** the upgrade-cost model ties a lookup (0.721 vs 0.727, S-L6), so R-ML fails.

### C. N3 curtailed-node flexible compute (the cleanest "ML schedules around a legible price" idea): too small. Revised 9.

- **Scale:** Soluna, the public pure play, has **123 MW energised**. Its 100 MW Dorothy campus sits behind the meter at a 150 MW wind farm, and its 300 MW AI expansion projects only $6-11M year-one EBITDA ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251113380316/en/Soluna-Completes-Project-Dorothy-2-Increasing-Operational-Capacity-by-64-to-123-MW/); [Soluna news](https://www.solunacomputing.com/news/page/6/)).
- **The edge erodes:** Soluna is seeking ERCOT "baseload" classification for Dorothy (conditional, Batch Zero), so the curtailment edge fades as the site goes firm.
- **Wrong workload:** AI tenants want uptime, and Emerald's own test only flexed batch training.
- **Step-up:** about 1-2x on cheap power. "Too small" kills it.

## 4. What kills what, once both laws are gone

| Law or rule | Binding share (top 20 by score) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| L6 + L7 (rung held; the ML prover is sold as a vendor product) | ~60% | Cloverleaf, Emerald, GridCARE, Earthrise, Formation, FTAI, Roivant |
| C1 (no discovery left; the proof is a build or negotiation) | ~55% | Closed plants, dams, BESS, trials, engines |
| R-ML (ML is ranking, dispatch or nothing) | ~50% | Queue buyer (AUC tie), turbines, miners, solar portfolios |
| L4 (utility, RTO or FDA controls the proof or the transfer) | ~35% | Breaker control, assignment consent, same-site CIRs, FERC co-location |
| L1 (cheap proofs pass) | ~20% | SIS, by-right, rezoning, UK promotion |
| Too small / step-up under 3x | ~30% | Soluna, EV packs, GPU leases, solar portfolios |

**Structural result:** with L2 and L3 gone, "own the valuable thing you find" becomes "own the valuable thing you *prove*". The prover is then either a build (C1 fails) or ML that a vendor can sell to every competitor (L7). So **L7, "a throughput/prover edge is not ownable", is the law that kills this pair**, with L6 as its visible face. Pass 3 should pair L6 with R-ML, or test L7 directly: an idea survives only if the ML prover cannot be bought, for example because it needs operating data only an owner-operator accumulates.

## 5. Most interesting finding

Flexible interconnection is the first place in nine hunts where **ML is the proof, not the search**: the utility grants capacity because a controller guarantees curtailment. The value gap is public and huge: about $28k/acre with no committed load against $0.46-2.8M with it (Altus). A $1.05B company (Emerald) formed around this in 18 months. Emerald chose to be the vendor, not the owner. An owner-operator that keeps its curtailment track record (event logs, verified response) as a proprietary asset would be the only way to make the prover ownable. Feasibility test for that variant: get one utility (an SVP-type muni, or a co-op outside ERCOT) to state in writing that a landholder with no tenant can hold a flexible-load allocation and assign it. If none will, the idea is dead on L4.
