# Sweep L2: what happens if we drop "someone always records the outcome"

Written 2026-10-19. This sweep relaxes law L2 only. Every other law and standing rule still applies.

## Verdict

**Nothing survives as a venture-scale company you could own.** Dropping L2 raises C4 by about 1-2 points on ideas killed for "the answer is in a legible field". But it brings L3 straight back: if the field is public, the owner can read it too, and the price already reflects it. Hawesville is the clearest case. An idle smelter with 480 MW of power sold for $200M plus a 6.8% stake, so the owner captured the value. The only pockets where owners really ignore a public field are small rights whose value is then unlocked by a bespoke physical step. Dormant generic drug approvals (ANDAs) sold for about $150k each are the example, so C1 and the ML rule fail instead. Under this relaxation ML becomes a join, ranking and speed engine rather than the thing that reads the answer. That fails "ML must make the pain solvable" almost every time, because a SQL join plus a fast deal team does the same job. Best score: 14/25 before verification, 10-11 after.

## How C4 is rescored under this relaxation

Old C4 asked for the outcome to sit in no legible field. New C4 asks four things:
- the signal may be legible;
- the edge must come from speed, scale, a join across jurisdictions nobody makes, or owners who ignore a public field;
- the right must still be cheap to buy;
- someone other than the owner must be able to capture the step-up.

A legible outcome now costs nothing, but "the owner can read it too" is charged in full, so L3 bites harder.

**ML rule:** in every idea below, ML is at best a ranker or join engine. When a deterministic join would do, I take 1 point off C3.

## 3. Top 3: adversarial checks with sources

### #1 Closed-plant load capture (N1 and old #3): dies on L3 and L6

**What the deals show**
- **Hawesville (Kentucky):** Century Aluminum idled the smelter in 2022 and sold it to TeraWulf for **$200M cash plus a 6.8% stake**, closing on 2 Feb 2026, for a 750-acre site with **480 MW** ([Messenger-Inquirer](https://www.messenger-inquirer.com/news/local/data-center-firm-buys-century-aluminum-site-for-200m/article_5e3f4a60-e23b-57a3-ad27-e59a14145006.html); [Spectrum News](https://spectrumnews1.com/ky/louisville/news/2026/02/07/hawesville-ai-database.html)).
  - That is about **$0.42M/MW** to the seller.
  - TeraWulf's build cost is $10-12M/MW ([Stocktwits/KY PSC](https://stocktwits.com/news-articles/markets/equity/terawulf-regulatory-green-light-for-45b-kentucky-ai/cZYKjOhRJFR)).
  - Century itself bought the plant in 2001 for $450M ([GlobeSt](https://www.globest.com/amp/2001/04/04/california-firm-buys-kentucky-aluminum-plant/)).
  - The owner read the legible field (energized MW) and kept the value, plus equity upside.
- **Eastalco (Maryland, 2021):** Alcoa sold the land for $100M to Quantum Loophole/TPG ([SEC exhibit](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1675149/000115752321000827/a52452003ex99_1.htm)).
- **Other deals:** Aligned announced a $1.5B campus on the closed Pixelle Chillicothe mill (July 2026). Amp Z holds an option on the 1,041-acre Lufkin paper mill (55 MW) ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/opinions/the-powered-mill-10gw-of-shovel-ready-data-center-sites-is-hiding-in-plain-sight/), via search summary).

**Base rate:** a DCD op-ed counts **~10 GW across ~220 operating US mill sites**, and its title says the sites are "hiding in plain sight". The thesis is already published in the trade press.

**Verdict:** the signal is legible and widely watched. Sellers are public companies with bankers, and the step-up from the seller's view is about 1x on a fair price. There is no 10x+ purchase discount. Whatever speed edge exists is a deal-sourcing edge (a brokerage), not ML. **Revised score: 10/25.**

### #2 CIR clock arbitrage in PJM (N2 and old #1): dies on L4 and L3

**Price signal:** the 2027/28 PJM capacity auction cleared at the cap of **$333.44/MW-day** (uncapped price **$529.80**), the second year in a row at the cap. The auction fell 6,623 MW short of the reliability requirement ([Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/business/pjm-capacity-auction-hits-price-cap-again-as-region-falls-short-of-reliability-target/); [Calpine](https://calpinesolutions.com/2025/12/18/)).

**The clock:** a generator **loses its CIRs one year after deactivation** unless a new request is filed ([PJM Willey response](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/planning/gen-retire/deactivation-notices/willey-battery-unit-pjm-response.pdf)).

**The 2026 replacement path is site-locked:**
- FERC accepted the replacement process effective 30 Jan 2026 ([FERC order ER26-403](https://www.pjm.com/pjmfiles/directory/etariff/FercOrders/8585/20260129-er26-403-001.pdf)).
- It requires the **same interconnection substation and voltage**, with CIRs no larger than the retiring unit's ([PJM IPS deck](https://pjm.com/-/media/committees-groups/subcommittees/ips/2024/20240422/20240422-enhance-cir-transfer-efficiency---pjm-solution-package.ashx)).
- Incumbents fight for even modest flexibility: Constellation sought a waiver to move Eddystone CIRs to Crane, and PJM opposed it citing the one-year rule ([ER26-2028](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/documents/ferc/filings/2026/20260421-er26-2028-000.pdf)).

**What owners pay:** 2025 PJM gas plants traded at **~$1,025/kW** (Capital Power, 2,147 MW for $2.2B) and **~$1,340/kW** (Talen, ~2,567 MW for $3.45B) ([Fidelity/Capital Power](https://fidelity.com/news/article/us-markets/202504141705MIDNIGHTUSEQUITY_A3352724); [Power Eng/Talen](https://www.power-eng.com/?p=136022)). Plant values have nearly doubled in five years (Enverus via [IndustrialInfo](https://www.industrialinfo.com/iirenergy/industry-news/article/cps-aep-join-buying-bonanza-for-us-natural-gas-fired-plants--346017)).

**Base rate:** deactivations are small and lumpy. Examples: Delaware 412 MW and Illinois 300 MW in 2025, with 2,699 MW of future Illinois notices ([PJM IL report](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/library/reports-notices/state-specific-reports/2025/illinois.pdf)). Several retirements were withdrawn (Morgantown, Fisk, Waukegan), meaning owners re-read the price and stayed.

**Verdict:** the right is tied to owning the point of interconnection. The sellers are IPPs and utilities pricing at $1,000+/kW, and RTO rules block windfalls (L4). Nobody here is an uninformed seller. **Revised score: 9/25.**

### #3 Discontinued ANDA × shortage relaunch (N3): the cheap right is real, but C1 and the ML rule kill it

**The cheap right is real:** Elite Pharmaceuticals sold **3 hydroxyzine ANDAs for $450k (~$150k each)**, below their $787k carrying value, citing GDUFA fees as "the primary catalyst" ([Elite 10-K FY2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1053369/000121390019011224/f10k2019_elitepharma.htm)). Marketed ANDAs, by contrast, go for $16-53M (Perrigo) and about $44M each (Dr. Reddy's, 8 for $350M) ([Perrigo 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1585364/000158536421000067/prgo-20210403.htm); [DRL 6-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001135951/000119312517250983/d439308d6k.htm)). So a 10-300x gap exists on paper between a dormant ANDA and a marketed one.

**Why it fails:**
- **The gap is the manufacturing.** Tech transfer, a CBE-30 or prior-approval supplement, and running a plant are the proof. That is bespoke and slow (C1 = 2). ANI's own playbook needed US plants and the WellSpring acquisition to re-commercialise ([Citeline](https://generics.citeline.com/GB001451/ANI-eyes-more-deals-to-follow-WellSpring)).
- **The rung is already held (L6).**
  - ANI bought 31 dormant Teva ANDAs in 2013 and 22 more for $25M in 2015 ([GaBI](https://gabionline.net/layout/set/print/generics/news/ANI-Pharmaceuticals-acquires-31-generics-from-Teva)).
  - Civica publicly asks generic makers for retiring ANDAs ([BusinessWire](https://businesswire.com/news/home/20200114005666/en/To-Generic-Drug-Manufacturers-Considering-Retiring-or-Withdrawing-ANDAs-for-Essential-Medicines-Civica-Rx-Is-Interested)).
  - Premier's ProvideGx revives short supply.
- **Shortages are being drugs nobody wants to make.** USP's 2025 count of active shortages fell from 98 to 75, but discontinuations are at their highest since 2019, driven by low prices ([CIDRAP](https://www.cidrap.umn.edu/resilient-drug-supply/us-drug-shortages-have-declined-now-drag-average-5-years-report-shows)). The legible shortage flag tends to mark products with structurally low margins.
- **ML is not needed.** Joining the Orange Book with the shortage list is a spreadsheet, and ANI did it by hand.

**Revised score: 10/25.**

## 4. What kills what, under this relaxation

| Law or rule | How often it is the binding killer once L2 is gone | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| L3 (the seller prices in the hope) | Most often. A legible field makes the seller informed. | Hawesville, powered land, CIRs, miners, CRP, Gate 2 |
| "ML must make it solvable" | Almost always weakened. A join or speed edge is a deal team, not ML. | ANDA, tax-delinquent × headroom, trademarks |
| C1 (the physical step dominates) | Second most common | Pipelines, ANDA manufacturing, hydro, transformers, mitigation banks |
| L4 (public bodies reprice or block windfalls) | Rights tied to a site or a holder | CIR same-site rule, FCC spectrum, ITU filings, slots, GSA |
| L6 (the rung is already held) | Everywhere the signal is good | TeraWulf, Aligned, Amp Z, ANI, Civica, Cloverleaf, Tract, Jupiter |
| L1 (cheap proofs usually succeed) | Unchanged | UK land promotion, mitigation banks, continuations |

**Bottom line:** L2 is not load-bearing. Each idea it killed has a second killer. Relaxing it mainly turns "discover a hidden asset" into "act faster on a public list". That is a sourcing or brokerage business, not an ML company that owns the asset. **The relaxation weakens the ML rule more than it strengthens C4.**
