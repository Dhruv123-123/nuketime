# Pass 4 quad: drop L1 (proof is the build), L3 (seller prices hope), L6 (rung held) and R-ML (ML must make it solvable), 19 Oct 2026

## Verdict

**No survivor, and what comes closest is not Terranox-shaped. It is an ordinary developer or manufacturer.**

With all four laws dropped, very little is left to filter on:
- L2: the outcome must sit in data nobody summarises.
- L4: public bodies.
- L5: nature owned.
- The standing rules: no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, venture scale.
- L8, L9 and L10, which are real. L7 and L11 lapse because both are about ML being the edge.

Even so, only five ideas in sector clear "read unsummarised data, then own the asset". After the web checks the best scores 14-15/25:

| Idea | After check | What kills it |
|---|---|---|
| **Flex micro-sites** (5-20 MW at half-loaded distribution substations) | ~15 | L4: a tenantless SPV cannot hold or assign the capacity. L8: Prologis plus EPRI go direct to utilities |
| **Netting-window gas sites (N1, new):** read EPA hourly emissions data and Title V permits to find retired coal sites whose creditable shutdown decreases let new gas generation "net out" of PSD review | 14 | L10 and pool size (see below) |
| **Records-orphaned LLPs, two-exit combination (N15)** | 13 | Records are private, not public |
| **Orphaned isotope-process plants (N3), the ASP Isotopes pattern** | 13 | Too small |

The netting mechanism behind N1 is real. Homer City's permit used the 2021-22 baseline of the retired coal boilers to net out of PSD for NOx (-889 tpy) and CO (-2,992 tpy). Approval came about 7.5 months after application. But N1 dies on three counts:
- **L10, insiders:** the sites are held by the owners who bought them before the boom (Homer City; Frontier at Mansfield, bought in 2022).
- **The pool is shrinking:** US coal retirements fell from 13.7 GW in 2022 to 2.6 GW in 2025.
- **The read is a week of an air consultant's time:** any owner who has a reason to ask can get it done.

**Is it still recognisably Terranox?** No. Terranox is three things together:
1. A machine reads an archive too big for people to read (SMAD).
2. It buys rights from owners who never look.
3. It proves a 10^3x step-up.

Dropping R-ML removes part 1. That turns the illegible read into a one-off expert task: one air-permit netting analysis, one records reconstruction, one capacity inquiry. Owners commission that task themselves once they have a reason to.

Dropping L3 removes the cheap right in part 2. Dropping L1 turns the proof into the build: a gas plant, a restart, a recertification.

What remains in every case is a recognisable existing business type, already practised by named firms:

| Business type | Who already does it |
|---|---|
| Coal-to-gas campus developer | Frontier, Homer City Redevelopment |
| Micro-site land developer | Astro (YC W25), Prologis |
| Aero part-out house | AerSale, FTAI |
| Isotope manufacturer | ASP Isotopes |
| Contract-built transformer brand | Ayr Energy |

None is a new company shape.

**Proposed law L12, the main finding of this pass: R-ML and L2 are one law, not two.** Illegibility protects a buyer only at volume. A human reader can do a few reads a year. An owner who suspects value pays a specialist to read its own file in a week. So L2 without ML is a delay, not an edge. Terranox's edge was never "the data is illegible". It was "the data is illegible **and** too large for anyone but a model to read **and** the owners have no reason to commission the read". Once R-ML is dropped, any idea that keeps L2 drifts back to a consultant-plus-capital developer.

## How the quad changes scoring
- **C1:** an expensive bespoke proof (a plan approval, restart, recertification, plant build or trial) costs nothing (L1 off). What still scores is a discrete rung with a capitalised buyer.
- **C2:** the step-up is measured against the price actually paid, including the hope price (L3 off).
- **C3:** any lever counts: expertise, capital, speed, relationships or ML. It is still capped at 1/base hit rate (R-ML off).
- **C4:** **L2 is kept.** The outcome must sit in no legible summary field. The signal may be public-but-unsummarised or private-but-unread. The right only has to be buyable, even at the hope price.
- **C5:** occupied rungs are fine (L6 off).
- **Laws still live:**
  - L4 (public bodies).
  - L5.
  - L8 (the end buyer funds the proof).
  - L9 (a policy-shock premium has a half-life).
  - L10 (insiders buy orphaned hardware).
  - Venture scale, own the asset, not Terranox, no prospecting or drilling.
- **Laws that lapse:**
  - L7 (vendor ML) only bites when ML is the edge.
  - L11 (an accepted ML proof is a method open to all) only bites when the proof is ML. Its general form still applies: an accepted method is open to everyone, and that is part of why the netting read is not scarce.
- **Extra columns:**
  - **L2?** Does the idea still need an unsummarised read? Y / partly / N.
  - **PS:** can a pre-seed team win rung one?
  - **T:** is it Terranox-shaped? Y / partly / N.

## 4. Adversarial web checks on the top three

### A. N1 / N6: netting-window gas sites. The mechanism is real; the opportunity is held by insiders and shrinking. Revised to 14.
- **The mechanism is verified.** Pennsylvania DEP's technical review memo for Homer City (plan approval 32-00457A, signed 18 Nov 2025) says: "The applicant has taken creditable emission decreases from the recent retirement of the former coal-fired boilers. Based on a baseline of actual emissions from 2021-2022 ... net change of -889.0 tpy for NOx and -2,991.7 tpy for CO, which allows the project to 'net out' of PSD review for these pollutants." ([PA DEP memo](https://files.dep.state.pa.us/regionalresources/NWRO/NWROPortalFiles/Homer-City/Air/Final-AQ-Plan-Approval/Tech%20Review%20Memo%20Signed%2020251118.pdf))
  - The application went in on 4 Apr 2025 and was approved on 18 Nov 2025. The project is 4.4-4.7 GW: 7 combined-cycle turbines plus 10 aeroderivatives.
- **Limits of the netting:**
  - PSD still applied to PM, PM2.5, H2SO4 and GHG.
  - NNSR still required buying **369 t of VOC ERCs** (same memo).
  - So netting removes part of the permit path, not all of it.
- **The owners are insiders who bought early.**
  - Homer City's coal plant shut in mid-2023, and its own owners are redeveloping it with a $5M state gas-line grant ([Utility Dive](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/homer-city-gas-fired-power-station-data-center-firstenergy/744332/), [ABC/AP](https://abcnews.go.com/amp/Technology/wireStory/pennsylvanias-largest-coal-fired-power-plant-now-retired-120412657)).
  - Frontier Group bought Bruce Mansfield in 2022 from Energy Harbor. It plans 2.7 GW of gas, and Aligned broke ground on a 2 GW campus in Sep 2026 ([DCD](https://www.datacenterdynamics.com/en/news/shippingport-industrial-park/), [Daily Signal](https://www.dailysignal.com/?p=232394)).
  - Both are L10, and both moved before 2024.
- **The pool is shrinking.**
  - US coal retirements: 13.7 GW in 2022, 2.6 GW across 4 plants in 2025. In 2025, 4.8 GW of planned retirements were delayed and 1.1 GW cancelled, with DOE 202(c) orders holding plants open ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/TodayinEnergy/detail.php?id=67427)).
  - The 5-year contemporaneous window means 2021-22 retirements expire in 2026-27.
- **L2-lite:** LandGate already markets "redevelopment of retired coal sites for data centers" ([LandGate](https://landgate.com/news/redevelopment-of-retired-coal-sites-brownfields-for-data-centers)). The *netting* number is not summarised anywhere, but a Trinity- or ERM-type air consultant computes it from the hourly emissions data in days once asked. **That is L12.**
- **Rescore:** C1 3, C2 3 (site value vs hope price; owners already market these), C3 2 (expertise anyone can hire), C4 2, C5 4 = **14**.
- **Business type:** a coal-to-gas campus developer, the Frontier and Homer City model. That is a capital developer, not Terranox.

### B. N3: orphaned isotope-process plants. L3 inversion verified, but the market is small and the edge is people. Revised to 13.
- **The orphan price is real.** ASP South Africa bought "a dormant Silicon-28 aerodynamic separation processing plant from Klydon for ZAR 6,000,000, roughly $354,000" ([SEC 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1921865/000147793223006112/aspi_10q.htm)). ASPI's market cap was about $640-694M in 2025; that figure is stale ([Yahoo](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/quote/ASPI)).
- **Revenue is small.** About $6M in 2025, with ~$14M projected for 2026, mostly from PET Labs radiopharmacy. ASPI targets $300M EBITDA by 2031, which is a management target ([Panabee CMD summary](https://www.panabee.com/news/asp-isotopes-targets-300-million-ebitda-by-2031-at-inaugural-capital-markets-day)).
- **Prices are high but markets are small.**
  - Yb-176 up to $30,000/g, with Russia holding >50% of centrifuge capacity ([UroToday PSMA 2026](https://www.urotoday.com/video-lectures/psma-and-beyond-2026-conference-videos/video/5511-market-and-raw-materials-stable-isotopes-why-are-they-so-important-and-where-to-find-them-presentation-nicolas-mario.html)).
  - Stable isotopes for semiconductors are about $186M (2025), from a syndicated report, so low confidence.
  - Venture scale only comes through HALEU and Li-6 (ASPI's QLE spin-off), which brings back L4: NRC and DOE pick incumbents.
- **The read was not the edge.** ASP won with ex-AEC engineers and the South African enrichment legacy. OSTI reports are public domain, so anyone can read them (the L5 analogue: nobody owns the knowledge). The rung is held by ASP; L6 is off here, but ASP would bid on the next orphan too.
- **Rescore:** C1 2, C2 4, C3 2, C4 2, C5 3 = **13**. Business type: a specialty isotope manufacturer.

### C. N15 / old #4: trace-broken engines and LLPs. The step-up is real per part, but the read is private. Revised to 13.
- **A broken trace means scrap.** "A disc worth a quarter of a million dollars becomes scrap" when its history cannot be proven. Lessors, who control more than half the fleet, enforce back-to-birth. Record gaps cut engine value 20-40%, per an advisory source ([SAS Sofia 2026](https://sassofia.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Review-of-Hard-Time-Life-Limited-Condition-Monitored-and-On-Condition-Requirements.pdf), [FlightLogger](https://www.flightlogger.net/glossary/life-limited-parts), [Safe Fly](https://safefly.aero/?p=15434)).
- **But at engine level** our earlier verification found a full serviceable CFM56 LLP stack at $1.4-1.8M, and teardown engines already sell for $1.2-1.8M. So the whole-engine step-up is under 2x. Only individual scrapped parts carry the ~100x.
- **Where the records sit.** They are operator records (14 CFR 91.417) in past operators' and shops' archives. An outsider cannot read them before buying. Part-out houses (AerSale, FTAI) already attempt trace recovery and already own the ground-power exit.
- We found no public volume figure for trace-recoverable scrap LLPs; a search for ProvenAir returned nothing.
- **Rescore:** C1 3, C2 3, C3 2, C4 2, C5 3 = **13**. Business type: an aero USM part-out house.

## 5. Answer

1. **Which in-sector ideas still need an unsummarised read plus an owned asset, with this little left?** Five, in the energy-site, nuclear-hardware and aero-hardware families:
   - flex micro-sites;
   - netting-window gas sites (N1/N6);
   - trace-broken engines (N15);
   - orphaned isotope plants (N3);
   - records-orphaned nuclear components (N4).

   After checks the best are about 14-15/25, and none clears L10 plus size plus L12. The family where L2 truly binds is **site development behind a utility or air-agency allocation**. There the unread fact is a regulatory number:
   - substation headroom;
   - PSD netting headroom;
   - an orphaned upgrade.

2. **Survivor?** No.
   - Small-rung variants (N6, flex micro-sites) are pre-seed winnable, but they are throughput developers that need 30-100 sites a year. That brings back L8 (Prologis plus EPRI; Crusoe and VoltaGrid develop direct).
   - Large variants (N1, N2) are capital developers whose sites are held by insiders who bought before 2024 (L10).

3. **Terranox-shaped or ordinary?** **Ordinary.** Every surviving idea is a known business type with named practitioners:
   - coal-to-gas campus developer: Frontier, Homer City;
   - micro-site or renewables land developer: Astro (YC W25);
   - part-out house: AerSale, FTAI;
   - isotope manufacturer: ASP;
   - contract-built transformer brand: Ayr.

   The Terranox triad (machine-scale read of a public archive, owners who never look, 10^3x step-up) is gone from all of them. The cause is that the quad dropped R-ML. That made the read a one-off expert job, which owners commission themselves (L12).

4. **If Dhruv still wants a kill test for the best in-sector thread (N6/N1, under $40k, about 6 weeks):**
   - Pull EPA hourly emissions data (free API) for every fossil unit retired 2021-26.
   - Compute 2-year baseline actual NOx, CO, SO2 and PM per stationary source.
   - Subtract the potential emissions of a reference 50 MW reciprocating-engine project and a 300 MW aeroderivative project.
   - List the sources that net out. Tag each owner as insider (IPP, utility, PE) or orphaned (muni, co-op, bankruptcy estate), and check whether it is already marketed (LandGate, press).
   - Pay an air-permit consultant about $15k to verify 3 netting analyses.
   - Call 10 orphaned owners.

   **Pass:** at least 5 sources net out, are not under development, are held by orphaned owners, and at least 2 of those owners will grant an option or equity SPV. **Kill:** fewer than 5, or every viable source is already held by its developer. Our expectation is the kill, given the Homer City and Mansfield pattern.
