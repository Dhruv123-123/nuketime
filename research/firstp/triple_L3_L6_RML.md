# Triple sweep: drop L3 (seller prices hope), L6 (rung held) and R-ML (ML must make it solvable) (2026-10-19)

## Verdict
**No clean survivor. The best is 16/25, conditional, and it is not a pre-seed company in its big form.**

With all three laws dropped, this is the "capital-led developer with ML as a speed tool" space. The idea can pay the owner's hope price or give the owner equity, enter a rung Cloverleaf or Biossil already holds, and win on capital, relationships and speed. The space is real and fundable:
- "Wildcatters" are about 50% of ERCOT large-load requests.
- Cloverleaf sold a 1.3 GW site to Vantage for $200M.
- Investors "look for powered-land developers" rather than buying data centres directly.

Three things the triple does not relax still kill it.
1. **L4: the utility owns the proof.** Capacity is allocated to a load customer, transfers need consent, and Texas froze every pending data-centre interconnection on 3 Aug 2026 pending an audit with no end date.
2. **Capital, the standing "own it / venture-scale" test seen from a pre-seed's side.** The big rung now needs $50k/MW of security in ERCOT plus a $100k study fee, and per Affinius "hundreds of millions" and investment-grade credit for a formal power commitment. No pre-seed team clears that.
3. **L8: the end buyer goes direct**, and C2 measured against the hope price is small. On the small rung a pre-seed *can* afford (5-20 MW micro-sites at distribution substations), Prologis plus EPRI already go straight to utilities. The step-up is about 2-5x on asset value and only reaches 10x+ as option or JV leverage on a ~$100k cash outlay, on $1-5M per site.

**Answer to the extra question:**
- **Smallest winnable rung:** one 5-20 MW micro-site, meaning land under option or JV plus written utility capacity confirmation at a half-loaded distribution substation, sold or contributed to an inference operator. It costs about $50-150k and takes 6-12 months.
- **Is the step-up still 10x+?** Only on the cash, not on the asset. It is roughly 10-40x on cash, roughly 2-5x against the hope price, and about 100x against farm value. The farm-value multiple is not ours to keep once L3 is dropped.

Venture scale then needs about 100 sites a year, which is a throughput business. That brings back L7 (the throughput tool is sold, e.g. LandGate) and L8.

Binding law once the triple is dropped: **L4, with L8 and capital access close behind.** The best idea is **flex micro-sites (N18, 16 before checks, about 14 after)**.

## How the triple changes the rubric
- **C2:** measured against the hope price, or as leverage on the option fee or equity cheque actually paid (L3 off).
- **C3:** credits any discovery or proving lever: capital, relationships, speed, and ML as a ranker. It is still capped at 1/base hit rate and only counts for finding or proving winners, not for building them (R-ML off).
- **C4:** a hope-priced, optioned, JV or retained-equity right counts as buyable. "Illegible" can mean private knowledge gained by asking (for example a utility capacity inquiry), as long as no public field records the outcome. L2 is kept.
- **C5:** an occupied rung is winnable if a second entrant has won one before (L6 off).
- **Kept:** L1, L2, L4, L5, the C1 frame, no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, a $1B+ path. **Added test:** can a pre-seed YC team actually win the first rung? This is reported separately from /25.

## 4. Adversarial web checks on the top three

### A. Flex micro-sites, 5-20 MW (N18/N1, 15-16): the real pre-seed rung, but small and contested. Revised about 14.

**Demand exists and is getting organised:**
- EPRI, Prologis, Nvidia and InfraPartners (Feb 2026) target **5-20 MW** micro data centres "at or near utility substations with available grid capacity", using "existing but underused distribution headroom", with at least 5 pilot sites by end-2026 ([DCK](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/build-design/nvidia-epri-prologis-infrapartners-team-up-to-build-smaller-data-centers), [Latitude](https://www.latitudemedia.com/industry-news/epri-nvidia-prologis-and-infrapartners-announce-collaboration-to-produce-smaller-data-centers-for-the-ai-era/), [HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/off-the-wire/epri-launches-distributed-inference-data-center-pilot-with-nvidia-and-prologis/)).
- Hitachi Energy splits AI capacity into 20-100 MW regional inference hubs and sub-10 MW edge nodes, with inference growing from 20.9 GW to 93.3 GW over 2025-30 ([Hitachi Energy](https://www.hitachienergy.com/news-and-events/blogs/2026/06/ai-data-centers-2030-2035-the-power-shift)).
- CNBC (via a secondary source) reports Anthropic and OpenAI shopping for 20-30 MW deals ([GDCH](https://www.globaldatacenterhub.com/p/openai-anthropic-small-data-center-deals)).

**The headroom is illegible until you ask:**
- "A substation can appear identical in satellite imagery whether it has 200 MW of available load headroom or zero", so confirming it needs a formal utility capacity inquiry ([Acres](https://www.acres.com/blog/transmission-line-proximity-vs.-substation-capacity-in-data-center-site-selection)).
- LandGate sells modelled offtake capacity per substation ([LandGate](https://www.landgate.com/energy-markets/offtake-capacity)), but it is an estimate, not a recorded outcome, so C4 is 3.
- A co-op CEO's example: a 22.5 MVA transformer carrying 10 MVA could serve a 10 MW data centre in about six months with no long-lead equipment (search snippet of an [AII](https://www.aii.org/the-rural-grid-squeeze/) PDF; the anecdote was not in the web article itself, so it is unverified).

**Below the big-load tariffs:**
- The Pennsylvania model tariff starts at 50 MW ([NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/pennsylvania-public-utility-commission-adopts-model-interconnection-tariff-large)).
- ERCOT security of $50k/MW applies above 75 MW ([White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-sets-financial-commitments-data-center-interconnection-governor-pauses-permit), [mgrid](https://mgrid.org/2026/09/21/texas-cuts-the-data-center-grid-deposit-from-100000-to-50000-per-mw-during-its-own-queue-pause/)).
- So a 10-20 MW ask avoids most security walls. This is the pre-seed rung.

**Step-up arithmetic:**
- A site needs 0.5-1 acre/MW, so 10 MW is about 5-10 acres ([Bisnow/Avison Young](https://www.bisnow.com/news/national/data-center-development/data-center-sites-are-warping-the-market-for-developable-land-134771)).
- Sale value range:
  - Primary-market powered land averages **$584k/MW** in 2026 YTD (Cushman, via search summary).
  - Cloverleaf's 1.3 GW Vantage sale was **$200M, about $154k/MW**, or about $105k/acre ([Commercial Observer](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/07/data-center-development-wildcatters/)).
  - So a 10-20 MW secondary-market micro-site is worth about $1.5-6M.
- Our cost: about $10-50k for the option, $0-100k for the inquiry, and legal fees, roughly $50-150k in all. That is **10-40x on cash**.
- Against the land at the hope price (5-10 acres at a 5-10x ask of about $100-300k/acre near a substation), the step-up is **about 2-5x on the asset**.
- Against farm value ($20k/acre to $400k/acre is the sellers' own pitch, per Ambrose in Commercial Observer), it is about 20x. Owners near substations now know this (L3 is dropped, so we pay it).

**Kill points:**
- **L8:** Prologis, the world's largest industrial landlord, already finds sites near substations and EPRI brings the utilities. The end buyer is funding the proof directly. With L6 dropped we may compete, but they need no middle rung.
- **L4:** capacity is reserved for a load customer. Whether a tenantless SPV can hold and assign a 10 MW confirmation is untested. It is the single question that decides the idea.
- **Venture scale:** at $1-5M a site, $100M of revenue a year needs 30-100 sites a year. That is a throughput developer, and the throughput tool is rentable (L7).
- **Demand is still a pilot:** five EPRI sites. Neoclouds mostly want 20-100 MW+.
- **Revised:** C3 3 to 2 (the lever is speed, and Prologis can rent it too), C5 4. Total **about 14**.

### B. Big-rung powered land, second or third Cloverleaf (old #1, N6, 16): fundable, but not by a pre-seed

**The rung is now crowded and cash-gated:**
- Wildcatters are about **50% of Texas large-load requests**, over 400 GW of requests on a ~100 GW grid.
- Per Affinius, tying up land and an LOI once took "a few hundred thousand dollars"; a formal power commitment now takes "hundreds of millions". One large-load application reportedly required investment-grade-equivalent credit.
- Arrow's Betesh expects about 2 wins in 10 bets.
- All from [Commercial Observer, Jul 2026](https://commercialobserver.com/2026/07/data-center-development-wildcatters/).

**L4 froze the proof:**
- Gov. Abbott's 3 Aug 2026 directive pauses all pending ERCOT data-centre interconnections until each passes an audit, with no deadline. ERCOT said it "effectively pauses all data center projects", and data centres are about 90% of 474 GW of requests. Batch Zero is paused ([Akin](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/texas-pauses-data-center-interconnections-pending-statewide-audit), [Seyfarth](https://www.seyfarth.com/news-insights/texas-pauses-pending-data-center-grid-interconnections-for-project-by-project-audit.html)).
- At least 15 states are considering bans, and New York has a one-year hyperscaler ban (Commercial Observer).

**Exit appetite exists but is selective:**
- Investors "look for powered-land developers" and buy at ready-to-build. But development platforms are now valued "largely unpriced" and are being broken up for parts. It is a barbell market ([projectfinance.law, Sep 2026](https://www.projectfinance.law/publications/ma-current-market-conditions)).

**Verdict:** a Series-A-plus PE developer play. A pre-seed team cannot post $5M of security on a 100 MW ERCOT request, cannot get IG credit, and starts behind Cloverleaf's 7 GW. Dead for YC.

### C. Is there a pre-seed YC precedent for "developer with ML as a speed tool"? Yes, and it shows the ceiling

- **Astro (YC W25)** "finds and buys land for new renewable energy projects, secures grid connections at minimal cost, and sells these development-ready projects to larger energy companies". It uses AI on satellite, grid and land data. The founder is ex-Citadel. Funding listed is about $500k pre-seed ([YC](https://www.ycombinator.com/companies/astro), [Green Prophet](https://greenprophet.com/2026/02/astro-uses-ai-to-help-procure-land-for-renewable-energy/), [VCBacked](https://www.vcbacked.co/company/astro-energy)).
- Other YC neighbours: Voxel Energy (W26, BTM DC power hardware) and Plume (S24, siting software) ([YC energy list](https://www.vcbacked.co/yc/industry/energy)).
- **What this proves:** YC will fund exactly this triple's company at pre-seed. YC funded it in renewables, and the same law firm notes the exit market prices renewable development pipelines low (projectfinance.law).
- **What it means for Dhruv:**
  - The micro-site version for compute is the higher-value variant.
  - The model is not novel: Astro could pivot into it in a quarter.
  - Its edge would be speed and relationships, not anything ownable. That is the R-ML and L7 problem, accepted rather than solved.

## 5. Answers

1. **Survivor?** No clean one. The best is **N18, flex micro-sites, at 16 before checks and about 14 after.** On paper the failed-Phase-3 rescue stays at 18 conditional and the shelved-drug NewCo at 17, but both are C1-bound and off-sector, and the triple adds nothing to them.

2. **The law that now kills it:** **L4.** Utilities and regulators own the proving step: they allocate capacity to a customer, gate assignment, and in Texas freeze the whole queue. Behind it:
   - **L8:** the end buyer (Prologis plus EPRI, Stream, TeraWulf) goes direct.
   - **Capital access:** every rung large enough to be venture-scale on one asset needs a $5M-to-$100M+ cheque.
   - Once L3, L6 and R-ML are all gone, the space reduces to "who can post collateral and who does the utility talk to". A pre-seed team wins neither.

3. **Smallest first rung a pre-seed YC team can win:**
   - **What it is:** one 5-20 MW micro-site. Land under option, or contributed by the owner for equity, plus *written* capacity confirmation at a half-loaded co-op or muni distribution substation, under the 50-75 MW large-load tariff thresholds.
   - **Buyer:** an inference operator, neocloud or Prologis-type developer.
   - **Cost and time:** about $50-150k and 6-12 months.

4. **Is the step-up still 10x+?**
   - **On cash: yes, about 10-40x** ($50-150k in, $1.5-6M of site value at $154-584k/MW).
   - **On the asset at the hope price: no, about 2-5x.** Farm-to-powered is about 20x, but L3 is dropped, so the owner keeps most of it unless they took equity instead of cash.
   - **Absolute size:** small, $1-5M per rung. Venture scale needs about 50-100 sites a year.

5. **If Dhruv wants to test it anyway (under $100k, about 8 weeks):**
   - Use LandGate plus our own model to pick 30 candidate distribution substations across 10 co-ops and munis outside ERCOT.
   - File formal capacity inquiries.
   - Get answers to three written questions:
     - (a) What share of substations confirm at least 10 MW within 12 months?
     - (b) Will at least one utility let a **tenantless SPV** hold the confirmation and assign it to a later tenant?
     - (c) Will 3 inference operators or neoclouds sign LOIs at at least $150k/MW?
   - **Kill criteria:** under 20% confirm; no utility allows tenantless reservation and assignment (L4 confirmed); or the LOI price is below $100k/MW (C2 under 10x on cash).
   - **Pass criteria:** all three clear. That gives the first rung and a real YC story ("10 MW in 9 months for $100k"). It would still be a speed-and-relationships developer, which is this triple by definition.

6. **Most interesting finding:** dropping L3, L6 and R-ML together does not create a new company. It describes Cloverleaf, the wildcatters and Astro, which are already funded and already crowded. What the triple *does* reveal is that the only rung a pre-seed team can afford is the one **below the large-load tariff thresholds** (5-20 MW). There the security walls vanish and the headroom is genuinely illegible, but the end buyer (Prologis plus EPRI) is already going straight to the utilities. That makes **L4 plus L8** the pair to test next, together with the "tenantless assignment" question above.
