# Pass 4 quad: drop L2 (legible outcome), L3 (seller prices hope), L4 (public bodies/utilities own or reprice the right) and L6 (rung held), 19 Oct 2026

## Verdict

**No clean survivor on the gold rubric. One new thread is the first in nine hunts where ML is genuinely the solver AND the asset is owned AND the gatekeeper is only a cost.** It is not one of the assets named in the question:

- **Congestion-rights book (FTR/CRR) plus congestion-pocket sites (Q1 + Q12 combo): 16/25 after checks.** The ML congestion forecast is the whole edge. The asset (an FTR, then a site at a congested node) is owned outright. PJM's gate ($1M tangible net worth, collateral) is a cost. The market is real and large: **$4.27B of FTR profit in Jun 2025 to May 2026, $3B of it in PJM, DC Energy alone ~$553M** ([Yes Energy](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/ftr-market-performance-and-trends-review-june-2025-may-2026)).
- **What still stops it:**
  - **C1/C2 shape.** There is no step-up from a cheap unproven right to a proven asset. It earns a yearly return on collateral, which makes it a trading firm, not a Terranox-shaped asset company.
  - **R-ML, partly.** The academic explanation for speculator profits is a **risk premium** for holding hard-to-forecast payouts ([IAEE](https://iaee.org/en/publications/proceedingsabstractpdf.aspx?id=15456)). That is the capital-wins pattern again, unless a backtest shows alpha above a naive "hold the prevailing-flow paths" book.
  - **Residual regulator risk, the existential form of L4.** PJM load has had $5.4B less congestion revenue than it should have (2011 to early 2025, per the market monitor), which is a standing case for cutting speculators out, as CAISO did with CRR paths ([PJM consultant note](https://pjm.com/-/media/committees-groups/task-forces/afmtf/2020/20200429/20200429-item-08-options-for-reforming-arr-ftr-market-design.ashx)).
- **Every named asset class fails, and none of them fails on L4 any more:**

| Named class | ML the solver? | Asset owned? | Now killed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flex micro-sites | **No.** PG&E itself issues the day-ahead operating limits ([Latitude, Aug 2026](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/what-pge-has-learned-in-two-years-of-flexible-interconnection/)). The flex controller is a vendor product (Emerald, GridCARE) | Yes, once L4 is dropped | L7, L11, L8 (Prologis plus EPRI), size. **15** |
| Closed-plant power capture | **No.** Mills are a named, legible fleet: ~220 mills, >10 GW, 10 operators hold >70% ([AFRY via DCD](https://bebeez.eu/en/opinions/the-powered-mill-10gw-of-shovel-ready-data-center-sites-is-hiding-in-plain-sight/)) | Yes | L8 (Aligned at Pixelle, Amp Z at Southland), L10. **15** |
| Queue positions | **Partly.** Cluster restudy cascades are physics, and the tool is sold (Enverus bought Pearl Street's SUGAR, Mar 2025; Nira) | Yes, though Order 2023 needs 90-100% site control | L7, L1-ish step-up of 2-10x, withdrawal penalties. **15** |
| Recertified transformers | No. IEEE C57 tests are the proof | Yes | R-ML, L1, L9 (half-life). **12** |
| Spectrum | No. SAS/AFC coordination is an FCC method open to all | Yes | L11, L7, L8 (carriers buy direct). **13** |
| Orbital slots / ITU filings | No. EPFD and coordination are deterministic calculation plus negotiation | Treated as ownable here | R-ML, C2 unsourced. **11** |
| Water rights | Partly. OpenET satellite ET is free and open, and historic consumptive use is a court burden | Yes | L11/L7, L1 (change cases pass), Front Range DC pauses. Off-sector. **14** |

**The law that kills the rest:** with L4 gone as a killer, the binding pair is **R-ML + L7/L11**.
- In every grid and land asset, the hard prediction is now made by the gatekeeper itself (PG&E's day-ahead limits, the RTO's cluster study) or by a vendor anyone can rent (Enverus/SUGAR, Nira, Emerald, GridCARE, OpenET).
- So ML ranks; it does not solve.
- The only place where the hard prediction stays private and pays directly is **price formation** (congestion), and assets priced by forecasts are traded, not stepped up.

**Proposed L12: where ML is the private solver, the asset is a priced financial claim, so the edge shows up as a yield, not a step-up.** Where the asset steps up 10-1000x, a gatekeeper or a vendor makes the prediction, and the ML is open.

## Rules in force for this quad

- **Dropped:** L2 (an outcome field may exist), L3 (we pay the hope price or give equity; C2 is measured against the price actually paid), L4 (utility or regulator consent, studies, collateral and assignment fees are **costs**; an allocation, position or licence is treated as holdable and assignable), L6 (an occupied rung costs no C5 points).
- **Kept:** L1, L5, R-ML (C3 scores only where ML makes it solvable), no prospecting or drilling, own the asset, not Terranox, $1B+ path.
- **L7-L11, which are real once the quad is dropped:**
  - **L7 (vendors sell the prover ML): real, and the main killer.** Enverus bought Pearl Street (SUGAR, "300 GW of queued projects processed") in Mar 2025 ([Enverus](https://enverus.com/newsroom/undo-the-queue-enverus-acquires-pearl-street-technologies-to-solve-for-a-more-reliable-resilient-grid)). Nira has modelled "700 GW" ([Infocast](https://www.infocastinc.com/2026-transmission-sponsors/nira-energy)). Emerald and GridCARE sell flex.
  - **L8 (the end buyer goes direct): real.** Aligned bought into Pixelle Chillicothe ($1.5B campus); Prologis plus EPRI work micro-sites.
  - **L9 (a policy-shock premium has a half-life): real** for transformers and turbine slots. It does not apply to congestion, which grows with load.
  - **L10 (insiders buy orphaned hardware): real** for closed mills. The DCD/AFRY piece tells buyers to approach P&P CEOs directly, because "most of the capacity isn't for sale".
  - **L11 (an accepted ML proof is a method open to all): real, and *not* removed by dropping L4.** It is about scarcity, not consent. PG&E's Flex Connect gives any customer a firm floor plus a dynamic ceiling; PS-16, SAS/AFC and OpenET are the same.

## 3. Adversarial web checks on the top 3

### A. Q1 congestion-rights book (with Q12): real money, real ML, wrong shape. 16.

**What holds up:**
- **Size.** FTR profit was $4.27B in Jun 2025 to May 2026: PJM $3.0B, MISO $898M, NYISO $114M, ERCOT $110M, CAISO $96M ([Yes Energy](https://www.yesenergy.com/blog/ftr-market-performance-and-trends-review-june-2025-may-2026), vendor using FERC data).
- **Pure financial firms are top earners.** DC Energy ~$553M, Appian Way ~$200M, Peakstone ~$176M, Saracen ~$149M, Elliott Bay ~$138M, Hamilton Liberty ~$103M. Utilities in the list (Dominion $437M, Exelon $347M) are mostly ARR hedges.
- **Edge is concentrated.** 97.5% of 3-year PJM FTRs went to a small number of financial participants ([PJM](https://pjm.com/-/media/committees-groups/committees/mic/20170913/20170913-item-11a-long-term-ftr-problem-statement.ashx)).
- **Congestion is growing.** PJM congestion costs are up 61% to $2.23B, with only 66.6% returned through FTRs in 2025-26 ([PJM IMM](https://www.pjm.com/-/media/DotCom/committees-groups/committees/mmuac/2025/20251205/20251205-arr-ftr-market-2025.pdf)).
- **The gate is a cost.** FTR participants need $1M tangible net worth or $10M tangible assets, possibly going to $2M (ER26-2651, May 2026) ([PJM](https://www.pjm.com/pjmfiles/directory/etariff/FercDockets/9695/20260527-er26-2651-000.pdf)), plus VaR-based collateral since the GreenHat default ($179M FERC penalty).
- **ML trading of this kind is a fundable shape.** Gridmatic claims to be the most profitable ERCOT DA participant, runs a $50M storage fund and is "profitable without VC" ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20231115697337/en/Gridmatic-Closes-%2450-Million-Energy-Storage-Fund-Underscoring-Importance-of-AI-to-Optimizing-Batteries)).

**What kills it on the gold frame:**
- **C2: no step-up.** An FTR is priced at auction by sophisticated bidders (L3 is irrelevant here), and returns are a yield on collateral. The "1000x on a cheap right" does not exist.
- **R-ML, partly.** The IAEE work attributes persistent speculator profit to a **risk premium** that reflects "the extreme difficulty of forecasting" ([IAEE](https://iaee.org/en/publications/proceedingsabstractpdf.aspx?id=15456)). If that is most of it, capital, not ML, earns it.
- **Regulator risk (L4 is dropped as a killer, but it is still the tail).** The IMM's $5.4B shortfall to load is a political target. CAISO already restricted CRR paths, and FERC has rejected PJM's ARR/FTR design and opened a show-cause on credit ([RTO Insider](https://www.rtoinsider.com/tag/auction-revenue-rights-arrs/page/2)).
- **Venture shape.** This is a prop-trading firm. DC Energy shows a $1B+ outcome exists, but VCs and YC rarely fund collateral-limited books. Gridmatic took fund capital, not VC.
- **Q12 (owning sites in congestion pockets)** fixes the "own a physical asset" and step-up objection only partly: a site at a congested node is worth more, but the step-up is the powered-land 2-5x again, and compute buyers want firm power.

### B. Q2 cluster-restudy arbitrage: ML is not ours to own. 15.

- **Base rate is low, so C3 has room.** Only 13% of capacity requested in 2000-2019/2020 reached COD, and 77% withdrew ([LBNL Queued Up 2025](https://emp.lbl.gov/sites/default/files/2025-12/Queued%20Up%202025%20Edition%20-%2012.15.2025.pdf); [2026 edition](https://emp.lbl.gov/queued-2026-edition-characteristics-power-plants-seeking-transmission-interconnection-end-2025)). Withdrawals are 40-50% at phase 1 and 25-35% at phase 2, and each one moves remaining costs ([Enverus](https://www.enverus.com/blog/the-variable-most-likely-to-kill-your-deal-late-network-upgrade-costs/)). SPP withdrawn projects faced costs about 5x those of projects that proceeded ([LBNL](https://emp.lbl.gov/news/spp-data-show-rising-network-upgrade-costs)).
- **But the predictor is for sale (L7).** Enverus (with SUGAR) and Nira sell exactly this cascade analysis to developers and financiers.
- **And the step-up is a developer's step-up.** Early-stage solar plus storage sells at about $0.02-0.05/W against $0.07-0.20/W late-stage ([Ascend](https://www.ascendanalytics.com/blog/increasing-early-stage-project-success-in-a-highly-competitive-renewable-energy-m-a-landscape), dated). That is 2-10x, and buyers are favouring late-stage in 2026.
- **The position is not a free-floating right.** Order 2023 requires 90% site control at request and 100% at LGIA, clusters share priority, and withdrawal penalties apply ([Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2023/08/ferc-generator-interconnection-process-reform)). So you are a renewable developer (the Astro YC W25 shape).
- No startup buying positions on analytics was found.

### C. Q4 / flex micro-sites with L4 gone: the utility is the ML. 15-16.

- **PG&E Flex Connect** gives a firm floor plus a dynamic ceiling "up to the total amount of capacity requested, depending on grid headroom". **PG&E provides the day-ahead operating limits**, and the customer's equipment only has to obey the cap ([Latitude, Aug 2026](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/what-pge-has-learned-in-two-years-of-flexible-interconnection/)).
- Seven sites are online and two have gone firm. Headroom was above the floor 90% of the time. There are about 85 sites in the pipeline (mostly EV charging), 10 large-load applications in 2026, and the first ~5 MW data centre this fall. Distribution projects over 2 MW now get a flexibility analysis by default.
- **Result:** the hard prediction (feeder headroom tomorrow) is made by the utility and offered to every applicant (L11). Our ML only ranks sites (L7: LandGate, Build, GridCARE).
- With L4 dropped, holding and assigning is fine. The asset is still powered land at 2-5x on the hope price and 10-40x on cash ($1-5M a site).

## 5. Answer and feasibility test

**Answer to the question.** Once utility and regulator gatekeeping is a cost:
- None of flex micro-sites, closed-plant capture, queue positions, transformers, spectrum, orbital slots or water rights has ML as the solver.
- In all of them, the gatekeeper's own study, forecast or test (PG&E limits, the RTO cluster study, C57 tests, FCC SAS, water court) or an open or rented tool makes the decisive prediction.
- The only owned asset where private ML is the decisive input is a **congestion right** (Q1), and its natural physical extension is **owning flexible load or storage in the congestion pockets the same model finds (Q12)**. That is the best result of the quad: 16, conditional on a backtest. It is a trading-plus-siting company, not a step-up company.

**Kill test, about $30-60k and 6 weeks, with public data only:**
1. Pull 2019-2026 PJM and MISO monthly and annual FTR auction clearing prices, plus DA congestion components by node (PJM Data Miner, MISO MISO-FTR reports). Cost is $0.
2. Build two books on walk-forward data:
   - (a) a naive "prevailing-flow, last-year congestion" lookup book;
   - (b) an ML book (weather, outages, load growth, new large loads in queues, topology changes from RTEP).
3. Kill criteria:
   - ML book return on VaR collateral is not at least 1.5x the naive book's (meaning profit is a risk premium, so R-ML confirmed);
   - or Sharpe below 1 after the 2018 GreenHat-style tail;
   - or edge confined to paths where DC Energy-scale players clear above our bid (book capacity under $20M).
4. Pass criteria: alpha over the naive book survives 2022-2026 out of sample. Then trade a $1-2M book in MISO or ERCOT CRRs, and use the node-level model to option 2-3 congestion-pocket sites (Q12) as the asset rung.

**Recommendation:** treat Q1/Q12 as the lead for the next pass (drop C2's step-up demand and test whether a "yield-on-owned-rights" company is acceptable to Dhruv and YC). If he insists on a Terranox-shaped step-up, the quad confirms there is none in grid assets: L12 above.
