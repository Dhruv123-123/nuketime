# Deep checks: the two strongest non-parts runners-up (19 Oct 2026)

Frame: `V5_OPTIMIST.md`. Tech is assumed to work. The bar is numbers and time, every vertical killer needs an AI counter-move with a stated effect, and a pass is 19+/25 with no leg below 3.

The two ideas checked here, with both checks run adversarially but fairly:
- (a) flotation reagents priced on extra metal recovered (`v5_deeptech.md` #1, scored 20);
- (b) an AI-native contract-surety carrier (`v5_broad.md` #1, scored 20).

Labels: **(src)** web-checked this session with the link inline; **(mem)** from memory and not re-checked; **(der)** derived here.

## Verdict

| Idea | Old | New | N | T | C | A | D | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Value-share flotation reagents | 20 | **17** | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 3 | **Fails (near-miss)**. Real headroom at well-run plants is smaller than modelled, there is no precedent for gain-share reagent pricing, and Giraffe Bio is already funded by BHP |
| (b) AI-native contract-surety carrier | 20 | **19** | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 3 | **Narrow pass.** It is not a blue ocean: InRev is the AI MGA comparable, and buying a T-listed shell is the fast path. Cycle risk is real and is now showing in the 2025-26 data |

**Bottom line:** surety survives the deep check, but only as a race against InRev-type MGAs and incumbents adding AI, not as an empty field. Its edge must come from loss selection, and a 6-10 week test can measure that edge before any capital is committed. The reagents idea drops below the bar because its key number, recoverable headroom that reagents alone can unlock, is unproven, and its business model (paid in metal) has no observed precedent in reagents.

### Re-derived economics (der)
- **Value of 1 pt.** On a 100 kt Cu/yr plant at 88%, 1 pt is +1.14 kt, or **$16.8M/yr** at $14.75k/t.
- **The model's +1.5 pts is the crux.**
  - Well-run tier-1 plants are already at 85-92% (src above). Their realistic reagent-only headroom is **0.3-1.0 pt** (der: Kennecott-scale plant gains, the >1 pt model-vs-LCT error, and the share of losses that are liberation-limited).
  - Mid-tier and variable-ore plants (Sungun- and Sarcheshmeh-type, 79-84%) have 2-4 pts, but they are smaller and pay worse.
  - Blended: **~0.8 pt × 30% share ≈ $4M/yr per 100 kt mine**, against the modelled $7.5M.
- **Ceiling.**
  - Flotation copper is ~18 Mt/yr (mem). +0.8 pt fleet-wide is ~165 kt, or **~$2.4B/yr of metal**.
  - At 30% share and 30% penetration, that is **~$220M/yr**, plus reagent margin.
  - Add gold, zinc, lithium and phosphate (×1.5-2), plus reagent sales at ~40% gross margin. **$1B revenue needs >50% penetration of big copper plants or a second large commodity.**
  - A $1-3B outcome is credible (Jetti $2.5B comparable). $10B is not. Hence C=3.

### Rescore
- **N 4 (unchanged).** Contribution per mine is still ~75% gross margin when a gain exists, and the business is asset-light.
- **T 3 (was 4).**
  - Bench campaign 2-3 months, then miner MOC and procurement, then a 4-12 week plant trial with on/off campaigns.
  - First *value-share* revenue is realistically **month 15-24**. Majors' procurement is slow, and no gain-share precedent exists to copy, so the legal terms are novel.
  - $100M revenue at ~$4M per mine needs ~25 mines: year 7-9.
- **C 3 (was 4).** See the ceiling above.
- **A 4.** The AI answers are good. Robotic bench screening cuts qualification cost by ~85%; the plant digital twin gives attribution to ~±0.3 pt. But AI cannot create headroom that is liberation-limited.
- **D 3 (was 4).**
  - BHP is already funding a rival (Giraffe). Majors (Freeport) run in-house AI and additive programmes.
  - Incumbents can match "price on metal" contractually once it is shown to work.
  - The ore→reagent→recovery dataset compounds, but slowly (one plant every few months).
- **Total 17 → fails, near-miss.**

### Top kill risks and AI answers
1. **Headroom is liberation-limited, not chemistry-limited.**
   - *AI answer:* QEMSCAN/MLA on tailings plus an ML loss-deportment model sorts plants by *reagent-addressable* losses before any sale. Only plants with ≥1.5 pts of addressable loss get pursued.
   - *Effect:* sales cost per qualified mine drops ~5×, and the hit rate rises from ~30% to ~70% (der).
   - It shrinks the market but saves the model.
2. **Miners refuse metal-share.**
   - *AI answer:* a hybrid price. Reagent at a premium per tonne, plus a capped bonus above an attribution threshold that the twin computes monthly. This mirrors Metso's performance contracts.
   - *Effect:* gross margin is ~50% instead of 75%, with faster procurement.
3. **Baseline disputes (feed grade swings of ±0.2% Cu move recovery by points).**
   - *AI answer:* a twin trained on 12-24 months of historian data, plus randomised on/off blocks.
   - *Effect:* attribution CI ~±0.3 pt. That only works if the gain is ≥1 pt, so it is tied to risk 1.
4. **Grade/penalty trade-off (pyrite, As).**
   - *AI answer:* optimise for NSR, not recovery: a multi-objective search over depressant chemistry.
5. **Copper-price exposure.** Revenue swings with Cu. That is a feature in 2026, a risk in a downturn.

### Cheapest 6-10 week feasibility test (~$80-120k)
1. **Headroom desk study (weeks 1-3, ~$5k).** Pull 15 S-K 1300 / NI 43-101 technical reports for operating porphyry concentrators. Record design vs actual recovery, tailings Cu grade, and any published tailings deportment (liberated vs locked vs oxide).
   - **Kill** if the median *reagent-addressable* loss is <1 pt.
2. **Commercial appetite (weeks 1-6, ~$10k).** 15 calls with concentrator superintendents and metallurgy managers (mid-tier Chile, Peru, Arizona, Zambia). Ask:
   - would you sign a hybrid premium-plus-bonus contract;
   - who signs it;
   - how long procurement takes.
   - **Pass** at ≥1 signed paid bench-study LOI ($30-50k).
3. **Bench proof (weeks 3-10, ~$60-100k).** Run 2 ores (purchased met-lab rejects) at SGS or ALS: the site's reagent suite against an ML-guided 50-100 formulation set.
   - **Pass** at ≥1.5 pts rougher recovery at equal concentrate grade on 1 of 2 ores. **Kill** at <0.5 pt on both.

## (b) AI-native contract-surety carrier: 19/25 (N4 T4 C4 A4 D3)

### Key sourced numbers
- **Market size.**
  - SFAA top-100 writers: **$8.84B direct premium written through Q3 2025**, top-100 direct loss ratio **20.7%**. Nine-month YTD, so this is not the full year; the earlier "2025 = 20.7%" was really 9M (src: [SFAA Q3 2025](https://mbasurety.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/SFAA-3rd-Quarter-Results-for-2025.pdf)).
  - Q1 2026 DWP **$3.13B**, +9.6% y/y (src: [Baldwin mid-year 2026](https://baldwin.com/insights/surety-industry-report/)). Run-rate is **~$12-13B/yr** (der).
- **Loss trend is turning.**
  - Q1 2026 direct loss ratio **23.8% vs 20.8%** a year earlier (Baldwin, above).
  - 2024 **26.4%**, 2023 **24.8%** (SFAA via TSIB) (src: [actuary.info](https://actuary.info/insights/surety-2025-loss-ratio-retreat-contract-backlog-pricing-2026)).
  - Net loss ratios are now 6-7 pts above direct (reinsurers are absorbing more).
  - Primary rate increases have been <1% in 13 of 14 quarters (Baldwin). **Pricing is flat while losses rise: late-cycle signals.**
- **Huge dispersion among writers (9M 2025, SFAA):**
  - Merchants Bonding (small/mid contractor specialist): **4.1%**. RLI 4.5%. Liberty 12.2%. Travelers 12.8%. CNA 13.9%.
  - Zurich 43.3%, Philadelphia 66.0%, Everest 103.6%.
  - Gray (mid-sized and emerging contractors): 20.3%.
  - **Selection quality, not market beta, drives the result.** That is exactly where an AI underwriter has to prove itself.
- **Cycle history.**
  - 2002 nine-month industry loss ratio **55%** (Enron plus contract frequency) (src: [Daily Reporter](https://dailyreporter.com/?p=51028), [Chubb 10-K 2003](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000020171/000095012304003193/y87394e10vk.htm)).
  - Great Recession lag: industry **~14% in 2011 → 22% in 2012**. The top 10 sat at 4.3-12.7% while the rest of the market ran **27.9-34%** (src: [Business Insurance](https://www.businessinsurance.com/contractor-failures-drive-increase-in-surety-losses-in-2012/), [PSF](https://psfinc.com/articles/the-state-of-the-surety-industry-and-you)).
  - CNA Surety, the small/mid contractor specialist: loss ratio **18.7% / 16.5% / 10.8%** in 2008/09/10 (src: [CNA Surety 10-K FY2010](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001044566/000095012311015792/c62181e10vk.htm)).
  - **Lesson:** disciplined small-contractor books survived 2008-12. Small and new writers took the losses, and a new carrier is by definition in the "rest of market" bucket until it proves otherwise.
- **The expense target is confirmed.**
  - CNA Surety expense ratio **54.5% / 55.3% / 54.5%** (2008-10), against loss ratios of 11-19% and combined ratios of **65-73%** (CNA Surety 10-K, above).
  - Commissions are not broken out. At ~25-30% agent commission (mem), internal underwriting and ops is **~25-30 pts**, more than the 22 assumed in `v5_broad.md`.
  - Defense and cost-containment expense (DCCE) was 2.9% of earned premium in 2024 (actuary.info).
- **Treasury listing is not the bottleneck it was assumed to be.**
  - 31 CFR 223 needs only **$250k paid-up capital**, a **$100k state deposit**, solvency, and evidence that the company "engage[s] in the business of suretyship", shown by premiums on the annual statement. Treasury weighs "the past history of the company". **There is no fixed years-in-business rule in the regulation text** (src: [31 CFR 223, 2018 ed.](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/CFR-2018-title31-vol2/pdf/CFR-2018-title31-vol2-part223.pdf)).
  - The binding limit is **10% of paid-up capital and surplus per bond**, net of reinsurance (§223.10-.11).
  - Certificates renew every 1 July.
  - In practice a de novo applicant needs ≥1 annual statement showing surety premium, so roughly 12-24 months (mem).
- **Buy path (fast and priced):**
  - Trisura bought T-listed **First Founders Assurance for ~$18.8M**, closing ~9 months after signing (Mar 2024) (src: [Insurance Business](https://insurancebusinessmag.com/us/news/breaking-news/trisura-group-eyes-us-expansion-with-treasurylisted-acquisition-456627.aspx), [Insurance Insider](https://www.insuranceinsiderus.com/article/2c1oarx9sx0ggth83x98g/specialty-lines/canadas-trisura-buys-treasury-listed-surety-company-amid-us-expansion-plans)).
  - Palomar bought FIA (~$10M premium) for **$29.3M at 1.7× book** (src: [The Insurer](https://theinsurer.com/news/palomar-to-acquire-10mn-premium-surety-carrier-fia-for-1-7x-book-value), [Palomar 10-K FY2024](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1761312/000143774925005069/plmr20241231_10k.htm)).
- **Exit and ceiling comparable.** Palomar bought **Gray Surety** (T-listed, mid-sized and emerging contractors, 50 states) for **$300M**, closing 31 Jan 2026 (src: [Palomar IR](https://ir.palomarspecialty.com/news-releases/news-release-details/palomar-holdings-inc-announces-agreement-acquire-gray-casualty), [Coverager](https://coverager.com/palomar-completes-gray-surety-acquisition-and-closes-450-million-credit-facility/)). Gray wrote ~$65M DPW in 9M 2025 (SFAA), so **~$87M/yr**: **~3.4× premium** (der).
- **Fronting and reinsurance.**
  - Fronts support ~20% of $128B US MGA premium, ~$22.6B (src: [Reinsurance News/Conning](https://www.reinsurancene.ws/conning-estimates-us-mga-premiums-reached-128bn-in-2025/)).
  - Fronting scrutiny is rising: Markel took a $205M charge on State National, and AM Best moved delegated authority to stable (src: [The Insurer](https://theinsurer.com/ti/analysis/is-the-fronting-juggernaut-stalling-2025-04-25/), [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/mga-premiums-hit-108-7-billion-in-2025-as-capacity-scrutiny-tightens/)).
  - Surety reinsurance capacity is "broadly available" with flat pricing at 1/1 (Baldwin).
  - **The proof that surety fronting works:** InRev writes on **Accelerant National** paper (src: [Accelerant](https://accelerant.ai/resources/pioneering-change-how-inrev-and-accelerant-are-transforming-the-u-s-construction-surety-bond-market/)).
- **Entrants.** The space is not empty.
  - **InRev:** $3.75M (Jan 2025) from Accelerant, Greenlight Re, Mastodon and Scrappy. Profitable in 2024. Automated contract bonds to **$2.5M single / $5M aggregate**. First bond in **~20 min vs 2 days-2 weeks**. This is the AI-native contract-surety MGA, already live (src: [citybiz](https://www.citybiz.co/?p=650834)).
  - **Propeller Bonds:** $6.4M seed in 2023, instant-issue surety MGA (src: [The Insurer](https://www.theinsurer.com/news/surety-mga-insurtech-propeller-raises-6-4mn-in-seed-funding/)).
  - **axiTrust:** $2.7M (per `v5_broad.md`).
  - **Corgi:** $108M in Jan 2026 for an AI carrier, but not surety (src: [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corgi_(insurance_company))).
  - **No AI-native *Treasury-listed carrier* was found.** The open rung is the balance sheet, not the MGA.
- **Program lever.** The SBA Surety Bond Guarantee covers **80-90%** of losses on contracts up to **$9M** ($14M federal). Preferred sureties issue without SBA prior approval (src: [CRS R42037, Jul 2025](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-07-18_R42037_3b4a54a58e13baff9614b0142bf2d017f4fdcbc2.html)).

### Re-derived economics (der)
| Per $100 premium | Specialist incumbent (CNA Surety-type) | AI-native (MGA phase) | AI-native (own carrier) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Losses + LAE | 15-25 | 22-28 (new-writer adverse selection) | 18-24 (after seasoning) |
| Agent commission | ~27 | 27 | 27 |
| Internal UW/ops | ~27 | 10 | 8-10 |
| Fronting | 0 | 5-7 | 0 |
| **Combined** | **~70-75** | **~64-72** | **~53-61** |

- The AI expense cut is **~17-19 pts** (27 → 8-10). That is bigger than the 14 pts first modelled, because the internal expense base turned out larger.
- The edge narrows in the MGA phase (fronting fee plus adverse selection) and opens fully only with an owned carrier.
- **Capital path.**
  - Seed $5M → MGA on fronted paper (Accelerant-type).
  - Series A $30-40M, including **~$20-30M to buy a T-listed shell**, the FFAC/FIA pattern. This collapses the 12-24 month de novo listing into a ~9-month change of control.
  - Series B $100-150M of surplus. At 10% of surplus per bond, $100M of surplus allows $10M single bonds net, which covers the wedge.
- **Revenue clock.**
  - MGA commission revenue from month 9-12.
  - Carrier earned premium from year 2-3.
  - **$100M net earned premium needs ~$150-200M GWP after a 30-50% quota share: about 1.3-1.6% share, year 6-7.**
- **Ceiling.**
  - At Gray's ~3.4× premium, $1.5B GWP is worth **~$5B** on surety alone.
  - Contractor working-capital credit from the WIP feeds plus subcontractor default insurance push toward $8-10B. Those adjacencies are unproven (der).

### Rescore
- **N 4.** The expense gap is confirmed and larger than assumed. Losses during seasoning are the swing.
- **T 4.** MGA revenue arrives in <12 months, and the shell purchase removes the listing delay. $100M revenue in year 6-7 is tight but derivable.
- **C 4.** There is a $5B surety-only path, with a priced exit comparable (Gray, $300M at ~$87M premium).
- **A 4 (was 5).** The expense and speed answers are quantified. The loss-selection answer is still unproven, and InRev shows the speed edge is copyable.
- **D 3.** InRev is first in the MGA rung. Incumbents with 4-13% loss ratios (Merchants, RLI) can adopt the same AI tools, and agents' loyalty follows capacity and service. What compounds is the monitored-WIP dataset and an owned T-listed balance sheet.
- **Total 19: narrow pass.**

### Top kill risks and AI answers
1. **Cycle: losses lag construction by 18-36 months.** Q1 2026 is already 23.8%, primary pricing is flat, and mid-market contractors are overextended (Baldwin). A 2012-type spike (rest of market 28-34%) or a 2002-type spike (55%) during seasoning would wipe out a young book.
   - *AI answer:* monthly WIP, job-cost and bank-feed monitoring flags margin fade and over-billing 2-3 quarters before default, so the carrier can cut aggregates and require funds control.
   - *Effect:* if it reaches the specialist level (CNA 11-19% in 2008-10), cycle losses are ~10-15 pts below the rest of market. Combined with a 50% quota share and the SBA 80-90% guarantee on sub-$9M bonds, a 2012-type year costs <1 year of underwriting profit, not the surplus (der).
   - This is the claim the backtest must prove.
2. **Adverse selection: fast approval attracts contractors other sureties declined.**
   - *AI answer:* a decline-reason classifier on submissions, checked against public signals (mechanics liens, court dockets, UCC filings, OSHA, state licence actions).
   - *Effect:* keeps the declined-elsewhere share under 15% of the book (der).
3. **Fronting and reinsurance tightening after the Markel/State National losses.**
   - *AI answer:* give reinsurers live portfolio telemetry, i.e. WIP on every account monthly.
   - *Effect:* supports a 25-30% ceding commission and a ≤6% fronting fee. **The shell purchase removes the fronting dependence by year 2.**
4. **Incumbents copy, and InRev scales first.**
   - *AI answer:* own the balance sheet and lend against the same data (working-capital lines), which an MGA cannot do.
   - *Effect:* raises account lifetime value ~1.5-2× (der).
5. **AM Best rating for private obligees.** The Treasury listing is enough for federal work. Many private owners require A- from AM Best, and a de novo rating takes 12-18 months (mem).
   - Counter: buy a rated shell, or front through a rated carrier until then.

### Cheapest 6-10 week feasibility test (~$40-80k)
1. **Loss-selection backtest (the decisive one).** Ask one MGA or regional surety (InRev's competitors, a Gray-type regional, or an SBA preferred surety) for ~300-500 historical anonymised bond files with outcomes. In exchange, offer the model free.
   - **Kill** if AUC lift over their own scores is <0.05, or if review time falls by <70%.
   - If no partner will share data within 6 weeks, that is itself a distribution red flag.
2. **Capacity term sheets.** Get indications from 2 fronts (Accelerant, plus one other) and 1-2 surety reinsurers on fronting fee, ceding commission and minimum premium.
