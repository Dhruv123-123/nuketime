# v4 slice C: rounds 2 and 3 re-scored (19 Oct 2026)

Scope: all 71 ideas in `converge/gold/nature_results.json` (round 2, where nature hides the answer) and all 64 in `converge/gold/proof_results.json` (round 3, markets with an accepted proof standard). That is 135 ideas.

Each idea is scored on the v4 legs, 0-5 each, for a total out of 25:
- **S1**: buy before the value is known.
- **S2**: step-up of 3x or more.
- **S3**: ML plausibility (judgement only, not tested).
- **S4**: discovery-heavy frame.
- **S5**: compounding edge.

A pass is 17+ with no leg at 0-1. The standing rules still apply: no prospecting or drilling, not Terranox, own the asset, and a path to a large company.

## Verdict

**One idea scores 17 after web checks, but it is not a clean pass.** Old Master sleeper attribution clears the number. It fails the standing rule on a path to a large company: Old Masters auction turnover is about $0.5-1B a year, and its proof is a contested expert opinion.

Everything else stalls at 15-16. Relaxing S3 to plausibility helped about 20 ideas by 1-2 points. It does not fix the round-2 and round-3 killers, which are structural and sit on other legs:
- **S1, nothing to buy.** Microbes, sequences and natural odorants are filed or free, not bought from an owner.
- **S2, no buyer at the proof stage.** Lab-scale proofs are not priced; buyers pay only after scale-up.
- **S4, the physical step is the business.** Recovery, beneficiation and construction dominate.
- **Small pools.** Pearls, coins, stamps and autographs have hit values in the hundreds to thousands of dollars.

Two ideas had only a **real negative prior** on the ML leg, and their S3 stays low:
- **Mendelian sampling** (Sirecraft, Mendel Herd). Elite merit above parent average is random, so ML cannot predict it.
- **Signal absent from the data** (off-type turf from aerial imagery, lunar stones at sub-pixel scale, km-scale gamma for metre-scale bone).

## Web checks on the top 3 (ML leg excluded, as v4 requires)

### 1. Old Master sleeper attribution: 18 -> 17 (S4 4 -> 3)

- **S1 holds at 3.** Sleepers still slip through regional sales:
  - A Turner, catalogued as "follower of Ibbetson", made £524.80 at Dreweatts in 2024. It resold at Sotheby's on 2 July 2025 for £1.9m with fees ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2025/07/04/%C2%A3500-turner-painting-sells-for-almost-%C2%A32m-in-sothebys-old-master-sale)).
  - An Artemisia bought for £30k in Denmark in September 2022 made £1.9m at Christie's within a year ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/14/its-time-to-end-the-predatory-practices-of-sleeper-hunters)).
  - The other half is real too: dealers bid lots up at the hammer. A "Circle of Tiepolo" made $75k against a $600-900 estimate at Clarke in 2024 ([Art History News](https://www.arthistorynews.com/articles/7690_Sleeper_Alert)). So S1 stays at 3.
- **S2 holds at 4.** Hits are 60x to 3,600x, and Sotheby's and Christie's evening sales are the buyers.
- **S4 falls to 3.** Proof is expert consensus, not a test. An art historian known for finding lost Old Masters doubts AI attribution without forensic examination ([Apollo](https://apollo-magazine.com/artificial-intelligence-authentication-artworks/), [Art Recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Recognition)). Restoration and scholarship take 1-5 years. A public campaign now calls sleeper hunting "predatory" and proposes resale rights, which is reputational risk ([The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2024/03/14/its-time-to-end-the-predatory-practices-of-sleeper-hunters)).
- **Size rule fails.** Old Master sales are buoyant: Sotheby's NY Old Masters rose 24% in H1 2025 ([ArtTactic](https://arttactic.com/editorials/mid-year-review-the-old-masters-market-at-sothebys-and-christies-in-2025)). But the segment is about $0.5-1B a year, so the ladder tops out at a dealer, art fund or lending business.
- **Verdict: 17/25, no leg below 3, but not a pass in spirit.** It fails "path to a large company".

### 2. PoolProof (SEP rescue into pools): 17 -> 16 (S2 4 -> 3, S5 3 -> 2)

Patlytics launched an SEP workflow in March 2026 ([Patlytics](https://www.patlytics.ai/news/next-generation-sep-analysis-workflow)), and XLScout offers the same ([XLScout](https://xlscout.ai/standard-essential-patents-in-europe-how-ai-claim-mapping-cuts-through-sep-licensing-complexity/)). Pools and aggregators can buy the same tools.
- **Proof is cheap, which helps S4.** The EU estimates an essentiality check at €4-5k ([Pinsent Masons](https://www.pinsentmasons.com/en-gb/out-law/news/independent-essentiality-assessments-standard-essential-patents-endorsed)). Access Advance calls evaluations expensive but finances them for SMEs ([Access Advance](https://accessadvance.com/?p=1370)).
- **S2 falls to 3.**
  - The brokered market is about $290M a year, and average asking prices fell from about $150k to $100k per asset by 2022 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intellectual_property_brokering)). Richardson Oliver says buyers keep gaining ground ([2025 report](https://www.richardsonoliver.com/publications/the-2025-richardson-oliver-patent-market-report)).
  - Avanci 5G charges $29-32 per vehicle across 75+ licensors, and the per-patent split is not public ([Avanci](https://www.avanci.com/vehicle/), [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2023/08/16/avancis-new-5g-vehicle-program-one-stop-shop/)).
  - No recent per-patent bankruptcy prices were found, so S1 stays at 3 on judgement.
- **Ladder.** It runs to InterDigital scale (a licensing company): large, but rent extraction.
- **Verdict: 16/25.** It misses on S5.

### 3. California lot legality (Certificates of Compliance): 16 -> 15 (S2 3 -> 2)

- **Hit routes narrowed again.** *Cox v. City of Oakland* (Cal. Sup. Ct., 23 Jan 2025, unanimous) held that conveyances that merely name several lots do not create separate parcels ([Casemine](https://www.casemine.com/commentary/us/clarifying-parcel-creation-under-the-subdivision-map-act:-cox-v.-city-of-oakland/view), [Courthouse News](https://www.courthousenews.com/california-supreme-court-rejects-use-of-antiquated-map-in-oakland-land-dispute/)). That is on top of *Gardner* (2003) ([Stanford SCOCAL](https://scocal.stanford.edu/opinion/gardner-v-sonoma-cty-33260)) and *Witt* ([Abbott & Kindermann](https://blog.aklandlaw.com/2009/05/articles/subdivision-map-act/revisiting-history-part-ii-when-is-a-recorded-1909-map-not-a-subdivision-map/)).
- **A CoC does not deliver the step-up.** A certificate "neither ensures that it is a buildable parcel, nor entitles... a construction permit" ([Campbell](https://campbellca.gov/DocumentCenter/View/2868)). Agencies can also issue conditional certificates carrying today's conditions ([SLO County guide](https://www.slocounty.ca.gov/departments/planning-building/forms-documents/land-division-(subdivision)-forms-and-documents/land-division-application-packages/certificate-of-compliance-application-package-user/certificate-of-compliance-user-guide)).
- **S1 stays at 4.** Title chains are illegible to long-tenure owners.
- **Ladder.** It runs to homebuilding: fragmented and capped.
- **Verdict: 15/25.**
