# Sweep L5: drop "nature hides it, nobody owns it, no buyer pays at proof" (19 Oct 2026)

## Verdict

**Nothing survives.** Dropping L5 fixes the "no seller of the right" problem: a free sequence, spectrum or composition can be claimed, filed or kept as a trade secret. But L5 was hiding the real killer. Once the company has to own value by manufacturing what it found, the proving step stops being a cheap test and becomes the bespoke build: scale-up, REACH/IFRA or fab or OEM qualification, plant capex. That is the geothermal counter-example again (L1's "proofs that usually fail are the expensive bespoke build"), so C1 and C3 fall as fast as C4 rises. The few rungs where ML discovery plus owned manufacture does work are already held by heavily funded startups (L6): Periodic Labs ($300M seed), CuspAI ($450M Series B at $2.6B), Orbital Industries ($50M Series B, PFAS-free coolant), Osmo ($130M, captive odorants), Basecamp Research (metagenome enzymes), Niron (rare-earth-free magnets, $605M plant). The best idea after adversarial checks scores 10/25.

## How the rubric was rescored with L5 dropped

- **C4 (data and right):** the right no longer has to be bought. Score the illegibility of the public data, plus how defensible a first filing or trade secret is: a composition of matter on a non-natural molecule or alloy, or a process secret. Natural products and wild microbes stay weak, because anyone can re-isolate them, and Myriad and product-of-nature doctrine still apply.
- **C1 (frame):** the rung may now be "sell the product you make", not "sell the proven right". Score it on whether discovery is still 80% or more of the work once manufacturing is owned. That is usually where it collapses.
- **C2 (step-up):** measured from the cost of discovery plus proof to the value of an owned, qualified product line, or a first-filer licence that a capitalised buyer actually pays for.
- **Unchanged:** C3, C5, L1-L4, L6, the ban on prospecting and drilling, the own-don't-serve rule, the ML-essential rule and the $1B bar.

## Task 3: adversarial checks on the top 3

### 1. Captive Scent plus own manufacture (old #1, 15 -> 10)

- **Incumbent with the same thesis:** Osmo has raised $130M in total, including a $70M Series B in Feb 2026 led by Two Sigma Ventures ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260204293785/en/Osmo-Digital-Scent-Design-Company-Announces-%2470M-in-Series-B-Funding-and-Executive-Leadership-Team-to-Scale-AI-Powered-Olfaction-Technology)).
- **No observable price for the step-up:** Osmo is auctioning exclusive perpetual licences to 10 AI-found captives. The auction launched 23 June 2026 with about 12 weeks of diligence before binding bids, and no clearing prices are public as of 19 Oct 2026 ([Cosmetics Business](https://cosmeticsbusiness.com/osmo-to-auction-ten-ai-developed-fragrance-ingredients), [BeautyMatter](https://www.beautymatter.com/articles/osmo-auctions-off-its-captives)). The first market for the rung exists and belongs to the incumbent, and its prices are unknown.
- **Time to product:** Osmo itself says a captive takes 7-10 years from discovery to use, and Givaudan needed 19 synthesis routes for Pomarose ([Osmo blog](https://osmo.ai/blog/osmo-uses-ai-to-discover-never-before-smelled-ingredients), [Chemistry World](https://www.chemistryworld.com/news/trifluoromethylation-made-easy/3004856.article)).
- **What breaks:** owning manufacture means owning scale-up, RIFM safety and REACH, the bespoke years. Even then the only buyers are the same 4-5 houses, which are a monopsony and run captive programmes in-house. So vertical integration does not escape the buyer; it only delays the sale. L6 plus the bespoke-build law.
- **Rescore:** C1 2, C2 2, C3 3, C4 2, C5 1 = **10**.

### 2. Germanium-free IR chalcogenide optics maker (new N1, 14 -> 9)

- **Demand is real:** under USGS MCS 2026, Ge metal went from $3,150 to $5,380/kg between Jan and Oct 2025 ([USGS](https://pubs.usgs.gov/periodicals/mcs2026/mcs2026-germanium.pdf)). The FY2026 NDAA directs DoD off optical glass from covered nations by 1 Jan 2030 (as cited by LightPath).
- **The incumbent proves manufacture, not discovery, earns the value:** LightPath's Ge-free BlackDiamond glass drove its business.
  - Q4 FY2026 revenue was $21.2M, up 73% year on year.
  - Backlog was $110.6M, up 196%.
  - It bought Amorphous Materials in Jan 2026 to double its glass-melting capacity.
  - It targets $300M+ in revenue within 5 years, and its market cap is about $680M.
  - Sources: [Q4 FY26 release](https://seekingalpha.com/pr/20647775), [Q3 FY26 release](https://www.streetinsider.com/PRNewswire/LightPath+Technologies+Reports+Fiscal+2026+Third+Quarter+Financial+Results/26452895.html), [Ge-free G5 cameras](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/33885591/lightpath-technologies-announces-first-germanium-free-g5-camera-variants-utilizing-proprietary-blackdiamond-glass), [Yahoo LPTH](https://es.finance.yahoo.com/quote/LPTH/community/).
- **Discovery is already done:** Ge-free compositions have been catalogue items for years. Schott's IRG26 is As40Se60 and Ge-free, and Edmund sells it as a Ge substitute ([Schott IRG26](https://schott.com/shop/advanced-optics/en/IRG-26/c/glass-IRG26)). - **What breaks:** the hard parts are high-purity melting, moulding yield and defence qualification, not finding a composition. So ML's lever is about 1x (C3 fails). A new entrant is a capex-heavy optics manufacturer behind LightPath, Schott, Umicore and RPO (L6).

## Task 4: what kills it instead of L5

1. **The bespoke build (L1's second half, the geothermal counter-example).** Vertical integration past the proof means the proof becomes scale-up plus customer qualification, years long and expensive. ML shortens discovery, which is now under 30% of the work, so C1 is 1-2 and C3 is 1-3. The step-up the rubric wants happens at qualification, not at discovery.
2. **L6, the ladder.** Every attractive "ML-discover then manufacture" rung we found has a funded holder: Orbital (coolants, sorbents), CuspAI and Periodic (general materials), Osmo (odorants), Basecamp (enzymes), Niron (magnets), LightPath (Ge-free optics), SES AI and Aionics (electrolytes).
3. **First-filer IP rarely gets a step-up price.** No public transaction shows a capitalised buyer paying a large sum for an unscaled filed composition. Osmo's auction is the first test, and its prices are undisclosed. Buyers still pay after qualification.

**Most interesting finding:** where demand is policy-shocked (China's controls on Ge and Sb, PFAS bans), the value goes to whoever owns the melt or the reactor, not to whoever finds the composition. LightPath went from small-cap to about $680M with a $110.6M backlog selling a Ge-free glass whose chemistry is decades old. When a known substitute exists, ML discovery has no lever. When none exists, the proof is still the plant. The demand shock can also fade quickly: antimony fell from $63 back to about $27/kg once the export ban was eased.
