# Round 2: wildcards around the exploratory drill hole (ideas the lanes missed)

Written 19 Oct 2026. Follows BRIEF.md. Builds on lanes A-H and the r2 files (royalty fund, geothermal, groundwater, verification).
Currency is given each time. "(memory)" = from prior knowledge, not re-verified this session. "(estimate)" = my arithmetic or judgement.
New primary measurement this round: I queried the live Saskatchewan drillhole compilation (33,490 records) to count historic
holes that appear to stop short of the unconformity (section 3).

## 10-line summary
1. **Nothing here survives as a venture-scale new company.** I generated 15 ideas. 12 die quickly on prior art or ceiling. The 3 I keep are worth a short follow-up, but each is either tool-sized or really a Terranox tactic.
2. **The best-shaped unowned mechanism is a "bottom-hole contribution" market for minerals.** In oil, a neighbour pays a fixed sum toward a wildcat in return for its data (AAPG; Statoil paid up to US$260k + US$175k at Bagpuss). I found no minerals equivalent and no drill-data exchange. The one hard price is KoBold paying US$175k for a package that included 13 holes (about US$13k per hole at most). Athabasca volume caps it at roughly C$3-25M a year of trades (estimate). Tool-sized.
3. **Historic holes that stopped short: there are more than I expected.** About 490 holes of 100 m or more (about 120k m drilled) have no recorded unconformity, yet holes within 2 km reach it deeper. Their median length is 192 m and the median shortfall is 84 m (my query, estimate). This is a one-time pool of C$20-60M in avoided re-drilling, and it is better used as a targeting list of "unfinished tests" inside Terranox.
4. **Covered-ground strat holes cause staking rushes.** GA/MinEx CRC drilled 10 strat holes (about 4,000 m) at East Tennant. Within about 2-3 years, around 30-33 companies had taken up 140,000-165,000 km² of new tenements. A private actor who holds the ground *before* drilling could capture that. This is drilling-based and the step-up leaks to neighbours. Third keep, weak.
5. **Government strat and TMEI money is too small to capture.** TMEI pays 25% of drilling, capped at **C$50k a year for uranium**, from a C$3.55M program for 2025-26. Kill.
6. **Flow-through is owned and on a clock.** FT LP managers (Maple Leaf C$26.2M for its 2025 fund, Middlefield C$14.7M, Ninepoint over C$43M for 2024) already package the subsidy. The 15% METC and the 30% CMETC both lapse on **31 Mar 2027** unless extended. An "FT fund that owns ground" is just an explorer, which is Terranox. Kill.
7. **Dry-hole or overrun insurance exists only for geothermal, and only with a state backstop** (Munich Re + KfW, Dec 2025). Mineral exploration has no actuarial base and a severe moral hazard. Kill.
8. **Shallow proxies (radon and helium) are a 40-year-old service.** RadonEx is the Athabasca standard and was used at Triple R. Kill.
9. **Core robotics is owned or tool-sized**: CSIRO HyLogger, KORE SPECTOR, Boart FREEDOM, Imdex. Kill. Oilfield transfers either already happened (MWD/LWD → Imdex/Devico, coiled tubing → MinEx CRC) or fail on ceiling (turnkey per-hole contracts, AFE benchmarking, multi-client libraries).
10. **Against the 3 Oct ban:** keep #1 (data market) never drills, so it sits inside the rule. Keep #2 (stopped-short holes) and keep #3 (strat-hole land play) are drilling-based. Bottom line for Dhruv: the round-1 verdict stands. The value is in owning the right, and every route to the drill-hole pain point leads back to the Terranox/KoBold model.

## 2. KEEP #1: a bottom-hole contribution / drill-data exchange for minerals

**The idea.** Each Athabasca hole tests a conductor that usually runs onto neighbouring claims. Its log (alteration, clay species, structure, graphite, depth to unconformity, drilling difficulty) is worth something to the neighbours, and most of all when it is a miss, because a miss lets a neighbour drop a hole. Oil has priced this for a century:
- **Dry-hole contribution**: paid only if the well is dry, for all the well data.
- **Bottom-hole contribution**: paid whatever the result.
- **Acreage contribution**: land in place of cash.

Source: [AAPG wiki](https://wiki.aapg.org/Special:MobileDiff/14073), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry-hole_clause), [HMRC OT13875](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/oil-taxation-manual/ot13875). At Bagpuss (North Sea), Statoil paid up to US$260k for drilling data plus up to US$175k for test data ([Offshore](https://www.offshore-mag.com/regional-reports/article/16769498/north-sea-bagpuss-partners-enter-into-data-deal)).

**Prior art in minerals.**
- **None as a market.** Data tools are internal (Seequent's [MX Deposit](https://investors.bentley.com/news-releases/news-release-details/bentley-systems-announces-seequents-acquisition-minalytix), Centric Explore).
- **One-off sales exist:**
  - Alaska Energy Metals sold KoBold a package for **US$175k**. It was clipped to KoBold's claims and included 13 drill holes' logs and assays plus geochem, airborne and maps, so the price is at most about US$13k per hole ([Global Mining Review](https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/30112023/alaska-energy-metals-sells-exploration-data-to-kobold-metals)).
  - Hannans sold its WA database for a free-carried 20% convertible to a 2% NSR ([Mining Weekly](https://www.miningweekly.com/article/hannans-sells-exploration-database-2015-07-02)).
  - AngloGold has sold core and results to explorers ([Creamer](https://newsletter.mw.creamermedia.com/article/anglogold-ashanti-to-release-exploration-data-for-research-purposes-sale-to-explorers-2021-03-24)).

**Numbers (estimate).**
- **Volume:** Athabasca drilling is about 250k m a year (lane A bottom-up, r2_verification) at about 450 m per hole, or about 550 holes a year.
- **Price per hole:** the cost avoided by a neighbour who drops a hole is C$200-550k (lane A). A fair data price is a fraction of that, C$20-50k, consistent with the KoBold ≤US$13k-per-hole comp at the low end.
- **Market:** if 30% of holes trade to 1-3 buyers, GMV is C$3-25M a year. A 15% take gives **C$0.5-4M a year of revenue**. Globally, S&P counts about 56k reported holes a year (lane B). Even 2% trading at US$20k is about US$22M GMV.

**Why it is hard.**
- (a) Juniors live on promotion. Selling negative data to a rival can be a material event they would have to disclose, and it tells the rival where *not* to bid.
- (b) Disclosure rules already force good news public, so the exchange trades mostly negatives.
- (c) The data stays confidential for at most 3 years in Saskatchewan anyway, and becomes free at once when a claim lapses (r2_verification), so buyers can wait.
- (d) There are few buyers per hole (1-3 neighbours).

**Gold rubric (each item /10):**

| Item | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Discovery-heavy rung | 6 | The physical step (drilling) is done by others |
| 2 Illegible public data, ML load-bearing | 5 | Only for parsing and normalising logs, the lane F W1 dependency |
| 3 Cheap asset | 6 | Negative data is near-worthless to its owner |
| 4 Buyer pays a step-up | 3 | Buyers can wait 3 years for free data |
| 5 Ladder | 4 | Exchange → data-funded generator, which is Terranox again |
| 6 Ceiling | 1 | |
| **Total** | **25/60** | |

**Deeper look worth doing (one day):** ask 3-4 Athabasca juniors whether they would pay C$20-50k for a neighbour's hole before collaring, and whether they would sell theirs. If yes, it is a **Terranox acquisition channel** (Terranox as the buyer of neighbours' misses, paid partly in NSR, the Hannans form), not a new company.
**Ban:** inside the rule; it never drills.

## 3. KEEP #2: historic holes that stopped short (new measurement)

**Method (my query, 19 Oct 2026).**
1. Pulled all 33,490 records from the Saskatchewan drillhole layer ([MapServer/3](https://gis.saskatchewan.ca/arcgis/rest/services/Economy/Mineral_Exploration/MapServer/3)). Its fields include `BASE_OF_ATHABASCA_SG_DEPTH_M`, `TOP_CRYSTALLINE_BSMT_DEPTH_M`, `TOTAL_DH_LENGTH_M`, `WEDGE_HOLE` and `COREAVAILABILITY`.
2. Flagged a hole as "likely stopped short" when all three of these hold:
   - it has neither a base-of-Athabasca nor a basement depth;
   - at least 2 holes within 2 km record a base of Athabasca deeper than 50 m;
   - the hole is shorter than their median unconformity depth.

**Results (estimate):**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Holes flagged (any length) | 1,296; about 159k m drilled; about 182k m still to go |
| Holes ≥100 m flagged | **493** |
| Metres drilled in the ≥100 m holes | **about 120k m** |
| Median length (≥100 m holes) | **192 m** |
| Median shortfall to the local unconformity | **84 m** |
| Median local unconformity depth | 370 m |
| Of the ≥100 m holes, shortfall under 200 m | about 345 |
| Wedge holes among them | 24 |
| Largest holders | Cameco 82, Hudson Bay Exploration 66, Denison 37, Uranerz 31, AREVA 22 |
| By decade | 2010s 139, 1980s 88, 2000s 78, 1990s 57 |

Core is held in the library for only about 730 holes in the whole compilation (`COREAVAILABILITY` set, mostly 481 "Complete core"). So re-logging legacy core is not an option for most of these holes.

**Caveats.**
- A missing depth may mean "not compiled" rather than "not reached". The 139 holes from the 2010s are suspicious on that count, though they also fit holes lost in altered sandstone, the CanAlaska Cree East problem (lane F).
- Many of the holes sit on Cameco/Orano ground today (memory).
- **True re-entry of a decades-old diamond hole is unproven here.** Casing is usually pulled, collars are lost, and holes through mineralisation are typically plugged (memory; I found no Saskatchewan rule this session). Nobody has documented an Athabasca re-entry (lane F, this round).

**Economics (estimate).**
- **Saving per hole if re-entry works:** about 190 m of avoided drilling at C$300-550/m (lane A drilling-line to all-in), so C$60-105k, less re-entry cost.
- **If re-entry fails,** you collar a new hole anyway, and the value is purely informational: a cheap list of *untested* conductors where an earlier operator gave up 84 m short.
- **The whole pool is one-time:** about 345 holes × about C$100k gives about C$35M (range C$20-60M), spread over many owners.

**Gold rubric (each item /10):**

| Item | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Discovery-heavy rung | 5 | |
| 2 Illegible public data, ML load-bearing | 6 | Reading why each hole stopped needs the SMAD free text |
| 3 Cheap asset | 6 | Where the ground has lapsed |
| 4 Buyer pays a step-up | 5 | Only on a hit |
| 5 Ladder | 3 | |
| 6 Ceiling | 1 | |
| **Total** | **26/60** | |

**Verdict.** This is not a company. It is a cheap, concrete **Terranox targeting feature**: rank the 493 holes by why they stopped (SMAD text) and by whether the ground is open on MARS.
**Ban:** drilling-based, though the screening step is pure data.

## 4. KEEP #3: private covered-ground strat-hole land play ("pre-staked East Tennant")

**Evidence that strat holes move land.**
- MinEx CRC's East Tennant campaign drilled 10 stratigraphic holes, nearly 4,000 m ([MinEx CRC](https://minexcrc.com.au/news/press-release-1-drilling-program-unlocks-hidden-mineral-potential-in-the-east-tennant/)).
- GA reported about 30 companies taking up 140,000 km² of new NT tenements by Feb 2022 ([Stockhead](https://stockhead.com.au/resources/theres-been-an-explosion-of-exploration-activity-across-the-northern-territory/); [GA EFTF](https://www.eftf.ga.gov.au/news/data-geoscience-australia-underpins-massive-exploration-activity-across-northern-territory)).
- The minister later cited 33 companies and over 165,000 km² ([minister](https://www.minister.industry.gov.au/ministers/pitt/media-releases/national-drilling-initiative-reveals-golden-opportunity-northern-australia)).
- These are government and promotional figures, and the uptake also followed the 2019 geophysics, not only the holes.
- The NDI repeats this in SA (Delamerian, Northern Gawler) and WA (Moonera), with CC-BY data releases ([SARIG](https://catalog.sarig.sa.gov.au/dataset/mesac109)).

**The private version.** Take up the whole covered region first (Australian ELs; the Athabasca analogues are the deep basin centre and the Phanerozoic-covered margins). Drill 5-10 strat holes, release the results selectively, then farm out blocks into the demand. **No private precedent found** (search, this round).

**Why it is weak.**
- (a) Value leaks: competitors simply stake the open ground next to yours, and tenure size per holder is capped (memory).
- (b) A strat hole proves *prospectivity*, not a deposit. Buyers pay an earn-in, not a cash step-up (lane E: C$9-22M of spend for 51-80%).
- (c) It competes with free government data in Australia.
- (d) The cost per strat hole is uncosted here. At Athabasca depths (more than 800 m in the basin centre) it is C$0.5-1M a hole (lane A, Hawk C$1.02M per hole).

**Gold rubric (each item /10):**

| Item | Score | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Discovery-heavy rung | 5 | |
| 2 Illegible public data, ML load-bearing | 4 | Cover targeting is mostly geophysics |
| 3 Cheap asset | 8 | Very cheap ground |
| 4 Buyer pays a step-up | 4 | |
| 5 Ladder | 5 | |
| 6 Ceiling | 3 | A generator, at best Orogen-sized: C$421M exit, lane E |
| **Total** | **29/60** | |

**Ban:** drilling-based. It is also the closest of the three to "Terranox in another basin".

## 6. Blunt verdict
Nothing in the wildcard set is a new very-large company. The three keeps are, in order of usefulness to Dhruv:
- **Keep #2: the stopped-short hole list.** Free, concrete, and a Terranox feature.
- **Keep #1: a neighbour data-buying channel.** A Terranox tactic, paid in NSR. It would be a company only if juniors prove willing to trade, which I doubt.
- **Keep #3: a pre-staked strat-hole land play.** A generator, drilling-based, and leaky.

Every idea that captures a real step-up does it by holding the right, which is the conclusion of rounds 1 and 2. Recommend closing the drill-hole hunt as "no separate company; fold #1 and #2 into Terranox".
