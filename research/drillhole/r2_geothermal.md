# Round 2: geothermal. Can a new company own a different rung than Zanskar?

Written 19 Oct 2026. Follows BRIEF.md. Builds on lane_G_adjacent_holes.md (section 1).
Currency is USD unless marked CAD. "(memory)" means not re-sourced this round; "(estimate)" means my
arithmetic or judgement.

## Summary (the 10 lines that matter)
1. **Zanskar is bigger than round 1 thought.** It claims more than 100 projects, more than 250k acres
   under site control across six states, and more than 300k ft drilled. It has $180M of equity plus a
   $40M development-capital facility (which can grow to $100M) and a 100 MW PPA with NV Energy. It is
   US-only and builds and operates its own plants. Nothing found shows it selling resources.
2. **Prove-and-sell pays badly.** Proven but undeveloped resources trade at about **$0.1-0.3M per MW in
   cash**:
   - the Geysers sale: $6.4M for 26 MW net with 5 drilled wells, about $0.25M/MW;
   - Ormat's farm-in at Crump Geyser: $2.5M cash plus a $15M carry for 50%;
   - EDC's farm-in to Alterra's Chile and Peru assets: "no premium", carry only.

   An operating plant sells for about $4.4M/MW. Nearly all of the step-up comes after the resource is
   proven, at the PPA and plant stage. That is why Zanskar builds its own plants.
3. **Proving one site costs about $6-13M (estimate).** That covers a gradient-well program of $1-5M and
   one or two full-size wells at $4-8M each, and the first well has about a 50% hit rate. Selling the
   result at $0.25M/MW needs a resource of roughly 50 MW or more, or a hit rate well above 50%, just to
   break even. Rubric 4 is weak.
4. **Drilling is about 85-95% of exploration spend (estimate).** Surface exploration costs $1-2M,
   against $15-25M for the whole exploration phase including test wells. Test drilling is up to 15% of
   total project capex. Thermal-gradient holes cost $100k-1M each, and a slim hole costs 25-35% of a
   full-size well of the same depth.
5. **Hyperscaler demand is real and growing, but almost all of it is in the US:**
   - Google: about 115 MW at Corsac Station with Fervo, a 150 MW PPA with Ormat, a reported 396 MW
     Cape Station deal with Fervo, and a 3 GW framework agreement with Fervo that the S-1 calls
     non-binding;
   - Meta: 150 MW with Sage and 150 MW with XGS;
   - Switch: 13 MW from Ormat.

   This demand is why US lease prices went from about $4/acre (Fervo, 2019-21) to $1,207/acre (top
   bid, Utah 2026). The cheap right (rubric 3) is going away in the US.
6. **US lease flipping is already crowded.** At the October 2025 Nevada sale, Invenergy won 11 parcels,
   Cordillera 10, Skyladder Capital 10, Ormat 8, Navram 8 and Zanskar only 2. Financial landholders are
   already in the land rung.
7. **The geographies outside the US are poor for prove-and-sell:**
   - BC: no permit sale since 2014.
   - Alberta: a $3.50/ha lease regime, but almost no applications in 2026, sedimentary low-temperature
     resources and no hyperscaler buyer.
   - Chile: 330k ha of concessions held, about one project; holding is cheap and speculative, and few
     buyers come.
   - Peru: no plants.
   - Mexico: concessions are now awarded directly by the energy ministry, and Carso got the first one.
   - Kenya: the state company GDC proves steam itself and sells it.
   - Indonesia: a single buyer (the state utility PLN) and a tariff ceiling.
   - Philippines: a draft circular requires 60% Filipino ownership.
8. **Turkey is the one market that looks like a real licence market.** It issued 412 exploration
   licences in 2024. The state survey agency MTA auctions explored fields at estimated prices of
   $0.5-1.7M each, and Margün bought 9 licences (505 MWt) in 2026. The buyers are domestic, and
   tariffs and the currency are a risk.
9. **Score.** The US prove-and-sell rung scores about 3.5/6 on the rubric and fails rubric 4 on price.
   The international versions score 2-3.5/6. None of them beats "Zanskar but somewhere else", and that
   fails rubric 4 or 6 everywhere except perhaps Turkey.
10. All of this is inside the 2026-10-03 ban (geothermal is named). Recommendation: **do not pursue**
    as a new company. The step-up sits at the plant, not at the proven resource, so the only big rung is
    the integrated one, and Zanskar holds it in the US.

**What the comparables show**
- The industry norm for an early resource is a **farm-in**: the buyer carries the drilling cost and
  pays little or no cash. Cash prices for a drilled resource sit around $0.1-0.3M/MW, against about
  $4.4M/MW once a plant runs with a PPA.
- The 15-40x step-up therefore sits between "proven" and "operating". The first-mover, Terranox-style
  step from "raw lease" to "flow-tested" is only about 2-10x on the money spent (estimate).
- IRENA describes plant costs as "1.8-5 USD per MW" (meaning $1.8-5M/MW) and says bankability comes
  only after a successful exploration drilling campaign
  ([IRENA](https://www.irena.org/Events/2017/Nov/Development-of-bankable-geothermal-power-projects)).

**Unit economics of a lease flipper (estimate)**
- Cost to reach a flow-tested state at one site:
  - land: $32-1,207/acre on about 4,000 acres, so $0.1-5M;
  - gradient holes: $1-5M (Ormat 10-K, via lane G);
  - one or two full-size wells: $4-8M each
    ([SLB](https://www.slb.com/products-and-services/scaling-new-energy-systems/geothermal/geothermal-exploration-drilling)).
  - **Total: about $6-15M per site.**
- The first well has about a 50% hit rate (IFC, lane G). At $0.25M/MW, a 30 MW success sells for
  $7.5M and a 100 MW success for $25M.
- At a 50% hit rate the expected value is roughly break-even to slightly positive, and only for large
  systems. Even if ML lifts the hit rate to 75%, the business is modest per site.
- The 2026 bull case: Ormat and Fervo are chasing hyperscaler PPAs, so they might pay more. Ormat's
  150 MW Google PPA is a "multi-project portfolio" coming online 2028-30
  ([Ormat IR](https://investor.ormat.com/news-events/news/news-details/2026/Ormat-Technologies-Announces-the-Signing-of-Geothermal-Portfolio-PPA-of-Up-to-150-MW-to-Support-Googles-Data-Center-Operations-Through-NV-Energy/default.aspx)).
  But no 2025-26 deal with a disclosed price for a proven, undeveloped resource was found. Treat
  higher prices as unproven.
- **Structural problem:** the buyers (Ormat, Fervo, Invenergy) all explore themselves and already hold
  land. Fervo has 256k acres of untouched "Prospects". They are competitors for the lease before they
  are customers for the resource.

**Verdict on (a):** this is a sellable rung, but the price the buyer pays for the proven state is
thin (farm-in norms). The rung is lumpy: a few sites a year, each worth $5-25M. To reach rubric 6 the
company must climb to building plants, and that puts it head to head with Zanskar.

**Drilling's share of exploration spend: about 85-95% (estimate).** Surface work costs $1-2M out of a
$15-25M exploration phase. That matches Dhruv's pattern: the expensive, usually-failing step is the
hole. But the share falls if the prove-and-sell company stops at slim holes and gradient wells, and the
buyers then discount the resource because it has no flow test from a production-size well.

## 7. Bottom line
- **(a)** Prove-and-sell is a real but thin rung. Buyers pay farm-in terms, not Terranox-style
  step-ups. The comparable at $0.25M/MW is from a distressed seller, and no 2025-26 comparable has a
  disclosed price. Hyperscaler demand helps but flows to developers, not to sellers of resources.
- **(b)** The geographies Zanskar does not cover are uncovered for good reasons: either the state owns
  discovery or there is no buyer. Turkey is the only one that warrants a follow-up.
- **(c)** Data-centre demand is US-centric and is making US leases expensive. It strengthens the
  incumbents (Zanskar, Fervo, Ormat) more than it opens a new rung.
- **Recommendation: kill as a new company.**

**Cheap checks to run before fully killing**
1. A 2025-26 price paid for any drilled, unbuilt US resource. Candidates: Ormat's 10-Q acquisition
   notes, Invenergy's entry into geothermal.
2. Whether MTA's well archives in Turkey are public and hard to read.
3. Whether Zanskar would buy proven resources from third parties, which would make it a customer
   rather than a competitor. No evidence either way.
