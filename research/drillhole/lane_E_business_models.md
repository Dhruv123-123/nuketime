## 10-line summary
1. **Drill-for-equity is real but small, and it is an Australian junior-contractor thing.** Deals run A$0.5-4M. The contractor takes 25-55% of the invoice in VWAP-priced, escrowed shares (Topdrill, Geodrill, Impact, Centurion, MMS). I found no evidence that Major Drilling or Boart Longyear do it as a strategy. The listed Canadian majors keep their contracts in cash.
2. **The generator / royalty model is the one proven way to capture the step-up without paying for the drilling.** Orogen kept a 1% NSR on Silicon/Merlin (AngloGold paid the drilling). Triple Flag bought Orogen for C$421M in 2025, and Altius turned a cost base under C$15M into about C$80M on that deal. But the long tail is tiny: Mirasol is about C$27M and Riverside about C$34M (Oct 2026).
3. **Earn-in terms in the Athabasca follow a pattern.** The partner spends about C$9-22M in cash and exploration over 3-6 years to earn 51-80%. Examples: Foremost/Denison (C$20M spend plus C$4.5M payments for 70%), Global Uranium/Cosa Astro (C$9.5M plus C$0.8M plus shares for 80%), Cameco/Uravan (C$22M for 70%). The landholder trades about 70% of the upside for about C$10-25M of drilling.
4. **Buyers pay for proven pounds at about C$9-12/lb in situ.** Paladin/Fission (PLS): C$1.14B, about C$8.7/lb of resource or C$12/lb of reserve. Rio/Hathor (Roughrider, 2011): C$654M, about C$11/lb. Denison/Fission Energy (Waterbury, 2013): about C$62.5M, about C$10/lb attributable. A distressed seller fetches less: UEC/Rio (Roughrider, 2022) was US$150M, about US$2.6/lb.
5. **The discovery step-up dwarfs the hole cost.** NexGen discovered Arrow in 2014 and is now worth C$7.93B. IsoEnergy (Hurricane) is about C$841M. On PLS ground, the Paladin price works out to roughly C$37k/ha (memory: PLS is about 31,000 ha). That supports the brief's $10,000/ha figure for drill-proven ground.
6. **The EV model (estimate):** a 5,000-ha block and a 5,000 m program, with a hit worth C$10,000/ha (C$50M). The program breaks even at a 6% hit rate if drilling costs C$600/m, 3% at C$300/m, and 1.5% at C$150/m. Cheap metres lower the hit rate needed in direct proportion. Any gain in targeting multiplies on top of that.
7. **Taking equity in someone else's junior as a cash-for-shares driller is weak.** Break-even is about a 7% hit rate (estimate). The driller gets a minority stake, dilution, escrow and selection bias (only cash-starved juniors pay in stock). Owning the target, or a royalty on a target you chose, captures 3-5x more of the step-up for the same metres.
8. **Flow-through (FT) subsidises the spender, not the driller.** In Saskatchewan, an FT investor can stack the 100% CEE deduction with the federal 30% CMETC (uranium eligible, memory) and the provincial 30% SMETC. The after-tax cost of C$1 of drilling falls to roughly C$0.2 (estimate). The issuer captures that as a share premium, and dealers and charity-structure intermediaries take a cut. A company that owns its own ground can raise FT money. A driller paid in shares cannot pass FT benefits through.
9. **Earth AI already runs the "own targets + own cheap rigs + sell to majors" model.** It raised a US$20M Series B in January 2025 and claims about US$100/m and a 75% "discovery" rate (company claim). It works in Australian greenfields, not the Athabasca. KoBold runs the well-funded version that owns targets but uses contract drillers (memory). The general model is taken; an Athabasca-specific version is not.
10. **Relation to the 3 Oct no-drilling rule.** Royalty/generator and "sell drill-ready targets for a carried interest" sit closest to the rule: you never pay for metres. Owning cheap rigs plus taking equity or royalties breaks it outright: it is a drilling company. FT-financed own-ground drilling is Terranox itself.

### How big, how it went
- The typical deal is A$0.5-1M, and the largest I found is A$4M (Brightstar). That is single-digit millions per counterparty, against a global exploration drilling market in the billions (other lanes cover market size).
- Topdrill grew from 55 to about 420 staff since 2014 "on the back of" drill-for-equity (https://stockhead.com.au/resources/driller-and-dealers-the-bold-contractor-youve-barely-heard-of-growing-was-exploration-scene/). The article reports no realised gains on the equity.
- The famous potential win: De Grey paid Topdrill partly in shares at about A$0.10-0.12 in 2016 (source above). De Grey later discovered Hemi (2019) and was taken over by Northern Star at about A$5B (memory). Whether Topdrill still held those shares is not disclosed. Treat it as the exception that keeps the practice alive, not as a measured return.
- **Major Drilling (TSX: MDI):** I found no drill-for-equity programme. Its share issuances are acquisition currency (Norex, McKay, Taurus) (https://www.majordrilling.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/11/Norex-Closing-Press-Release-Final.pdf ; https://www.majordrilling.com/wp-content/uploads/McKay-Closing-Press-Release-June-2021-Final.pdf). Its policy is cash; when juniors cannot pay, MDI cuts rigs rather than take paper (memory).
- **Energold Drilling:** held 6.98M IMPACT Silver shares, worth C$11.4M in April 2012, about C$4.7M above carrying value (https://www.mining.com/energold-strikes-record-profits-after-drilling-a-million-metres-in-2011/). I could not confirm they were drilling-for-shares. Energold later took on 12.85% convertible debt (https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en-ca/about/client-work/24425442/energold-drilling-corp-completes-135-million-private-placement-of-secured-convertible-debentures) and was delisted after financial distress around 2019-2020 (memory). Its equity book did not save it.
- **Boart Longyear:** no drill-for-equity programme found. It was owned by American Industrial Partners from 2024 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boart_Longyear).

### Adversarial read
- **Selection bias.** Juniors that can raise cash pay cash, so the paper a driller receives comes from the weakest balance sheets.
- **Share terms.** Shares are priced at VWAP with no discount, often escrowed, so the driller takes market risk on top of execution risk.
- **Minority position.** The driller does not choose the target, so its EV is the junior's hit rate, not its own.
- **Verdict.** It is a sales and financing tool for a contractor in a soft market, not a way to own the step-up. Dhruv's rubric fails on #1: the physical step is the whole business and the discovery step belongs to someone else.

## 2. "Drill contractor as discoverer": did any contractor ever own discoveries?
- **Boart Longyear's origin is the closest case.** Edmund J. Longyear drilled the Mesabi Iron Range from 1890. His first contract was for Mallmann Iron Mining (May 1891), after which he formed a contract diamond-drilling company (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boart_Longyear ; https://www.mining.com/web/boart-longyear-is-125-years-old-today/). Longyear also explored and dealt in Mesabi iron lands (memory; the Longyear family fortune came partly from iron-land interests). The company that survived was the contractor, not the land holder.
- **No modern listed contractor owns a discovery** that I could find. Majors and juniors do not want their driller as a competitor for ground. Giving a contractor a free option on the client's best results is a conflict clients avoid (estimate, consistent with the absence of examples).
- **Earth AI is the modern inversion:** a targeting company that built its own rigs, not a driller that learned targeting (section 7).
- Search for driller-turned-explorer history returned nothing useful (BC/Yukon assessment reports only). Treat the "no examples" finding as moderately confident.

## 3. Prospect generators and royalty generators

### Model
A generator stakes ground cheaply using its own targeting (often public-data reinterpretation). It options the ground to a funded partner, who pays cash and shares, funds all drilling to earn 51-80%, and leaves the generator with a carried or diluting interest plus a 1-3% NSR. The generator never pays for metres on optioned ground. It captures the step-up through the royalty and partner equity. The cost is that it gives the operator the asset's control and most of its value.

### Returns read (adversarial)
- **Power law.** EMX's value rests mostly on Timok and Caserones, and analysts put copper royalties at about 65% of fair EV (https://asymmetricresearch.substack.com/p/three-more-small-cap-names-to-watch). EMX bought Caserones rather than generating it (memory).
- **Cyclical.** Orogen's CEO says demand for generator projects tracks capital availability (https://www.newswire.com/news/orogen-royalties-announces-profitable-q1-2024-results).
- **Most generators stay small.** Mirasol and Riverside have been at it for about 20 years and sit around C$30M. In this model the outcome comes from one royalty landing on a giant, not from the number of options signed.
- **Ceiling.** One great royalty can make a C$0.4-3B company (Orogen, EMX, Altius). Royalty companies trade at about 1-2x NAV on producing assets (memory). That passes rubric #6 only if the targeting gives repeated giants.
- **Athabasca-specific generators already exist** (memory): CanAlaska (partner-funded, e.g. Cameco and Orano JVs historically), Skyharbour (prospect-generator wing), Purepoint (Orano/Cameco partnerships) and Denison itself (optioning non-core ground to Foremost and Cosa, below). The Athabasca generator niche is contested, and the incumbents all use the same public SMAD data.

## 5. Flow-through financing (Canada): who captures the subsidy
- **Mechanism.** The issuer renounces Canadian Exploration Expense (CEE) to the subscriber, who deducts 100% of it.
- **Federal credits (memory).** The 15% METC applies generally. The 30% Critical Mineral Exploration Tax Credit (CMETC) covers 15 critical minerals and applies to agreements signed 7 Apr 2022 to 31 Mar 2027 (https://www.mondaq.com/canada/income-tax/1405558/reflecting-on-flow-through-shares-as-we-wrap-up-2023). The search did not confirm that uranium is on the CMETC list. My memory says it is, and uranium juniors market FT deals as CMETC-eligible (e.g. https://webfiles.thecse.com/2026-06-04_STMN_News_Release_FT_Financing.pdf).
- **Saskatchewan.** SMETC rose from 10% to 30% in 2023, applies to all Saskatchewan FT exploration (not only critical minerals), and can be claimed alongside the federal credit (https://www.mltaikins.com/insights/saskatchewan-introduces-critical-minerals-strategy).
- **Stacked after-tax cost for a top-bracket SK investor (estimate).** Credits of about 0.30 + 0.30 on C$1, plus a deduction on the remaining pool of about 0.4 × ~47.5%, mean the investor's net cost is roughly C$0.2-0.25 per C$1 of CEE before any share value. Both credits are non-refundable, so the real figure depends on the investor's tax capacity.
- **Charity FT.** The investor donates the shares to a charity, which sells them to a pre-arranged buyer at market. The donation credit stacks on top: one Ontario example cut the after-tax cost of a C$100k gift from about C$53.6k to C$20.6k (https://www.bakertilly.ca/insights/donation-of-flow-through-shares). The promoters (PearTree) claim it costs the treasury nothing, which is self-interested (https://ourcommons.ca/Content/Committee/412/FINA/WebDoc/WD6264805/412_FINA_PBC2013_Briefs\PearTreeFinancialServices-e.pdf).
- **Who captures it.**
  - The issuer captures it as a premium over the common share price. Stallion's FT units sold at C$0.09 against C$0.085 hard units, about 6% (https://www.streetinsider.com/Globe+Newswire/Stallion+Uranium+Closes+Non-Brokered+Private+Placement+Financing/23533091.html). Charity FT premiums run 30-100%+ (memory).
  - Structurers and the end buyers of the discounted shares capture the spread.
  - The driller captures it indirectly as demand: FT money must be spent on CEE by deadline, which props up Canadian drilling demand and pricing. Example: CanAlaska's C$10M bought FT deal (https://www.newsfilecorp.com/release/233507).
- **Implication for a new company.** FT benefits flow only to an issuer spending on its own (or JV) Canadian ground. A driller paid in a junior's shares receives ordinary shares; the FT benefit went to whoever subscribed cash (memory, standard FT rule). So FT favours the "own the ground" model: in Saskatchewan, a targeting company that owns claims can fund its holes at a premium paid out of the public purse. That is exactly Terranox's position, not a new company's.

## 6. Streaming
- Streams fund construction and production for a share of metal, so they are not exploration capital. They buy proven value and cannot capture the discovery step-up. Uranium has thin streaming. URC is the specialist, buying royalties and holding physical uranium (https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tsx/URC/) (memory on its holdings).
- **Relevance.** Streamers and royalty majors (Triple Flag, Franco, Wheaton, URC) are the buyer of last resort for royalties a generator creates. Triple Flag/Orogen is the template for exiting a generated royalty (section 3). They are not a model for a new company to run at the drill hole.

## 7. Earth AI's model: owns targets, drills with own rigs (and KoBold)
- **Model.** AI targeting plus proprietary "mobile low-disturbance" rigs; it validates by drilling, then sells the rights to large miners. It raised a US$20M Series B in Jan 2025 (Tamarack Global, Cantos; YC is an existing investor). It plans 50+ sites and 100,000 m of drilling capacity at about US$100/m, and claims a 75% "discovery success rate" against a 0.5% industry average (company claims) (https://www.mining.com/?p=1170737 ; https://www.globalminingreview.com/mining/31012025/earth-ai-raises-us20-million-for-ai-driven-mineral-exploration/).
- **Deal form.** At Fontenoy it earned 80% for A$4.5M of work, including at least 3,500 m of diamond drilling, and holds an alternative royalty of up to 3% (https://earth-ai.com/projects/fontenoy ; https://stockhead.com.au/resources/legacys-thick-pge-hit-paves-way-for-jv-with-earth-ai-to-accelerate-fontenoy-exploration). It is a generator that drills its own targets and earns into third-party tenure.
- **Adversarial.**
  - The 75% "discovery" claim defines discovery loosely (mineralised intercept, not an economic deposit).
  - US$100/m is plausible for shallow Australian regolith and short holes. It does not carry over to 300-1,000 m holes through Athabasca sandstone in boggy, winter-access terrain (memory; other lanes have Athabasca $/m).
  - There is no public sale of a proven project yet (not verified).
  - KoBold Metals runs the same "own the targets" thesis with >US$1B raised, using contract rigs (memory). Its big win (Mingomba, Zambia) was a bought, already-known deposit, not a greenfield discovery (memory).
- **Verdict.** The generic model (AI targets + cheap own rigs + equity in ground) is owned by Earth AI (Australia, greenfield) and KoBold (global, capital-heavy). No one runs it specifically for unconformity uranium. Earth AI's slim-rig approach probably would not make sense at Athabasca depths (estimate).

## 8. Expected value per program: rigs + targeting + equity/royalty (all estimate)

### Inputs
- Block: 5,000 ha staked at about C$1/ha (brief). The FT subsidy is ignored.
- Program: 10 holes × 500 m = 5,000 m.
- All-in cost: C$600/m (status quo Athabasca, memory), C$300/m (halved), C$150/m (aggressive cheap-rig case).
- Step-up on a hit: C$10,000/ha × 5,000 ha = C$50M. Comps: C$37k/ha on PLS at development stage (Paladin); IsoEnergy C$841M (https://www.stockwatch.com/News/Item/Z-C!ISO-3839616/C/ISO); NexGen C$7.93B (https://stockanalysis.com/quote/tsx/NXE/).
- p = chance that a program produces a market-recognised discovery intercept.

### A. Own 100% of the ground (Earth AI / Terranox shape)
EV = p × C$50M − cost

| $/m | Program cost | p=2% | p=5% | p=10% | p=20% | Break-even p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 600 | C$3.0M | −2.0 | −0.5 | +2.0 | +7.0 | 6.0% |
| 300 | C$1.5M | −0.5 | +1.0 | +3.5 | +8.5 | 3.0% |
| 150 | C$0.75M | +0.25 | +1.75 | +4.25 | +9.25 | 1.5% |

Values in C$M. Break-even p = cost / step-up, so halving $/m halves the hit rate needed. A rubric-style 1.5-3x targeting lift on a 3-5% base takes p to 5-15%. Combined with C$300/m, a program then returns about 1.7-5x its cost (estimate). With FT in Saskatchewan, the equity-investor cost falls a further ~75% (section 5).

### B. Driller takes 50% of invoice in a third party's junior (Topdrill shape)
- Junior pre-money C$5M. Driller takes C$1.25M of the C$2.5M program (C$500/m) in shares, about 20% post-money.
- Hit: the junior re-rates to about C$50M and the stake is worth about C$10M before later dilution.
- Miss: the junior falls to about C$3M and the stake is worth about C$0.6M.
- EV = p × 10 + (1 − p) × 0.6 against C$1.25M forgone cash, so break-even p ≈ 7%.
- Haircuts: the driller cannot choose targets, so p is the junior's base rate. Selection bias pushes p lower. Escrow and illiquidity cost more. Expected value is about zero to negative unless the driller is also the targeter.

### C. Targeter drills at cost for a royalty on someone else's ground (Orogen/Earth-AI-royalty shape)
- 2% NSR on a block that becomes a 100 Mlb deposit at US$80/lb: about US$160M undiscounted gross over mine life.
- Discounted at the hit stage, it is worth perhaps US$15-40M (10+ years to production, development risk). The Silicon 1% NSR fetched about C$343M, but that was after it had become a 15 Moz resource.
- EV per program ≈ p × US$15-40M − drilling cost at cost. That is worse than owning 100% (case A), but needs no land and no FT, and can be sold to URC or a royalty major.
- Break-even p ≈ 4-10% at C$300/m.

### D. Portfolio (rung ladder)
- 20 programs/yr at C$300/m: C$30M/yr of drilling. At p = 10%, that is 2 hits/yr ≈ C$100M of step-up/yr on owned ground. The model works on paper if the targeting lift is real and the capitalised buyers below show up.
- **Main sensitivity.** The C$10,000/ha re-rate does not apply to every intercept. Most "hits" are uneconomic mineralised intercepts that re-rate a junior 2-3x for months and then fade (memory). The key unknown is the probability that a hit is a hit buyers pay for.

## 9. The capitalised buyer at each stage and what they pay

**Reading the table.**
- Majors (Cameco, Orano, Rio) mostly buy at resource stage or later, and they compete. Cameco lost Hathor and was outbid.
- At the intercept stage the buyer is the public market plus mid-tier strategic placements, not a major writing a cheque.
- The "pays a step-up at once" condition (rubric #4) therefore holds at intercept stage only through the equity market. A direct sale to a major comes at resource stage, after roughly 20,000-60,000 m more of definition drilling (memory) and 2-5 years.
- Cameco/Orano JV structures (memory): McArthur River is Cameco 69.8% / Orano 30.2%; Cigar Lake is Cameco about 54.5% / Orano 40.5% / TEPCO 5%. Both are legacy JVs formed in exploration, where partners earned in by funding. This shows how the majors historically "bought" discoveries: by funding drilling for a share, not by paying a lump sum.

## 10. Ideas scored against the rubric and the 3 Oct no-drilling rule

| Idea | Who already does it | Rubric fit | vs no-drilling rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contractor drill-for-equity in third-party juniors | Topdrill, Geodrill, Centurion, Impact, MMS (AU/W. Africa) | Fails #1 and #2: no targeting edge, minority, selection bias. Ceiling of a contractor | Breaks it (is drilling) |
| Generator: stake via ML targeting, option to funded partners, keep NSR + carried equity | Orogen (exit C$421M), EMX (US$444M), Altius; Athabasca: CanAlaska, Skyharbour, Purepoint, Denison's non-core optioning | Best fit for #1-#4 without paying for metres. Ceiling depends on royalties landing on giants. Crowded in the Athabasca on the same SMAD data | Sits inside the rule: never drills, partner drills |
| Targeter that drills its own targets with cheap rigs, keeps equity/royalty | Earth AI (AU, US$20M Series B), KoBold (contract rigs, >US$1B, memory) | Strongest EV per program if both $/m and hit rate improve. Needs C$30M+/yr capital per 20 programs | Breaks it outright |
| Drill-for-royalty: targeter funds or performs drilling on others' ground for 1-3% NSR | Earth AI's Fontenoy royalty alternative; generators' retained NSRs | Mid EV. Exit to URC or royalty majors is proven (Triple Flag/Orogen) | Breaks it (pays for metres) unless the partner pays |
| FT-financed own-ground drilling in SK | Every SK uranium junior; Terranox itself | Large subsidy (net investor cost about C$0.2-0.25 per C$1, estimate) | This is Terranox's own core, not a new company |
| Streaming at exploration stage | none (streams buy production) | Does not capture the step-up | n/a |

**Bottom line for the parent.** The way to own the discovery step-up without being a contractor is the generator/royalty model, paired with ML targeting that buyers trust. The Orogen exit (C$421M from a 1% NSR on ground a major drilled) is the existence proof.

The drill-for-equity version (own cheap rigs, take paper) only beats this if the company also picks the targets. At that point it is Earth AI, which already exists and is funded, and it breaks the no-drilling rule.

In the Athabasca, FT stacking already subsidises any targeter that owns its ground. That pushes value toward owning claims (Terranox), not toward a separate drilling-for-equity company.
