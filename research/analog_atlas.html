01What the atlas says

Nine of the twenty already have their regulatory hook

For nine opportunities the nuclear rule or programme that could carry the transfer exists today (SFCP and NEI 04-10, 10 CFR 63.114, MARSSIM, the approved reload methods, ANS-8.26, RG 1.203, ITAAC, Part 40 excursion limits, the financing gates). Those are graded direct: the work is engineering and adoption, not rule-making.

The gains cluster at 15 to 30 percent, and the outliers are owned data

Turnaround overrun, mining unit cost, North Sea decommissioning, rigless abandonment, JCL cost growth, Brugge, Twin Creeks: the independently measured gains are 10 to 30 percent. The two-fold and larger results (Rolls-Royce time on wing, McLaren changeovers, NASA's 45-to-2 percent) came from an owner of the data and the risk, or from choreography, not from software alone. The map's 'exponential' verdicts survive only where the software changes what gets built or tested, not how fast.

Every credit was preceded by a validator, a floor or a verifier

No regulator in the set credited a model directly. They credited a schema with a validator (P1), a process with a floor (P2), or a deterministic verifier around the optimiser (P3). The nuclear ask should be phrased in one of those three forms; asking the NRC to approve a model is the wrong question.

The regulator's own modellers are the rate-limiting resource

FDA's Bayesian approvals were run by FDA statisticians; Florida's commission audits code on site; ECMWF ran its own parallel. The NRC's fastest-moving asset is its own use of language models on reviews; an office of computational review at NRC would do more for the twenty than any applicant tool.

Nuclear pays the whole bill and still lacks the benchmark

NSTA's leverage came from the Treasury's share of decommissioning cost; DOE EM and the NDA pay all of it and have no per-facility public baseline. The single cheapest institutional change in the atlas is a published, annually re-estimated P50 per site.

The seeded-set protocol is one page and nobody has written it for nuclear

Knapp-Kushner, precisionFDA and the FRA test programme all reduce to: build a set with planted defects, run the machine and the certified humans, publish both scores. TRISO inspection, waste sorting and automated NDE records each need exactly that document, and it is the shortest path to a first regulatory credit for a learned classifier in nuclear.

02Ten patterns behind every successful transfer

Every case in the atlas where a regulator credited a model, a process or an unattended operation fits one of these. They are the forms a nuclear ask should take.

P1

Schema and validator first, mandate date second

Every successful structured-submission programme published the data schema and an automated validator before setting a hard deadline, and measured its first gain as day-zero rejection rather than shorter review. FDA eCTD (2017), eSTAR (2023), Singapore IFC-SG (2023 to 2026), NASA JCL Appendix J (2009), DO-331 (2011). Nuclear has the endorsement machinery (10 CFR 50.55a, RG 1.28, RG 1.203, ITAAC, GDA) and has never used it to endorse a data standard rather than a document.

Opportunities: 1, 5, 19

P2

Approve the process and a monitoring floor, never the number

Regulators that let analytics move an interval or a scope approved a methodology plus a floor that cannot be escalated, then audited the feedback loop: MSG-3 with CASS and life-limited parts, API 580/581 with corrosion-rate data, FRA track waivers with a visual minimum, Solvency II internal models with annual re-validation. NRC's Surveillance Frequency Control Program (TSTF-425, NEI 04-10) already has this shape and is the cheapest place in the whole vertical to insert condition data.

Opportunities: 7

P3

License the verifier, not the optimiser

Where an optimiser or a learned component produces the decision, the credited object is a separate deterministic check: PJM's security-constrained dispatch post-checks the mixed-integer solution, ASTM F3269 run-time assurance bounds an unassured controller with a certified monitor, FDA's predetermined change control plan pre-approves the retraining envelope rather than each model. Reload optimisation, RL plasma control and microreactor autonomy all fit this shape; the NRC-approved core-physics and safety-analysis chain is the verifier.

Opportunities: 13, 14, 18

P4

Borrow strength with a declared prior

Scarce experiments were stretched by borrowing from prior data with the borrowing weight reported as a sensitivity: FDA's robust-mixture-prior review of belimumab, the 47 Bayesian device approvals, AC 25.307-1's similar-versus-new structure taxonomy, V&V 40's credibility-by-context-of-use. Fuel qualification, criticality validation and nuclear data evaluation all have the legacy data; none has the declared-prior template.

Opportunities: 2, 9

P5

The machine must beat the qualified human on a seeded set

Automated inspection earned release authority through a head-to-head against certified inspectors on a set with planted defects: Knapp-Kushner studies for pharma visual inspection (EU GMP Annex 1, USP 1790), FRA's automated track geometry versus visual inspection, semiconductor defect classifiers judged on nuisance rate. TRISO particle inspection and decommissioning waste sorting have no such template yet, and it is a one-page protocol.

Opportunities: 4, 8

P6

Publish the baseline, re-estimate annually, name the laggards

The clearest causal evidence for cost improvement is a regulator-owned public baseline with an annual re-estimate and per-asset benchmarking: NSTA's North Sea decommissioning estimate fell 25 percent in five years, NASA's joint confidence level cut development cost growth from about 45 percent to under 2 percent, HM Treasury's optimism-bias uplifts are mandatory at business-case stage, EPA tracked 645 optimisation recommendations to closure. DOE EM and the NDA pay the whole bill and still have no per-facility public benchmark.

Opportunities: 4, 6, 11, 12, 16

P7

An ensemble of realisations, optimised against all of them

Subsurface and rare-event problems were solved by replacing the single best model with an ensemble and optimising the decision against the whole set: closed-loop reservoir management (Brugge), stochastic mine planning (COSMO), weather ensembles trained on probabilistic scores, KoBold's thousands of geological models, USACE's two-loop Monte Carlo. ISR well control, drill-hole selection, tank-waste mission planning and repository assessment are the same mathematics with different chemistry.

Opportunities: 3, 10, 11, 15, 17

P8

A delegated signatory over an automated pipeline

High-volume safety analysis was industrialised by codifying the routine tiers into software and leaving a qualified individual as signatory of record: FAA designated engineering representatives over Airbus and Boeing stress pipelines, API 579 Level 1 and 2 in fitness-for-service software, RSTRENG named in pipeline regulation. Criticality safety already qualifies engineers under ANSI/ANS-8.26; the pipeline half is missing.

Opportunities: 9

P9

Unattended inherits the attended rules

Removing people from a hazardous site succeeded where the existing goal-based safety case absorbed it without a new law: HSE treats a normally unattended installation as attended for risk accounting, Havtil consented to Oseberg H under unchanged regulations, ONRSR accredited AutoHaul as a safety-management change, UL 4600 gives the claims-arguments-evidence form. NRC's proposed Part 57 (2026) is the first nuclear rule written this way.

Opportunities: 13

P10

The regulator employs the modellers

Model credit arrived fastest where the regulator had its own modelling staff who could run the analysis themselves: FDA's Office of Biostatistics and pharmacometrics division, Florida's ten-person hurricane-model professional team doing on-site code review, ECMWF running a learned model alongside physics for sixteen months. NRC has no equivalent office for learned models; its own use of language models on reviews is the nearest thing.

Opportunities: 1, 2, 17

The machine-readable safety case

Licensing runs on prose: safety analysis reports, requests for additional information and safety evaluations are documents whose consistency is checked by reading.

Adapt

The twin

Pharma dossiers of 100,000 pages, device submissions as free-form PDFs, aircraft compliance matrices against thousands of paragraphs, and defence safety cases as narrative.

Who solved it

  • Pharma and devices (FDA). eCTD turned the dossier into an XML backbone with versioned, addressable leaves and a day-zero validator; eSTAR turned the 510(k) into a structured template that enforces completeness.
  • Aviation (FAA and EASA). Model-based development and requirement-to-evidence traceability became the certification artefact under ARP4754A and DO-331.
  • Defence and safety-critical systems. Goal-based safety cases in a claims-arguments-evidence metamodel (Def Stan 00-56, GSN, SACM, ISO 15026-2) with tool support and automated evidence collection (DARPA ARCOS).
  • Building control (Singapore). One openBIM model checked concurrently by seven agencies against executable rules, with a 15-working-day service level.

How, and how much

  • FDA eCTD (ICH M2/M8): mandatory for NDA, ANDA and BLA on 5 May 2017 and commercial INDs in 2018 under FD&C Act section 745A; about 800,000 electronic submissions a year; adoption near 100 percent in North America. independent
  • The first measured effect was earlier failure, not faster review: a 2019 FDA study found more than 20 percent of 2016 to 2018 applications had format errors that were a top cause of refuse-to-file, and FDA delayed the master-file deadline because uptake data predicted high rejection rates. Lesson: ship the validator before the mandate. independent
  • CDISC study data standards, binding for studies started after December 2016: FDA's JumpStart service runs automated fitness checks before human review; the 2006 CDISC and Gartner business case put the resource reduction at up to 60 percent per trial. independent
  • FDA eSTAR, mandatory for all 510(k)s from 1 October 2023, replaced the refuse-to-accept checklist with a 15-day technical screening because the template enforces completeness. independent
  • FAA AC 20-174 (ARP4754A), AC 20-115D (DO-178C with DO-331 model-based supplement), AC 25.307-1 (analysis supported by test evidence) and AC 20-146A (seat certification by validated modelling); EASA accepted multibody simulation in place of physical functional tests on A350-1000 control surfaces. independent
  • Singapore CORENET e-PlanCheck (2006) automated about 92 percent of building-code rules; CORENET X (soft launch December 2023, mandatory for all projects from 1 October 2026) claims up to 20 percent shorter approval time. independent
  • Structured assurance cases: Def Stan 00-56 (2004 and 2007), OMG SACM 2.x, ISO/IEC/IEEE 15026-2:2022; Adelard is running NRC-sponsored research on an assurance-case framework for digital instrumentation and control, and two UK generic design assessment applicants used ASCE for safety-assessment-principle mapping. independent

The precedent

  • FD&C Act section 745A(a) and the FDA eCTD guidance (2017, 2018 dates)
  • FDA eSTAR final guidance (September 2022, effective 1 October 2023)
  • FAA AC 20-174, AC 20-115D, AC 25.307-1, AC 20-146A
  • UK Def Stan 00-56 Issue 4 (2007)
  • OMG SACM 2.1 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 15026-2:2022
  • Singapore Building Control Act and BCA circular APPBCA-2023-02 on CORENET X

Existing nuclear hook

NRC's own skeleton already exists (RG 1.206 and the NUREG-0800 review-plan chapter structure, ITAAC, the technology-inclusive framework in NEI 18-04 and RG 1.233); ONR's claims-arguments-evidence expectation in its safety assessment principles is the same shape as SACM.

What transfers

  • An 'eSAR' with an XML backbone, section-level lifecycle operators (new, replace, append, delete) and a day-zero validator is a direct port of eCTD onto the standard review plan chapter structure.
  • ARP4754A-style requirement-to-evidence traceability maps onto ITAAC and onto ONR's claims-arguments-evidence; SACM is a ready metamodel.
  • The eSTAR lesson: completeness enforced by the template removes the acceptance-review loop that today takes months.
  • CORENET's rule: the regulator must own the schema and the executable rule set, or nobody trusts the checker.

What does not

  • FDA sees thousands of submissions a year, so reviewers have pattern data and statutory review clocks (PDUFA); NRC sees a handful a decade, so the tooling return must come from the applicant side.
  • Aviation's simulation credit rests on decades of flight-test-calibrated models per aircraft family; severe-accident and seismic codes have nothing equivalent, and RG 1.203 EMDAP is the nearest thing.
  • In every precedent the applicant keeps full liability and the model earns credit only against the regulator's own acceptance cases.

Barriers

NRC has no eCTD-style validator and ADAMS is a document store, not a data store; reviewers at NRC and ONR are trained on prose; there is no nuclear information schema to validate against; the standards bodies whose rules would be encoded sell PDFs.

  • FDA CDER Office of Business Informatics alumni. how the validator and JumpStart were built and rolled out
  • Boeing and Airbus DER and ODA holders. ARP4754A and DO-331 model-based certification
  • Adelard, University of York, NCC Group. assurance-case engineering, ASCE, GSN
  • DARPA ARCOS performers (SRI, GrammaTech, Galois). automated evidence collection into assurance cases
  • BCA CORENET X team, Solibri, novaCITYNETS. regulator-owned executable rule sets over a model schema

Fuel qualification by design of experiments

Every advanced fuel waits on a few irradiation capsules and hot-cell slots; a campaign is a multi-year batch and the licensing case is built on dozens of specimens.

Adapt

The twin

Every patient-trial slot is scarce and slow; every wind-tunnel, sled and flight test is expensive; a fracture-critical engine disc cannot be tested to failure enough times to set a life.

Who solved it

  • Pharma (FDA). Bayesian borrowing from prior data and adaptive trials designed by simulation; model-informed drug development replaces whole studies with physiologically based models.
  • Aerospace (FAA, EASA, NASA). Certification by analysis replaces selected physical tests with validated simulation inside a hybrid test pyramid; probabilistic damage tolerance sets inspection intervals from a fracture-mechanics risk model.
  • Vehicle safety (Euro NCAP, UNECE). Virtual testing accepted for whole load-case families, anchored by a few physical tests and spot-checked.

How, and how much

  • FDA approved intravenous belimumab for paediatric lupus (April 2019) on a 93-patient trial that was not powered on its own; FDA's own reviewers ran a robust-mixture-prior Bayesian analysis borrowing from adult phase 3 data. Between 1998 and 2022 about 47 device approvals were supported by Bayesian statistics. independent
  • FDA's model-informed drug development pilot: 34 requests, 30 granted by early 2021; sponsors reported six cases where modelling eliminated studies, time savings up to two years, and three programmes reporting 30 to 70 million dollars saved (Madabushi et al., 2021). independent

The twin

  • Norwegian Petroleum Directorate pressure for data-driven improved recovery (ERT is partly its product)

Existing nuclear hook

What does not

  • ISR needs coupled reactive transport rather than two-phase Darcy flow; reactions make the forward model 10 to 100 times costlier, so ensembles must be smaller or surrogate-based.

  • Albemarle and SQM hydrogeology. calibrated brine-field models under a cap

Learned plasma controllers work on research machines inside conventional interlocks; first-of-a-kind power plants need sim-to-real transfer and a way to credit a learned controller.

Partial

The twin

Certifying a learned component in aviation; bounding an unassured controller with a certified monitor; multidisciplinary design with cost in the loop.

Who solved it

  • Aviation learning assurance (EASA, FAA). A W-shaped learning-assurance process for assistance-level AI, an explicit split between learned (frozen) and learning (in-operation) systems, and run-time assurance as a means of compliance.
  • Medical devices (FDA). A predetermined change-control plan pre-authorises retraining within an envelope.
  • Aerospace multidisciplinary design (NASA OpenMDAO). Gradient-based coupling of aerodynamics, propulsion, structures and mission with cost as an objective.

How, and how much

  • DeepMind and EPFL (Nature, February 2022): a reinforcement-learning policy trained in the FGE simulator drove all 19 TCV coils to produce and hold new plasma shapes; the 2023 to 2024 follow-up reports up to 65 percent better shape accuracy in simulation and three times faster task learning, validated on TCV. The policy sat inside conventional plant interlocks and no regulator was involved. independent
  • EASA AI Concept Paper Issue 2 (March 2024) gives usable guidance for levels 1 and 2 via the W-shaped process (data management, learning-process verification, inference-model verification, independent data validation); Daedalean's ML traffic detector has passed two of four stage-of-involvement audits under DO-178C and no safety-critical ML function is yet certified. independent
  • FAA Roadmap for AI Safety Assurance (July 2024): use existing processes, treat AI as an algorithm, distinguish learned from learning, and for learning AI 'it is not possible to qualify each version'. ASTM F3269-21 run-time assurance is an accepted means of compliance for unmanned aircraft. independent
  • FDA predetermined change control plan guidance (December 2024, FD&C section 515C): a submission may pre-authorise future model changes with a modification protocol and impact assessment. independent
  • OpenMDAO (NASA Glenn, 2010 onward) underpins X-57, urban-air-mobility and turbofan studies; Rolls-Royce and Airbus value-driven design puts lifecycle cost in the objective. independent

The precedent

  • EASA AI Concept Paper Issue 2 (2024) and RMT.0742

  • ASTM F3269-21 run-time assurance
  • RTCA DO-178C with DO-331 and DO-333
  • FDA PCCP guidance (2024) under FD&C section 515C

Existing nuclear hook

Fusion in the US is regulated under a byproduct-material framework (NRC decision of 2023, 10 CFR Part 30), so the control-credit question is lighter than for fission; the plant interlock and the deterministic disruption-mitigation system are the natural certified monitor.

What transfers

  • A frozen policy plus a deterministic run-time monitor with a licensed fallback can be credited with the monitor carrying the safety claim.
  • A predetermined change-control envelope for retraining between campaigns.
  • OpenMDAO-style coupling of plasma, coils, neutronics and cost for inverse design.

What does not

  • A continuously learning controller cannot be credited under any of the three regimes; all stop at pre-specified envelopes.
  • Tokamak disruptions are faster than any human loop and the monitor must be as capable as the policy on the failure modes that matter, which is untested outside research devices.

The twin

Design optimisation with cost and licensability in the objective

Reactor design is siloed by discipline and cost is estimated afterwards from data nobody shares, so the design that is cheapest to build and license is never the one optimised for.

Partial

The twin

Aircraft and spacecraft design had exactly the two defects: discipline silos and no trusted bottom-up cost model; chips had to put yield and cost into the same objective as performance.

Who solved it

  • Aerospace multidisciplinary design optimisation (NASA OpenMDAO, DoD CADE, NASA CADRe). An open gradient-based framework coupling disciplines, plus a statutory duty to deposit actual costs so cost-estimating relationships can be regressed.
  • Semiconductor design-technology co-optimisation (imec, TSMC). Power, performance, area, cost and manufacturable yield in one objective with design rules as constraints.
  • Design for manufacture (Boothroyd Dewhurst, SpaceX, GM and Autodesk). Part-count and process-driven design loops with measured cost cuts.

How, and how much

  • OpenMDAO (NASA Glenn, 2010 onward) is standard in conceptual aircraft design and underpins X-57 and turbofan studies; commercial equivalents are Siemens HEEDS, Ansys optiSLang and Dassault Isight. independent
  • DoDI 5000.73 and DoDM 5000.04 force cost and software data reports on every programme above 100 million dollars into CAPE's CADE repository; NASA's CADRe documents at each milestone go into the ONCE portal. Trust in cost models came from a statutory data duty plus independent estimating offices, not from a clever model. independent
  • imec's roadmap credits design-technology co-optimisation with sustaining the per-node 0.7 shrink (about 50 percent area, 35 percent cost per device) after pitch scaling stalled; universal at 7 nm and below. independent
  • GM and Autodesk seat bracket (2018): 40 percent lighter, 20 percent stronger, eight parts to one. Boothroyd Dewhurst Xerox case: part count down 41 percent, manufacturing cost down 45 percent. The precedent

  • DoDI 5000.73 and DoDM 5000.04 (cost and software data reporting, 100 million dollar threshold)

Existing nuclear hook

DOE's ARDP, GAIN and NRIC awards are the place a CADRe-like duty to deposit actual costs could attach; NQA-1 design control is the documentation regime an automated design loop must satisfy.

What transfers

  • Coupling neutronics, thermal-hydraulics, structures, cost and manufacturability through gradients.
  • A CADRe-like duty on DOE-funded projects to deposit actual costs.
  • Design-for-manufacture part-count metrics for vessel internals, piping and instrumentation.

What does not

  • Aerospace regresses cost relationships from hundreds of comparable programmes; the West has fewer than twenty reactor builds since 1990.
  • Licensability has no continuous surrogate: an NRC finding is a step function, so 'licensability in the objective' realistically means design-rule checks as constraints, as in chip design rules, not an objective term.

Barriers

EPC cost data are proprietary; NQA-1 design control makes automated design changes a documentation burden; the discipline codes (MCNP, RELAP) are not differentiable.

  • Boothroyd Dewhurst and SpaceX production engineering. design for manufacture

Regulator-credible surrogates for the back end

Repository performance assessment and spent-fuel characterisation need learned surrogates with calibrated uncertainty for horizons no one can observe.

Direct

The twin

A learned weather model displacing a physics code operationally; a regulator crediting a stochastic model of rare events it cannot observe within its decision horizon.

Who solved it

  • Operational weather forecasting (ECMWF, DeepMind). A learned model run side by side with the physics model for sixteen months against the same standardised scores, then made operational, with ensembles for calibrated uncertainty.
  • Florida hurricane-model certification. A standing commission with about 35 standards, a professional team doing on-site code review under trade-secret protection, and a 'replicate known events' standard; seven vendors accepted.
  • Insurance internal models (Solvency II). Approval of the model plus its governance, calibration, use test, profit-and-loss attribution and annual re-validation, never of a number.
  • Dams and levees (USACE), environmental regulation (EPA CREM). Two-loop Monte Carlo separating natural variability from knowledge uncertainty; documented model evaluation for regulatory use.

How, and how much

  • ECMWF AIFS Single: pre-operational from October 2023, operational 25 February 2025 after sixteen months in parallel with IFS: 5 to 15 percent lower medium-range error, about 10 percent precipitation gain, tropical-cyclone track gains up to 20 percent, about a thousand times less energy per forecast. GraphCast (Science 2023) beat HRES on 90.3 percent of 1,380 targets. independent
  • Florida Commission on Hurricane Loss Projection Methodology (statute section 627.0628, since 1995): standards revised every odd year, models re-certified against them, seven accepted vendors, and since 2023 insurers may average two or more accepted models. independent
  • Solvency II Articles 120 to 125: use test, statistical quality, calibration to a 99.5 percent one-year value at risk, profit-and-loss attribution, validation, documentation. independent
  • USACE ER 1110-2-1156 (2014) risk-informed portfolio and RMC tolerable-risk guidelines with nested two-loop Monte Carlo; EPA CREM guidance (2009) for models in regulatory decisions. independent
  • Credibility frameworks: NASA-STD-7009B (2024) eight-factor scale; ASME V&V 10, 20 and 40; FDA adopted V&V 40 in its November 2023 guidance. independent

The precedent

  • Florida Statutes section 627.0628 and the biennial hurricane standards
  • Directive 2009/138/EC Articles 120 to 125 and Delegated Regulation 2015/35 Articles 222 to 247
  • USACE ER 1110-2-1156; EPA/100/K-09/003
  • NASA-STD-7009B; ASME V&V 10-2019, 20-2009, 40-2018; FDA computational-modelling credibility guidance (2023)
  • WMO verification standards used by ECMWF

Existing nuclear hook

NRC already requires probabilistic performance assessment with uncertainty quantification under 10 CFR 63.114 and reviews it under NUREG-1804; RG 1.203 is the evaluation-model template for physics codes; the SKB and Clab calorimetry data are the 'known events' a decay-heat surrogate must replicate.

What transfers

  • A V&V 40-style context-of-use credibility case for each surrogate.
  • Florida's on-site professional-team code audit and replicate-known-events standard.
  • Weather-style long parallel running of surrogate against full-physics code with published headline scores.
  • Solvency II's use test: the credited surrogate must be the one designers actually use.

What does not

  • Weather has daily ground truth; repositories have none beyond natural analogues, so acceptance must rest on emulator fidelity to the physics code plus the physics code's own validation, never on outcome data.
  • Insurance regulators accept model averaging; NRC does not.

Barriers

NRC has no surrogate-specific guidance; ML uncertainty methods (conformal prediction, deep ensembles) are unfamiliar to reviewers; waste organisations are politically paced.

Who to hire

  • Moody's RMS, KCC, Verisk Florida-submission teams. certifying a rare-event model to a standing commission
  • Lloyd's and PRA Solvency II model-validation actuaries. model governance and use tests
  • USACE Risk Management Center cadre. two-loop uncertainty
  • FDA CDRH CM&S staff and ASME V&V 40 committee members. credibility by context of use
